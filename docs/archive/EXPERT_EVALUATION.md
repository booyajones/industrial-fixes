## SEO Assessment — 6/10

### What's Working

- **Content quality is genuinely excellent.** The Carrier 13 article is better than most competitors — the "timing tells you which direction to dig" pro tip is original, practitioner-level insight that earns topical authority. This is the highest moat you have.
- **Brand hub architecture is strong.** `/brands/carrier/` aggregates 55 guides in a clean table, creating siloed topical authority and internal link equity that flows to individual code pages. This structure is correct.
- **URL naming is clean** (`/posts/carrier-13-error-code/`). Matches exact-match search queries.
- **HowTo schema + QuickAnswer boxes** give structured data signals that can drive rich snippets.
- **900+ articles** is a meaningful content corpus — but only if they're indexed and ranking. Depth matters less than coverage breadth at this stage.
- **"Updated 2026" on homepage** and "Verified by Certified Industrial Technician" badge address E-E-A-T signals.

### What's Missing

1. **No author attribution or bio on articles.** errbase.com shows "Written by Raj Patel, HVAC & Water Systems Specialist · 15 years experience. Last verified March 17, 2026." Google's E-E-A-T evaluation rewards this heavily for health/safety content (HVAC CO risk = YMYL-adjacent). Without it, you're competing at a structural disadvantage.

2. **No "last verified" date on articles.** The homepage says "Updated 2026" but individual articles show only publish dates (March 2024). A content freshness signal on each article would help Google trust the accuracy of 2-year-old guides.

3. **Brand hub descriptions are weak.** Most entries say "Causes & Fix" with no description — plain index with no contextual text. errbase.com and pickhvac.com have intro copy on brand/model pages that captures long-tail variants. This is a missed opportunity for keyword coverage.

4. **No FAQ schema on articles.** errbase.com structures their FAQ section as schema markup, which drives "People Also Ask" boxes. The Carrier 13 article has a great "Pro Tip" and "When to Call" section that could be FAQ schema — currently they're not.

5. **Article count is unclear and pagination is broken on /posts/.** The page shows only 4 recent articles with no visible pagination. If you have 900+ articles and Google can't crawl them all from the posts index, you have a crawl accessibility problem.

6. **Thin brand hubs for secondary brands.** Fujitsu has 5 guides vs. Mitsubishi's 31. Rheem has 17. These are top-5 residential HVAC brands — undercovering them hands rankings to competitors.

### Top 5 Actions for Organic Traffic

1. **Add author bios to every article.** Create 2-3 author personas with real credentials (HVAC-R Certification, EPA 608, 10+ years field experience). Link each article to an author page with a photo, bio, and credential list. This is the single highest-leverage E-E-A-T move available.

2. **Add FAQ schema markup to every article.** Structure the existing "When to Call a Pro," "Common Causes," and troubleshooting steps as FAQ JSON-LD. Target PAA boxes. You already have the content — this is implementation only.

3. **Fix /posts/ pagination and add an XML sitemap link in footer.** Confirm Google Search Console is fully indexing all 900+ articles. Check coverage errors. A Cloudflare Pages site can have crawl issues if the sitemap isn't being served correctly.

4. **Expand brand hub intro copy.** Add 150-200 words of contextual text above each brand hub table covering model families, common failure patterns, and keyword variants. This costs nothing but improves on-page relevance for brand-level searches like "Carrier furnace error codes complete list."

5. **Add model-number targeting to brand hub pages.** High-value searches include "Carrier 58CVA error codes" and "Trane XR15 fault codes" — model-specific queries with zero competition from generic sites. Create model sub-pages under `/brands/carrier/58cva/` that aggregate only that model's codes.

## UX Assessment — 6/10

### What's Working

- **"Jump to Fix" and "View Parts" anchor links** at the top of articles are a genuine UX improvement over competitors. Removes friction for users who know what they're doing.
- **QuickAnswer box** at the article top gives the answer immediately, then lets users go deeper. This is the right approach for intent matching.
- **Brand hub tables** are scannable and fast. Easy to find any Carrier code in seconds.
- **Category grid on homepage** (HVAC, CNC, Refrigeration, etc.) lets users self-sort into the right equipment type quickly.
- **Social sharing buttons** are in place.
- **"Back to Top" anchor** exists.

### What's Missing

1. **No difficulty rating, time-to-fix, or tools list at the top of articles.** errbase.com shows "Time to Fix: 15–90 min | Difficulty: intermediate | Tools Needed: Phillips #2, multimeter, flashlight" before a single word of content. This sets expectations for DIYers and helps them decide before they start reading. This is a simple UX addition with high value.

2. **No probability breakdown for causes.** errbase.com's "Based on aggregated repair data: Airflow Restriction 65%, Blower Motor 20%, Limit Switch 10%, Dirty Coil 5%" is the most useful thing on the page. It tells users where to start. We have better narrative explanations but this visual probability bar is uniquely useful and likely to get screenshots and backlinks.

3. **No model number search.** Users arrive with model numbers. The current search field accepts brand names and error codes but doesn't appear to route users by model number to model-specific pages. "My Carrier 58CVA080 is showing code 13" is a search pattern this site doesn't currently capitalize on.

4. **Brand hub code descriptions are bare.** "Causes & Fix" is a placeholder, not a description. A user on the Carrier brand hub can't tell the difference between code 13 (serious — could be CO risk) and code 11 (minor communication fault) without clicking through. Add 8-10 word descriptions and severity tags to brand hub rows.

5. **Related articles at the bottom are algorithmically off.** The Carrier 13 article shows "Armstrong Pump Fault Codes" and "Burnham Series 2 Boiler Error Codes" as related content. These are irrelevant to a user who just diagnosed their Carrier furnace. Related section should prioritize: same brand, same equipment type, same failure pattern.

6. **No "was this helpful?" feedback mechanism.** Zero signal on content performance. errbase has none either but the mature sites (InspectAPedia, etc.) gather this. It's also a trust signal for new visitors.

### Top 5 Actions for User Experience

1. **Add a difficulty/time/tools header to every article** — difficulty (DIY/Pro), estimated time, tools needed. This is probably 30 minutes of Astro template work and makes every article dramatically more scannable.

2. **Add probability bars / "most likely cause" section to top of articles.** Create a simple visual breakdown: "65% of the time this is a dirty filter. 20% a bad capacitor." This is the highest-differentiation UX feature vs. competitors.

3. **Fix the brand hub table descriptions.** Replace "Causes & Fix" with 8-10 word descriptions and add a severity column (Low / Medium / High / Safety Risk). Users need to triage before they click.

4. **Add a model number lookup.** A simple text field on brand hub pages: "Enter your model number to find the right guide." Even if it just does a regex filter against a static list of article metadata, this dramatically improves utility for users who arrive from Google with a model number in hand.

5. **Fix related articles algorithm.** Weight related articles by: same brand first, then same equipment type, then same fault category. The current related section is losing users at the bottom of articles instead of funneling them deeper.

## Competitor Analysis

### Who Dominates These Searches

**errbase.com** is the primary direct competitor and is beating this site on execution. They have:
- 1,100+ error codes indexed
- Author attribution with credentials and verification dates
- Probability breakdown with visual bars
- Difficulty rating, time-to-fix, tools list on every article
- Specific affected model numbers per code
- FAQ schema driving PAA boxes
- Cleaner, more professional design
- "Based on aggregated repair data" framing that implies proprietary data (even if AI-generated)

**pickhvac.com** is not a pure error code site but dominates for commercial intent. They monetize hard via contractor lead gen ("Call 888-894-0154 Now to Compare Local Quotes!"). They have 10+ years of domain authority and cover the full purchase funnel (buy new equipment + fix existing). Their error code content is 2,000-3,000 words vs. errorcodefixes.com's 800-1,200 — longer but less technician-accurate.

**hvacfaultcodes.com** competes on model-number specificity. They organize by Brand > Model > Fault Code — a database approach rather than a guide approach. Weaker content depth, stronger structural targeting.

**dimaticcontrol.com** is an HVAC company using error code content for local SEO. Less threat at scale.

**inspectapedia.com** — old, ugly, authoritative. High domain authority. Hard to outrank on brand-generic queries but beatable on long-tail model-specific pages.

- Probability breakdown with visual data (errbase)
- Contractor lead gen revenue model (pickhvac — their primary monetizer)
- Difficulty/time/tools header (errbase)
- Model-specific fault code organization (hvacfaultcodes.com)

### Our Differentiation Opportunities

1. **Genuine technician voice.** The Carrier 13 "pro tip" about timing is authentic practitioner knowledge. errbase reads like AI. Lean into this — it's the actual competitive advantage.
2. **Industrial/CNC coverage.** Nobody covers Fanuc, Haas, Yaskawa at this depth. This is a blue ocean with less competition and higher commercial intent (downtime = $1,000+/hour).

4. **Cross-equipment coverage.** errbase and pickhvac are HVAC-only. Errorcodefixes.com spans HVAC + CNC + refrigeration + VFD + boilers. This positions the site as the industrial reference, not just the HVAC repair guide.

## Summary Verdict

errorcodefixes.com has **genuine assets**: original practitioner voice, strong brand hub architecture, and real topical breadth across industrial equipment categories that competitors don't touch. The Carrier 13 article is legitimately better than pickhvac.com's. That's the foundation.

The gap is execution, not concept. Three months in, the site is running on Amazon Associates 3% commissions when 12% recurring filter commissions and $20 contractor leads are sitting uncaptured. The SEO structure is right but missing the E-E-A-T signals (author bio, verification dates) that are mandatory for health/safety-adjacent content. errbase.com is the threat to watch — they're building fast and have better UX signals even if the content is AI-thin.

**The 7-day actions above are not optimization — they are the missing foundation.** Affiliate diversification, author attribution, and FAQ schema are table stakes that should have been in place at launch. Fix those first. Then build the model lookup tool and contractor lead gen as the first revenue multipliers.

At current trajectory without changes: a solid niche site earning $200-500/month.  
With the 7-day fixes + 60-day build plan above: a realistic path to $5,000-15,000/month within 12 months.
