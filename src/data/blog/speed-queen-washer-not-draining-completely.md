## Speed Queen Washer Not Draining Completely — What's Happening

A Speed Queen washer that is not draining completely means water remains in the tub at the end of a wash or spin cycle. The control expects the water level to drop within a set time, but the drain system is restricted, the pump is weak or blocked, or the machine is not sensing the water level correctly. On some electronic models this triggers a fault code like F9E1, but many units simply leave standing water without displaying a code.

If the machine drains most of the water but leaves a small amount behind, the issue is often a partially restricted hose, a weak pump, or a pressure-sensing problem rather than a complete pump failure. The drain timeout or incomplete drain symptom points to a drain-system fault that requires inspecting the hose, pump, and household plumbing.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Kinked, crushed, or blocked drain hose** A sharp bend, kink, or physical crushing of the drain hose between the washer and standpipe restricts water flow and is the most common cause of incomplete draining.
- **Debris in the pump or impeller** Coins, buttons, lint, socks, or small clothing items lodged in the pump housing or impeller prevent the pump from moving water efficiently.
- **Blocked standpipe or household drain plumbing** A clogged or slow-draining standpipe, sink drain, or laundry drain line can prevent the washer from draining completely even when the pump runs.
- **Failed or weak drain pump motor** A pump motor that has lost power or is intermittently failing will not generate enough pressure to empty the tub within the expected time.
- **Clogged pump filter or trap** On models equipped with a filter or trap, accumulated lint and debris block water flow and reduce drain performance.
- **Lid switch or lid lock issue on top-load models** A faulty lid switch or lock prevents the washer from initiating the spin and drain cycle, leaving water in the tub.
- **Water-level sensing or pressure hose problem** A blocked, damaged, or disconnected pressure hose can cause the control to misread tub water level and not advance the drain cycle properly.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the washer and remove standing water from the tub using a wet/dry vacuum or by draining the hose into a shallow pan before accessing internal components.
2. Inspect the drain hose from the washer to the standpipe for kinks, sharp bends, crushed sections, or blockages and straighten or clear as needed.
3. Verify the standpipe or sink drain is not blocked by pouring a gallon of water into the drain and confirming it flows freely without backup.
4. Remove the appropriate access panel and inspect the pump inlet hose, pump housing, and outlet hose for debris such as coins, buttons, or fabric, and clear any obstructions.
5. Spin the pump impeller by hand to check for binding, damage, or failure to turn freely, and remove any debris or replace the impeller if damaged.
6. If the model is equipped with a pump filter or trap, remove it, clean out lint and debris, and reinstall it correctly.
7. On top-load models, verify the lid closes fully and the lid switch or lock actuates properly by checking continuity with a multimeter and replace the switch if it fails the test.
8. Check the pressure hose for blockage, damage, or disconnection if the washer still appears to misread water level or does not transition into drain and spin.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared the hose and pump path but the washer still will not drain, if electrical testing of the pump shows voltage is present but the pump does not run, or if you are uncomfortable removing panels or working around water and electrical components. A technician can also diagnose water-level sensing faults or control board issues that are not obvious from visual inspection.
