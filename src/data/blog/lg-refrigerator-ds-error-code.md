## LG Refrigerator dS Error Code — What It Means

The dS code on an LG refrigerator signals a defrosting sensor fault. LG states it indicates a short circuit or disconnection of the defrost sensor. On dual-evaporator models, F dS refers to the freezer evaporator defrost sensor and r dS to the refrigerator evaporator defrost sensor. The appliance cannot properly monitor defrost cycle temperatures when this fault is present.

LG's support documentation explicitly states that dS-related errors require service. The sensor is either electrically shorted, open (broken circuit), or physically disconnected. In many cases the defrost sensor itself has failed, but the code can also appear when wiring is damaged or when heavy ice buildup and a blocked drain prevent normal defrost operation. LG ties defrost errors to failures in the heater, thermal fuse, drainage system, or main control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first, assuming electronics are at fault. Instead, inspect the defrost sensor and its harness for opens, shorts, or loose connectors before replacing any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed defrost sensor or thermistor (~50%)** The sensor has electrically opened, shorted, or drifted out of specification and can no longer signal accurate temperature to the control board.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded sensor harness connection (~20%)** The wiring or connector between the defrost sensor and control board is open, pinched, corroded, or pulled loose.
- **Heavy evaporator frost or ice buildup (~15%)** Ice accumulation from a failed defrost heater, thermal fuse, or blocked drain prevents the sensor from accurately detecting temperature and can coexist with a sensor fault.
- **Defrost heater disconnection or failure (~8%)** A broken or disconnected defrost heater prevents normal defrost, which can lead to ice buildup and secondary sensor errors.
- **Defrost thermal fuse or temperature fuse open (~5%)** The safety fuse has blown, breaking the defrost circuit and preventing normal defrost operation.
- **Main PCB (control board) failure (~2%)** The main printed circuit board has failed in a way that prevents accurate sensor reading or defrost cycle execution.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the refrigerator safely** by unplugging it or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker, and verify the exact code displayed: dS, F dS, or r dS.
2. **Remove the evaporator cover panel** in the affected compartment (freezer for F dS, refrigerator for r dS, or the main freezer evaporator for dS) to inspect for heavy frost or ice buildup.
3. **Locate the defrost sensor** (also called a thermistor), usually clipped to the evaporator coil or mounted nearby, and inspect its wiring harness for damage, pinched wires, loose connectors, or corrosion.
4. **Test the defrost sensor** using a multimeter set to measure resistance, and compare the reading to your model's service manual specifications (consult your model's table if not provided in the research).
5. **Inspect the defrost heater and thermal fuse** on the same evaporator for continuity, physical damage, or disconnection, because LG identifies these components as causes of defrost failure.
6. **Check the evaporator drain hole and drain pan** for blockage or ice, and clear any obstructions to restore proper water flow after defrost cycles.
7. **Replace the failed component** (defrost sensor, thermal fuse, or heater) or repair damaged wiring, reassemble the evaporator cover, and restore power to the unit.
8. **Clear the fault code** (consult your owner's manual for the reset procedure, often a long press of specific buttons), and monitor the refrigerator through several defrost cycles to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

LG explicitly states that dS, F dS, and r dS errors require service. Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable disassembling the evaporator compartment, testing electrical components with a multimeter, or interpreting resistance values from a service manual. A technician has the training and tools to safely access refrigerant-circuit areas, diagnose control board faults, and replace sensors or heaters without damaging sealed components. If the code returns after a simple reset or if you see repeated ice buildup, professional diagnosis is warranted to prevent compressor damage or food spoilage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
