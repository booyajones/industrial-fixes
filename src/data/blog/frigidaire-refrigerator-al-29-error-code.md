## Frigidaire AL-29 Error Code — What It Means

Frigidaire does not publish an AL-29 code in standard owner documentation. The documented high-temperature alarm for Frigidaire refrigerators displays as H or HI, and it means the freezer has reached 26°F or greater or the refrigerator has reached 55°F or greater. If your display shows AL-29, it may be a misread alarm, a model-specific code, or a variation of the high-temp warning. In all cases, the unit is alerting you that the cabinet temperature has exceeded the safe threshold and food may be at risk.

## Common Causes

- **Door left open or not fully closed** The most common cause is a door that was left ajar or not pushed firmly closed, allowing warm air to enter and temperatures to climb.
- **Worn or damaged door gasket** A gasket with tears, debris, or poor contact allows warm air infiltration and prevents the cabinet from maintaining temperature.
- **Blocked air vents or heavy frost buildup** Food items blocking evaporator vents or excessive ice on the evaporator coil restrict airflow and prevent proper cooling.
- **Recent warm food load or power outage** Loading a large amount of warm food or a brief power interruption can cause a temporary temperature spike that triggers the alarm.
- **Defective temperature sensor or thermistor** A faulty sensor can send incorrect temperature readings to the control board, causing false alarms or preventing the compressor from running correctly.
- **Evaporator or condenser fan failure** If either fan stops running, cold air cannot circulate or heat cannot be expelled, leading to rising cabinet temperatures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the alarm and cabinet temperatures.** Use an independent thermometer to measure actual freezer and refrigerator temperatures, and confirm whether the display shows H, HI, or another code.
2. **Check both doors for full closure.** Push each door firmly closed and listen for the latch, then inspect for any items blocking the door or interfering with the gasket seal.
3. **Inspect the door gaskets.** Run your hand around the entire perimeter of each door gasket, feeling for gaps, cold air leaking out, or visible tears and debris, and clean or replace as needed.
4. **Clear any airflow obstructions.** Remove food items that block the evaporator vents inside the freezer or refrigerator, and check for heavy frost or ice buildup on the evaporator coil.
5. **Listen for fan operation.** Open the freezer door and listen for the evaporator fan running, then check behind or below the unit (depending on model) for the condenser fan, and replace any fan that does not run when commanded.
6. **Reset the alarm and monitor.** Press the Alarm Reset or Clear Alerts button if your model has one, or unplug the refrigerator for 30 seconds to clear the code, then plug back in and monitor temperatures for 24 hours.
7. **Test the temperature sensor if the alarm persists.** Locate the thermistor (usually near the evaporator coil or in the fresh-food section), disconnect it, and measure resistance at room temperature against the model's specification table, replacing if out of range.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified the doors seal properly, cleared all obstructions, confirmed both fans run, and the alarm returns within a few hours. A pro can perform sealed-system diagnostics (checking refrigerant charge and compressor health), test the control board for correct sensor input and relay output, and accurately decode model-specific error displays that do not appear in standard owner documentation. Also call if you see oil residue, hear unusual compressor noises, or if the cabinet temperature continues to rise despite the compressor running.
