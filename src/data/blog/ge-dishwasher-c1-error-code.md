## GE Dishwasher C1 Error Code — What It Means

The C1 code on a GE dishwasher means the pump-out during drain has exceeded 2 minutes. The control board expects the tub to empty within that window, and when standing water remains the cycle stops and the code appears. This is a drain-time fault, not a generic electronic failure.

In practical terms, water is not leaving the dishwasher fast enough. The drain pump may be running but cannot move water through a restricted path, or the pump itself has failed and cannot push water at all. The machine protects itself by halting the cycle rather than flooding or running indefinitely.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump or control board before checking the external drain path. Clean the air gap, inspect the drain hose for kinks, and verify the garbage disposal knockout plug was removed before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged air gap (~30%)** The sink air-gap fitting becomes blocked with debris and restricts drain flow, causing the pump-out to time out.
- **Kinked or crushed drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose is routed too tightly, pinched under the dishwasher, or crushed by cabinetry so water cannot pass.
- **Garbage disposal blockage or knockout plug still installed (~20%)** The disposer inlet is clogged with food, or the knockout plug was never removed when the dishwasher was first connected.
- **Sump or filter debris (~15%)** Food, labels, glass, or other objects block the sump or pump inlet and prevent water from reaching the pump.
- **Failed drain pump or damaged impeller (~8%)** The pump receives power but the impeller is broken or the motor has failed, so water does not move even when the pump cycles.
- **Control board or sensor misreport (~2%)** The machine thinks it has not drained even when the tub is empty, usually after all mechanical paths and the pump have been confirmed good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset the dishwasher** by pressing Start/Cancel or disconnecting power for 30 seconds, then run a short cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Check the air gap** at the sink (a small chrome or plastic cap near the faucet) and remove the top cover to clean out any debris blocking the ports.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** from the dishwasher to the disposal or sink drain for kinks, crushing, or blockage and straighten or replace as needed.
4. **Verify the garbage disposal connection** by running the disposer to clear any food buildup and confirming the knockout plug was removed when the dishwasher was installed.
5. **Clean the dishwasher filter and sump** by removing the lower rack, lifting out the filter assembly, and checking the sump area for food, labels, glass, or other debris.
6. **Test the drain pump** by starting a drain cycle and listening for the pump motor, then checking whether water actually leaves the tub within two minutes.
7. **Replace the drain pump** if it receives power but does not move water, or if the impeller is broken or jammed and cannot be cleared.
8. **Re-test the cycle** after any repair and confirm the tub empties completely and the C1 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the air gap, inspected the entire drain hose, verified the garbage disposal connection, and cleared the sump and filter but the C1 code persists. If the drain pump runs and the path is clear yet water does not leave the tub, the pump may need replacement and a pro can confirm pump operation and voltage. If all mechanical components pass inspection and the code still appears with an empty tub, the control board or a water-level sensor may be misreporting the drain status and diagnostic equipment will be needed to pinpoint the fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
