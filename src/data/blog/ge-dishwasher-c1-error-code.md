## What this code means
GE's C1 code means the pump-out during drain has exceeded 2 minutes. The control board commanded the dishwasher to drain, but water did not evacuate within the programmed two-minute window. This is a drain-timeout fault, not a generic electrical problem.

The code typically appears when something is slowing or blocking the drain path. GE's published guidance focuses on clearing the air gap, checking the drain hose for kinks or blockages, and running the garbage disposer to clear any restriction. If those steps do not resolve the fault, the manufacturer recommends contacting service.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the drain pump when the real problem is a clogged air gap or a newly installed garbage disposer with the knockout plug still in place. Always clear the drain path before buying a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Air gap restriction or blockage (~30%)** Debris in the sink air gap slows or stops water flow, causing the drain cycle to time out before the dishwasher empties.
- **Drain hose kink, blockage, or bad routing (~25%)** A kinked, collapsed, or improperly looped drain hose restricts flow and prevents the dishwasher from draining within two minutes.
- **Garbage disposer or sink drain restriction (~20%)** A clogged disposer or a newly installed disposer with the knockout plug still in place blocks the drain path.
- **Drain path debris in sump or filter (~15%)** Food particles or broken glass in the filter, sump, or impeller area slow the pump and prevent complete evacuation.
- **Failing drain pump or damaged impeller (~8%)** A weak pump or stripped impeller cannot move water fast enough to meet the two-minute drain window.
- **Control or sensor false drain detection (~2%)** A faulty pressure sensor or control board may incorrectly interpret water level or fail to command the pump properly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or unplug the dishwasher to prevent inadvertent starts during inspection.
2. **Remove and clean the air gap** on the sink (if fitted) by taking off the chrome cap and flushing out any debris with water.
3. **Run the garbage disposer** for 15-30 seconds with cold water to clear any food or blockage in the shared drain line.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** under the sink for kinks, collapse, or low loops that trap water. Reposition or replace the hose if damaged or improperly routed.
5. **Check the disposer connection** to confirm the dishwasher knockout plug was removed (if the disposer is new). Remove the plug if it is still in place.
6. **Clean the dishwasher filter and sump** by removing the lower rack, lifting out the filter assembly, and clearing any debris from the sump cavity and pump inlet.
7. **Run a drain cycle** and observe whether water evacuates. If the pump does not run or water remains, test the drain pump for proper voltage and mechanical function, and replace the pump assembly if it is weak or silent.
8. **Restore power and test** the dishwasher through a full cycle. If the C1 code persists after all drain-path checks, inspect the pressure sensor or consult a technician to diagnose the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you have cleared the air gap, repositioned the drain hose, run the disposer, and cleaned the filter but the C1 code still appears. A technician will test the drain pump's electrical command from the control board, measure voltage at the pump terminals (typically 120 V when commanded), and check the pressure sensor or float-related feedback for false readings. If the pump runs but water does not move, the impeller may be stripped or the pump body cracked, requiring replacement. If the drain path is completely clear and the pump is strong, the fault may lie in the main control board, which a pro can diagnose with a wiring diagram and voltage trace.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (hose or pump), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
