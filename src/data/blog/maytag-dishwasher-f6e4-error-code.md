## What this code means
The F6E4 code on a Maytag dishwasher indicates an unlevel condition that has actuated the overfill or floater switch. Maytag's product-help documentation confirms that incorrect leveling is the primary cause of this fault. When the dishwasher is tilted too far forward, backward, or side-to-side, the float mechanism inside the base pan can shift and close the overfill switch, triggering the error even when no actual overfill has occurred.

This is a mechanical installation issue, not a generic water-fill or drain fault. The dishwasher detects that the float switch has been tripped and assumes a flood risk, so it halts operation and displays F6E4. Correcting the installation angle and ensuring the unit sits properly in the cabinet opening will typically clear the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the inlet valve or drain pump first, assuming F6E4 is a water-flow fault. Before ordering parts, use a spirit level to check the dishwasher's installation angle in all directions.

## Common Causes

- **Dishwasher not level side-to-side or front-to-back (~70%)** Maytag states that incorrect leveling can physically trip the overfill floater switch, causing F6E4.
- **Unit not fully seated in cabinet opening (~15%)** If the dishwasher has shifted forward or is resting unevenly against the countertop, the tub can tilt enough to actuate the float.
- **Overfill or floater switch stuck or damaged (~10%)** The switch itself can fail in the closed position, reporting an overfill condition even when the machine is level.
- **Wiring fault to the overfill switch (~5%)** A loose connector or damaged wire in the float-switch circuit can send a false overfill signal to the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dishwasher before any inspection or adjustment work.
2. **Remove the lower access panel** (usually the kickplate at the base) so you can reach the leveling legs and see the base pan area.
3. **Place a spirit level** on the door sill or the top edge of the open door and check side-to-side level, then check front-to-back by placing the level on a flat part of the tub bottom or the door when fully open.
4. **Adjust the leveling legs** by turning them clockwise to raise or counterclockwise to lower each corner until the dishwasher reads level in both directions, then confirm the unit is stable and does not rock.
5. **Push the dishwasher back** into the cabinet opening and verify it is fully seated, with mounting brackets engaged and the door aligned with surrounding cabinetry.
6. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to see if F6E4 returns, watching the first few minutes for any fault indication.
7. **If the code persists after confirmed leveling**, locate the overfill float switch in the base pan (a small dome or cup attached to a microswitch), check that it moves freely, and test continuity across the switch terminals to confirm it opens when the float drops and closes when lifted.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have confirmed the dishwasher is level in all directions, the float switch moves freely and tests correctly, and F6E4 still appears. At that point the fault may lie in the wiring harness or the main control board's input circuit for the overfill switch. A technician can perform voltage and resistance checks on the control-board side and trace any short or open in the float-switch wiring. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working around 120-volt connections or if your installation requires modifying cabinetry to achieve proper leveling.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-15 (leveling legs adjustment only), 15-30 min. A pro service call runs about $100-180 service call for leveling and installation check.
