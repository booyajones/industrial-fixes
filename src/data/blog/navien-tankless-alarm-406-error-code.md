## Navien 406 Error Code — What It Means

The 406 error code is not documented in standard Navien manufacturer materials, so its exact meaning depends on your specific model and control board version. However, Navien flow-related faults typically mean the flow sensor inside the unit is not spinning properly or the system is not detecting adequate water flow through the heat exchanger. When water flows through a tankless heater, a small turbine (the flow sensor) spins to signal the control board that demand is present. If the sensor is stuck, clogged with debris, or the flow rate is too low, the unit cannot start or maintain burner operation.

Navien recommends opening and closing the pressure relief valve near the outlet as a first diagnostic step when a flow-sensing issue occurs. This action can dislodge sediment or air that may be blocking the sensor. If the fault persists after that check, the flow sensor itself may need cleaning or replacement.

## Common Causes

- **Flow sensor not spinning** Debris, scale, or sediment jamming the turbine is the most common cause identified by Navien for flow-detection problems.
- **Low inlet water pressure** Insufficient supply pressure or a partially closed isolation valve can prevent the sensor from reaching its minimum rotation threshold.
- **Air trapped in the heat exchanger** Air pockets can interrupt flow sensing and prevent the sensor from registering correctly.
- **Failed or damaged flow sensor** The sensor's Hall-effect pickup or turbine bearings may wear out over time and stop sending a signal to the board.
- **Clogged inlet filter screen** The strainer at the cold-water inlet can collect rust, sand, or pipe scale and choke flow before it reaches the sensor.
- **Faulty wiring or connector to the flow sensor** Corroded pins or a loose harness connection can break the communication path between the sensor and the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and water** to the unit at the circuit breaker and the isolation valves on both the inlet and outlet.
2. **Open the pressure relief valve** near the hot-water outlet and let a gallon of water run into a bucket to flush out any debris or air in the system.
3. **Remove the front cover** and locate the inlet filter screen on the cold-water service valve, then unscrew and rinse it under running water to clear sediment.
4. **Locate the flow sensor** (a small cylindrical assembly in the inlet manifold with two wires), disconnect its harness, and remove the retaining clip or screws.
5. **Inspect the turbine** inside the sensor body for scale buildup, foreign objects, or a seized rotor, then rinse it with white vinegar or a descaling solution and spin it by hand to confirm it turns freely.
6. **Reinstall the sensor and filter**, reconnect all wiring, and restore water flow slowly to purge any remaining air from the heat exchanger.
7. **Power the unit back on** and open a hot-water tap to verify the heater fires and the error does not return, then check the display for normal operation or any remaining fault codes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if the error returns after you have cleaned the sensor and verified good inlet flow, if you cannot safely remove the front panel or service valves, or if you see other fault codes appearing alongside the 406. A technician can run board-level diagnostics to confirm the flow sensor is sending the correct Hall-effect pulses and can perform a full descaling flush if your heat exchanger has heavy mineral buildup. Also call a pro if your unit is still under warranty, since self-service on certain internal components may void coverage.
