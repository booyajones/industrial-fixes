## Maytag F5 E2 Error Code — What It Means

F5 E2 on a Maytag washer means the control has detected that the door is not locking properly or cannot confirm a valid locked-door condition. The washer thinks the door is closed, but the lock state is not correct or not being confirmed. The cycle is blocked because the machine's safety logic will not allow operation unless the door lock condition is satisfied.

Maytag identifies this as a door-locking fault. The door must be completely closed for operation, and if the lock assembly cannot engage or the control cannot verify the locked state, the code will appear and prevent the wash cycle from starting.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but a simple inspection of the door strike area and a power reset will identify most lock-assembly and wiring faults without any board work.

## Common Causes

- **Door not fully latched (~40%)** Clothing protrudes into the door opening or the washer is overloaded, preventing the door from closing completely and allowing the lock to engage.
- **Faulty door lock assembly (~35%)** The door lock or lid lock assembly itself has failed, preventing the lock mechanism or switch from signaling a valid locked state to the control.
- **Wiring or connector problems (~15%)** Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring between the lock assembly and the control board interrupts the lock-status signal.
- **Temporary control glitch (~10%)** A transient fault in the control board logic triggers the code even though the lock hardware is functioning correctly.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the door close fully with a solid click and no laundry visible in the gap?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The door is latching properly, so move on to a power reset and then inspect the lock assembly and wiring.<br><strong>No:</strong> Remove any clothing blocking the door, reduce the load size, and try the cycle again.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After a 5-minute power reset, does the code return immediately when you start a cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault is persistent and points to a hardware problem in the lock assembly or wiring rather than a temporary glitch.<br><strong>No:</strong> The control glitch has cleared and the washer should operate normally now.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you see obvious damage, cracks, or debris around the door strike and lock assembly?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The lock assembly or strike is damaged and will need replacement to restore proper latching and lock confirmation.<br><strong>No:</strong> The problem is likely internal to the lock assembly or in the wiring, so proceed to diagnostics and resistance checks.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Open the door and inspect the closure area** for any clothing, debris, or objects blocking the door strike or latch, and remove the obstruction.
2. **Reduce the load size** if the drum is packed tightly or clothing is visible around the door seal, then close the door firmly and listen for a solid click.
3. **Disconnect power to the washer** by unplugging it or switching off the circuit breaker, wait 5 minutes, then restore power to reset the control board.
4. **Run a test cycle** to see if the code clears after the reset.
5. **Inspect the door lock assembly** by removing the front panel or top cover (consult your model's service manual), looking for broken tabs, cracks, or corrosion on the lock mechanism and connectors.
6. **Check the wiring harness** at both the lock assembly and the main control board for loose or corroded connectors, and reseat or repair any damaged conductors.
7. **Run the washer's diagnostic mode** (consult your model's tech sheet or service manual) to confirm whether the door-lock input and lock actuation pass self-tests.
8. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the lock mechanism or switch is defective, or replace the wiring harness if conductors are damaged.
9. **Reassemble the washer and run a normal cycle** to verify the code does not return and the door locks properly throughout the wash.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable removing panels to access the lock assembly, if diagnostic mode results are unclear, or if you have replaced the lock assembly and wiring but the code persists. A pro can perform continuity and voltage tests on the lock circuit and determine whether the main control board is failing to drive or read the lock correctly. Most F5 E2 faults are straightforward lock or wiring issues that do not require board-level work, but a technician with a meter and the factory diagnostic routine will isolate the root cause quickly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–120 for a door lock assembly, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call with lock replacement.
