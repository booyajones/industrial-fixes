## What this code means
The Maytag F5 E2 code indicates the washer has detected a lid or door lock problem. The machine is not seeing the door or lid lock in the expected locked state, so it will not proceed with operation. On Maytag front-load washers, F5 E2 means the door is not locking properly. On top-load models, the same code signals a lid lock or door switch failure condition.

In practical terms, the control board is not receiving the correct lock-status feedback, so the washer aborts the cycle as a safety measure. The issue may be mechanical (door not latching), electrical (failed lock assembly or loose wiring), or a temporary control glitch.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but the real culprit is usually the lock assembly itself or a loose connector. Run the diagnostic lid lock test and check all connectors before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Door or lid not fully closed (~30%)** Laundry may be blocking the door, or the door is not seated squarely against the striker so the lock cannot engage.
- **Striker or latch misalignment (~25%)** The door looks closed but the striker is out of position, preventing the lock from clicking into place and sending the locked signal.
- **Failed lid or door lock assembly (~25%)** The lock mechanism or internal switch has failed and cannot latch or report its status to the control board.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors (~15%)** Connectors to the lock assembly or control board are loose, corroded, or physically damaged, interrupting the lock feedback signal.
- **Control board glitch or temporary fault (~5%)** A power surge or software hiccup causes the control to lose track of the lock state until you cycle power.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait 5 minutes to reset the control board and clear any temporary fault.
2. **Open and close the door or lid** firmly, making sure no laundry is trapped in the seal or blocking the striker.
3. **Inspect the striker and latch area** for soap scum, debris, or visible damage, and clean with a damp cloth if needed.
4. **Restore power** and run a short cycle to see if the code clears. If it returns, proceed to diagnostics.
5. **Enter the diagnostic or service test mode** (consult your model's tech sheet) and run the lid lock test to verify the lock actuates and reports status.
6. **Check the lock assembly connectors** at both the lock and the control board for looseness, corrosion, or damaged pins, and reseat firmly.
7. **Replace the lid or door lock assembly** if the diagnostic test shows no actuation or incorrect feedback, then retest the machine with a full wash cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable entering diagnostic mode, if the wiring harness shows extensive damage that requires tracing circuits behind panels, or if replacing the lock assembly and reseating all connectors does not clear the code. A pro can use a multimeter to verify supply voltage and feedback signals at the lock and control board, and can access the full tech sheet for your specific model to confirm test-point values and part numbers. If the control board itself has failed (rare with F5 E2), a technician will have the tools and experience to swap it safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
