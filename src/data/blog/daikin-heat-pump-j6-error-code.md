## Daikin J6 Error Code — What It Means

The J6 error code on a Daikin heat pump indicates a malfunction of the outdoor unit heat exchanger thermistor. This is the temperature sensor that monitors the heat exchanger coil. When the control board detects abnormal resistance, an open circuit, or a short circuit from this sensor, it triggers the J6 fault and shuts down operation to protect the system.

Daikin's self-diagnosis documentation identifies three primary fault sources for J6: a bad thermistor, a faulty connector or harness connection, or a problem with the outdoor unit PCB. The vast majority of J6 errors are resolved by replacing the thermistor or repairing a damaged wire or connector at the sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor** The heat exchanger temperature sensor has drifted out of specification, is reading open, or is internally shorted.
- **Loose or corroded connector** The thermistor plug at the outdoor unit has worked loose, corroded, or developed a high-resistance connection that the board interprets as a sensor fault.
- **Damaged wiring or harness** The thermistor lead wires have been cut, pinched, or have insulation damage that causes an intermittent or open circuit.
- **Thermistor displaced from heat exchanger** The sensor has come loose from its mounting clip or tube contact point and is no longer reading accurate coil temperature.
- **Outdoor unit PCB failure** The control board itself has a failed input circuit or solder joint that prevents it from reading the thermistor correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the system** at the disconnect or breaker, wait 60 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the J6 code returns immediately or after a heating/cooling cycle.
2. **Locate the outdoor heat exchanger thermistor** by opening the outdoor unit service panel and tracing the sensor lead from the control board to the coil. The thermistor is typically a small bead or capsule clamped or inserted into the coil tubing.
3. **Inspect the thermistor mounting** to confirm the sensor is seated firmly against the heat exchanger tube and has not slipped out of its clip or insulation pocket.
4. **Check the connector and harness** at both the sensor end and the board end for loose pins, corrosion, water intrusion, or damaged insulation. Unplug and replug the connector to break any film of oxidation.
5. **Measure thermistor resistance** with a digital multimeter at the sensor leads or connector. Compare the reading to the temperature-versus-resistance table in your model's service manual. A typical NTC thermistor reads several thousand ohms at room temperature, but consult your model's table for the exact specification.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the resistance reads open (infinite), shorted (near zero), or clearly outside the acceptable range for ambient temperature. Use the exact Daikin replacement part specified for your outdoor unit model.
7. **If the sensor and wiring test good**, suspect the outdoor unit PCB. Test the board's thermistor input circuit per the service manual or replace the control board if all other components check out.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside the outdoor unit with line voltage present, if you do not have a multimeter and the skills to interpret thermistor resistance readings, or if replacing the sensor does not clear the J6 code. Heat pump refrigerant circuits and high-voltage controls require EPA certification and specialized tools. A qualified Daikin service technician can access model-specific resistance tables, retrieve additional fault history from the control board, and perform board-level diagnostics that are not published in homeowner literature.
