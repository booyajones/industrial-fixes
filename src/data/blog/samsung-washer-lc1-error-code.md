## What this code means
The LC1 code on Samsung washers, especially FlexWash models, means the machine has detected a water leak condition or the water level dropped below the sensor's reset point during the wash or rinse cycle. Samsung explicitly documents that 1LC1 is the FlexWash variant of the same leak indication, used for the upper washer on Flex models. When this fault is triggered, the drain pump runs continuously to protect against flooding.

The code does not always mean an actual external leak is present. It can also be triggered by water or suds accumulating in the base pan or around the leak-detection sensors, making the machine interpret normal operation as a leak. Over-sudsing from too much detergent or fabric softener is a frequent cause, as foam can push water into the sensor zone and trip the fault even when the cabinet is dry.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the leak sensor or wire harness first, but the real cause is usually excess detergent causing suds to enter the sensor area or a kinked drain hose allowing water to back up. Tilt the machine to dry the base pan and run a test cycle with no detergent before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Water or suds at the leak sensor (~40%)** Excess detergent, fabric softener, or a spill pushes water or foam into the base pan or sensor area, causing a false leak detection even when no external leak exists.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~25%)** A drain hose that is kinked, clogged, or improperly routed can cause water to back up or move abnormally inside the cabinet, triggering the leak sensors.
- **Actual water leak from hoses or connections (~20%)** Loose or cracked inlet hoses, drain hose fittings, dispenser plumbing, or pump connections can allow water to seep into the base and trip the leak sensor.
- **Contaminated or corroded leak sensor connector (~10%)** Moisture, detergent residue, or corrosion on the leak sensor terminals or harness connector can create a false signal and trigger the code.
- **Faulty leak sensor or wiring harness (~5%)** A failed leak sensor or damaged wiring can send an incorrect leak signal to the control board, causing the code to appear even when the machine is dry.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves at the wall to make sure safe access to all components.
2. **Inspect for visible leaks** by checking the inlet hoses, drain hose, hose clamps, dispenser area, and pump connections for seepage, cracks, or loose fittings.
3. **Examine the drain hose** to confirm it is not kinked, clogged, or improperly routed, and that the discharge end is elevated correctly per the installation guide.
4. **Tilt the washer back** (with a helper) to inspect the base pan and leak sensor area for standing water, then use towels or a wet-dry vacuum to dry the area completely.
5. **Check for over-sudsing** by running an empty rinse cycle and watching for excessive foam; if present, run additional rinse cycles and reduce detergent or softener dosage going forward.
6. **Inspect the leak sensor connectors** by accessing the sensor locations (consult your model's service manual) and cleaning any corrosion, detergent residue, or moisture from the terminals and harness.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** with no detergent to verify whether the code returns; monitor the machine during fill, wash, and rinse to confirm normal operation and no false triggers.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the leak sensor, if the code persists after drying the base and clearing suds, or if you are uncomfortable tilting the machine or accessing internal wiring. A technician has the tools to test the leak sensor circuit electrically, verify proper drain-pump operation, and inspect the internal plumbing and cabinet for hidden leaks. Also call for help if you discover a damaged tub seal, pump housing crack, or other structural issue that requires disassembly or sealed-component replacement.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200.
