## Weil-McLain Boiler A21 Error — What It Means

An A21 error code cannot be confirmed in Weil-McLain's own documentation from the available sources. The code A21 is verified as a Worcester Bosch wireless thermostat communication fault, meaning the boiler control is not receiving the RF signal from the wireless room thermostat or receiver. If your Weil-McLain boiler displays A21, it is likely equipped with an aftermarket or third-party wireless thermostat system (such as Worcester Bosch) rather than using a native Weil-McLain control. The fault indicates a breakdown in the radio-frequency link between the wall thermostat and the receiver module at the boiler.

Because Weil-McLain uses model-specific control systems and fault codes, you should confirm your exact boiler model and CP number (on the rating label) and consult that model's service documentation. If the code truly appears on your display, treat it as a wireless-thermostat communication issue rather than an internal boiler fault. The boiler itself is typically fine but cannot receive heating demand signals.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the entire thermostat or receiver when only fresh batteries or re-pairing the devices is needed. Swap batteries and test range before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dead or weak thermostat batteries (~50%)** Low battery voltage prevents the thermostat from transmitting its RF signal to the receiver at the boiler.
- **Thermostat out of range or obstructed (~25%)** Metal objects, thick walls, or excessive distance between the thermostat and receiver block or weaken the wireless signal.
- **RF interference from other devices (~10%)** Nearby wireless routers, baby monitors, or other 2.4 GHz equipment can jam or disrupt the thermostat's frequency.
- **Lost pairing between thermostat and receiver (~10%)** A power interruption or accidental reset can unpair the thermostat from the receiver module, requiring manual re-pairing.
- **Failed thermostat or receiver unit (~5%)** If batteries, range, and pairing are all good, the thermostat transmitter or the boiler receiver module itself may have failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the boiler model and control type** by reading the rating label on the boiler jacket for the CP number and model name, then check whether the thermostat is a Weil-McLain factory control or an aftermarket wireless system.
2. **Replace the thermostat batteries** with fresh alkaline AA or AAA cells (depending on the thermostat model) and wait at least 2 minutes for the display to power up and attempt reconnection.
3. **Check the receiver module** at the boiler to verify it has power (usually indicated by an LED) and is mounted in a location free of metal obstruction or dense walls.
4. **Move the thermostat closer** to the boiler temporarily to test whether distance or physical barriers are blocking the RF signal, then observe if the A21 clears within a few minutes.
5. **Reduce wireless interference** by turning off or relocating nearby 2.4 GHz devices (Wi-Fi routers, baby monitors, cordless phones) and see if communication restores.
6. **Re-pair the thermostat and receiver** following the exact pairing or binding procedure in the thermostat manufacturer's instructions (usually a sequence of button presses on both units within a short window).
7. **Replace the failed component** (thermostat or receiver) if all checks pass but the A21 persists, using the model-specific part number from the control manufacturer or boiler parts list.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician if you have replaced batteries, re-paired the thermostat, and tested range but the A21 returns immediately or intermittently. A pro can use diagnostic tools to test the receiver's power supply, check for control-board voltage issues, and verify that the RF module is functioning. Also call if you cannot locate model-specific documentation for your Weil-McLain boiler or if the displayed code does not match any documented fault for your control system. A technician will confirm the exact control type and replace the correct failed component rather than guessing. Finally, if your boiler uses a proprietary Weil-McLain control and truly shows A21 (which is not verified in manufacturer sources), a trained service provider can cross-reference the CP number and access the factory service bulletins to identify the correct meaning and repair procedure.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $5-15 for batteries or $50-120 for a replacement thermostat or receiver, 15-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 service call plus parts.
