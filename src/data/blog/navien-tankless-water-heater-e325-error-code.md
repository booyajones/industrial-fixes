## Navien E325 Error Code — What It Means

The E325 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem related to flame sensing or ignition. The unit has detected an issue that prevents it from establishing or maintaining a stable flame. This can be caused by problems with the gas supply, the flame rod (which detects the presence of flame), the igniter assembly, or the control board logic that monitors combustion. Because exact code definitions can vary between Navien models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front cover for the specific meaning on your unit.

This code often appears when the flame sensor is dirty or misaligned, when gas pressure is insufficient, or when the igniter fails to create a spark. In some cases, the venting system or air intake may be blocked, affecting combustion and flame stability. The heater will shut down to prevent unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or control board without first cleaning the flame rod. A simple wire-brush cleaning of the flame sensor often resolves the E325 code and costs nothing.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame sensor prevent it from detecting the flame signal, triggering a false fault.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~25%)** Insufficient gas pressure, a closed valve upstream, or air in the gas line stops ignition or causes flame dropout.
- **Failed igniter assembly (~15%)** The igniter does not produce a spark or produces a weak spark, so the burner cannot light.
- **Blocked venting or air intake (~10%)** Restricted exhaust or intake pipes starve combustion of oxygen or trap exhaust gases, preventing stable flame.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The PCB fails to send or interpret ignition signals correctly, registering a false flame error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the dedicated switch or circuit breaker for the water heater.
2. **Close the gas shut-off valve** to the unit.
3. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners; consult your model's service manual for the exact procedure.
4. **Locate the flame rod**, a small metal probe near the burner assembly, and the igniter electrode.
5. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a wire brush to remove any carbon or oxidation; do not bend or damage the rod.
6. **Inspect the igniter electrode** for cracks or heavy carbon buildup; clean it carefully with a soft brush.
7. **Check the burner and heat exchanger** for soot or debris; if present, professional cleaning is required.
8. **Reinstall the cover**, open the gas valve, restore power, and test the unit by opening a hot-water tap.
9. **Observe the display**; if the E325 code returns or ignition fails, call a licensed technician to test gas pressure, check the control board, and inspect venting.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the flame rod cleaning does not resolve the error, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a control board fault. Tankless water heaters involve combustion, gas piping, and high-voltage ignition circuits that require proper diagnostic tools and training. A professional will measure gas inlet pressure, test flame rectification signals with a multimeter, inspect venting for blockages or backdraft, and replace components as needed. If the heater is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
