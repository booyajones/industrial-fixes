## Maytag Dishwasher F8E4 Error Code — What It Means

The F8E4 code on a Maytag dishwasher is a water-detection or overfill leak alert. It signals that water has been detected in the drip tray or float-switch area underneath the tub, not simply a drain fault. When moisture accumulates in the base pan, the float switch trips and puts the dishwasher into a protective shutdown to prevent further flooding.

Maytag's own guidance treats F8E4 as a leak-related condition and directs technicians to check water supply connections, inspect the drip tray for standing water, and verify the float switch connection. The code does not clear itself until you remove the water, correct the leak source, and manually cancel the alarm twice.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when they see F8E4, but this code is triggered by the float switch detecting real water in the base pan. Always check for leaks, inspect the drip tray, and tighten supply fittings before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or improperly seated water supply connection (~40%)** A poorly seated rubber washer, cross-threaded 3/4" elbow fitting, or hand-loose supply nut allows water to weep into the base pan and trip the float switch.
- **Water already in the drip tray from a previous leak (~25%)** Even after the original leak stops, standing water in the base pan keeps the float switch active and the F8E4 code locked until you dry the tray completely.
- **Float switch disconnected or faulty harness connector (~15%)** The float-switch wire can pull loose during service or if the drip pan is yanked too hard, causing intermittent or persistent F8E4 codes.
- **Leaking fill hose or internal plumbing connection (~12%)** Cracks in the braided supply line, a loose hose clamp at the valve inlet, or a split rubber grommet where the line exits the tub can drip water into the base.
- **Oversudsing or suds overflow (~8%)** Using too much detergent or the wrong soap formulation produces foam that overflows the tub and routes down into the drip pan, triggering the leak sensor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power at the breaker** and shut off the water supply valve under the sink or at the wall before you begin any disassembly.
2. **Press Cancel once** to silence the alarm beeps, then **press Cancel a second time** to clear the F8E4 code from the display after the condition is corrected.
3. **Tilt the dishwasher back** slightly or remove the lower front access panel and inspect the drip tray or base pan for standing water. Use towels or a shop vacuum to remove all moisture.
4. **Remove the drip pan assembly carefully** by releasing its clips or screws. Do not pull hard because the float-switch wire is still attached and can be damaged if yanked.
5. **Check the float switch and its connector** in the base pan. Confirm the wire is properly seated and locked into the connector housing. If the connector is loose, push it firmly until it clicks.
6. **Inspect the water supply connection points** at the fill valve. Verify the rubber washer is seated in the 3/4" elbow fitting, hand-tighten the elbow to avoid cross-threading, then use pliers to snug it an additional 1/4 to 1/2 turn to compress the gasket. Check that the fill hose routes out the rear left side without kinks.
7. **Dry the base pan completely** with towels, reassemble the drip tray and any panels, restore power and water, then run a short rinse cycle while watching the base pan and all connections for any renewed leakage.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the leak source after inspecting all visible supply connections and the drip tray, if the float switch tests open or shorted and you are not comfortable with harness repairs, or if the leak appears to come from a hidden tub seam or internal manifold that requires pulling the dishwasher out and disassembling major components. Also call a pro if the code returns repeatedly after you have dried the base pan and confirmed all fittings are tight, since intermittent leaks can be difficult to trace without pressure testing the fill system. A technician has leak-detection dye and specialized tools to pinpoint slow weeps that only show up under full supply pressure.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (washer, hose, or switch), 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call plus parts.
