## Kenmore Microwave F5 Error Code — What It Means

The F5 error code on a Kenmore microwave indicates a failure of the door switch (door interlock) or the door switch circuit. The microwave's control board detects that the door is not properly closed, has become open during operation, or that the signal from the door switch is inconsistent or missing. The appliance will immediately stop heating and will not resume operation until the door switch circuit is verified as closed (door secured).

This code is specifically reserved for the door switch system in Kenmore units. It is not related to the humidity sensor (which typically triggers F2 or F3 codes in Kenmore and GE commercial-style microwaves). The F5 code means the safety interlock system cannot confirm that the door is securely latched.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first, assuming it is defective. Test the door switch for continuity with a multimeter before spending on a board. A failed switch costs under $20, while a control board runs over $100.

## Common Causes

- **Mechanical door switch failure (~50%)** The internal actuator of the door switch wears out, breaks, or becomes stuck, preventing electrical contact even when the door is closed.
- **Obstructed or misaligned door (~20%)** Food debris, grease, or a warped door frame prevents the door from closing fully enough to engage the switch.
- **Worn door latch or hook (~15%)** The plastic hook on the door that engages the switch may be broken or worn down, failing to push the switch actuator.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~10%)** The wires connecting the door switch to the main control board may be loose, corroded, or pinched and cut.
- **Failed control board (~5%)** The main control board fails to read the closed signal from a functional switch due to an open input circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and disconnect power to make sure safety during all inspection and repair work.
2. **Clean the door area** thoroughly, removing any food debris, grease, or obstructions around the door seal, latch, and switch area.
3. **Inspect the door latch hook** on the door edge for cracks, wear, or breakage. Open and close the door several times, listening for the click of the switch engaging.
4. **Access the door switch** by removing the control panel cover or the cabinet side panel (consult your model's service manual for exact location, usually near the door hinge or top control panel).
5. **Test the door switch for continuity** using a digital multimeter set to Ohms or Continuity mode. Disconnect the wires from the switch terminals. With the door open (or switch actuator not pressed), the meter should read infinity (open circuit). Manually press the switch actuator to simulate a closed door. The meter should read 0 ohms (closed circuit) or show continuity.
6. **Check wiring integrity** between the door switch and the control board if the switch tests good. Look for broken conductors, loose connections, or corrosion. Repair or replace the wiring harness if damaged.
7. **Replace the defective component** (door switch, latch, or wiring). Reassemble the unit, plug in, and test by running the microwave with the door closed. The F5 code should clear if the repair was successful.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or accessing the internal switch and wiring. A technician should also be consulted if the door switch and wiring test good but the F5 code persists, indicating a possible control board failure that requires diagnostic equipment. If the microwave is still under warranty, contact Kenmore service to avoid voiding coverage. Finally, if you discover arcing, burn marks, or unusual odors during your inspection, stop work and call a qualified appliance repair technician immediately.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200.
