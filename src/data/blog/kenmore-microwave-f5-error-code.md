## Kenmore Microwave F5 Error Code — What It Means

The F5 code on a Kenmore microwave is not universal across all models. On some platforms it signals a failed humidity sensor inside the cavity. On other Kenmore models, especially the 721.86013010 and related 86012/86019 series, F5 indicates a damper switch sensing error. Because Kenmore rebrands appliances from multiple manufacturers, the exact meaning depends on your model and OEM platform. You must identify your full model number before diagnosing the fault, since the same F5 code can point to different components in different units.

## Common Causes

- **Failed humidity sensor** The humidity sensor inside the microwave cavity has failed and cannot report moisture levels to the control board.
- **Damper switch or damper mechanism fault** On certain Kenmore 721-series models the F5 code points to a damper switch sensing error rather than a humidity sensor.
- **Loose or corroded wiring and connectors** Intermittent opens or shorts in the harness to the sensor or switch can trigger the F5 fault even when the component itself is good.
- **Thermistor circuit failure** Some Kenmore 721-series reports cite thermistor-related faults as another cause for the F5 presentation.
- **Moisture intrusion in the control cavity** Temporary control faults from humidity buildup can sometimes be cleared by drying the cavity and power cycling the unit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your exact model number** on the door frame or inside cavity, and look up which OEM platform it belongs to, since F5 meaning varies by model.
2. **Unplug the microwave** and wait at least two minutes to let high-voltage capacitors discharge before removing any panels.
3. **Remove the outer cover** and locate the humidity sensor (often mounted near the vent), damper switch assembly, or thermistor, depending on your model family.
4. **Inspect all connectors and wiring** to the suspect component for loose terminals, corrosion, pinched insulation, or damaged conductors.
5. **Test the component electrically** using a multimeter: check sensor or switch continuity and compare readings with your model's service data, or verify thermistor resistance if your platform uses one.
6. **Replace the failed part** with the correct OEM or authorized replacement rather than attempting to reset a confirmed hardware fault.
7. **Reassemble the unit** and run a test cycle with heat and moisture to confirm the F5 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable discharging high-voltage capacitors or working inside microwave circuitry. If the F5 code returns after you replace the sensor or switch, the control board itself may have a fault that requires professional diagnosis. Also call a pro if you cannot identify your exact model family or locate the correct replacement part, since Kenmore rebrands many different OEM platforms and using the wrong component will not clear the code.
