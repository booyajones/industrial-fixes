## LG Mini Split CH61 Error Code — What It Means

CH61 is a protection code that appears when LG detects an overtemperature condition. In cooling mode, it means the outdoor unit heat exchanger or evaporator temperature has risen too high. In heating mode, it typically signals that the indoor unit temperature has climbed excessively, most often because a clogged indoor filter is restricting airflow. LG displays this code to prevent fire hazards and equipment malfunction by shutting down the system before damage occurs.

## Common Causes

- **Poor outdoor ventilation** The outdoor unit is installed in an enclosed balcony, against a wall with closed windows, or behind mosquito screens that limit exhaust airflow and trap heat.
- **Blocked outdoor unit airflow** Objects, debris, or vegetation are too close to the condenser or fan discharge, leaving insufficient clearance at the front, sides, or rear of the unit.
- **Dirty outdoor heat exchanger** Dust and dirt have accumulated on the outdoor coil, reducing its ability to shed heat and causing the exchanger temperature to climb.
- **Clogged indoor air filter** In heating operation, a heavily dust-loaded indoor return filter restricts airflow over the indoor coil and drives the indoor unit temperature up.
- **Hot ambient environment** Extreme outdoor temperatures or direct sunlight on the condenser unit create heat buildup that the system cannot overcome.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the operating mode (cooling or heating) and note the symptoms, because LG ties CH61 to different temperature locations depending on which mode was active when the code appeared.
2. Inspect ventilation around the outdoor unit for closed balconies, screens, obstructions, or insufficient free space, and remove anything blocking airflow or trapping exhaust heat.
3. Clean the outdoor heat exchanger coil carefully, removing all accumulated dust and debris from the fins and surface to restore heat-transfer capacity.
4. Check and clean the indoor air filter, especially if the unit was in heating mode, and replace the filter if it is heavily dust-loaded or damaged.
5. Restore power by disconnecting the unit for three minutes, then restart and observe whether the code returns during normal operation.
6. Monitor the system through a full cycle in the same mode where the fault occurred, confirming that airflow is unobstructed and temperatures remain stable.
7. Escalate to a qualified technician if CH61 reappears after you have corrected all airflow and filter issues, because persistent codes indicate a deeper mechanical or control problem.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if CH61 returns after you have cleared all obstructions, cleaned both the outdoor coil and indoor filter, and restored proper ventilation around both units. Persistent overtemperature codes after airflow corrections suggest a failing temperature sensor, refrigerant issue, compressor problem, or control board fault that requires diagnostic tools and model-specific service data. LG recommends professional inspection whenever the code cannot be cleared by restoring airflow and cleaning, because the system is protecting itself from a condition that could cause fire or equipment damage.
