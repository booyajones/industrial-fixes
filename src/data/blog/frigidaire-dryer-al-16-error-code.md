## What this code means
AL-16 does not appear as a documented fault code in Frigidaire dryer service materials. The most common Frigidaire heat-related codes are E61 (heater relay or control board fault), E63 (heating circuit fault), and E64 (open circuit in the heating system). If your display shows AL-16, verify you are reading it correctly or check your owner's manual for that specific model. The code may be a variation or abbreviation not widely published.

If the actual code is E61, E63, or E64, these point to problems in the heating circuit. E61 typically means a failed relay on the main control board that powers the heater. E63 and E64 usually indicate an open heating element, broken wire, or short to ground in the heater assembly. A hard reset (unplug for a few minutes) is always the first step before diagnosing further.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first when seeing heat-related codes. Test the heating element for continuity and check for shorts to ground before replacing any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Open or broken heating element (~40%)** The heater coils inside the element can break or open, triggering E63 or E64 codes and preventing heat.
- **Failed heater relay on control board (~25%)** The relay that switches power to the heater can stick or burn out, causing an E61 fault.
- **Loose or burned wiring in heater circuit (~20%)** Connectors at the heater assembly or control board can corrode, arc, or pull loose, opening the circuit.
- **Short to ground in heater or wiring (~10%)** A damaged wire or element touching the cabinet frame creates a ground fault that trips the code.
- **Restricted airflow from clogged vent (~5%)** Blocked exhaust ducting can cause the dryer to overheat and throw heat-related faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker.
2. **Perform a hard reset** by leaving the dryer unplugged for at least three minutes, then reconnect and test to see if the code returns.
3. **Access the heater assembly** by removing the lower front panel or rear cover, depending on your model, and locate the heating element housing.
4. **Inspect the heater wiring and connectors** for burn marks, loose terminals, corrosion, or arcing damage at the element and control board.
5. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter set to ohms. Disconnect the element wires and measure continuity across the element terminals (good elements typically read in the range of 20-30 ohms, but consult your model's wiring diagram for exact values).
6. **Check for shorts to ground** by testing continuity from each element terminal to the dryer cabinet frame. Any continuity indicates a grounded element that must be replaced.
7. **Verify the heater relay** on the control board if the element and wiring are good. Listen for a click when the dryer calls for heat, or swap the control board if the relay is suspect.
8. **Clear the vent system** by disconnecting the exhaust duct and removing all lint buildup from the duct, vent hood, and blower housing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you cannot safely access the heater assembly, or if your multimeter tests show conflicting results. A pro should also handle the repair if the control board needs replacement and you are not confident soldering or swapping connectors. If the dryer uses 240 volts and you see arcing, scorched wiring, or melted plastic anywhere in the heater circuit, stop and call for service to avoid shock or fire hazard.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-90 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
