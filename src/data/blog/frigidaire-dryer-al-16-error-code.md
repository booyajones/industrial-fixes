## Frigidaire Dryer AL-16 Error Code — What It Means

AL-16 does not appear in verified Frigidaire dryer documentation. The closest confirmed fault code for Frigidaire dryers is E64, which indicates an open circuit in the heating element path. Without your exact model number and service sheet, the meaning of AL-16 cannot be confirmed. If your dryer is not heating or displays this code, the control board is likely detecting a break or fault in the heater circuit. This can be caused by a failed heating element, broken wiring, a defective relay on the control board, or a grounded component in the heater assembly.

Because manufacturer documentation does not define AL-16, locate the full model number on the door jamb or rear panel and consult the wiring diagram or tech sheet for your specific dryer. Treat this code as a heating-circuit fault and follow the diagnostic steps below to find the open or damaged component.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Test the heating element for continuity and inspect all heater-circuit wiring and connectors before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Open heating element or coil (~50%)** The heater coil develops a break and no longer completes the circuit, triggering a fault.
- **Damaged or disconnected heater wiring (~25%)** Burned, broken, or loose wires in the heating circuit prevent current flow and cause an open-circuit reading.
- **Failed control board relay (~15%)** The relay that energizes the heater circuit sticks open or burns out, so the board cannot close the circuit.
- **Grounded or shorted heater component (~10%)** A short in the heater assembly or wiring can trip the board's fault detection and mimic an open circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker, then pull the dryer away from the wall.
2. **Locate the heating element assembly**, usually behind the lower front panel or rear access panel depending on your model.
3. **Inspect the element and terminals** for cracks, breaks, burned spots, or loose wires, and photograph any damage.
4. **Test the element for continuity** using a multimeter set to ohms, with both power disconnected and all connectors removed from the element.
5. **Check all heater-circuit wiring** from the element to the control board for breaks, burns, or grounded conductors, and repair or replace damaged wire.
6. **Inspect the control board heater relay** if the element and wiring test good, or replace the control board if the relay is stuck or burned.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the fault is cleared and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if you cannot locate the heating element on your model, or if the fault persists after replacing the element and inspecting all wiring. A pro can pull the wiring diagram for your exact model, test the control board relay with specialized tools, and verify that no additional faults are present. If the dryer is still under warranty or you lack a multimeter and basic hand tools, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent incorrect part replacements.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–80 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
