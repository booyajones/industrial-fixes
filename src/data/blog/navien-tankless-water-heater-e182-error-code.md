## Navien Tankless Water Heater E182 Error — What It Means

The E182 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a communication fault between the main control board and another internal component or sensor. This can mean wiring has come loose, a connector is corroded, or one of the control boards has failed. Because the exact component involved can vary by model, consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific signal path.

In many cases the fault is intermittent and caused by a loose connector or vibration over time. The unit may still attempt to fire but will shut down or fail to respond to demand when the communication path is broken.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, which can cost several hundred dollars. Before buying a board, inspect every connector for corrosion or looseness and test continuity on the harness between boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~50%)** Vibration or moisture causes connectors between the main board and other modules to lose contact or develop corrosion, breaking the communication signal.
- **Failed main control board (~25%)** The primary printed circuit board that coordinates all functions can develop a fault in its communication circuits, though this is less common than wiring issues.
- **Failed secondary board or sensor module (~15%)** A downstream component such as a sensor interface board or display module stops responding, triggering the communication error.
- **Electrical noise or grounding issue (~10%)** Poor grounding or nearby electrical interference can disrupt low-voltage communication signals between boards.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners (typically Phillips-head screws) and lifting the panel away to expose the control boards.
3. **Inspect all wiring connectors** on the main control board and any secondary boards for looseness, corrosion, or damage; press each connector firmly to reseat it.
4. **Check the wiring harnesses** that run between the main board and other modules; look for pinched, chewed, or burnt insulation and test continuity with a multimeter if you have one.
5. **Clean any corroded terminals** with contact cleaner or a pencil eraser, then apply dielectric grease and reconnect firmly.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then reset the unit by pressing the reset button or cycling the power; observe whether the E182 code reappears.
7. **If the code persists**, consult your model's service manual to identify the specific communication path for E182, or call a qualified technician to test the boards with diagnostic tools.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the water heater cabinet, if you cannot locate the source of the communication fault after inspecting connectors and harnesses, or if diagnostic testing indicates a failed control board. A qualified technician has access to manufacturer diagnostic tools and software that can pinpoint which component is failing to communicate. Also call a pro if you find signs of moisture intrusion or electrical damage that may indicate a larger system fault or safety hazard.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-400.
