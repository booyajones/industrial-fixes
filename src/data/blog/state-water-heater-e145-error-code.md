## State Water Heater E145 Error Code — What It Means

The E145 code on State water heaters indicates an overtemperature or safety shutdown condition. The control has detected that water temperature exceeded a safe limit or that combustion conditions triggered a high-limit safety lockout. This fault is typically tied to poor combustion airflow, vent blockage, flame problems, or a failed temperature sensor or limit switch in the safety circuit. Because State rebrands multiple control platforms and fault codes are not universal across all models, you must verify the exact meaning against your specific model's manual or the QR-code documentation provided by State before proceeding with repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked vent or combustion air inlet** Obstructed vent termination, bird nests, or restricted air intake disrupt combustion and trip safety limits.
- **Scale buildup in heat exchanger** Mineral deposits reduce heat transfer and cause water to overheat beyond the safety threshold.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or high-limit switch** A failed or tripped thermal cutoff or temperature sensor in the safety chain triggers a lockout.
- **Combustion blower or inducer failure** Reduced or absent airflow from a failing blower on power-vented models creates unsafe combustion conditions.
- **Pressure switch circuit problem** Plugged nipple, cracked tubing, loose hose connection, or a stuck-open pressure switch prevents proper airflow verification.
- **Improper venting or installation** Incorrect vent slope, damaged pipe, or improper termination causes exhaust restriction and overheating.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Verify your exact model number from the rating plate and consult the model-specific manual or QR-code resource provided by State to confirm the fault definition for your control platform.
2. Check the vent termination and combustion air inlet for blockages, bird nests, debris, or damage that could restrict airflow and cause overheating.
3. Inspect the combustion blower or inducer on power-vented units to confirm it spins freely, operates during a call for heat, and delivers proper airflow.
4. Examine the pressure switch circuit (if equipped) for plugged nipples, cracked or loose tubing, and verify the switch is not stuck open or failed.
5. Test the high-temperature limit switch or thermal cutoff for continuity and check if it has tripped and requires manual reset (model-dependent).
6. Flush the water heater and inspect the heat exchanger for scale buildup that reduces heat transfer and drives overtemperature conditions.
7. Clear the root cause completely, then reset the control per the manufacturer instructions and monitor for return of the fault before returning the unit to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot locate your model-specific fault definition, if the vent system or combustion blower requires disassembly you are not comfortable performing, or if the fault returns after you have cleared blockages and flushed scale. Overtemperature and combustion-safety faults involve gas, venting, and pressure-switch circuits that require proper diagnostic tools and training. If the high-limit switch has tripped repeatedly or the control board itself is suspected, professional testing and replacement with the correct OEM part is the safest path forward.
