## GE Dishwasher C7 Error Code — What It Means

The C7 error code on a GE dishwasher indicates a temperature sensor fault. The control board is not receiving a valid signal from the water temperature sensor or its wiring circuit. This is not a drain or wash fault but rather a specific sensor and circuit problem.

The fault can be triggered by a failed sensor, damaged wiring, corroded or loose connector terminals, or in rare cases a faulty control board input. The dishwasher will not run a normal cycle until the sensor circuit is restored.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first. Instead, inspect the sensor connector for browned or loose terminals and test the wiring before replacing any electronic boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose, corroded, or browned sensor connector terminals (~45%)** Heat and moisture can damage the plug where the sensor connects to the wiring harness or control board, creating intermittent contact.
- **Failed water temperature sensor (thermistor) (~35%)** The sensor element itself can fail and no longer provide a readable resistance value to the control board.
- **Damaged or pinched sensor wiring harness (~12%)** Cuts, pinches, or internal wire breaks in the harness prevent the signal from reaching the control board.
- **Faulty control board input circuit (~8%)** If the sensor and wiring test good, the control board may have a failed input channel for the temperature signal.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault may be intermittent; monitor the dishwasher and inspect the sensor connector for loose or corroded terminals.<br><strong>No:</strong> The fault is steady; proceed to inspect and test the sensor wiring and connector.</div>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are the sensor connector terminals clean, tight, and free of browning or corrosion?</summary>

</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the fault persist after replacing the temperature sensor?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The control board likely has a faulty input circuit; call a technician or replace the control board.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor was the problem; the dishwasher should now operate normally.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dishwasher and wait three to five minutes for a full reset.
2. **Restore power** and test the dishwasher to see if the C7 code returns.
3. **Remove the lower access panel** (usually held by screws at the bottom front) to expose the sensor and wiring.
4. **Locate the water temperature sensor** near the sump or circulation pump and inspect its connector for loose pins, corrosion, heat discoloration, or browned terminals.
5. **Tug-test the wiring harness** gently and look for cuts, pinches, or damage along the entire run from the sensor to the control board.
6. **Replace the temperature sensor** if the connector and wiring are sound but the fault persists after reset.
7. **Replace the main control board** only if the new sensor and verified wiring do not resolve the C7 code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the dishwasher cabinet, if you cannot locate the temperature sensor, or if the fault persists after replacing both the sensor and verifying all wiring. A technician can measure sensor resistance values against the service manual, test control board inputs with a multimeter, and access model-specific fault data that may not appear in consumer documentation. If the control board needs replacement and your dishwasher is still under warranty, a service call may be covered.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-75 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
