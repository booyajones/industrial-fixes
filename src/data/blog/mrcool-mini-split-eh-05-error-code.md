## MRCOOL Mini Split EH 05 Error — What It Means

The EH 05 code on a MRCOOL mini split indicates a fault with the electric auxiliary heating element or its control circuit. This code appears when the system detects the heater is not operating as expected, either because of a wiring issue, a failed heater element, a stuck relay, or a problem with the control board that commands the heater on and off.

Because mini splits typically use heat-pump operation for most heating, the auxiliary electric heater only runs during defrost cycles or when extra heat is needed in very cold weather. The code may appear only in those conditions. Consult your model's installation and service manual for the exact diagnostic procedure and component locations, as internal layout varies across MRCOOL's product line.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board thinking the relay has failed, when in fact a loose wire or a burned-out heater element is the real problem. Use a multimeter to measure continuity across the heater terminals and check wire connections before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded heater wiring (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen spade terminals or corrode the connections between the control board and the heater element, breaking the circuit.
- **Failed auxiliary heater element (~30%)** The resistance coil inside the electric heater may burn out or develop an open circuit, preventing current flow.
- **Stuck or failed heater relay on the control board (~20%)** The relay that switches power to the heater can weld shut, fail to close, or burn contacts, causing the board to flag a fault.
- **Faulty control board heater driver circuit (~10%)** A damaged transistor or trace on the board may prevent the heater relay from receiving the correct signal.
- **Incorrect DIP switch or parameter setting (~5%)** If the board is configured for a unit without auxiliary heat, or if installer settings are wrong, the code may appear even though hardware is intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and confirm the unit is de-energized with a non-contact voltage tester.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** according to your service manual to access the control board and heater assembly.
3. **Inspect all wire connections** between the control board and the auxiliary heater. Look for loose spade terminals, corrosion, or burn marks.
4. **Use a multimeter** set to resistance (ohms) to measure continuity across the heater element terminals. Consult your model's table for the expected resistance range.
5. **Check the heater relay** on the control board. Listen for a click when the relay should engage, or use the multimeter to test the coil and contacts if you have the wiring diagram.
6. **Reconnect or replace** any damaged wires, terminals, or the heater element if it shows an open circuit. make sure replacement parts match your unit's voltage and wattage specifications.
7. **Restore power** and test the system in heating mode. Monitor for the EH 05 code during a defrost cycle or when outdoor temperatures are low enough to call for auxiliary heat.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring, if you cannot locate the heater element or control board in your unit, or if the code persists after you have verified wiring and heater continuity. Diagnosing relay and driver-circuit faults requires specialized tools and knowledge of the board schematic. A technician can also confirm that the unit's refrigerant charge and defrost cycle are operating correctly, since poor defrost performance can increase reliance on auxiliary heat and mask other issues.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $180-400.
