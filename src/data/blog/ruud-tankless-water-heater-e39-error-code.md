## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E39 Error — What It Means

The E39 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with a sensor circuit or flame detection system, though the exact meaning can vary by model. Because error code definitions are not standardized across all Ruud tankless models, the first step is to consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit's service panel to confirm what E39 means for your specific heater.

In general, E39-type codes point to communication failures between the control board and a sensor, a faulty sensor reading, or a flame rod that cannot detect combustion properly. The heater shuts down to prevent unsafe operation. Most often, the issue is a loose connection, a dirty flame rod, or a sensor that has drifted out of specification rather than a failed control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board when the real problem is a dirty flame rod or a loose sensor wire. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and check all sensor connectors before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can cause connectors at temperature sensors or the flame rod to loosen or corrode, breaking the signal path to the control board.
- **Dirty or worn flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or mineral deposits on the flame rod insulate it and prevent proper flame detection, triggering a fault code.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~20%)** Inlet, outlet, or exhaust temperature sensors can drift out of range or fail completely, sending invalid readings to the control board.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** The main PCB may lose the ability to process sensor signals correctly due to component failure or water damage.
- **Wiring harness damage (~7%)** Rodents, pinching during installation, or heat exposure can break or short wires between sensors and the board.
- **Poor grounding (~3%)** If the unit lacks a solid earth ground, flame sensing and sensor circuits may produce intermittent or false fault signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off** the gas supply valve and switch off electrical power at the breaker or unplug the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners to access the burner chamber and control board.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe near the burner, and inspect it for carbon or white mineral deposits.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with 220-grit sandpaper or a fine wire brush, then wipe with isopropyl alcohol and let dry.
5. **Inspect all sensor connectors** on temperature sensors and the flame rod harness for loose pins, corrosion, or moisture; disconnect, clean with contact cleaner, and reconnect firmly.
6. **Check the wiring harness** between sensors and the control board for pinched, burned, or chewed wires; repair or replace damaged sections.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then restart the heater and observe whether the E39 code returns; if it does, measure sensor resistance with a multimeter and compare to your model's specification table.
8. **Replace the failed sensor or control board** if testing confirms a component is out of range or if the code persists after all connections are verified clean and tight.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas lines, if you lack a multimeter to test sensors, or if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and reseating all connectors. Gas-fired appliances require careful handling to avoid gas leaks and carbon monoxide hazards. A technician can measure sensor resistance, verify proper grounding, inspect the gas valve and burner for related issues, and replace the control board safely if needed. If your heater is still under warranty, professional service may be required to keep coverage valid.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
