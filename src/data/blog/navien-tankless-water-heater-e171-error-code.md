## Navien Tankless Water Heater E171 Error — What It Means

The E171 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically signals a problem with the flow detection system or internal communication between the flow sensor and the control board. This code appears when the unit cannot properly read water flow through the heat exchanger, either because the sensor itself has failed, the sensor signal is being interrupted, or debris is blocking accurate flow measurement.

Because Navien uses proprietary diagnostics and the exact meaning of error codes can vary by model series, consult your owner's manual or the technical label inside the unit for model-specific troubleshooting steps. The code often appears during startup or when hot water demand suddenly changes, and may be accompanied by the unit shutting down or failing to ignite.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a dirty inlet filter or loose sensor connector. Always clean the filter and check wiring connections before replacing any electronic components.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked inlet filter (~35%)** Sediment and mineral deposits restrict water flow and prevent the sensor from reading flow rate accurately.
- **Flow sensor failure (~30%)** The flow sensor turbine or Hall-effect sensor stops generating the correct signal due to wear, mineral buildup, or electronic failure.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~15%)** The wiring harness connector at the flow sensor becomes loose, oxidized, or damaged and interrupts the signal to the control board.
- **Control board communication fault (~12%)** The main PCB cannot process or receive flow sensor data due to a board-level fault or firmware glitch.
- **Low water pressure (~8%)** Incoming water pressure falls below the minimum threshold needed for the flow sensor to register demand.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the inlet filter visibly clogged with sediment or debris?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Clean or replace the filter and restart the unit. If the error clears, the blockage was the cause.<br><strong>No:</strong> Proceed to check the flow sensor and electrical connections.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Do you hear water flowing through the unit when a hot tap opens, but the burner does not ignite?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The flow sensor or its wiring is likely at fault. Inspect the sensor connector and test the sensor.<br><strong>No:</strong> Check for low water pressure or a closed isolation valve upstream of the heater.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error appear immediately on power-up, even with no demand?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> A control board or sensor wiring fault is likely. Test continuity in the sensor harness and inspect board connections.<br><strong>No:</strong> The error is triggered by flow conditions. Focus on the filter, sensor, and water pressure.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the cold water inlet isolation valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the access panel fasteners and locate the inlet water filter housing at the cold water inlet pipe.
3. **Unscrew the filter housing cap** (usually hand-tight or requiring a wrench) and pull out the filter screen, then rinse it under clean water to remove sediment.
4. **Locate the flow sensor** on the inlet manifold (a small cylindrical component with a wiring connector) and unplug the connector, inspect pins for corrosion, and clean with contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Reconnect the flow sensor** firmly, ensuring the locking tab clicks into place, and reinstall the inlet filter and housing cap.
6. **Open the cold water valve** slowly and check for leaks around the filter housing and sensor, then restore electrical power at the breaker.
7. **Run a hot water tap** for one minute and observe whether the error clears and the burner ignites; if the code persists, test or replace the flow sensor or consult a technician for board diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you have cleaned the filter, verified all wiring connections, and replaced the flow sensor but the E171 error persists. Control board diagnostics require specialized tools and knowledge of the unit's CAN-bus communication protocol. Also contact a professional if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, electrical wiring, or water shut-off valves, or if the unit is under warranty and you want to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
