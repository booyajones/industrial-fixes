## Maytag Washer F27 Error Code — What It Means

The F27 code on a Maytag washer signals an overflow condition. The control board sees the pressure switch overflow contact closed for more than 60 seconds and treats this as the tub overfilling. When F27 appears, the machine typically locks the door and runs the drain pump continuously to remove excess water.

In practice, this code rarely means the washer actually filled with too much water. It usually points to a problem in the pressure switch circuit, a blocked drain path, or wiring and control issues that make the switch think the tub is overflowing when it is not.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the pressure switch or control board first. Before buying parts, inspect the drain hose for kinks and clean the pump filter and drain pump inlet for debris.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~35%)** A bent, crushed, or debris-blocked drain hose prevents water from leaving, which keeps the pressure switch overflow contact closed long enough to trigger F27.
- **Debris in drain pump or filter (~30%)** Coins, lint, or small objects stuck in the pump impeller or filter block water flow and hold the pressure switch in the overflow state.
- **Faulty pressure switch or stuck overflow contact (~15%)** The pressure switch itself can fail, or its overflow contact can stick closed and falsely signal an overfill condition even when the tub is empty.
- **Weak or failed drain pump (~10%)** A pump that runs but has low output or is partially jammed cannot remove water fast enough, leaving the pressure switch triggered.
- **Blocked or disconnected pressure hose to the switch (~7%)** The air tube from the tub to the pressure switch can be clogged, kinked, or pulled off, sending the wrong signal to the control.
- **Loose wiring or connectors (~3%)** Corroded or unplugged harness connections at the drain pump, pressure switch, or control board can cause intermittent or false overflow readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** for at least 30 minutes to reset the control board and clear any temporary fault memory.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** from the back of the pump to the standpipe for kinks, clogs, or damage, and straighten or replace it as needed.
3. **Remove the pump filter or clean-out trap** (usually at the front lower corner) and clean out lint, coins, or debris, then check the pump inlet behind the filter for obstructions.
4. **Test the drain path** by disconnecting the hose from the standpipe and running water through it or using a shop vac to pull air and confirm no blockage.
5. **Check wiring and connectors** at the drain pump, pressure switch, and control board for loose, corroded, or damaged terminals, and reseat or repair them.
6. **Test the pressure switch** by locating it (usually on the control panel or near the tub), checking that the overflow contact is not stuck closed, and verifying it clicks or changes state when air pressure is applied or removed from its tube.
7. **Replace the failed component** (drain pump, pressure switch, or damaged hose) based on your test results, then reassemble and run a full wash cycle to confirm F27 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared the drain path, cleaned the pump, and reset the control but F27 still returns. A technician can safely test the pressure switch with a manometer or pressure gauge, trace wiring faults with a multimeter, and replace the control board if the overflow circuit itself has failed. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable tilting the washer to access the pump or working near live 120 V AC wiring during diagnostics.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-85 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
