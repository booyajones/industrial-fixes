## Maytag Washer F27 Error Code — What It Means

F27 on a Maytag washer signals an overflow condition or water-level fault. The machine is detecting either too much water in the tub or a problem in the pressure-sensing system that monitors water level. The control board receives a signal from the pressure switch or sensor that something is wrong, and it halts the cycle to prevent flooding.

This code does not always mean water is actually overflowing. It can also appear when the pressure hose is blocked, kinked, or disconnected, which sends a false high-level reading to the control. Real overfill can occur if the inlet valve sticks open or if a drain restriction causes water to back up. The fault lies somewhere in the chain from the tub air dome through the pressure hose to the pressure switch and onward to the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or pressure switch first. Always inspect the pressure hose for blockages, cracks, and secure connections before ordering any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Pressure hose blocked, cracked, or disconnected (~40%)** The small air hose running from the tub to the pressure switch can become clogged with detergent residue, kinked behind the washer, or pulled loose during a previous repair, sending a false overflow signal.
- **Faulty pressure switch or sensor (~25%)** The pressure switch itself can fail mechanically or electronically, reading high pressure even when the tub is empty or at the correct fill level.
- **Drain pump filter or drain path restricted (~15%)** A clogged pump filter, kinked drain hose, or blocked standpipe can slow drainage enough that water backs up and triggers the overflow contact during fill or rinse.
- **Water inlet valve stuck open (~10%)** If the inlet valve fails to close completely, water continues to flow into the tub even after the control stops calling for fill, causing a true overfill condition.
- **Wiring harness or connector corrosion (~7%)** Corroded pins, loose connectors, or damaged wire insulation between the pressure switch and control board can produce intermittent or persistent false signals.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The central control unit or main control board may misinterpret a good pressure signal or fail internally, posting F27 even when the pressure system is functioning correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and shut off both hot and cold water supply valves to make the work area safe.
2. **Remove the front lower panel or rear panel** to access the drain pump filter and pressure hose, depending on your model's service access points.
3. **Inspect and clean the drain pump filter** by unscrewing the filter cap, pulling out any debris, and rinsing it under running water to make sure unrestricted drainage.
4. **Locate the pressure hose** running from the air dome on the outer tub to the pressure switch, usually mounted on the control housing or back panel, and check for kinks, cracks, disconnections, or blockages inside the hose.
5. **Disconnect the pressure hose** at both ends, blow through it gently to confirm it is clear, and reconnect it securely, making sure the fittings seat fully and any retaining clips are in place.
6. **Inspect the water inlet valve** by removing the top or rear panel, checking for mineral deposits or a stuck solenoid plunger, and replacing the valve if water drips through when power is off.
7. **Test the pressure switch** by using a multimeter to check continuity across its terminals while applying gentle suction to the hose port, or replace it if the hose and wiring are good and the code persists, then reassemble the washer, restore power and water, and run a test cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the cabinet, if the wiring harness shows widespread corrosion that requires soldering or connector replacement, or if the code persists after you have verified that the pressure hose is clear, the pressure switch tests good, and the inlet valve closes properly. A technician can perform voltage and resistance checks on the control board and pressure circuit with diagnostic equipment, and can safely handle any high-voltage wiring. Also call for help if the washer continues to overfill even after replacing the inlet valve, since that may indicate a control-board fault or a rare issue with the water-level pressure chamber in the tub itself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
