## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E24 Error — What It Means

The E24 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame sensing or ignition issue. The unit is detecting a problem with establishing or maintaining a flame during the heating cycle. This can mean the burner failed to ignite, the flame was lost during operation, or the flame sensor is not reading the flame correctly.

Because tankless water heaters rely on precise gas flow, air supply, and electronic ignition, this code can appear for several reasons ranging from simple gas valve shutoff to a failed component. Always consult your specific model's manual for the exact definition, as error codes can vary slightly across Ruud's product line.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is a dirty flame rod that simply needs cleaning. Inspect and clean the flame sensor before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod sensor (~40%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensor prevents it from detecting the flame even when ignition occurs.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an empty propane tank prevents proper ignition or flame stability.
- **Failed igniter or weak spark (~15%)** The igniter electrode may be cracked, fouled, or positioned incorrectly so it cannot create a reliable spark.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructed air intake or exhaust pipes starve the burner of oxygen or cause pressure imbalances that extinguish the flame.
- **Faulty gas valve (~7%)** The gas valve may be sticking, failing to open fully, or have a bad solenoid that interrupts gas flow during operation.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~3%)** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a failing control board can cause intermittent flame detection failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the water heater at the breaker and the manual gas shutoff valve before any inspection or service.
2. **Check gas supply** by confirming the gas valve is fully open and testing another gas appliance in the home to verify adequate pressure.
3. **Inspect venting** by looking at both the air intake and exhaust pipes for blockages, bird nests, ice buildup, or disconnected sections.
4. **Power cycle the unit** by leaving the breaker off for at least 30 seconds, then turn it back on and attempt to restart the heater.
5. **Remove the front cover** (consult your model's service manual for screw locations) to access the burner chamber and flame sensor rod.
6. **Clean the flame sensor** by gently wiping the rod with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits and corrosion, then reinstall it securely.
7. **Check the igniter electrode** for cracks, carbon buildup, or incorrect gap, and clean or adjust as needed according to the manual's specifications.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician whenever you smell gas, lack the tools to safely access the burner chamber, or are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. Professional service is also required if cleaning the flame sensor and checking gas supply do not resolve the E24 code, since diagnosing gas valve operation, testing pressure with a manometer, and replacing control boards involve specialized skills and safety procedures. Tankless water heaters operate under precise combustion parameters, and incorrect repairs can create carbon monoxide hazards or void your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
