## Navien E334 Error Code — What It Means

The E334 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a problem with the combustion fan system. This fan pulls air into the burner and exhausts combustion gases safely outside. When the control board detects that the fan is not operating correctly or cannot reach the required speed, it halts the heating process and triggers this code.

The error can stem from mechanical failure of the fan motor itself, electrical wiring problems, or obstruction in the air intake or exhaust system. Because the combustion fan is a critical safety component that manages ventilation and prevents dangerous gas buildup, the unit will not fire until the issue is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the actual problem is a failed fan motor or a loose connector at the fan. Check the fan wiring and listen for the fan starting before replacing any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed combustion fan motor (~50%)** The fan motor bearings seize or the windings burn out, preventing the fan from spinning at the speed the control board expects.
- **Loose or corroded fan wiring (~20%)** Connector pins at the fan motor or control board can corrode or work loose, breaking the electrical signal that powers the fan.
- **Blocked intake or exhaust vent (~15%)** Debris, bird nests, or ice blocking the vent pipes create back-pressure that prevents the fan from reaching proper speed.
- **Faulty control board fan output (~10%)** The control board's fan driver circuit can fail, sending incorrect voltage or no power to the fan motor.
- **Capacitor failure on fan motor (~5%)** Some models use a run capacitor to start the fan, and a failed capacitor will prevent the motor from spinning up.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shut-off valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to access the combustion fan, which is typically mounted near the burner assembly.
3. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vent pipes** for blockages, debris, or ice, and clear any obstructions you find.
4. **Check the fan motor wiring connector** for loose pins, corrosion, or damage, and reseat or clean the connector as needed.
5. **Test the fan motor** by gently spinning the blades by hand to confirm they rotate freely without binding or grinding.
6. **Restore power** and observe whether the fan starts when the unit calls for heat. Listen for unusual noise or hesitation.
7. **Replace the combustion fan motor** if it does not spin, spins erratically, or shows no continuity across its terminals when tested with a multimeter.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician for any E334 error. Combustion fan diagnostics require safe handling of gas connections, electrical testing at the control board, and sometimes disassembly of the burner chamber. A technician will confirm whether the fan motor has failed, test the control board's output, and check for vent blockages or pressure switch faults that can mimic fan problems. Incorrect repairs to the combustion system can create carbon monoxide hazards or void your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $250-$450.
