## Navien Tankless Water Heater E153 Error — What It Means

The E153 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a problem with the flow sensor circuit or the main control board (PCB). The exact meaning can vary slightly between Navien models, so consult your owner's manual for the specific definition. Generally, the unit is detecting an abnormal signal from the flow sensor, which monitors water movement through the heat exchanger. This can prevent the heater from firing or cause it to shut down during operation. The flow sensor sends a pulse signal to the PCB, and when that signal is missing, inconsistent, or out of range, the E153 fault appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main PCB when the real problem is a failed flow sensor or a corroded connector. Test the flow sensor's resistance and check all plug connections before ordering a new control board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty flow sensor (~50%)** The flow sensor paddle or turbine can wear out, or the sensor's internal electronics can fail, sending no signal or an erratic signal to the PCB.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~25%)** The plug or individual pins at the flow sensor or PCB can oxidize or work loose over time, breaking the circuit.
- **Scale or debris blocking the sensor (~15%)** Hard water deposits or small particles can jam the flow sensor's moving parts, preventing it from generating a proper pulse signal.
- **Failed main PCB (~10%)** The control board itself can develop a fault in the flow sensor input circuit, though this is less common than sensor failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the inlet isolation valve to stop water flow.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners, then locate the flow sensor (typically a cylindrical device mounted inline in the cold water path).
3. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the flow sensor and inspect both the plug and the pins for corrosion, moisture, or damage. Clean with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
4. **Remove the flow sensor** by unscrewing the retaining bolts or clips and gently pulling it from the pipe. Check the paddle or turbine inside for scale buildup or obstructions and flush with vinegar if necessary.
5. **Test the flow sensor** with a multimeter set to measure resistance. Consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance range. If the reading is infinite or zero, the sensor has failed.
6. **Install the new or cleaned flow sensor** with fresh O-rings or gaskets, reconnect the wiring harness, and torque fasteners to the manufacturer's specification.
7. **Restore power and water** to the unit, open a hot tap, and verify the error code does not reappear and the burner fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with water and electrical connections, if the error persists after replacing the flow sensor and checking all wiring, or if you suspect the main PCB needs replacement. PCB diagnosis often requires specialized test equipment and familiarity with the unit's wiring diagram. A pro can also descale the heat exchanger and flush the entire system if hard water buildup is widespread, which may prevent future sensor problems.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
