## Samsung Oven E-28 Error Code — What It Means

The E-28 code on a Samsung oven indicates a temperature sensor circuit fault, specifically a shorted sensor or a sensor reading abnormally low resistance. The oven uses an NTC thermistor to monitor cavity temperature. As the oven heats up, the thermistor's resistance should fall in a predictable way. When the control board sees a resistance reading that is too low (less than about 930 Ω at room temperature), it flags an E-28 fault and halts the heating cycle to protect the appliance.

This code points to a problem in the sensor circuit itself rather than a heating element failure. The fault may lie in the sensor element, the wiring and connectors between the sensor and the control board, or the control board's ability to read the sensor correctly. Although a power reset can sometimes clear temporary faults, a persistent E-28 after cycling power indicates that physical inspection and resistance testing are required.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when the sensor or its wiring harness is actually at fault. Always measure the sensor's resistance at room temperature (around 1080 Ω at 25°C) and inspect the connector and wiring for damage before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed temperature sensor element** The NTC thermistor itself shorts internally or drifts out of specification, causing a resistance reading outside the normal range.
- **Damaged sensor wiring or connector** Insulation on the sensor leads can melt, fray, or short against the oven cavity, or the connector pins may corrode or bend.
- **Main control board fault** The electronic control board may misread a healthy sensor circuit due to a failed input stage or corrupted firmware, particularly on certain Samsung models like the NE595R0ABSR.
- **Physical damage to sensor or harness** Scrapes, bent leads, or pinched wires during prior service or self-cleaning cycles can create intermittent shorts that trip the E-28 code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** to the oven by switching off the circuit breaker or unplugging the range from the wall outlet.
2. **Remove the oven racks** and locate the temperature sensor, which is a metal probe protruding into the rear or side wall of the oven cavity.
3. **Unscrew the sensor bracket** (usually one or two screws) and gently pull the sensor and its wiring harness out far enough to access the connector on the back of the oven.
4. **Inspect the sensor, wiring, and connector** for visible damage such as melted insulation, bent pins, corrosion, or bare wire touching metal.
5. **Measure the sensor resistance** with a digital multimeter set to the ohms range. At room temperature (around 25°C), a healthy Samsung oven sensor should read approximately 1080 Ω. A reading below 930 Ω indicates a short and an E-28 fault.
6. **Replace the temperature sensor** if the resistance is out of range or if the sensor shows physical damage. Disconnect the old sensor's plug, remove it from the bracket, install the new sensor, and reconnect the harness.
7. **Restore power** and run a test bake cycle at a moderate temperature. If the E-28 code returns after sensor replacement and the wiring is intact, the main control board is the next diagnostic target and should be replaced or serviced by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt appliances, if the sensor and wiring both test good but the code remains, or if you need to diagnose the control board's input circuitry. A technician can use specialized meters and wiring diagrams to pinpoint board-level faults and will have access to OEM parts and firmware updates for your specific Samsung model. Also call for service if the sensor is difficult to access due to built-in cabinetry or if you lack a multimeter and are unsure how to measure resistance safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
