## State Tankless Water Heater E34 Error Code — What It Means

The E34 error code on State tankless water heaters is not explicitly defined in widely available manufacturer documentation, but based on A. O. Smith service procedures (State is part of the A. O. Smith family), codes in this range typically indicate a thermistor or temperature sensor fault. The control board has detected an out-of-range reading from one of the unit's temperature sensors, which monitor inlet water temperature, outlet temperature, or heat exchanger conditions. This fault prevents the heater from firing or causes it to shut down to protect internal components.

Because State does not publish a universal E34 definition across all models, you should verify the exact meaning in your unit's installation and service manual using your model number. The manufacturer-recommended troubleshooting workflow focuses on checking thermistor connections, testing sensor resistance, and replacing the faulty sensor or control board if the error persists after sensor checks.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded thermistor connector** Vibration or moisture can loosen the plug at the sensor, causing intermittent or open-circuit readings that trigger the fault.
- **Failed thermistor** The temperature sensor itself has drifted out of specification or has an internal break, sending incorrect resistance values to the control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** Pinched, nicked, or burned wires between the thermistor and the board create open or short circuits that the board reads as a sensor fault.
- **Control board fault** The circuit board may have a failed input channel or solder joint that misreads a healthy sensor, generating a false E34 code.
- **Scale buildup on sensor probe** Hard water mineral deposits insulate the thermistor tip from the water, causing slow or inaccurate temperature response and triggering the error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas.** Flip the circuit breaker and close the manual gas valve to make the unit safe for inspection.
2. **Record the error code and model number.** Write down your exact State model number from the rating plate and look up the E34 definition in your installation manual or call State technical support to confirm the specific sensor indicated.
3. **Locate and inspect the thermistor connectors.** Follow the wiring from the control board to each temperature sensor (usually inlet and outlet on the heat exchanger). Unplug each connector, examine the pins for corrosion or moisture, and reconnect firmly.
4. **Measure thermistor resistance.** With power still off, disconnect one thermistor at a time and use a multimeter on the Ohms setting to measure resistance across the sensor leads. Compare readings between sensors. If one reads significantly higher than the others or shows infinite resistance, that sensor has failed.
5. **Check sensor wiring for damage.** Trace each thermistor wire harness from sensor to board, looking for pinch points, burned insulation, or melted jackets. Repair or replace damaged wire runs.
6. **Clear any scale from sensor probes.** If your water is hard and sensors are accessible, gently clean mineral deposits from the thermistor tip using white vinegar or a descaling solution, then rinse and reinstall.
7. **Replace the faulty thermistor or control board.** If a sensor reads out of range, order the correct thermistor for your model and swap it. If all sensors test normal and connections are clean, replace the control board. Restore power and gas, run hot water, and confirm the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error returns after replacing the thermistor, or if you cannot locate model-specific documentation for your State unit. A professional has access to manufacturer service bulletins, can perform precise resistance and voltage tests on the control board, and will verify safe gas and electrical operation after any repair. If your heater is still under warranty, contact State or your installer before opening the unit to avoid voiding coverage.
