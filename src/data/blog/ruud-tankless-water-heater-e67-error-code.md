## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E67 Error — What It Means

The E67 code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame-sensing or ignition-stability problem. The control board detects that the flame is either not forming properly, is unstable, or is not being reliably sensed by the flame rod. This can stem from fuel-delivery issues, sensor contamination, or electrical faults in the ignition circuit.

Because the exact definition can vary slightly across Ruud models, consult your owner's manual or the label inside the service panel to confirm. In general, E67 points to the flame-proving circuit rather than a simple ignition lockout, meaning the burner may light but the controller cannot verify stable combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or igniter when the real problem is a flame rod with carbon buildup. Clean the flame rod with fine emery cloth before ordering new valves.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame-sensing probe prevents reliable flame current from reaching the control board.
- **Insufficient or unstable gas supply (~25%)** Partially closed valve, low inlet pressure, or regulator faults can cause weak or flickering flames that fail the sensing check.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Obstructed air intake or exhaust creates poor combustion and irregular flame shape, disrupting the flame sensor signal.
- **Faulty flame rod or wiring (~7%)** A cracked ceramic insulator, broken wire, or loose connection interrupts the flame current path to the board.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The circuit that reads flame current can fail, misinterpreting a good flame as absent or unstable.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the breaker and close the gas shut-off valve upstream of the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws, then locate the flame rod inside the burner chamber (consult your model's diagram if needed).
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for black carbon deposits, white corrosion, or cracks in the ceramic insulator.
4. **Clean the rod** gently with fine-grit emery cloth or steel wool until the metal is shiny, taking care not to bend or stress the probe.
5. **Check the flame-rod wire** from the probe to the control board for fraying, loose spade connectors, or corrosion at the terminals.
6. **Verify venting** by walking outside to the termination cap and removing any leaves, snow, or bird nests from the intake and exhaust pipes.
7. **Reassemble the cover**, restore gas and power, and run a hot-water tap to test. Monitor for stable ignition and watch for the E67 code to clear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed gas technician if you are not comfortable working around gas piping, if cleaning the flame rod does not resolve the code, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a faulty gas valve. Professionals have manometers to measure inlet and manifold pressure, combustion analyzers to tune the air-fuel mix, and the training to safely replace igniters, valves, and control boards. Any work on gas connections, pressure regulators, or sealed burner assemblies should be left to a pro to avoid fire, explosion, or carbon-monoxide hazards.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
