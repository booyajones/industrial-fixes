## GE Refrigerator PF Error Code — What It Means

PF on a GE refrigerator stands for power failure or power interruption. The control board detected that the appliance lost its power supply or experienced a voltage disturbance. This code is typically a stored alert rather than a fault in the cooling system itself. It usually appears after a utility outage, a tripped breaker, a loose plug, or another brief electrical disruption. The refrigerator may still be cooling normally once power is restored.

Unlike component faults, PF does not point to a failed compressor, fan, or sealed-system part. Instead, it tells you the unit saw an interruption in its supply voltage. If PF appears once after a known outage and clears without returning, no repair is needed. If the code returns repeatedly, the problem may be an unstable household circuit, a damaged power cord, or an internal control board issue rather than a temporary power loss.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board thinking PF means a board failure, when the real cause is often a loose plug or tripped breaker that costs nothing to fix.

## Common Causes

- **Utility outage or power flicker (~50%)** A brief loss of line voltage triggers the PF alert and the code remains on the display until you clear it.
- **Tripped breaker or blown fuse (~25%)** The circuit supplying the refrigerator opened and the control logged the interruption when power returned.
- **Loose power cord or poor outlet connection (~15%)** A plug that is not fully seated or a worn receptacle can cause intermittent power loss and repeated PF codes.
- **Damaged power cord (~5%)** A frayed or pinched cord creates an open circuit or intermittent connection that the control reads as a power failure.
- **Control board or power supply fault (~5%)** If line power is verified stable and PF keeps returning, the internal power electronics may be failing and misreading the supply.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the refrigerator has stable power** by checking that the plug is fully inserted into the outlet and that the breaker supplying the circuit is on and not tripped.
2. **Inspect the power cord** along its entire length for cuts, pinches, or fraying, and replace the cord if you find damage.
3. **Test the outlet** with a multimeter or plug in another appliance to verify the receptacle is delivering consistent voltage and is not loose or worn.
4. **Clear the PF code** using your refrigerator's control interface (consult your owner's manual for the exact button sequence, as it varies by model) and monitor whether the code returns.
5. **Verify cooling operation** by checking that the compressor is running and the interior temperature is dropping to the set point after you clear the code.
6. **Check the breaker panel** for signs of a weak or intermittently tripping breaker, and replace the breaker if it has been opening without cause.
7. **If PF returns repeatedly**, inspect all wiring connections between the outlet and the control board, and test or replace the main control board if the supply side is verified stable and the code persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if PF returns after you have verified stable line voltage, a tight plug connection, and an undamaged cord. Persistent PF codes with good supply power usually point to a control board or internal power-supply fault that requires diagnostic tools and board-level troubleshooting. Also call if you are uncomfortable working around household voltage or if your refrigerator is not cooling even after the PF code is cleared, since that suggests an independent cooling fault that needs separate diagnosis.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0 (if plug or breaker), or $50–120 for power cord replacement if damaged, 15–30 min. A pro service call runs about $100–250 service call if control board diagnostics or replacement is needed.
