## What this code means
On Maytag commercial-grade residential dryers, F4E3 signals restricted airflow. The dryer detects that air is not moving through the drum and vent system as freely as it should. The dryer may continue running and you can clear the display by touching any key, but the underlying airflow problem still needs correction or the code will return. Note that on Whirlpool all-in-one washer/dryer combo units, F4E3 instead indicates a dryer blower fan failure, so the exact meaning depends on which product family you own.

Restricted airflow is almost always a venting issue rather than a component failure. Lint buildup, a crushed vent hose, or a duct run with too many elbows will all starve the dryer of air and trigger the code. Fixing F4E3 is about restoring a clear path from the drum through the vent to the outside.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or blower motor when F4E3 appears. Start by pulling the dryer away from the wall and inspecting the vent hose for kinks or blockage, which costs nothing and solves the majority of cases.

## Common Causes

- **Lint screen clogged or coated (~35%)** The lint screen is blocked with lint or has a film of fabric softener residue that stops air from passing through.
- **Vent hose crushed or kinked (~30%)** The flexible vent hose behind the dryer is pinched between the dryer and the wall or has a sharp bend that blocks airflow.
- **Exhaust duct blocked with lint (~20%)** The rigid or semi-rigid duct run from the dryer to the outside is packed with lint, or the vent run is too long with too many elbows.
- **Exterior vent hood blocked (~10%)** The flap on the outdoor vent hood is stuck closed, clogged with lint, or obstructed by a bird nest or debris.
- **Blower housing lint buildup (~5%)** Lint has accumulated inside the blower housing or around the blower wheel, reducing airflow even when the vent path is clear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the code** by touching any key on the control panel or cycling power off and back on.
2. **Remove and inspect the lint screen** for heavy lint buildup or a waxy film from dryer sheets. If water beads on the screen instead of soaking through, wash the screen in warm water with mild detergent, rinse thoroughly, and let it dry completely before reinstalling.
3. **Pull the dryer away from the wall** and inspect the vent hose for crushing, kinks, disconnections, or sharp bends. Straighten or replace the hose as needed and secure both ends with metal clamps.
4. **Trace the entire exhaust duct run** from the dryer to the outside. Remove any sections you can reach and clear lint buildup. Count the elbows and measure the total run length. If the vent is excessively long or has many turns, consider rerouting or shortening it.
5. **Check the exterior vent hood** where the duct exits the house. Confirm the flap opens freely and is not blocked by lint, a bird nest, or other debris. Clean or replace the hood if necessary.
6. **Run a timed dry heated cycle** to verify that airflow is restored and the code does not return. Place your hand near the exterior vent to feel strong, warm exhaust.
7. **If the code persists after all venting is verified clear**, call a qualified technician to inspect the blower motor, blower housing, and internal sensing components for faults or lint accumulation inside the cabinet.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the lint screen, inspected and cleared the entire vent path, verified the exterior hood opens freely, and the F4E3 code still returns on a heated cycle. At that point the fault may be in the blower motor, blower wheel, internal wiring, or a temperature or airflow sensor. A technician can perform component-level tests and access the blower housing inside the cabinet to check for internal lint buildup or mechanical problems. Also call a pro if your vent run is inside a wall or ceiling and you cannot reach it to clean, or if you are uncomfortable working behind the dryer or on a ladder to access the exterior hood.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts if vent hose needs replacing, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 for vent cleaning or service call.
