## Speed Queen Washer Overflowing — What's Happening

When your Speed Queen washer overflows, the control has detected that the tub water level is too high or is rising when it should not. On many Speed Queen commercial platforms this appears as Er, OF, or a related overflow fault, and the control cannot reduce the water level within the expected time window. In some models the control attempts to lower the water level for up to 5 minutes before locking out.

The machine is either taking on water when it should be stopped, misreading the actual water level due to a pressure-sensing problem, or failing to drain water fast enough to prevent overflow. The fault does not mean a specific part has failed until you isolate which system is at fault.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Water inlet valve stuck open or leaking internally** The inlet valve solenoid fails to close completely, allowing water to continue trickling or flowing into the tub even when the control is not calling for a fill.
- **Pressure hose kinked, cracked, disconnected, or blocked** The air-pressure hose between the tub air trap and the pressure switch develops a leak, fills with water, or becomes obstructed so the control cannot accurately sense the tub level.
- **Drain pump weak, clogged, or failed** The drain pump cannot evacuate water fast enough or has stopped working, preventing the control from lowering the tub level within the timeout period.
- **Drain hose kinked, clogged, or routed too high** A restriction in the drain path or improper standpipe height prevents water from leaving the tub, causing the control to detect an overflow condition.
- **Coin trap or sump blockage** Debris lodged in the coin trap or sump housing restricts water flow to the pump and prevents normal drainage.
- **Inlet valve screens or filters clogged with sediment** Mineral buildup or debris in the inlet valve screens restricts normal fill control and can contribute to erratic fill behavior or slow shutoff.
- **Pressure switch or sensor electrical fault** The level sensor itself or its wiring has failed and sends incorrect signals to the control, causing false overflow detection even when water level is normal.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Immediately turn off both hot and cold water supply valves at the wall and unplug the washer if water is actively rising or spilling over.
2. Watch the tub for 5 minutes with power and water off to see if the water level continues to rise on its own, which indicates a leaking inlet valve.
3. Remove the inlet hoses and inspect the valve inlet screens for sediment, debris, or mineral buildup and clean or replace as needed.
4. Access the rear or lower panel and locate the pressure hose running from the tub air trap to the pressure switch or sensor, then check for cracks, kinks, disconnection, water inside the hose, or blockage at the air trap.
5. Run a manual drain test by selecting drain/spin or entering service diagnostics if available, and verify the pump evacuates water strongly and the drain hose is not kinked or restricted.
6. Check the house standpipe or laundry sink drain for slow flow or standing water that could back up into the washer and prevent proper drainage.
7. If the tub fills with power off, replace the water inlet valve assembly.
8. If the pressure hose is damaged or wet inside, replace the hose and clear the air trap, or replace the pressure switch if the hose is intact but the control still misreads level.

## When to Call a Pro

If you have verified that water supply valves are off and the tub still fills, or if you have cleaned screens, inspected hoses, and cleared the drain path but the overflow fault persists, call a qualified appliance technician. Diagnosing pressure-switch circuits and control-board signaling requires a multimeter and service-mode access. A pro can also identify whether the control board itself has failed or whether wiring between the sensor and control has developed an intermittent fault that causes false overflow readings.
