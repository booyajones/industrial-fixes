## State Water Heater E156 Error Code — What It Means

The E156 code on a State tankless water heater most likely indicates a combustion, air-fuel, or venting fault that has triggered a safety lockout. While the exact definition of E156 is not published in general support materials and must be verified against your specific model's manual (accessible via the QR code on the rating plate), this code family typically appears when the unit cannot ignite properly, detects abnormal combustion conditions, or experiences restricted airflow. The controller shuts down the heater to prevent unsafe operation.

Common triggers include blocked air intake screens, insufficient gas pressure, restricted venting, debris in the fan or combustion air path, dirty inlet water filters, or scale buildup inside the heat exchanger. Unlike simple sensor faults, E156-type codes usually point to a physical obstruction or supply problem rather than an electronic component failure. The heater will remain in lockout until you identify and correct the root cause, then perform a proper reset.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Dust, lint, or debris covering the intake grilles restricts combustion air and prevents reliable ignition or stable flame operation.
- **Insufficient gas supply or low gas pressure** A partially closed gas valve, undersized gas line, or regulator problem starves the burner and causes ignition failure or combustion abnormality.
- **Blocked or improperly installed vent system** Obstructions in the exhaust pipe, incorrect termination, or excessive vent length create back-pressure that disrupts combustion and triggers a safety shutdown.
- **Debris in the fan motor or combustion air path** Foreign material inside the blower housing or air passages reduces airflow and prevents the unit from achieving the correct air-fuel mixture.
- **Dirty inlet water filter or low flow rate** A clogged inlet screen reduces water flow below the minimum threshold, preventing the heater from firing or maintaining stable operation.
- **Scale buildup in the heat exchanger** Mineral deposits restrict water passages and reduce heat transfer efficiency, leading to overheating or repeated fault codes even after airflow corrections.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your model number** on the rating plate and scan the QR code (or visit State's support site) to download the exact manual and confirm the official E156 definition and diagnostic chart for your unit.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the exterior of the heater, removing any dust, lint, or blockage that could restrict combustion air supply.
3. **Check the vent system** for blockages, damage, improper termination, or installation errors, verifying that the exhaust pipe is clear and meets the manufacturer's length and clearance requirements.
4. **Verify gas supply and pressure** by confirming that the gas valve is fully open, the supply line is correctly sized, and no other appliances are starving the heater during operation.
5. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** to make sure unrestricted flow, flushing the screen under running water and reinstalling it securely.
6. **Inspect the fan and combustion air path** for debris or abnormal noise, clearing any foreign material and confirming that the blower operates smoothly when the unit attempts to fire.
7. **Reset the heater** only after correcting the underlying cause, then monitor the first few heating cycles to confirm stable ignition, combustion, and exhaust without fault recurrence.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate or clear an obvious blockage, if the gas supply or pressure is uncertain, if the vent system requires modification or repair, or if the fault returns immediately after reset. Combustion and venting problems involve carbon monoxide risk and local code compliance, so professional diagnosis and testing with a combustion analyzer is the safest path when the cause is not obvious. Also call for help if you suspect scale buildup and lack the pump and descaling chemicals to flush the heat exchanger correctly, or if the fan motor shows physical damage or abnormal operation that points to an internal component failure.
