## Weil-McLain Boiler A166 Error — What It Means

The A166 code on a Weil-McLain boiler indicates an ignition lockout. The boiler attempted to start and light the burner but failed to prove flame during its ignition sequence. This family of faults typically means the control board did not detect a stable flame after opening the gas valve and firing the ignitor. The lockout is a safety feature to prevent unburned gas from accumulating in the combustion chamber.

Because the exact A166 definition does not appear in the manufacturer documentation available, treat this as a general ignition or flame-proving failure. Common underlying problems include no gas reaching the burner, a dirty or misaligned flame sensor that cannot prove the flame is present, a failed ignitor that does not create spark or heat, or grounding and wiring issues in the flame-proving circuit. Always consult your specific model's installation and service manual for the precise fault table and diagnostic steps.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor or closed gas shutoff valve. Clean the flame rod with fine abrasive cloth and verify the gas valve is open before ordering electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or weak flame sensor (~35%)** Soot, corrosion, or mineral deposits on the flame-sensing rod prevent the control from detecting flame current, causing lockout even when the burner lights.
- **Closed or insufficient gas supply (~25%)** A closed manual shutoff valve, low meter pressure, or empty propane tank stops fuel from reaching the burner during ignition.
- **Failed ignitor (~20%)** A cracked hot-surface ignitor or fouled spark electrode cannot create the heat or spark needed to light the gas.
- **Poor ground or flame-circuit wiring (~10%)** Loose chassis ground connections or corroded flame-sensor wiring prevent the control from reading flame current properly.
- **Gas valve failure (~5%)** The gas valve may not open during the ignition sequence even when commanded, blocking fuel delivery despite correct upstream pressure.
- **Blocked venting or combustion air intake (~5%)** Restricted flue pipes or air intakes can prevent proper combustion and cause pressure-switch or flame-proving faults, depending on the model.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the boiler at the service switch and turn the gas valve to the off position.
2. **Record the exact model and serial number** from the boiler nameplate and download the matching Weil-McLain service manual from the manufacturer website or contact a distributor.
3. **Verify gas supply** by checking that all manual shutoff valves between the meter or tank and the boiler are fully open and that other gas appliances in the building are working.
4. **Remove the burner access panel** and locate the flame sensor rod, a thin metal probe near the burner. Gently remove it and clean the sensing tip with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth until shiny.
5. **Inspect the ignitor** for cracks, breaks, or heavy carbon buildup. Replace the ignitor if the ceramic or metal element is damaged.
6. **Check electrical connections** at the flame sensor, ignitor, and gas valve for corrosion, looseness, or broken wires. Verify the boiler chassis has a solid ground connection to building ground.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then initiate a call for heat and observe the ignition sequence. If the burner lights but the code returns, measure flame-current microamps according to the service manual or call a qualified technician for advanced diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame sensor and verifying gas supply do not clear the A166 code, or if you lack the tools to measure gas pressure and flame current. Any repair that involves testing or replacing the gas valve, pressure regulator, or control board should be performed by a trained professional. Gas leaks, improper combustion, and incorrect venting can create carbon monoxide hazards. If you smell gas or suspect a leak, leave the building immediately, do not operate electrical switches, and call your gas utility or fire department from a safe location outside.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
