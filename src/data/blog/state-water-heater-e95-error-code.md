## State Water Heater E95 Error Code — What It Means

The E95 code on a State tankless water heater is not formally defined in most manufacturer documentation, but it is widely interpreted as a combustion-related fault or ignition lockout on State/A. O. Smith tankless platforms. It typically appears when the control board cannot establish or maintain proper ignition, which can be caused by restricted airflow, insufficient gas supply, low water flow, or dirty flame-sensing components.

Because State tankless units share technology with A. O. Smith models, the E95 fault behaves much like other ignition-failure codes: the burner will not light, or it lights briefly and then shuts down. The unit may lock out and require a power reset before attempting another ignition cycle. Most cases are resolved by clearing obstructions in the air intake, cleaning the inlet water filter, or verifying gas supply pressure.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, or debris blocking the combustion air inlet is one of the most common causes of ignition failure on State tankless heaters.
- **Dirty cold-water inlet filter** A clogged inlet screen reduces flow below the minimum threshold needed to trigger ignition, causing the unit to fault out.
- **Low gas supply pressure or closed valve** Insufficient gas flow from a partially closed shutoff, low line pressure, or undersized piping prevents the burner from lighting reliably.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing electrode prevents the board from detecting ignition, triggering a lockout.
- **Blocked exhaust vent** An obstructed or improperly installed vent restricts exhaust flow and can cause combustion pressure issues that prevent ignition.
- **Failed igniter or control board** A weak igniter or faulty control board may fail to produce spark or properly sequence the ignition cycle, resulting in a persistent E95 fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the unit** by switching off the circuit breaker or unplugging the heater for 30 seconds, then restore power to clear any temporary lockout and see if the fault returns.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the front or side of the unit, removing any lint, dust, pet hair, or outdoor debris that could block combustion airflow.
3. **Remove and rinse the cold-water inlet filter** by closing the cold supply valve, releasing pressure, unscrewing the filter housing, and flushing the screen under running water.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve at the heater is fully open, checking that other gas appliances operate normally, and listening for gas flow when the unit calls for heat.
5. **Check the exhaust vent termination** outside for blockages, bird nests, or ice, and confirm the vent piping inside is properly sealed and free of obstructions.
6. **Clean the flame rod** by accessing the burner chamber according to your model's service manual, gently wiping the sensor tip with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth, and reassembling.
7. **Test operation** by opening a hot tap for at least one minute and observing whether the burner ignites smoothly and the E95 code clears, or if the fault returns immediately or after a short run.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E95 code returns after you have cleaned the air intake, inlet filter, and flame rod and confirmed gas supply and venting are clear. Diagnosing a faulty gas valve, testing ignition voltage, or replacing the control board requires specialized tools and safe handling of gas connections. Professional descaling is also recommended if your unit has several years of hard-water service and the fault persists, since scale buildup in the heat exchanger can mimic flow and combustion problems. Any work involving gas piping, electrical board replacement, or combustion-chamber disassembly should be performed by a qualified technician to maintain warranty coverage and safety.
