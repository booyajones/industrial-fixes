## LG Mini Split CH06 Error Code — What It Means

CH06 indicates the indoor unit outlet pipe temperature sensor is reporting an open circuit or short circuit condition. This thermistor monitors the temperature of the refrigerant pipe leaving the indoor coil. When the sensor signal is out of range or missing, the control board cannot regulate operation properly and throws the CH06 fault. This is a sensor input problem, not a refrigerant or pressure issue.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or unplugged sensor connector** The thermistor plug at the indoor PCB has backed out, is corroded, or has damaged pins preventing proper contact.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** The sensor lead is cut, pinched, or has broken insulation that is causing a short or open circuit.
- **Failed thermistor element** The sensor itself has drifted out of range, opened internally, or shorted and no longer reports accurate resistance.
- **Sensor physically out of position** The thermistor has slipped off the pipe or is not secured in the correct location, producing erroneous readings.
- **Indoor PCB input failure** The sensor and wiring test normal but the control board input circuit is faulty and cannot read the signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Disconnect power to the mini split at the breaker or disconnect and wait two minutes for capacitors to discharge.
2. Remove the indoor unit cover to access the PCB and locate the outlet pipe temperature sensor connector on the board.
3. Inspect the sensor plug for loose fit, backed-out terminals, corrosion, or broken pins and reseat the connection firmly.
4. Trace the sensor wiring along the pipe and check for cuts, compression damage, or exposed conductors.
5. Verify the sensor is clipped or taped securely to the correct location on the indoor unit outlet pipe.
6. Disconnect the sensor from the PCB and measure its resistance with a multimeter, then compare the reading to the LG thermistor resistance-temperature chart for your model (typical values are around 10 kΩ at 10°C or 4 kΩ at 30°C, but consult your model's table for the exact curve).
7. Replace the sensor if the resistance is open, shorted, or far out of specification, or replace the indoor PCB if the sensor and wiring test correctly but the fault persists after reconnecting power.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with line voltage, if you cannot locate the sensor or access the indoor PCB safely, or if the sensor tests correctly but the fault does not clear after reseating connections. Technicians have the model-specific resistance charts, refrigerant handling tools if further diagnosis is needed, and warranty-approved replacement parts. If your unit is under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.
