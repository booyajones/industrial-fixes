## Maytag Dryer F4E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F4E1 code on a Maytag dryer indicates a problem with the heater or heater relays, according to Maytag's official product support documentation. The control board has detected an issue in the heating circuit that prevents the dryer from producing heat properly. This code applies to both electric and gas models, though the specific components involved differ. On electric dryers, the fault typically involves the heating element or its relay. On gas models, the ignition system or gas valve coils may be involved. The code does not mean the dryer is completely inoperable, but it will not heat until the fault is repaired.

Maytag recommends starting with a power reset before assuming a part has failed. In many cases the code is triggered by a temporary electrical glitch or voltage sag rather than a permanent component failure. If the code returns after a reset, the problem lies in the heater circuit wiring, the heater relay on the control board, or the heating element itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first. Before ordering a board, use a multimeter to test the heating element for continuity and inspect the heater relay terminals for visible burns or looseness.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heater relay (~40%)** The relay that switches power to the heating element becomes stuck, burned, or intermittent, preventing the heater from energizing.
- **Open or damaged heating element (~30%)** The heating element has an internal break or a grounded coil that stops current flow and triggers the fault.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~15%)** Terminals, connectors, or harness wires in the heater circuit develop heat damage, corrosion, or looseness that interrupts the circuit.
- **Low or unstable supply voltage (~10%)** The dryer does not receive proper line voltage under load, causing the heater circuit to drop out and set the code.
- **Control board heater-driver failure (~5%)** The section of the main control board that drives the heater relay has failed, even though the relay itself is intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker panel. Wait five full minutes to allow the control board to reset, then restore power and start a timed dry cycle to see if the code returns.
2. **Access the heater compartment** by unplugging the dryer, removing the lower front panel or rear access panel (depending on your model), and locating the heating element canister and the main control board.
3. **Inspect all heater circuit wiring** for loose connectors, burned terminals, or damaged insulation. Pay close attention to the plug that connects the heating element to the harness and the relay terminals on the control board.
4. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter set to continuity or resistance. Disconnect the element leads and check for an open circuit (infinite resistance) or a short to ground. Replace the element if it is faulty.
5. **Check the heater relay** on the main control board. Look for visible damage, burned contacts, or a relay that does not click when the board calls for heat. If the relay is part of the board, you may need to replace the entire board.
6. **Verify supply voltage** at the dryer terminal block with the unit running. Confirm that both legs of the 240-volt supply (for electric models) are present and stable under load. Correct any household wiring issues before replacing parts.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a full heated dry cycle. Monitor the display to confirm the F4E1 code does not reappear and that the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt electrical circuits, if the dryer uses gas and you need to test ignition or valve components, or if you have tested the heating element and relay and both passed but the code still returns. A technician can use a service manual and advanced metering to trace intermittent faults in the control board or harness that are difficult to find without schematics. Also call for help if your home's electrical supply voltage is unstable or incorrect, as that requires work at the breaker panel or service entrance by a licensed electrician.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
