## Mitsubishi P5 Error Code — What It Means

The P5 error code on Mitsubishi Electric ductless indoor units signals a condensate drain pump problem. The system has detected that water is not being removed from the drain pan as expected. Either the pump failed to move water when called, the drain line is blocked, or the water level in the pan rose high enough to trip the internal safety. This is a water-protection fault, not a refrigerant or outdoor-unit issue. The unit will shut down to prevent overflow and water damage until the drainage system is restored.

## Common Causes

- **Failed condensate pump or weak motor** The pump assembly has burned out or lost power and cannot discharge condensate water when the float calls for it.
- **Clogged drain line or pump reservoir** Sludge, algae, or debris has accumulated in the drain tubing, trap, or pump chamber and blocks normal water flow.
- **Stuck or faulty float switch** The water-level switch in the drain pan or pump assembly is mechanically jammed, fouled by debris, or electrically open.
- **Improper drain line installation** The discharge tube is kinked, pitched incorrectly, air-locked, or disconnected so water cannot leave the unit.
- **Loose or damaged pump wiring** Connectors to the pump or float switch are corroded, unplugged, or severed so the control board cannot operate the circuit.
- **Control board output failure** The indoor PCB is not sending power to the pump even though the pump and wiring test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power to the indoor unit at the breaker or disconnect switch and verify the unit is de-energized before opening any panels.
2. Remove the front cover and access panel to expose the drain pan, pump assembly, and drain line at the bottom of the indoor unit.
3. Inspect the drain pan and pump reservoir for standing water, sludge buildup, algae, or debris and clear any blockage by hand or with a shop vacuum.
4. Pour clean water slowly into the drain pan or pump chamber and watch whether the pump starts and discharges water through the drain line.
5. Check the float switch for free movement and verify it is not stuck or fouled, then confirm the drain discharge tube is connected, unkinked, and not clogged.
6. Trace the wiring from the pump and float switch back to the indoor control board and inspect all connectors for looseness, corrosion, or damage.
7. If the pump does not run when water is added and wiring is intact, test for voltage output from the control board during a drain demand or replace the pump assembly.
8. Restore power, reset the unit, and run a cooling cycle to confirm condensate drains normally and the P5 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside the indoor unit or if the pump runs but the P5 code persists. A professional can test control-board outputs, verify float-switch logic, and diagnose installation issues such as air traps or improper drain pitch that are not obvious from a visual inspection. If the indoor PCB requires replacement or refrigerant-side airflow restrictions are causing excessive condensate, a licensed contractor has the tools and training to repair the system safely and restore normal operation.
