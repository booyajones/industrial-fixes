## GE Microwave F1 Error Code — What It Means

On GE microwave models, the F1 error code points to a thermal sensor (thermistor) problem. The most consistent service-level interpretation is that the oven cavity thermistor is open or reading out of range. This sensor monitors the cavity temperature during operation to prevent overheating and make sure safe cooking cycles. When the control board cannot read the thermistor correctly (because of an open circuit, short, or value outside the expected range), it halts operation and displays F1.

In some GE product families, F1 may also indicate a defective control board or control/keypad module, so the exact meaning can vary by model. Always verify your specific model number and consult the service manual or wiring diagram to confirm which component the code targets on your unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first, but a simple multimeter resistance check of the cavity thermistor and its wiring harness will identify the real fault in most cases.

## Common Causes

- **Failed cavity thermistor (~60%)** The thermal sensor in the oven cavity goes open or drifts out of specification, preventing the control from reading temperature.
- **Overheating condition (~15%)** Extended use or blocked vents cause the sensor to read abnormal until the unit fully cools down.
- **Wiring or connector problems (~10%)** Loose, corroded, or heat-damaged connectors and harness wires between the thermistor and control board create intermittent or permanent opens.
- **Defective control board (~10%)** The electronic control board itself fails and cannot interpret the thermistor signal correctly, even when the sensor is good.
- **Defective control/keypad module (~5%)** On some GE models the control or keypad module (distinct from the main board) can trigger F1 when it malfunctions.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill all power** by unplugging the microwave from the wall outlet and wait at least 10 minutes for the unit to cool and for internal high-voltage capacitors to discharge.
2. **Verify your model number** on the rating plate inside the door or on the back panel, then download the service manual or wiring diagram from the GE Appliances support site to confirm F1 definitions and thermistor specifications for your exact model.
3. **Remove the outer cabinet panels** (usually a few screws at the back and sides) to expose the oven cavity and locate the cavity thermistor, a small bead sensor with two wires, often clipped or taped near the magnetron or waveguide.
4. **Inspect the thermistor and its harness** for obvious damage such as burn marks, melted insulation, loose or corroded connectors, or physical breaks in the wires.
5. **Test the thermistor with a multimeter** set to resistance (ohms). Disconnect the sensor's plug, measure across the two terminals, and compare the reading to the service manual's table. An open (infinite resistance) or very low resistance indicates a failed thermistor.
6. **If the thermistor tests good**, inspect the electronic control board and control/keypad module for burned components, swollen capacitors, or heat damage. Replace the faulty board if you find visible defects or if all other tests pass.
7. **Reassemble the microwave**, restore power, and run a brief test cycle to confirm the F1 error is cleared and the unit heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working inside a microwave oven. Even when unplugged, the high-voltage capacitor can retain a lethal charge. A technician has the insulated tools, discharge procedures, and multimeter skills to safely test the thermistor, check the wiring harness for intermittent opens, and replace the control board if needed. Professional diagnosis is also the best path when the F1 code returns intermittently or when you lack the service manual data to interpret resistance readings for your specific model.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
