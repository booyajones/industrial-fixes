## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E79 Error — What It Means

The E79 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a communication fault or internal control board issue. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit cover for precise details. In general, this code suggests the main control board is not receiving proper signals from sensors or components, or there is a problem within the board itself.

Because tankless water heaters rely on multiple sensors and a central control board to manage gas flow, ignition, and temperature, a communication error can prevent the unit from firing or maintaining hot water. The fault may be intermittent or persistent depending on whether loose wiring, a failed sensor, or a faulty control board is the root cause.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board immediately, but many E79 codes are caused by loose connectors or a single failed sensor. Inspect all wiring harnesses and test sensor continuity before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~40%)** Vibration or moisture can cause connector pins at the control board or sensors to lose contact, interrupting communication and triggering the error.
- **Failed temperature or flow sensor (~30%)** A malfunctioning sensor sends incorrect or no data to the control board, which interprets this as a communication fault.
- **Control board failure (~20%)** The main PCB may develop solder cracks, component failures, or memory corruption that prevent proper operation.
- **Power supply issue (~10%)** Low or unstable voltage from the home electrical supply can cause erratic board behavior and communication errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners and lifting the panel away to expose the control board and wiring.
3. **Inspect all wire harness connectors** at the control board, temperature sensors, flow sensor, and igniter for looseness, corrosion, or damaged pins. Reseat any suspect connectors.
4. **Check the display** or LED indicators on the control board for additional error codes or fault lights that may point to a specific sensor.
5. **Test sensor resistance** using a multimeter if you have the service manual specifications. Disconnect each sensor connector and measure resistance across the sensor terminals, comparing readings to the table in your manual.
6. **Replace any out-of-spec sensor** by disconnecting the old unit, removing mounting hardware, and installing the new sensor with thermal paste if required.
7. **If wiring and sensors test good**, the control board is the likely fault. Replace the board by disconnecting all harnesses, removing mounting screws, transferring any jumpers or settings, and securing the new board in place.
8. **Restore power and gas**, then run a hot-water tap to verify the unit fires normally and the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after reseating connectors and resetting power, or if you lack a multimeter and the service manual needed to test sensors. Tankless water heaters integrate gas valves, ignition systems, and high-temperature components that require proper tools and training to service safely. A technician can read live diagnostic data from the control board, test voltage and sensor signals under load, and safely handle gas-line connections during part replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
