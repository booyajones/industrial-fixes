## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E50 Error — What It Means

The E50 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater indicates a flame detection or ignition issue. The unit is unable to sense a proper flame after attempting to ignite, or the flame is not stable enough to remain lit. This safety feature shuts down the burner to prevent gas buildup.

The code can mean the control board is not receiving a signal from the flame sensor, the igniter is not creating a spark, or combustion conditions are preventing a stable flame. Because the exact definition can vary slightly across Ruud tankless models, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cover for model-specific troubleshooting steps.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame sensor without checking gas supply or combustion air blockages. Verify gas pressure and clean the burner assembly first, which costs nothing and solves many E50 codes.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor rod (~35%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the sensor prevents the signal from reaching the control board, even when a flame is present.
- **Low or no gas pressure (~25%)** Insufficient gas flow from a partially closed valve, regulator problem, or empty propane tank stops the burner from igniting or maintaining a flame.
- **Blocked or restricted combustion air intake (~15%)** Debris, nests, or lint in the intake vent starve the burner of oxygen and prevent stable combustion.
- **Failed igniter assembly (~12%)** A weak or broken igniter produces no spark or an intermittent spark that cannot reliably light the gas.
- **Loose or corroded wiring to flame sensor or igniter (~8%)** Poor electrical connections interrupt the signal path between the sensor and control board or prevent the igniter from firing.
- **Faulty control board (~5%)** The board may fail to send ignition commands or misread the flame sensor signal, though this is less common than sensor or supply issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater before beginning any work.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and locate the burner assembly and flame sensor rod, which is a thin metal probe near the burner.
3. **Inspect the flame sensor** for soot, corrosion, or white oxidation and gently clean the rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth until the metal is shiny.
4. **Check all electrical connections** to the flame sensor, igniter, and control board for tightness and corrosion, and reseat any loose connectors.
5. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the shutoff valve is fully open, checking inlet screen for debris, and testing gas pressure at the inlet with a manometer if you have one.
6. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside the building for nests, leaves, ice, or other blockages and clear any obstructions.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore power and gas, and initiate a call for hot water to test whether the burner ignites and the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot safely access the burner compartment, or if the error persists after cleaning the sensor and verifying gas supply. Professionals have manometers to measure gas pressure accurately, combustion analyzers to check air-fuel mix, and the training to diagnose control board faults. Any work involving gas lines, pressure regulators, or sealed combustion chambers should be left to a qualified technician to prevent safety hazards and maintain warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
