## Maytag Dishwasher E4 Error Code — What It Means

The F8E4 or E4 code on a Maytag dishwasher indicates that water has been detected in the drip tray or base pan underneath the unit, triggering the float switch in the leak protection system. This is not a drain pump fault. The dishwasher will not operate until you find and fix the leak, remove any standing water from the base, and clear the code.

Maytag's own troubleshooting guides direct technicians to check the water supply connections, the float switch assembly, and the drip pan first. The code protects your floor by shutting down the machine when water collects where it should not be.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the drain pump or control board first, thinking E4 is a pump or electronic fault. The real problem is almost always a leak that collects in the base pan. Before replacing any part, remove the drip tray and look for water.

## Common Causes

- **Loose water supply connection (~35%)** The 90° elbow or inlet valve fitting is not hand-tightened correctly, allowing water to seep into the base pan during fill cycles.
- **Rubber washer not seated (~25%)** The rubber gasket in the fill hose connection is missing, damaged, or twisted out of position, causing a slow drip.
- **Water in the drip tray (~20%)** An earlier leak, overfill event, or splash left standing water in the base pan that has now activated the float switch.
- **Float switch disconnected or damaged (~10%)** The float switch connector is not fully latched or the switch itself is broken, sending a false leak signal to the control board.
- **Fill hose routed incorrectly (~10%)** The supply hose is kinked, pinched, or not routed out the rear left side as specified, stressing the connection and causing a leak.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off the water supply valve** under the sink or at the wall connection before you begin any inspection or repair work.
2. **Silence the alarm** by pressing Cancel once on the control panel, keeping the power on and the door closed during the clearing process.
3. **Pull the dishwasher forward** and tilt it back slightly to access the base area, using towels to protect the floor and catch any residual water.
4. **Remove the drip tray** by pressing the side snaps inward and pulling the tray toward you, then empty and dry it completely if water is present.
5. **Inspect the water supply connection** at the 90° elbow and inlet valve, checking that the rubber washer is seated correctly and the fitting is hand-tightened to the 3/4 in. threads.
6. **Check the float switch and wiring** to confirm the connector is fully latched and that the power supply wire is not touching the motor or lower tub area.
7. **Correct any leaks** you find, replace damaged washers or hoses, re-route the fill hose out the rear left side if needed, and reassemble the drip tray.
8. **Clear the code** by pressing Cancel twice, then run a short test cycle and recheck the base pan for any new water accumulation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the source of the leak after inspecting all accessible supply connections and the drip tray, if the leak appears to come from internal pump seals or the tub assembly, or if the float switch and wiring test good but the code returns immediately. A technician has the service manual, pressure gauges, and experience to diagnose internal leaks at the spray-arm manifold, door gasket channels, or circulation pump seals that are not visible from the base. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working under the dishwasher or if your unit is still under warranty, since opening the base area may affect coverage on some models.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (gasket, hose, or float switch), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 service call.
