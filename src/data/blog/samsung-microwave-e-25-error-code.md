## What this code means
Samsung microwave error code E-25 stands for Micro Cook Abnormal Temperature Error. This code appears when the control board detects an unusual temperature reading during cooking operation. The fault may come from the temperature sensor circuit itself, from blocked ventilation that lets the cavity overheat, or from a control board that misreads sensor input.

Samsung's published guidance for E-25 is to perform a power reset and contact service if the code returns. The company does not publish a detailed list of root causes in consumer documentation, so the specific component responsible must be determined by testing the sensor circuit, airflow path, and control board in that order.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Test the temperature sensor for open or short circuits and check all connectors before ordering expensive boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed temperature sensor or thermistor** The sensor may be open, shorted, or reading outside normal range, triggering the abnormal-temperature fault.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector** Wiring harness connections can develop high resistance or intermittent contact, causing erratic temperature readings.
- **Blocked ventilation or airflow restriction** Obstructed vents or a failing cooling fan let the cavity or monitored zone overheat during cooking cycles.
- **Main control board detection error** The MICOM board may incorrectly interpret sensor input even when the sensor circuit is healthy.
- **Heat-damaged wiring harness** Prolonged high temperatures can degrade insulation and create intermittent opens or shorts in the sensor circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** or turn off the circuit breaker and wait at least 60 seconds to reset the control board.
2. **Restore power** and test the microwave on a short cook cycle. If E-25 does not return, the fault may have been a transient overheating event.
3. **Check all ventilation** openings on the unit for dust, grease, or obstructions that restrict airflow. Clean any blocked grilles and confirm the cooling fan runs during operation.
4. **Disconnect power again** and access the temperature sensor, typically mounted on the cavity wall or magnetron assembly. Inspect the sensor body and connector for burn marks, corrosion, or loose pins.
5. **Test the temperature sensor** with a multimeter. Consult your model's service manual for the expected resistance at room temperature. Adjacent Samsung codes E-21 and E-22 indicate open or short sensor faults, so out-of-range readings confirm sensor replacement.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the sensor to the main control board for cuts, pinches, or heat damage. Wiggle connectors gently while monitoring for intermittent opens.
7. **Replace the failed component** identified by testing. If the sensor and wiring test normal but E-25 persists, the main control board is the remaining suspect and should be replaced by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the error returns after a power reset and you are not comfortable working inside the microwave cabinet. Microwave service requires knowledge of high-voltage capacitors that remain charged even when unplugged. A technician has the meters and service documentation to test the temperature sensor circuit accurately, verify control board input signals, and safely replace components. If you have already replaced the sensor and checked all connectors but E-25 persists, professional diagnosis of the control board is the next step.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–300.
