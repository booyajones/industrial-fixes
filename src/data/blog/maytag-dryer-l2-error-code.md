## Maytag Dryer L2 Error Code — What It Means

L2 on a Maytag dryer indicates low or no line voltage in the heating supply path. The dryer's control is detecting that the heater is not receiving full supply voltage. The dryer will continue to tumble and run, but it will not heat your clothes.

In practical terms, the machine is missing one leg of the 240 V electrical supply or seeing voltage low enough that the heating element cannot turn on. This is almost always a problem with the home power supply or the connections between the outlet and the dryer terminal block, not a failed internal component.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the heating element or control board when the real fault is a tripped breaker or loose power cord. Before ordering any parts, use a multimeter to measure 240 V at the wall outlet and confirm both breaker poles are reset.

## Common Causes

- **Tripped half of the 240 V breaker (~40%)** One pole of the double-pole breaker trips while the other stays on, leaving the motor running on 120 V but cutting power to the heater circuit.
- **Loose, burned, or corroded terminal block connection (~25%)** The power cord attaches to the dryer at a terminal block, and loose screws or heat damage at this junction can interrupt one leg of the 240 V supply.
- **Damaged power cord (~15%)** A cracked, pinched, or overheated power cord can lose continuity on one conductor, especially at the plug blades or terminal connections.
- **Faulty wall receptacle or house wiring (~15%)** A burned or loose connection inside the dryer outlet or in the branch circuit can drop one leg of the 240 V feed.
- **Internal heating-circuit or relay fault (~5%)** Less commonly, a failed heater relay or control board issue can prevent voltage from reaching the heating element even when house supply is correct.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by switching the dryer circuit breaker fully off or unplugging the cord. Do not work on any wiring while the machine is energized.
2. **Reset the breaker completely** by moving both poles of the double-pole breaker all the way to the OFF position, then back to ON. One side can trip silently while the handle looks partially engaged.
3. **Test the wall outlet voltage** with a multimeter set to AC volts. Measure hot-to-hot for about 240 V and each hot-to-neutral for about 120 V. If readings are low or missing, stop and call an electrician to repair the house supply.
4. **Inspect the power cord and plug** for cracks, bent blades, discoloration, or signs of overheating. Check that the cord is firmly seated in the wall outlet.
5. **Open the rear panel** of the dryer and examine the terminal block where the power cord attaches. Look for loose screws, burned or discolored wire ends, melted plastic, or corrosion.
6. **Tighten or replace damaged connections** at the terminal block. If the terminal block itself is cracked or burned, replace it. If the power cord shows heat damage, replace the entire cord.
7. **Reassemble and test** the dryer on a short cycle. If the L2 code clears and the dryer heats, the repair is complete. If voltage is correct and connections are sound but the code persists, the fault may be in the heating element, thermal fuse, or control relay and requires further circuit testing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician if you measure low or missing voltage at the wall outlet, if you are uncomfortable working with 240 V circuits, or if you see signs of burned or damaged house wiring inside the outlet box. Call an appliance technician if you have confirmed correct supply voltage and all power connections are tight and undamaged, yet the L2 code persists. At that point the fault is likely inside the dryer's heating circuit or control board, and further diagnostic testing of relays, thermostats, and the heating element is needed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if a power cord or outlet needs replacement, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for a service call to diagnose and repair power supply or connection issues.
