## State Water Heater E05 Error Code — What It Means

The E05 error code on State water heaters typically indicates a problem with the temperature sensor circuit, control board communication, or wiring. Because State water heaters use different control systems across models, the exact meaning of E05 can vary. Some models use it for a high-limit sensor fault, while others may flag a communication error between the thermostat and control board. Always consult your specific model's owner's manual or the diagnostic label on the water heater itself to confirm the exact definition for your unit. The code appears on the digital display and usually prevents the water heater from firing until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the entire gas control valve or thermostat assembly when the real problem is a loose wire or corroded connector at the temperature sensor. Inspect all sensor wiring and connectors first before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~40%)** Vibration or moisture causes connectors at the temperature sensor or high-limit sensor to loosen or corrode, breaking the signal to the control board.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~30%)** The thermistor or thermocouple sensor itself develops an open circuit or short, sending incorrect resistance readings to the control.
- **Faulty control board (~15%)** The electronic control board develops a fault in the sensor input circuit or internal communication path.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Wires between the sensor and control board are pinched, chafed, or have broken insulation causing intermittent shorts or opens.
- **Grounding issue (~5%)** Poor grounding or a ground fault in the sensor circuit creates erratic signals that the control interprets as a sensor error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or switch the gas control to pilot mode, then wait at least 30 seconds.
2. **Locate the temperature sensors** on your model by consulting the wiring diagram inside the access panel or in the owner's manual.
3. **Inspect all sensor wire connectors** at both the sensor end and the control board end for looseness, corrosion, or damage, and firmly reseat each plug.
4. **Check the sensor wiring** along its entire length for pinches, chafing, or breaks in the insulation.
5. **Restore power** and observe whether the E05 code clears or returns immediately.
6. **Test the suspect sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode, comparing the reading to the resistance table in your manual (if available) or replace the sensor if readings are open or shorted.
7. **Replace the faulty sensor or control board** as indicated by your tests, ensuring all connectors are clean and snug, then cycle power and verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water heater technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas connections, if you cannot locate the sensors or wiring diagram for your model, or if the error persists after replacing the sensor and inspecting all wiring. A pro can perform precise resistance tests, check for grounding faults, and safely replace the gas control valve if the board itself has failed. Also call a pro if the water heater is still under warranty, as DIY repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
