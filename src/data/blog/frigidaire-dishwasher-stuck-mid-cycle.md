## Frigidaire Dishwasher Stuck Mid-Cycle — What's Happening

A Frigidaire dishwasher stuck mid-cycle means the control has stopped advancing through the wash program and won't move to the next step. This is not an error code by itself but a symptom that a safety input or component is preventing the cycle from continuing. The machine may be paused, completely dead, or sitting with standing water.

Frigidaire's own guidance for cycle interruption points first to door closure, Start/Cancel reset behavior, delay start or child lock settings, water supply, and power issues. If the unit stopped after you opened the door, you may simply need to close it and press Start/Cancel again to resume. If it's completely unresponsive or won't progress past a certain point, a hardware failure is blocking the cycle.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Door latch or door switch not proving closed** This is the most common cause of mid-cycle stoppage because the control won't advance if the door isn't securely latched and the switch doesn't signal closure.
- **Water inlet valve clogged or failed** A restricted or bad inlet valve prevents proper filling, and the dishwasher stalls waiting for water that never arrives.
- **Drainage blockage or standing water** Clogged filters, drain hose, or air gap leave water in the tub and trigger a safety stop that halts the cycle.
- **Thermal fuse blown or power interruption** If the unit is completely dead, a blown thermal fuse or electrical supply fault is the leading suspect.
- **Circulation pump or wash motor failure** A weak or failed pump can't move water through the spray arms, so the control stops advancing the cycle.
- **Electronic control board or timer failure** If the machine runs one step but never moves forward, a failed timer or control board is a known cause after other components are ruled out.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the complaint is not Delay Start, Child Lock, or an interrupted cycle that just needs the door closed and Start/Cancel pressed again.
2. Check power and water supply by confirming the breaker is on, the cord is plugged in, and the house water valve is fully open.
3. Inspect the door latch and switch assembly for positive closure, alignment, and continuity across the switch terminals when the door is closed.
4. Remove and clean the filter at the tub bottom, then check the drain hose and air gap for blockages that would leave standing water.
5. Test fill performance by starting a cycle and watching for adequate water entry; if slow or none, inspect the inlet valve screen and test the valve coil for resistance and power.
6. Listen for wash motor and circulation pump operation during the cycle; abnormal hum, grinding, or silence indicates motor or pump failure.
7. Consult the service data sheet inside the front panel for the diagnostic mode procedure and interpretation if controls are suspected.
8. If all mechanical and input checks pass, test wiring and connectors for damage, then replace the timer or electronic control board as a last resort.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you're not comfortable working with live voltage or testing components with a multimeter. If the diagnostic mode points to a control board fault or you've replaced the door latch and inlet valve without resolving the issue, a technician with the service sheet and parts access can isolate timer, wiring harness, or pump motor problems more quickly. Also call if you find water leaking around the door or tub during your inspection, since that points to gasket or structural issues that need disassembly and sealing.
