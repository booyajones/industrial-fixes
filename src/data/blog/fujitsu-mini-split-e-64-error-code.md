## Fujitsu E:64 Error Code — What It Means

Error code E:64 (or 64) on a Fujitsu mini-split indicates an outdoor unit active filter or power factor correction (PFC) circuit fault, according to Fujitsu's published troubleshooting tables. This is not a simple refrigerant or sensor issue. It points to a problem in the inverter power electronics inside your outdoor unit. The fault typically involves the PFC section of the main circuit board or a wiring problem in the outdoor unit power path.

Because error code meanings can vary slightly between Fujitsu model families, always confirm the exact definition in your unit's service manual. Some third-party sources mention fan motor faults for similar codes on other brands, but Fujitsu's own documentation maps code 64 to the outdoor PFC circuit error.

## Common Causes

- **Failed outdoor inverter PCB or PFC circuit components** The power factor correction section of the outdoor unit's main board can fail and trigger code 64, which is the primary cause indicated by the code definition.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wiring and connectors** Poor connections at the outdoor inverter PCB, power harness plugs, or service terminals can interrupt signal paths and cause electronics faults.
- **Abnormal supply voltage or grounding issues** Voltage drop, unstable incoming power, or poor grounding at the outdoor unit service connection can trigger PFC circuit protection and throw the error.
- **Mis-wiring or incorrect wire gauge at installation** Improper wire routing, undersized wiring, or reversed polarity during install can stress the PFC circuit and lead to board failure over time.
- **Overheated or physically damaged inverter board traces** Burnt traces, swollen capacitors, or heat-damaged components on the outdoor PCB indicate electronic failure and will prevent normal operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the exact model number and error definition** in your Fujitsu service manual before ordering parts, because code meanings can vary by platform.
2. **Cycle power at the breaker** and wait two minutes, then restart the system to confirm the fault reappears and is not a transient glitch.
3. **Check incoming supply voltage and grounding** at the outdoor unit disconnect using a multimeter to rule out voltage drop, phase imbalance, or poor ground.
4. **Inspect all outdoor unit wiring and connectors** for looseness, corrosion, pin damage, or open circuits, paying close attention to the inverter PCB harness plugs and molex connectors.
5. **Remove the outdoor unit cover and visually inspect the inverter PCB** for burnt components, discolored traces, swollen capacitors, or other signs of failure in the PFC circuit area.
6. **Replace the outdoor inverter PCB or main control board** if wiring and voltage checks pass and visible damage is present, following the manufacturer's replacement procedure.
7. **Restore power and run a full cycle** to confirm the E:64 error clears, the compressor starts, and the system operates normally without re-triggering the fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live 220V power, do not have a multimeter and wiring diagrams, or cannot confirm the exact meaning of code 64 for your specific Fujitsu model. Inverter PCB replacement requires EPA-certified refrigerant handling in most jurisdictions if you need to recover refrigerant to access the board, and mis-diagnosis can lead to expensive part swaps. A qualified tech will measure supply voltage, trace circuits with the service manual, and confirm the PFC fault before replacing the board, saving you time and money on incorrect repairs.
