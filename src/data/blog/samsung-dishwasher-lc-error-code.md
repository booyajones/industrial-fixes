## What this code means
The LC code (sometimes shown as LE) tells you that your Samsung dishwasher has detected moisture in the leak-detection area under the tub. This does not always mean you will see a puddle on the floor. The sensor can trip from condensation, a slow drip into the base pan, or water splashed into the wrong area during a cycle. Samsung instructs you to turn off the dishwasher at the circuit breaker as soon as the code appears to prevent further water damage or electrical hazards.

The code does not point to one single failed part. Instead it signals that water has gone somewhere it should not be. Common sources include supply-hose connections, the door gasket, poor leveling that allows spray to escape, or clogged nozzles that misdirect water. Your job is to trace the moisture back to its origin.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or leak sensor when the real problem is simply a worn door gasket or a loose threaded fitting on the supply hose. Always inspect hoses, gasket, and leveling before ordering electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Water supply hose leak** Loose, cracked, or worn fittings at either end of the inlet hose let water drip into the base pan.
- **Damaged door seal** A torn, stiff, or dirty rubber gasket around the door allows spray to escape during the wash cycle.
- **Dishwasher not level** If the unit tilts forward or the cabinet opening is wider than 22 inches at the screw points, water can run out the door.
- **Clogged or misdirecting spray nozzles** Blocked jets spray water against the door or up into areas without proper drainage, triggering the sensor.
- **Drain clog or backup** A plugged filter or kinked drain hose can cause standing water that sloshes into the base pan or seeps past seals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker before you touch anything inside or underneath the dishwasher.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** and tilt it gently to inspect the base pan underneath the tub for standing water, wet foam insulation, or damp spots.
3. **Inspect the water supply hose** from the shut-off valve to the dishwasher inlet, checking both threaded connections and the hose body for cracks, kinks, or moisture.
4. **Check the door seal** by running your hand around the entire rubber gasket, looking for tears, stiffness, food debris, or gaps when the door is closed.
5. **Verify leveling** by placing a spirit level on the door and adjusting the front feet so the unit sits level front-to-back and side-to-side, then measure the cabinet opening at the screw points to confirm it is exactly 22 inches.
6. **Remove and inspect the spray arms** and nozzles for clogs, then clear any blockages with a toothpick or small brush.
7. **Dry the base pan completely** with towels or a shop vacuum, let the unit air out for several hours, then restore power and run a short rinse cycle to confirm the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have dried the base pan, inspected every hose and seal, confirmed the unit is level, and the LC code returns immediately on the next cycle. The leak may be internal to the circulation pump, the sump area, or a cracked tub weld that requires disassembly to reach. Also call if you see corrosion on wiring connectors near the leak sensor or control board, since moisture damage to electronics often requires board replacement and proper grounding checks that are beyond typical DIY scope.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (hose or gasket), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250 service call and diagnosis, more if parts needed.
