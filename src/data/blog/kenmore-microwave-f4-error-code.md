## Kenmore Microwave F4 Error — What It Means

The F4 error on a Kenmore microwave signals a humidity sensor open or humidity sensor sensing error. The microwave's humidity sensor (also called the humidity thermistor) monitors moisture during auto-cook cycles. When the control board detects an open circuit, a short, or readings outside the expected range from this sensor, it throws F4 and stops operation to prevent damage.

Because Kenmore is a brand name and microwaves are built by different manufacturers (the first three digits of your model number tell you who actually made the unit), the exact implementation can vary slightly. On Whirlpool-built Kenmore models, a closely related code F4E4 specifically points to the humidity thermistor or its wiring. In all cases, the root problem is the control board cannot read a valid signal from the humidity sensor circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first, assuming any error code means a bad board. Before ordering a control board, disconnect the humidity sensor connector and measure its resistance to confirm the sensor itself has failed or the wiring is open.

## Common Causes

- **Failed humidity sensor (~55%)** The humidity thermistor develops an internal open or drift and the control board reads infinite resistance or values outside the acceptable window.
- **Open or damaged sensor wiring harness (~25%)** Wires to the humidity sensor are pinched, broken, corroded, or the connector has backed out, creating an open circuit the board interprets as a sensor fault.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~10%)** The plug between the humidity sensor and the control board has poor contact due to corrosion, moisture intrusion, or vibration loosening the pins.
- **Control board input circuit failure (~8%)** The main control board's sensor-input circuit has failed and cannot properly read a good sensor signal, even when the sensor and wiring test correct.
- **Intermittent short in the sensor circuit (~2%)** High heat or steam inside the cavity has caused insulation to crack, allowing the sensor wire to short intermittently against chassis ground.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and flip the circuit breaker off for one full minute to perform a hard reset, then restore power and check if F4 reappears.
2. **Remove the outer cabinet or control-panel cover** using a screwdriver to access the cavity interior and locate the humidity sensor, which is typically mounted on the top or side wall near the waveguide cover.
3. **Inspect the humidity sensor wiring harness** for pinches, burns, or breaks, and check the connector for corrosion, moisture, or loose pins.
4. **Disconnect the humidity sensor connector** from the control board or intermediate harness and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure the sensor's resistance at room temperature (consult your model's service manual for the expected value).
5. **Replace the humidity sensor** if you measure an open circuit (infinite ohms), a dead short (zero ohms), or a value far outside the model's specification, securing the new sensor in the original mounting location.
6. **Reassemble the cabinet**, restore power, and run a short auto-cook cycle to verify the F4 code does not return and the humidity sensing functions correctly.
7. **Replace the main control board** only if a known-good sensor and intact wiring still produce F4, indicating a failed sensor-input circuit on the board itself.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working around high-voltage microwave components, cannot locate the humidity sensor in your particular model, or if you have replaced both the sensor and wiring but F4 continues to appear. Microwave ovens contain a high-voltage capacitor that retains a lethal charge even after unplugging, so technicians use special discharge procedures and insulated tools. A pro can also cross-reference your Kenmore model prefix to identify the true manufacturer, access the exact resistance specification for your humidity thermistor, and perform control-board diagnostics with factory service software. If your microwave is older than eight years and a control board is needed, a technician can help you weigh board replacement cost against the price of a new unit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-45 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
