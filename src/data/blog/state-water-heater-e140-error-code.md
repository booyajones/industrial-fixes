## State Water Heater E140 Error Code — What It Means

The E140 error code on State tankless water heaters indicates a failed ignition attempt or combustion fault. While this specific code is not widely documented in official State manuals, it behaves consistently with ignition-related failures seen across the State and A. O. Smith tankless product families. The unit is detecting that the burner did not light properly during a call for hot water, triggering a safety lockout to prevent unburned gas from accumulating.

This fault typically means one or more conditions required for successful ignition are not being met: adequate gas pressure, sufficient combustion air, proper venting, enough water flow to justify ignition, or functioning igniter and flame-sensing components. The most common real-world causes in these units are blocked air intakes, restricted inlet water filters, and low or interrupted gas supply.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Lint, debris, or obstructions in the intake or vent termination prevent proper combustion airflow and are a leading cause of ignition failure in State tankless units.
- **Dirty inlet water filter** A clogged inlet screen restricts flow below the minimum threshold needed to trigger ignition, causing the unit to abort the heating cycle.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or closed shutoff valve** Low inlet gas pressure, undersized piping, or a partly closed manual gas valve can starve the burner and prevent the flame from establishing.
- **Failed igniter or dirty flame rod** A weak igniter, cracked ceramic insulator, or corrosion and soot buildup on the flame-sensing rod will prevent ignition or flame detection.
- **Improper venting or excessive vent length** Vent runs that exceed the model's maximum length or elbow count, or terminations too close to obstructions, create backdraft or inadequate exhaust flow.
- **Loose or damaged wiring to ignition components** Corroded connectors or broken wires in the igniter or flame-rod circuit interrupt the signal needed to light and confirm the flame.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off hot-water taps** and any recirculation pump, then power-cycle the unit by switching it off for 30 seconds and back on to clear the fault and attempt a fresh ignition.
2. **Inspect the air intake and exhaust terminations** outside for blockages like lint, leaves, ice, or debris, and remove any obstructions you find.
3. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** by shutting the cold-water isolation valve, unscrewing the inlet union, pulling the mesh screen, rinsing it under clean water, and reinstalling it.
4. **Verify the gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve is fully open and checking that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally to rule out a supply interruption.
5. **Check the burner area and igniter assembly** by removing the front cover and visually inspecting the igniter, flame rod, and burner for corrosion, soot, or physical damage.
6. **Measure inlet gas pressure under firing demand** if you have a manometer and compare the reading to the rating plate specification for your model.
7. **Replace faulty ignition components or call a licensed technician** if the igniter is cracked, the flame rod is heavily corroded, or the error persists after cleaning and verifying gas, air, and water supply.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the error returns after you have cleaned the air intake, inlet filter, and burner area and verified the gas supply is on. Measuring gas pressure under load, testing igniter and flame-rod continuity, diagnosing control-board faults, and replacing sealed combustion components require specialized tools and training. Any work involving gas-line connections or venting modifications must meet local code and should be performed by a qualified professional to avoid safety hazards.
