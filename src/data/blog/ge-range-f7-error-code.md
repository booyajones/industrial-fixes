## GE Range F7 Error Code — What It Means

The GE F7 error code on a range or oven indicates the appliance is detecting a keypad or key panel fault. In most cases it signals a stuck, shorted, or otherwise invalid key input on the membrane touchpad assembly. The control board thinks a button is continuously pressed or the keypad circuit is sending garbled data. On some GE models the fault can also point to the oven control board itself if the keypad is not the cause.

The error is often seen on older units and may appear after a self-clean cycle, which is consistent with heat damage or aging of the control panel. Because the keypad is the input interface between you and the control electronics, any contamination, oxidation, or pinched wire in that circuit will trigger F7.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the oven control board first when F7 appears. Disconnect the keypad ribbon cable from the board and power up. If the code disappears, the keypad is the problem. Only replace the control board if the code remains with the keypad unplugged.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or continuously-registered button (~35%)** A key on the membrane touchpad is mechanically stuck or the circuit interprets it as pressed all the time, triggering F7.
- **Contamination or oxidation on ribbon-cable contacts (~30%)** Dirt, oxidation, or moisture on the flat ribbon connector between the keypad and control board creates intermittent or false signals.
- **Damaged or failing membrane keypad (~20%)** Cracks, delamination, or internal short circuits in the touchpad assembly send invalid input to the control board.
- **Loose, pinched, or poorly seated ribbon cable (~10%)** The ribbon harness is not fully inserted, is folded, or is pinched during reassembly, causing erratic keypad signals.
- **Defective electronic control board (~5%)** If the keypad is disconnected and the F7 persists or reappears immediately, the oven control board is misreading the input circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker, then wait at least five minutes to allow the control board to reset.
2. **Restore power and observe** whether the F7 code returns immediately or stays cleared, noting whether the fault is intermittent or continuous.
3. **Remove the control-panel access** by taking out the screws along the front edge or top of the range (consult your model's manual for the exact fasteners), then carefully pull the panel forward to expose the ribbon cable that connects the keypad to the control board.
4. **Disconnect the ribbon cable** by releasing the locking tab or lifting the connector, then use a clean pencil eraser or electrical contact cleaner to gently clean both the ribbon contacts and the socket pins on the control board.
5. **Reseat the ribbon cable** firmly, ensuring the connector tab locks and the cable is not folded, pinched, or twisted, then reassemble the control panel and restore power to test.
6. **Isolate the keypad** by disconnecting the ribbon again and powering up the range. If F7 disappears, replace the membrane keypad assembly. If F7 remains, replace the oven control board.
7. **Install the new part** (keypad or control board), reconnect all harnesses, reassemble the panel, and run a test cycle to confirm the code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the control panel or if the range is still under warranty. Also call a pro if the isolation tests are ambiguous, the wiring harness shows signs of arcing or melting, or you have already replaced both the keypad and control board and F7 persists. A qualified appliance technician has the diagnostic tools to measure keypad resistance and control-board signals directly and can source the correct parts for your exact GE model. If your range uses gas burners, a technician can also verify that any control-board replacement has not affected the ignition or gas-valve circuits.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts (keypad or control board), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350 service call and parts.
