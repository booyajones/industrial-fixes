## Samsung Dryer HE1 Error Code — What It Means

The HE1 code on a Samsung dryer signals an overheating or heat-abnormality condition. Samsung groups HE codes under heat and overheat-related faults, though HE1 is not separately documented in all Samsung dryer support materials and is best understood as a variant of the general overheat warning. The dryer's internal sensors have detected temperatures above the safe threshold and paused the cycle to prevent damage or fire.

In the vast majority of cases, the root problem is restricted airflow. When hot, moist air cannot escape freely through the lint screen and exhaust vent, heat builds up inside the drum and blower housing faster than the dryer can compensate. Less often, a temperature-sensing safety component or a grounded heating element is at fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the heating element or control board first. Before ordering parts, clean the entire vent system thoroughly and test the thermal fuse and thermostats for continuity with a multimeter.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged lint screen or exhaust vent (~65%)** Lint buildup in the screen, vent duct, or exterior vent hood restricts airflow and traps heat inside the dryer cabinet.
- **Failed thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat (~20%)** A safety thermostat or thermal fuse near the heating element or blower has opened due to excessive heat and no longer allows the cycle to complete.
- **Grounded heating element (~10%)** The heating element coil has broken and touched the metal housing, creating a short that triggers the overheat sensor.
- **Faulty control board or wiring (~5%)** The main PCB or related wiring is sending incorrect signals to the temperature sensors or heating circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker before any inspection or service work.
2. **Remove and clean the lint screen** under warm running water with a soft brush to clear any residue or fabric-softener film that reduces airflow.
3. **Disconnect and clean the exhaust vent duct** from the dryer to the exterior hood, using a long vent brush or shop vacuum to remove all lint buildup and check for crushed or kinked sections.
4. **Plug in the dryer and run a test cycle** to see if the HE1 code clears now that airflow is restored.
5. **Access the rear or bottom panel** (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations) to expose the blower housing and heating-element assembly if the code persists.
6. **Test the thermal fuse and high-limit thermostat** with a multimeter set to continuity mode. Both should show near-zero resistance when cool. Replace any open device.
7. **Check the heating element for a ground fault** by measuring continuity between each element terminal and the metal outer casing. Any continuity indicates the element is grounded and must be replaced.
8. **Reassemble panels, restore power, and run a full dry cycle** to verify the code does not return. If it does, the control board or related wiring may need professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the HE1 code returns after you have thoroughly cleaned the lint screen and exhaust vent, tested and replaced the thermal fuse and thermostats, and confirmed the heating element is not grounded. Persistent or intermittent overheat faults often point to control-board logic problems, faulty sensor wiring, or a blower motor that is slowing down and cannot move enough air. A technician has the model-specific wiring diagrams and signal-tracing tools to isolate the fault without replacing parts by guess. Also call a pro immediately if you smell burning plastic or see scorch marks inside the cabinet, since those indicate a serious fire risk that requires expert inspection.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
