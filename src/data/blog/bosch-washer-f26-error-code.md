## What this code means
The F26 (or E26) error code on a Bosch washer indicates that the analogue pressure sensor is faulty or not correctly reporting water level to the control board. This sensor tells the machine whether the drum has been filled or emptied. Bosch states explicitly that this fault cannot be rectified by the user and the washer should not be operated until it has been professionally serviced.

Unlike simpler drain or door-lock codes, F26 points to a sensor circuit problem. The pressure sensor monitors water level through a small air hose connected to the tub. When the sensor signal is out of range or missing, the control board throws F26 and stops the cycle to prevent overfilling or incorrect water management.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, assuming any sensor code means a board failure. Always test or swap the pressure sensor and inspect its air hose for blockages or cracks before ordering a new PCB.

## Common Causes

- **Failed analogue pressure sensor (~60%)** The sensor itself has drifted out of spec or failed internally so the control board cannot read accurate water-level data.
- **Blocked or kinked pressure hose (~25%)** The small air tube running from the tub to the sensor is clogged with detergent residue, lint, or pinched behind the tub, preventing the sensor from seeing pressure changes.
- **Faulty control board (PCB) (~10%)** The circuit that reads the analogue sensor signal has failed on the main board, though this is much less common than sensor failure.
- **Corroded or loose connector at the sensor (~5%)** The wiring harness plug at the pressure sensor has oxidized contacts or has backed out, breaking the signal path.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the wall outlet or circuit breaker and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves.
2. **Remove the top or rear panel** of the washer (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations) to access the pressure sensor, which is usually mounted on the side or back of the outer tub.
3. **Locate the pressure sensor** and trace the small-diameter air hose from the tub sump or air trap to the sensor body.
4. **Inspect the air hose** for kinks, cracks, or blockages. Disconnect it at both ends and blow through it gently to confirm it is clear. If blocked, flush with water or replace the hose.
5. **Check the sensor wiring connector** for corrosion, moisture, or loose pins. Unplug and replug the harness firmly.
6. **Test or replace the pressure sensor**. If you have a service manual with resistance or voltage specs, measure across the sensor terminals. Otherwise, install a new OEM analogue pressure sensor for your model.
7. **Reassemble the washer**, restore power and water, and run a short test cycle to verify F26 does not return. If the code persists after sensor replacement, the control board may need service.

## When to Call a Pro

Bosch explicitly states that F26 cannot be rectified by the user and recommends contacting Bosch Customer Support or arranging professional service. The pressure sensor is not difficult to reach, but diagnosing whether the fault lies in the sensor, its wiring, or the control board requires a multimeter and familiarity with analogue sensor circuits. If you are uncomfortable working inside the washer cabinet, tracing hoses, or testing live circuits with the panel open, schedule a service call. A qualified technician will have the correct test procedures, OEM sensor part numbers, and board-level diagnostic tools to pinpoint the fault quickly and avoid replacing parts unnecessarily.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–300 for sensor replacement including labor.
