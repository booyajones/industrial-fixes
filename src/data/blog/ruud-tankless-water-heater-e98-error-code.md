## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E98 Error — What It Means

The E98 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater indicates a system communication or internal control fault. This code typically appears when the main circuit board detects an unexpected condition, often related to wiring connections, internal sensor communication, or a problem within the control board itself. Because Ruud uses proprietary diagnostic codes that vary by model year and series, the exact meaning of E98 can differ. Always consult your specific model's owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel to confirm what E98 means for your unit. In many cases, the fault is temporary and can be cleared by power cycling the heater.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but many E98 faults are caused by a loose wire harness or corroded terminal. Inspect and reseat all low-voltage connections before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~40%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen wire terminals on the control board or sensor harnesses, interrupting communication and triggering the E98 fault.
- **Failed main control board (~30%)** The circuit board itself may have failed due to age, power surge, or component wear, preventing normal operation and displaying the error.
- **Intermittent sensor fault (~15%)** A temperature or flow sensor may be sending erratic signals to the board, causing the system to flag a communication error.
- **Power supply issue (~10%)** Low or unstable incoming voltage can cause the control board to malfunction and log an E98 code.
- **Software glitch or transient fault (~5%)** Temporary electrical noise or a brief power interruption can trigger the error without any underlying hardware problem.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker dedicated to the tankless water heater and wait 30 seconds before restoring power to reset the control board.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the access panel fasteners (typically Phillips-head screws) and set the panel aside.
3. **Inspect all wire harnesses** connected to the main control board, flame rod, thermistors, and flow sensor; press each connector firmly to reseat and look for any corrosion or burn marks.
4. **Check the inlet water filter** for debris or sediment that could restrict flow and cause secondary faults; rinse or replace the filter if clogged.
5. **Restore power** and observe the display; if the E98 error returns immediately, note any other codes or sounds and consult the wiring diagram inside the front panel for model-specific fault details.
6. **Test incoming voltage** at the heater's supply terminals using a multimeter; confirm you have stable 120V or 240V (depending on your model) with less than 5 percent variation.
7. **Replace the main control board** if all wiring is secure, power is stable, and the error persists; order the correct board using your model and serial number to avoid compatibility issues.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if the error persists after resetting the unit and reseating all wiring connections. Diagnosing internal board faults, testing sensor circuits with a multimeter, and working near gas lines requires specialized knowledge and tools. A professional can pinpoint whether the issue is a sensor, the control board, or a wiring fault and will have access to factory service bulletins and OEM parts specific to your Ruud model. If your unit is still under warranty, professional service is often required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
