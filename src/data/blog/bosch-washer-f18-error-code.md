## What this code means
Bosch's F18 or E18 fault code indicates the washer is not draining water properly or is taking too long to drain. You may notice water still sitting in the drum or tub after a cycle. Bosch states that F18, E18, and d02 are identical faults on some models, all pointing to the same underlying drainage problem.

The machine has detected that water remains in the tub beyond the expected drain time. This can happen when the pump cannot move water out, when the drain path is blocked, or when debris prevents the impeller from spinning freely. The washer will usually stop mid-cycle and display the code rather than risk overfilling or damaging the pump.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump when the real culprit is simply a clogged filter or a sock wedged in the pump housing. Always remove and inspect the filter and check that the impeller spins freely by hand before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked pump filter or housing (~50%)** Lint, coins, buttons, hairpins, and other debris collect in the filter and pump chamber, preventing water from reaching the impeller or jamming the impeller itself.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose can become pinched against the wall, twisted during installation, or clogged internally with lint and soap residue, restricting flow.
- **Defective drain pump or seized impeller (~20%)** The pump motor may fail electrically, or the impeller can seize due to debris or bearing wear, preventing it from rotating even when power is applied.
- **Wiring or control board fault (~5%)** Loose connectors, corroded terminals, or a failed control module can prevent the pump from receiving power, though this is less common than a physical blockage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the wall outlet or circuit breaker and close the water supply valves.
2. **Place towels** on the floor and have a shallow pan ready. Open the small access door at the front-lower corner of the washer.
3. **Pull out the emergency drain hose** (if equipped) and direct it into the pan to drain residual water, or simply open the filter cap slowly and let water flow into the pan.
4. **Unscrew and remove the pump filter cap** completely. Inspect the filter, pump housing, and impeller area for lint, coins, buttons, and other debris. Clear everything and confirm the impeller spins freely by hand.
5. **Check the drain hose** at the back of the machine. Straighten any kinks, disconnect the hose from the standpipe or sink, and look inside for blockages. Flush with water if needed.
6. **Test the drain pump electrically** if the impeller spins freely but the code persists. Disconnect the washer, access the pump from inside, and use a multimeter to check for an open circuit or no reading across the pump terminals. Replace the pump if it shows no continuity or fails to run.
7. **Reinstall the filter cap** snugly, close the access door, reconnect hoses, and restore power. Run a drain or spin cycle to verify proper drainage and check for leaks around the filter housing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared the filter and hose, confirmed the impeller spins freely, yet the F18 code returns immediately. A technician can test the pump motor with a multimeter, trace wiring for breaks or corrosion, and check the control board for relay or triac failures. Also call if you are uncomfortable working around water and electrical components, if the pump is difficult to access in your model, or if you see signs of a leak around the pump housing that may require seal replacement and reassembly with new gaskets.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–80 in parts if pump replacement is needed, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 for service call and parts.
