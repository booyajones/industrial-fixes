## Bosch Washer F18 Error Code — What It Means

The F18 error code (also displayed as E18 or d02 on some Bosch washer models) means the machine is not draining water properly or is draining too slowly. You will typically see water still sitting in the drum at the end of a cycle. Bosch confirms that F18, E18, and d02 are identical drainage faults across its model range.

The code triggers when the control detects that the tub has not emptied within the expected time window. The root cause is almost always a physical blockage or a mechanical failure in the drain path, not an electronic glitch. The pump, filter, hose, and drain line are the areas to inspect first.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often buy a new drain pump when the real problem is a blocked filter or a jammed impeller. Always clean the pump filter housing and confirm the impeller spins freely before replacing the pump.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked drain pump filter or chamber (~50%)** Lint, coins, buttons, hair grips, and other debris collect in the pump housing and prevent the impeller from spinning or water from flowing.
- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~25%)** A hose crushed behind the machine, a fold in the corrugated line, or a partial blockage at the siphon connection will slow or stop drainage.
- **Jammed pump impeller (~15%)** A small object wedged between the impeller blades stops the pump from turning even when the motor runs.
- **Failed drain pump motor (~8%)** If the filter is clean, the hose is clear, and the impeller moves freely by hand but the pump does not run during a drain cycle, the motor has failed.
- **Wiring or control board fault (~2%)** A broken wire to the pump or a control board relay failure can prevent the pump from receiving power, but this is rare compared to mechanical blockages.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Switch off and unplug the washer** to isolate power before opening any service panels or touching internal components.
2. **Drain the tub manually** by opening the service flap (usually lower front right) and pulling out the emergency drain hose if your model has one, or by slowly unscrewing the pump cover and catching water in a shallow pan.
3. **Remove and clean the pump filter** by unscrewing the large round cover inside the service flap, pulling out the filter, and rinsing away all lint, coins, and debris under running water.
4. **Inspect the pump chamber and impeller** by shining a flashlight into the now-empty filter housing and turning the impeller blades by hand to confirm they rotate smoothly without resistance or grinding.
5. **Check the drain hose routing** by pulling the washer forward, tracing the corrugated drain hose from the pump outlet to the standpipe or sink connection, and looking for kinks, crush points, or blockages.
6. **Reassemble and run a spin or drain test cycle** with the filter seated and the cover tight, then monitor the drain hose outlet to confirm water pumps out freely and the error does not return.
7. **Test or replace the drain pump** if the filter and hose are clear, the impeller spins freely, but the machine still will not drain. Use a multimeter to check for voltage at the pump connector during a drain cycle, or swap in a new pump if voltage is present but the pump does not run.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the filter and hose, confirmed the impeller spins freely, and run a test cycle but the F18 error returns and water still will not drain. A pro can test the pump electrically, check the pressure switch and associated tubing, and inspect wiring and control board connections. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water near electrical components, if the pump is difficult to access on your model, or if you need the washer running quickly and do not want to order parts and troubleshoot yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (if pump or hose needed), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300 for service call and pump replacement.
