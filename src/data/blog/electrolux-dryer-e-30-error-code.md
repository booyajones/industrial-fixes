## Electrolux Dryer E30 Error Code — What It Means

The E30 error code on Electrolux dryers typically indicates a temperature-related fault. The control board is detecting an issue with heating or temperature monitoring, though the exact meaning can vary by model. This code often appears when the dryer cannot maintain proper temperature or when a temperature sensor is reporting readings outside the expected range.

The dryer may stop mid-cycle or refuse to start when this code displays. Because different Electrolux models use slightly different diagnostic systems, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for the precise definition on your unit. In most cases, the fault involves the thermistor, high-limit thermostat, or the heating element circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the heating element first, but the thermistor and thermostats are the usual culprits. Test resistance on the thermistor and continuity on the thermostats before ordering a new element.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty thermistor (~40%)** The thermistor monitors drum temperature and sends resistance values to the control board; when it drifts out of range or fails open, the board throws the E30 code.
- **Tripped or failed high-limit thermostat (~30%)** The high-limit thermostat is a safety cutoff that opens when the dryer overheats; once tripped or if it fails, the control sees an abnormal temperature signal.
- **Blocked exhaust vent (~15%)** A clogged vent restricts airflow and causes the dryer to overheat, which trips the high-limit or puts the thermistor reading outside normal limits.
- **Heating element grounded or broken (~10%)** A shorted or open heating element can cause erratic temperature behavior that the control interprets as a fault.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~5%)** Connector pins at the thermistor or thermostats can corrode or back out, creating intermittent signals that trigger the error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and move it away from the wall for access to the rear panel or service panels.
2. **Remove the lint filter** and clean the filter housing with a vacuum, then inspect the exhaust vent hose for blockages or kinks and clear any obstructions.
3. **Access the blower housing** by removing the lower front or rear panel (fasteners vary by model) and locate the thermistor and high-limit thermostat, usually mounted on the heater duct or blower housing.
4. **Disconnect the wire connectors** from the thermistor and use a multimeter to measure its resistance; consult your model's table for the expected value at room temperature (typically a few thousand ohms).
5. **Check the high-limit thermostat** for continuity using a multimeter; it should show a closed circuit (zero or near-zero ohms) at room temperature; if open, it has tripped or failed.
6. **Replace the faulty component** by removing the mounting screw or clip, installing the new thermistor or thermostat, and reconnecting the wires.
7. **Reassemble the panels**, plug the dryer back in, and run a timed dry cycle to verify the error does not return and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections inside the dryer, if you have tested the thermistor and thermostats and both measure correctly but the error persists, or if the control board itself appears damaged. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board and wiring harness, and can safely handle any high-voltage components. If your dryer is still under warranty, contact Electrolux service to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
