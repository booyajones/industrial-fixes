## Bosch Washer E02 Error Code — What It Means

The E02 error code on Bosch washing machines typically indicates a drain system fault. The machine is unable to pump water out within the expected time, or it detects a blockage or malfunction in the drain path. This code will usually appear during or after the wash cycle when the washer attempts to drain water.

Because Bosch uses different control platforms across its washer models, the exact meaning can vary slightly. Always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the machine for model-specific details. In most cases, the root cause is a clog or obstruction preventing normal water flow rather than a failed electronic component.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the drain pump when the real problem is a clog in the filter or a coin wedged in the impeller. Clean the filter and inspect the pump housing for foreign objects before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain pump filter (~45%)** Lint, coins, buttons, and small clothing items collect in the filter housing and block water flow, triggering the error when the machine cannot drain in time.
- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~25%)** A hose bent behind the machine or filled with soap scum and lint restricts flow and prevents proper drainage.
- **Failed drain pump (~15%)** The pump motor can burn out or the impeller can crack, leaving the machine unable to expel water even when the filter and hose are clear.
- **Foreign object in pump impeller (~10%)** A coin, hairpin, or broken zipper tab jams the impeller so it cannot spin, mimicking a complete pump failure.
- **Faulty pressure switch or sensor (~5%)** The water-level sensor may send incorrect signals to the control board, reporting that the tub is still full when it has already drained.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves to make sure safe working conditions.
2. **Open the drain pump filter access panel** at the lower front of the machine, usually a small rectangular door secured by a clip or screw.
3. **Place a shallow pan or towels** on the floor and slowly unscrew the filter cap counterclockwise, allowing trapped water to drain into the pan.
4. **Remove the filter completely** and rinse it under running water, clearing away lint, coins, hair, and debris from the mesh and housing.
5. **Inspect the pump impeller** through the filter opening by shining a flashlight inside and rotating the impeller blades by hand to check for obstructions or cracks.
6. **Pull the washer forward** and disconnect the drain hose from the standpipe or sink drain, then straighten it and flush it with water from a utility sink to clear any internal clogs.
7. **Verify the standpipe or drain** is not clogged by pouring a bucket of water into it and confirming rapid flow.
8. **Reinstall the filter** by screwing it clockwise until snug, then close the access panel and reconnect the drain hose securely.
9. **Plug in the washer** and run a short rinse-and-drain cycle without laundry to test whether the error clears and the machine drains normally.
10. **If the error persists**, remove the top or rear panel to access the drain pump, disconnect the wiring harness, and test the pump motor windings with a multimeter set to resistance mode; replace the pump if you measure infinite resistance or hear no hum when powered.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared the filter and hose but the error returns, if you lack confidence working inside the machine's cabinet, or if testing reveals a faulty control board or pressure switch that requires diagnostic equipment. A technician can also handle situations where the pump is difficult to access due to cabinet design or when multiple fault codes appear together, suggesting a wiring harness or main-board issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
