## LG Oven Temperature Not Accurate — What's Happening

When your LG oven temperature is not accurate, the appliance heats but doesn't match the setpoint you selected. This is a performance symptom, not a single error code. LG's troubleshooting treats it as a problem with how the oven senses or regulates cavity temperature, rather than a complete failure to heat.

The oven may underheat, overheat, cycle poorly, or cook food unevenly. LG directs technicians to verify heating behavior, check sensor readings, inspect wiring, review calibration, and perform a power reset before replacing components. If a sensor-related error code is present, that's a different fault and requires its own diagnostic path.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Oven temperature sensor out of range, open, or shorted** The thermistor that monitors cavity temperature drifts, breaks, or shorts internally and sends incorrect resistance readings to the control board.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded sensor wiring or connectors** Broken wires, backed-out terminals, or corrosion at the sensor plug cause intermittent or incorrect readings even when the sensor itself is good.
- **Control board misreading the sensor or failing to regulate** The electronic oven control receives correct sensor data but its input circuit or logic fails and the oven cycles incorrectly.
- **Oven calibration or thermostat adjustment needed** The oven heats consistently high or low by a fixed offset and the thermostat setting requires adjustment in 5-degree steps within the 0 to 35 degree range LG allows.
- **Power or control glitch requiring a reset** A transient fault in the control locks incorrect calibration or logic until the breaker is cycled for 30 seconds.
- **Door gasket leak, convection fan fault, or heating element issue** Heat escapes through a worn seal, the fan doesn't circulate air properly, or the bake element cycles unevenly and affects real cavity temperature independent of sensor accuracy.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the complaint by setting the oven to 350°F, placing an oven thermometer on the center rack, and comparing actual temperature after preheating to see if it undershoots, overshoots, or cycles poorly.
2. Turn off the circuit breaker feeding the range for 30 seconds, restore power, and retest to clear any transient control fault.
3. Check the display for any error codes related to sensor open, sensor short, or oven hot conditions and address those codes if present.
4. Locate the oven temperature sensor (usually a metal probe at the upper rear wall of the cavity), disconnect the harness, and inspect the sensor, terminals, and wiring for damage, corrosion, or loose pins.
5. Measure sensor resistance at room temperature using a multimeter set to ohms and compare to the expected range (commonly around 1,080 Ω for many LG models, with roughly 1,060 to 1,100 Ω acceptable in technician guides).
6. If the sensor tests correct but temperature remains inaccurate, suspect the control board input circuit or electronic oven control and verify voltage or data at the control connector.
7. Inspect door gasket seal, convection fan operation if equipped, and bake element cycling to confirm the heating system itself performs correctly.
8. If the oven heats consistently high or low by a fixed amount and your model supports adjustment, enter the thermostat calibration menu and adjust in 5-degree increments within the 0 to 35 degree range, then retest with the oven thermometer.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt power, if sensor resistance readings fall outside the acceptable range and you need confirmation before ordering parts, or if the sensor and wiring check good but the oven still will not regulate temperature correctly. A tech can verify control board inputs, perform live voltage checks at the sensor circuit, and access service-mode diagnostics that are not available through the user interface. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
