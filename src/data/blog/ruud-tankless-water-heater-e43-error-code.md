## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E43 Error — What It Means

The E43 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a flame failure or ignition issue. The unit is unable to establish or maintain a flame after the ignition sequence begins. This can result from problems with the gas supply, ignition system components, or flame-sensing hardware.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary by model and year, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for the specific definition of E43 for your appliance. General flame and ignition faults are the usual culprits, but the precise diagnostic path depends on your heater's design.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter first, but a simple check of the gas valve position and main gas pressure will rule out supply issues that cost nothing to fix.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply restricted or valve closed (~35%)** The manual shutoff valve upstream may be partially closed, the meter may be out of gas, or line pressure is too low for the heater to ignite.
- **Faulty or fouled igniter (~25%)** The hot-surface igniter may be cracked, coated with carbon deposits, or electrically open, preventing spark or glow needed for ignition.
- **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor (~20%)** The flame-sensing rod may be covered with soot or positioned too far from the burner, so the control cannot detect flame even when present.
- **Low inlet water flow (~10%)** If flow through the heat exchanger is below the minimum threshold, the unit may refuse to ignite or shut down during the attempt.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~10%)** Loose connections to the igniter, sensor, or gas valve, or a failed relay on the main control board, can interrupt the ignition sequence.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Gas delivery is likely adequate; move on to inspect ignition components and flame-sensing hardware.<br><strong>No:</strong> Open the valve completely or contact your gas utility to restore service, then clear the error and test the heater.</div>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you see or hear the igniter attempt to spark or glow when the unit calls for hot water?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The igniter is receiving power; check for dirty burner ports, a fouled flame sensor, or weak gas pressure at the valve.<br><strong>No:</strong> The igniter may be failed, or wiring and control-board signals to it are interrupted; a technician should test voltage and continuity.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error clear and the unit fire successfully after a power-cycle and purge of air from the gas line?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The problem may have been temporary air in the line or a transient fault; monitor for recurrence.<br><strong>No:</strong> A persistent ignition failure points to a bad igniter, sensor, gas valve, or board that requires replacement by a qualified technician.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or disconnect switch and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the access panel fasteners to expose the burner compartment and control board.
3. **Inspect the igniter** for cracks, heavy carbon buildup, or loose wire terminals; if damaged, mark it for replacement.
4. **Check the flame sensor rod** for soot or corrosion; gently clean it with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth and verify it is positioned near the burner flame zone.
5. **Examine all wire connections** to the igniter, flame sensor, and gas valve for looseness or corrosion, and reseat any suspect plugs.
6. **Restore gas and power**, open a hot-water tap to initiate a call for heat, and observe whether the igniter glows or sparks and whether flame appears.
7. **If no ignition occurs**, measure gas pressure at the valve inlet with a manometer (if you have the tools) or call a licensed technician to test the igniter, gas valve, and control-board outputs.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician whenever you need to test or adjust gas pressure, replace a gas valve, or diagnose control-board signals with a multimeter. Gas appliances require proper combustion tuning and venting checks after any repair to prevent carbon monoxide hazards. A technician will also have the manometer and combustion analyzer needed to verify safe, efficient operation once ignition is restored. If you are uncomfortable working around natural gas or propane, or if the error persists after basic checks, professional diagnosis is the safest choice.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
