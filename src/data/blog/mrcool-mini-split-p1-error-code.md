## MRCOOL Mini Split P1 Error Code — What It Means

The P1 error code (also displayed as PC-01 on some MRCOOL models) indicates a voltage-related protection fault detected by the air handler or outdoor unit. The system has sensed abnormal supply voltage or a fault in the inverter power circuitry and has shut down to protect itself. This is not an airflow or refrigerant issue. It is a power electronics or incoming voltage problem.

## Common Causes

- **Incoming power out of specification** The supply voltage at the disconnect, breaker, or unit terminals is too high, too low, or unstable.
- **Loose or damaged wiring connections** Terminal blocks between indoor and outdoor units, or at the line supply, are loose, corroded, or burned.
- **Failed outdoor control board or inverter board** The main PCB in the outdoor unit has sustained damage or component failure in its voltage detection or inverter circuits.
- **Faulty PFC circuit components** Power factor correction components such as the reactor coil, diode, or capacitors in the outdoor unit have failed.
- **Home electrical supply instability** Breaker problems, voltage sags, or utility supply fluctuations cause intermittent abnormal voltage at the unit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the system.** Shut off the breaker or disconnect for at least one minute, restore power, and check whether the P1 code returns.
2. **Measure supply voltage at the disconnect and breaker** using a digital multimeter set to AC volts. Confirm line-to-neutral reads approximately 120 V and neutral-to-ground reads approximately 0 V (for 115 V systems).
3. **Check voltage at the outdoor unit terminals.** Measure L to N and L to ground at the outdoor line connections, comparing readings to those taken at the disconnect to detect voltage drop or wiring faults.
4. **Inspect and test interconnect wiring** between indoor and outdoor units. Check terminals 1, 2, and 3 for correct voltage behavior and look for loose, burned, or corroded connections.
5. **Tighten all terminal screws and repair damaged wiring** if you find loose connections or visible damage. Re-test voltage and reset the unit.
6. **If voltage is correct and wiring is intact, inspect the outdoor control board** for burn marks, swollen capacitors, or other visible damage. Test PFC reactor resistance (should be 0 to 1 ohm on typical inverter boards) and check diode and capacitor health.
7. **Replace the failed component or control board.** If diagnostics point to a failed inverter board, PFC reactor, or related power electronics, install the correct replacement part for your MRCOOL model and retest.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with live voltage, if you cannot safely access the outdoor control board, or if voltage measurements and wiring checks do not resolve the P1 code. Power electronics repair requires specialized tools and knowledge of inverter circuits. If your utility supply is unstable or you suspect a main panel issue, contact a licensed electrician to evaluate and correct the incoming power before replacing any mini-split components.
