## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E30 Error — What It Means

The E30 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater indicates a flame failure or ignition problem. The unit has detected that either the flame did not ignite within the expected time window or the flame was lost during operation. This safety lockout prevents raw gas from entering the combustion chamber without ignition.

Because the exact definition of fault codes can vary by model and firmware version, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit for the precise meaning. Common triggers include gas supply issues, blocked venting, dirty flame rods, or faulty ignition components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame sensor first, but many E30 codes trace to simple gas supply or venting problems. Check that the gas valve is fully open and that all vent pipes are clear before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low or no gas supply (~35%)** A closed valve, empty propane tank, or pressure regulator problem starves the burner and prevents ignition.
- **Blocked or improper venting (~25%)** Vent pipes obstructed by debris, ice, or condensation cause poor combustion air flow and flame rollout safety trips.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~20%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing electrode prevents the control board from detecting flame current.
- **Faulty igniter (~10%)** A cracked or weak igniter fails to produce a spark hot enough to light the gas within the allowed time.
- **Failed gas valve (~7%)** The internal solenoid or diaphragm in the gas valve sticks closed or does not modulate correctly.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The PCB may fail to send ignition signals or correctly read flame-sensor feedback, causing false lockouts.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the dedicated service switch or circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the heater.
2. **Check gas supply** by confirming the valve is fully open and that other gas appliances operate normally; for propane, verify tank level.
3. **Inspect all vent pipes** from the unit to the termination outdoors; remove any debris, ice, bird nests, or pooled condensate.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the heater (typically secured by screws) to access the combustion chamber and ignition components.
5. **Examine the flame rod** for carbon buildup or corrosion; gently clean it with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth if dirty.
6. **Verify igniter operation** by restoring power and watching for a visible spark when the unit attempts to fire; replace the igniter if no spark appears.
7. **Test the unit** by opening the gas valve, restoring power, and calling for hot water; observe whether ignition succeeds and the error clears; if the fault persists, call a licensed technician to test gas pressure and the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the gas pressure at the inlet needs testing with a manometer, or if both the flame rod and igniter check out but the error persists. Tankless water heaters operate under precise combustion and venting parameters, and misadjusted gas valves or control boards require diagnostic tools and training. Always call a pro when you smell gas or suspect a leak.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
