## Gree PH Error Code — What It Means

The PH error code on most Gree inverter mini-split systems signals a DC bus-bar high-voltage protection fault. The inverter's DC bus voltage has exceeded the safe threshold for your specific model family, and the system has shut down to prevent damage. Because Gree uses different code definitions across product lines, you must confirm the exact meaning in your indoor and outdoor unit model numbers and their matching service manual before ordering parts or resetting the system.

This is an electrical protection fault, not a refrigerant or sensor issue. In most cases the problem originates either from the building power supply (voltage spikes or instability) or from a failed component in the outdoor unit's inverter power section. Do not repeatedly reset the unit if the code returns immediately, the outdoor unit runs hot, you smell burning, or the breaker trips.

## Common Causes

- **Unstable or high incoming line voltage** Voltage spikes, surges, or sustained overvoltage from the building supply can drive the DC bus above the protection threshold.
- **Loose or damaged electrical connections** Poor contact at terminal blocks, corroded connectors, or damaged wire harnesses between indoor and outdoor units trigger protection faults.
- **Failed outdoor inverter board or power section** The inverter drive board, PFC section, or power-supply protection circuit can fail and cause high bus voltage readings.
- **Faulty inverter module (IPM)** The intelligent power module itself may have internal faults that result in incorrect DC bus regulation and high-voltage shutdown.
- **Weak or aging DC-link capacitors** Field reports (not official Gree documentation) suggest that capacitors in the DC bus section can degrade and contribute to PH or PL family faults.
- **Installation or load anomalies** Incorrect electrical installation, under-sized wiring, or an abnormal load condition can put the system into a state that repeatedly trips high-voltage protection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Stop resetting the unit** if PH returns immediately, the outdoor unit feels unusually hot, you detect a burnt smell, or the breaker trips, and disconnect power at the breaker.
2. **Record the exact model numbers** from the label on both the indoor and outdoor unit so you can match your system to the correct Gree service manual and error-code table.
3. **Measure incoming supply voltage** at the outdoor unit's terminal block with a multimeter to confirm line voltage is stable and within specification for your region before suspecting unit-side components.
4. **Inspect all electrical connections** at the outdoor unit terminal block, the indoor-outdoor harness connectors, and inside the outdoor unit cover for loose terminals, corrosion, overheating marks, or damaged wire insulation.
5. **Check the outdoor inverter board and module** for visible burn marks, swollen capacitors, or cracked solder joints, and look for any burnt smell near the power section or inverter module heat sink.
6. **Test under operating conditions** if you find and correct a wiring or voltage issue, run the system through a full cooling or heating cycle and monitor for 15 to 20 minutes to confirm PH does not return.
7. **Replace the failed outdoor board or module** if supply voltage and all connections are verified normal but the fault persists, and consult the Gree service manual for your model family to identify the correct inverter drive board or IPM module part number.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician or a Gree-authorized service provider if you are not comfortable working with live line voltage, if you cannot measure and verify stable supply voltage, or if the fault returns after you have checked wiring and connections. High-voltage DC bus faults require load testing of the inverter board and module under operating conditions, and misdiagnosis can lead to repeated part replacement or further damage. A qualified technician has the model-specific service manual, the correct replacement board or module part number, and the test equipment to confirm the repair before closing the unit. If your unit is still under warranty, contact Gree or your installing dealer first, because unauthorized repairs may void coverage.
