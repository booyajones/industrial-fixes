## GE Microwave High-Voltage Transformer Replacement — What This Part Does

The high-voltage transformer converts standard household line power into the high voltage needed to operate the magnetron, which generates the microwaves that heat food. It works with the high-voltage capacitor and diode to supply the magnetron circuit. The transformer fails when its primary or high-voltage windings short, open, or go out of spec. Sustained heat damage, burning, charring, or physical stress on the windings and wire connections cause most failures.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **Microwave runs but won't heat food** The turntable spins and lights come on, but the unit produces no heat because the transformer can't supply high voltage to the magnetron.
- **Burning smell or visible charring on the transformer** Heat damage, discoloration, or burned insulation on the transformer body or wire connections indicates the windings or terminals have failed.
- **Breaker trips or fuse blows when microwave operates** Shorted transformer windings draw excessive current and trip household protection or internal fuses.
- **Loud humming or buzzing during operation** A failing transformer with compromised windings produces abnormal noise when energized.
- **Primary winding resistance above 1 ohm** Testing the primary winding with a multimeter shows more than 1 ohm, indicating an open or degraded winding.
- **High-voltage winding reads outside 35 to 150 ohms** The high-voltage winding measures outside the typical 35 to 150 ohm range, showing a faulty secondary.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Unplug the microwave from the wall outlet and verify it is fully de-energized.
2. Discharge the high-voltage capacitor by contacting both terminals with an insulated screwdriver across a resistor or insulated discharge tool, never with bare metal or your hands.
3. Remove the outer cabinet panels by loosening the screws on the rear and sides of the microwave.
4. Locate the high-voltage transformer (a large metal component with wire connections on top) and photograph or label all wire terminals and their positions before disconnecting anything.
5. Disconnect all wiring from the transformer terminals, noting the primary (line voltage) and secondary (high-voltage) connections.
6. Remove the transformer mounting screws that secure it to the chassis and lift the transformer straight out.
7. Position the replacement transformer in the same mounting location and secure it with the mounting screws.
8. Reconnect all wiring to the correct transformer terminals, matching your photos or labels exactly.
9. Reinstall the cabinet panels and tighten all fasteners, checking that all panels, filters, and the power cord are properly routed and secured.

## When to Call a Pro

High-voltage microwave repair involves lethal stored voltage in the capacitor and exposed high-voltage terminals that remain dangerous even after unplugging. If you are not trained in high-voltage appliance service, not comfortable discharging capacitors with proper insulated tools, or unsure about identifying and testing transformer windings with a multimeter, hire a qualified appliance technician. Incorrect handling can cause fatal electric shock or damage other high-voltage components like the magnetron, capacitor, or diode.
