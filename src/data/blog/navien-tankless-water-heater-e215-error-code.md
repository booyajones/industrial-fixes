## Navien Tankless Water Heater E215 Error — What It Means

The E215 error on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with the flow sensor or circulation system. The control board is not detecting the expected water flow signal, which can prevent the unit from heating properly or cause it to shut down. This code often appears when the flow sensor itself has failed, when scale deposits restrict flow through the sensor, or when the internal recirculation pump is not operating correctly. In some cases wiring connections to the flow sensor may be loose or corroded.

Because tankless water heaters depend on accurate flow measurement to modulate burner output, any disruption in the flow sensing circuit will trigger a protective shutdown. The error may appear intermittently at first, especially during low-flow conditions, or it may persist and prevent all heating. Check your owner's manual for the exact definition on your model, as error codes can vary slightly between Navien series.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flow sensor when the real problem is scale deposits blocking the sensor port or a failing recirculation pump. Flush the heat exchanger and inspect pump operation before ordering a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Scale buildup on flow sensor (~40%)** Hard water deposits can coat the sensor paddle or turbine and prevent it from spinning freely or sending a clean signal to the control board.
- **Failed flow sensor (~30%)** The flow sensor's internal Hall-effect sensor or wiring can fail over time, sending no signal or erratic pulses to the board.
- **Recirculation pump failure (~15%)** If the internal recirculation pump is not running or is weak, flow through the sensor drops below the threshold and triggers the error.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~10%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen connector pins or corrode terminals at the flow sensor harness, interrupting the signal.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** Rarely the main PCB loses the ability to read the flow sensor input, though this is much less common than sensor or pump issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the inlet isolation valve to stop water flow.
2. **Remove the front cover panel** and locate the flow sensor, usually a cylindrical assembly with a turbine or paddle inside, mounted in the cold inlet path.
3. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the flow sensor and inspect the terminals for corrosion or moisture, cleaning with contact cleaner if needed.
4. **Remove the flow sensor** by unscrewing its housing or releasing clips, then inspect the turbine or paddle for scale deposits or damage.
5. **Flush the sensor and inlet plumbing** using a descaling pump and appropriate solution, following the manufacturer's flushing procedure for your model, or replace the sensor if it is damaged or shows no continuity when tested with a multimeter.
6. **Check recirculation pump operation** by listening for motor hum or vibration, and verify pump wiring connections are secure.
7. **Reinstall the sensor** or replacement part, reconnect all wiring, restore water flow, and power on the unit to verify the error clears and normal operation resumes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien service technician if you are not comfortable working with gas-fired appliances, if the error persists after flushing and checking wiring, or if you suspect the recirculation pump or control board has failed. Tankless water heaters involve both gas and electrical systems, and incorrect repairs can create safety hazards or void your warranty. A qualified technician can perform flow tests, measure sensor output with diagnostic tools, and safely replace internal components. Professional service is also recommended if your unit is still under warranty, as DIY repairs may affect coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
