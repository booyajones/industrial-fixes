## Goodman B9 Error Code — What It Means

The B9 code on a Goodman heat pump indicates a high-stage pressure switch failure. The control board is not seeing the pressure switch in the state it expects during the operating sequence. This fault usually means the pressure switch is not closing or opening correctly, not that the switch itself has failed.

In most cases, the root cause is something blocking airflow or pressure: clogged venting, a weak inducer motor, a cracked or disconnected pressure switch hose, restricted condensate drainage, or loose wiring at the switch or control board. Less often, the pressure switch itself is bad. Because this code relates to a pressure-sensing circuit rather than refrigerant diagnostics, focus first on the venting and draft system.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted venting or intake** Obstructions in the vent pipe or intake prevent the pressure differential needed to close the switch.
- **Cracked, kinked, or disconnected pressure switch hose** A damaged or loose hose cannot transmit the pressure signal from the inducer housing to the switch.
- **Weak or failed inducer motor** If the inducer does not spin fast enough or at all, it will not create the draft pressure required to activate the switch.
- **Plugged condensate trap or drain line** Standing water or debris in the drain path can create back-pressure that holds the switch open or closed incorrectly.
- **Loose or corroded wiring and connectors** Poor connections at the pressure switch terminals or control board prevent the board from reading the switch state.
- **Faulty pressure switch** The switch itself can stick, corrode, or fail internally so it does not change state even when correct pressure is present.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault code** in your model's published fault table to confirm B9 means high-stage pressure switch fault for your exact unit.
2. **Inspect the vent and intake** for blockages, bird nests, ice, lint buildup, or any restriction that would prevent proper draft or combustion air.
3. **Check the pressure switch tubing** for cracks, kinks, pinches, or disconnections, and confirm the hose is routed correctly and securely attached at both ends.
4. **Listen to the inducer motor** during startup to confirm it spins up smoothly and reaches full speed, and look for any unusual noise or hesitation that indicates a weak motor.
5. **Examine the condensate drain and trap** for clogs, standing water, or debris, and clear any blockage that could create back-pressure in the system.
6. **Test the pressure switch electrically** by checking continuity across its terminals with the unit off, then observing whether the switch changes state when the inducer runs (consult service literature for your model's 24 V control-circuit checks).
7. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** at the pressure switch and control board for corrosion, looseness, or damage, and repair or replace any compromised connections or harnesses.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with 24 V control circuits, testing continuity, or interpreting your model's service manual. If you have cleared all venting and drainage blockages, verified the inducer runs at full speed, confirmed the hose is intact, and the B9 code still appears, a technician with a manometer and wiring diagrams can pinpoint whether the issue is the switch, the control board, or a wiring fault. Professional diagnosis is also the safest choice if you smell gas, see active water leaks, or hear unusual mechanical sounds from the inducer.
