## LG Refrigerator gF Error Code — What It Means

The gF code on an LG refrigerator indicates a flow sensor error or low water pressure in the water supply path to the ice maker or water dispenser. The refrigerator's control board expects to see water flow when dispensing or making ice, but the flow sensor is either not detecting any flow or reporting insufficient pressure. LG also notes that if the ice maker is overfilling, the flow sensor itself may be malfunctioning and sending incorrect readings to the control.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the water inlet valve or flow sensor before checking the supply side. First verify that the household shutoff is fully open and the supply line is not kinked, crushed, or frozen, which fixes the problem in many cases at no cost.

## Common Causes

- **Low incoming water pressure or partially closed supply valve (~35%)** The household water supply is restricted, the shutoff valve is not fully open, or a saddle valve is clogged, so the flow sensor sees insufficient flow.
- **Kinked, pinched, or frozen water line (~25%)** The supply tube between the wall and the refrigerator, or the internal tubing to the ice maker, is bent, crushed, or ice-blocked, reducing flow to zero.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~20%)** The inlet valve is stuck, weak, electrically open, or restricted by debris, so it does not deliver enough water even when the supply is adequate.
- **Malfunctioning flow sensor (~15%)** The flow sensor itself is defective and not reporting flow correctly, or it misreports overfilling conditions as LG describes.
- **Clogged household shutoff valve or saddle valve (~5%)** Mineral deposits or debris in the supply-side valve restrict flow before it reaches the refrigerator.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** or turn off the circuit breaker to cut power before inspecting water components.
2. **Verify the household shutoff valve** is fully open. If it is a saddle valve, check for clogs and consider replacing it with a modern quarter-turn valve.
3. **Inspect the supply line** from the wall to the refrigerator for kinks, pinches, or damage. Straighten or replace any crushed sections.
4. **Disconnect the supply line** at the refrigerator inlet and run water into a bucket to confirm strong, steady flow. If flow is weak, the problem is in the supply line or valve.
5. **Check the inlet valve** at the back of the refrigerator. Remove the fill line and run the dispenser test cycle to see if the valve opens and delivers water. If not, test the coil terminals with a multimeter or replace the inlet valve assembly.
6. **Inspect for frozen tubing** behind the refrigerator or inside the door. Thaw any ice blockages with a hairdryer on low heat and address the root cause of freezing (for example, low freezer temperature or a damaged door seal).
7. **Replace the flow sensor** if supply and valve are good but the code persists or if the ice maker overfills, as LG identifies the sensor as a primary fault source.
8. **Restore power** and run a dispenser or ice-maker test cycle. Clear the gF code by pressing and holding the appropriate control-panel buttons (consult your model's manual) or by power-cycling the unit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the flow sensor or inlet valve, if the refrigerator continues to show the gF code after you have verified good supply pressure and replaced both the inlet valve and flow sensor, or if you are uncomfortable working with water connections and electrical terminals. A technician has the wiring diagram and test procedures to isolate whether the control board itself is misreading the sensor or whether another component in the water path is at fault. Also call a pro if your model requires disassembly of the door or sealed compartments to reach the flow sensor, as improper reassembly can cause air leaks and temperature problems.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–120 in parts (inlet valve or flow sensor), 30–90 minutes. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
