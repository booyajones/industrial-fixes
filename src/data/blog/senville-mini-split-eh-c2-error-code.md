## Senville Mini Split EH C2 Error Code — What It Means

The EH C2 error code on a Senville AURA-series mini split indicates that the refrigerant leak-detection sensor is reading out of range and has detected a potential leak. According to Senville's official error-code reference, this fault means the indoor unit's sensor (part number TS06-N10) has either failed or is detecting an actual refrigerant line leak.

Senville specifies two corrective actions: replace the sensor and inspect the refrigerant line. This dual approach recognizes that the code can appear both when the sensor itself is faulty and when a real leak is present. The system will not operate normally until the sensor is replaced and any leak is repaired.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty refrigerant leak-detection sensor (TS06-N10)** The sensor has drifted out of its normal operating range or failed internally, triggering the EH C2 code even if no leak exists.
- **Actual refrigerant line leak** A leak in the refrigerant piping or indoor coil allows gas to escape, and the sensor correctly detects it and raises the alarm.
- **Damaged or corroded sensor wiring** Wiring to the TS06-N10 sensor is broken, shorted, or corroded, causing an out-of-range signal that the control board interprets as EH C2.
- **Loose or disconnected sensor connector** The sensor harness connector at the indoor unit control board or sensor itself has vibrated loose or was not fully seated during installation.
- **Control board misreading sensor signal** Less common, but the indoor unit's control board may be faulty and incorrectly interpret a normal sensor signal as out of range.
- **Refrigerant overcharge or undercharge** Extreme refrigerant levels can cause abnormal pressures or temperatures that confuse the leak sensor or indicate a prior leak and improper recharge.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the disconnect switch and wait two minutes to allow capacitors to discharge before opening the indoor unit cover.
2. **Verify the displayed code** is truly EH C2 on the indoor unit display or remote, and not a temperature readout or different alarm.
3. **Inspect all refrigerant piping** (both liquid and suction lines) and flare connections for signs of oil staining, frost buildup, or hissing sounds that indicate an active leak.
4. **Locate the TS06-N10 sensor** inside the indoor unit (usually mounted near the evaporator coil or on the control board housing) and check that its wiring connector is firmly seated and shows no corrosion or damage.
5. **Replace the TS06-N10 sensor assembly** if inspection reveals corroded wiring, a loose connector that won't stay seated, or if the code persists after verifying connections.
6. **Repair any confirmed refrigerant leak** by brazing or replacing the damaged section of line, then evacuate and recharge the system to the nameplate specification before restoring power.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** for at least 15 minutes in cooling mode to confirm the EH C2 code clears and does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Refrigerant leak detection and repair require EPA 608 certification and specialized tools (vacuum pump, refrigerant scale, leak detector, manifold gauges). If you see EH C2 and are not certified, call a licensed HVAC technician to perform the leak inspection, sensor replacement, and any required refrigerant work. Attempting to recharge the system without fixing a leak or replacing the sensor will cause the code to return and may violate environmental regulations. A pro can also verify that the control board is not misreading a healthy sensor before you buy a replacement part.
