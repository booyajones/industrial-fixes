## Mitsubishi P6 Error Code — What It Means

On Mitsubishi Electric heat pumps, a P6 error code signals that the system has activated its coil freeze or overheating protection. The indoor coil temperature sensor has detected conditions outside safe limits, either too cold during cooling mode (risking ice buildup) or too hot during heating mode (risking compressor damage). The unit shuts down or limits operation to protect itself. The exact threshold varies by model, so always confirm the meaning in your service manual. This fault is almost always tied to either restricted airflow over the coil or a refrigerant charge or flow problem.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or clogged air filter** Blocked filters starve the indoor coil of airflow, causing it to freeze in cooling or overheat in heating.
- **Low refrigerant or refrigerant leak** Insufficient charge prevents proper heat exchange, driving coil temperatures outside safe limits.
- **Blocked indoor coil or return vents** Dust buildup on the coil fins or obstructed return grilles reduce airflow and trigger the protection.
- **Faulty coil thermistor** The temperature sensor may be reading incorrectly or intermittently, causing false fault signals.
- **Weak or failed indoor blower** A dirty blower wheel or failing fan motor reduces airflow enough to push the coil out of range.
- **Control board fault** The indoor or outdoor control board may be misreading sensor data or have a logic error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Power off the unit at the breaker and wait two minutes, then inspect and clean or replace the indoor air filter.
2. Check all supply and return vents for blockages, furniture, or curtains and clear any obstructions.
3. Remove the indoor unit cover and visually inspect the evaporator coil for dust or debris. If dirty, gently vacuum or use coil cleaner and let it dry before reassembling.
4. Power the system back on and observe the indoor fan. Confirm it spins freely and reaches full speed during operation.
5. If the error returns immediately, check the area around the indoor coil for signs of ice (cooling mode) or unusual heat (heating mode) and note which mode triggered the fault.
6. Use a multimeter to test the coil thermistor resistance if you have access to the wiring harness. Compare the reading to the resistance table in your model's service manual and replace the sensor if out of spec.
7. If airflow is good, filters are clean, and the sensor tests normal, call a licensed HVAC technician to check refrigerant pressures, diagnose leaks, and inspect control boards.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the P6 code returns after cleaning filters and confirming good airflow, or if you see ice on the coil in cooling mode or smell burning in heating mode. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification and specialized gauges. A technician will measure superheat and subcooling, leak-check the system, test the thermistor against factory specs, and inspect control boards with a service tool. Do not repeatedly reset the breaker to clear the code without fixing the root cause, because running with a frozen or overheated coil can damage the compressor.
