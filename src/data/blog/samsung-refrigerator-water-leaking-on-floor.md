## Samsung Refrigerator Water Leaking on Floor — What's Happening

A Samsung refrigerator leaking water onto the floor is not an error code. It is a troubleshooting symptom with several possible causes. Samsung's official support treats this as water dripping from the dispenser or autofill system, standing water around the unit, or water appearing on the floor due to an issue in the water delivery path, installation level, or drainage.

Most often the leak comes from air in the water line after a filter replacement, a filter that is not seated correctly, low household water pressure, a partially closed supply valve, a kinked or leaking water line, or the refrigerator being out of level. If water pools in the freezer or crisper area, a frozen or clogged defrost drain is a frequent cause specific to Samsung models, where ice blocks the drain path and water overflows onto the floor.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Air trapped in the water line after filter change** Samsung identifies this as the most common cause after installation or filter replacement, causing water to drip or sputter from the dispenser and pool on the floor.
- **Water filter not seated correctly or wrong filter installed** A filter that is not locked in place or an old, incorrect, or non-genuine filter can restrict flow and cause leaks around the housing.
- **Low household water pressure or partially closed supply valve** Samsung specifies that pressure should be 30 to 130 psi; low pressure or a valve that is not fully open will cause poor dispenser flow and may produce drips.
- **Water line or coupler leak** Cracks, kinks, loose fittings, or damaged couplers in the supply tubing allow water to escape and pool under or around the refrigerator.
- **Refrigerator not level** Samsung states that improper leveling can affect drainage and contribute to water appearing on the floor, especially if the unit tilts forward.
- **Frozen or clogged defrost drain** Ice or debris blocking the drain passage in the freezer section causes defrost water to overflow into the crisper drawers or onto the floor, a widely reported Samsung-specific issue.
- **Cracked or mispositioned drain pan** If the pan under the refrigerator is cracked, out of place, or unable to evaporate water properly, it will overflow onto the floor.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Identify where the water is coming from: dispenser area, rear water line, bottom freezer section, or under the unit.
2. Remove and reinstall the water filter, making sure it clicks and locks into place, and use a genuine Samsung filter if required for your model.
3. Purge air from the water line by dispensing water into a large container until the stream stops sputtering, repeating the cycle several times until flow is steady.
4. Perform Samsung's flow test: fill a measuring cup with 6 ounces in less than 10 seconds; if the test only passes with the filter removed, pressure is too low or the filter is restricting flow.
5. Check that the household shutoff valve is fully open and inspect the water line and all couplers for leaks, kinks, cracks, or loose fittings.
6. Verify the refrigerator is level front to back and side to side using a bubble level, and adjust the leveling feet or wheels as needed.
7. If water pools in the freezer or crisper drawers, inspect the defrost drain for ice or blockage, thaw any ice, clear the drain passage, and confirm water flows to the pan.
8. After any repair, run the dispenser and monitor for dripping, standing water, or renewed ice buildup over the next 24 hours.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if the leak persists after purging air and checking the filter, if household water pressure is below 30 psi and requires plumbing work, if the defrost drain remains frozen after manual thawing, or if you find a refrigerant line, sealed-system component, or electronic control board involvement. A qualified appliance technician has the tools to diagnose internal drain heater failures, compressor-area leaks, and complex water-valve or autofill module faults that are not accessible during basic troubleshooting.
