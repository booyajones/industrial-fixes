## Bosch Dishwasher E12 Error Code — What It Means

The E12 error code on Bosch dishwashers typically indicates a problem with the heating system. The control board has detected that water is not reaching the correct temperature during the wash cycle, or the heating circuit is not functioning as expected. This can prevent the dishwasher from completing its cycle properly, leaving dishes poorly cleaned.

Because error code meanings can vary slightly across different Bosch dishwasher models and production years, always consult your specific model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the exact definition. In general, E12 points to an issue with the heating element, the temperature sensor (thermistor), or the wiring and connections between these components and the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, assuming it has failed. Test the heating element for continuity and the thermistor for proper resistance before replacing any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element (~50%)** The heating element has burned out or developed an open circuit, preventing water from heating during the wash cycle.
- **Faulty thermistor (temperature sensor) (~25%)** The thermistor is reading incorrect temperatures or has failed completely, causing the control board to flag a heating error.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~15%)** Wire terminals at the heating element or thermistor have corroded or come loose, breaking the electrical path.
- **Control board relay failure (~8%)** The relay on the main control board that powers the heating element has failed and cannot energize the heater.
- **Wiring harness damage (~2%)** The wiring harness between the control board and heating components has chafed, melted, or broken internally.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the heating element show continuity when tested with a multimeter (typically 15-30 ohms)?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The element is likely good. Move on to testing the thermistor and checking wiring connections.<br><strong>No:</strong> The heating element has failed and needs replacement. This is the most common cause of E12.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the thermistor show a reasonable resistance reading (consult your model's table, often 50-60k ohms at room temperature)?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The thermistor is working. Inspect all wire connectors and the control board relay for the heater circuit.<br><strong>No:</strong> The thermistor is faulty and should be replaced.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are all wire terminals on the heating element and thermistor clean, tight, and free of corrosion?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Connections are sound. The problem likely lies in the control board or internal wiring harness.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean the terminals, reseat the connectors, and test again before replacing any parts.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the lower access panel** at the front of the dishwasher to expose the heating element and wiring underneath the tub.
3. **Locate the heating element** at the base of the tub and the thermistor, usually clipped or screwed to the side wall of the tub near the sump.
4. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter set to ohms, placing probes on the two element terminals (expect a reading in the 15-30 ohm range for most models).
5. **Test the thermistor** by disconnecting its wire connector and measuring resistance across its terminals (consult your model's service chart for the correct room-temperature value, often 50-60k ohms).
6. **Inspect all wire connectors** on the heating element, thermistor, and control board for corrosion, loose fit, or burn marks, and reseat or clean as needed.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (heating element or thermistor) if testing shows an open circuit, short, or out-of-spec resistance, then reassemble the panel, restore power, and run a test cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if testing reveals the control board relay has failed, which requires soldering or board replacement. Also call a pro if you have replaced both the heating element and thermistor and the E12 error persists, as this points to a wiring harness fault or a more complex control board issue that requires diagnostic equipment and experience with Bosch appliance electronics.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $60-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
