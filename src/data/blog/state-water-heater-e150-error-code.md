## State Water Heater E150 Error Code — What It Means

E150 is not a universally documented State Water Heaters error code in official service literature, so the exact meaning depends on your specific model. On State and A. O. Smith tankless units, codes in this range typically indicate ignition failure or lockout. The heater detects a problem preventing the burner from lighting or staying lit, often related to airflow, gas supply, or water flow restrictions.

Because State uses model-specific fault codes, you should locate your unit's rating plate QR code or model number and consult the corresponding manual or contact State technical support for the precise E150 definition and troubleshooting path for your equipment.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screen** Lint, dust, and debris on the combustion air intake is the most common cause of ignition failure on State and A. O. Smith tankless heaters.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Closed gas valve, low line pressure, or undersized gas piping prevents the burner from lighting.
- **Restricted water flow** A clogged inlet water filter or insufficient flow rate can trigger ignition lockout on tankless models.
- **Blocked or improper venting** Exhaust blockage, excessive vent length, or too many elbows restricts combustion airflow and causes ignition faults.
- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger** Hard water deposits inside the heat exchanger or combustion chamber interfere with flame sensing and ignition.
- **Failed ignition or flame-sensing components** Worn igniter, dirty flame rod, or faulty control board can prevent ignition or reliable flame detection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your model number** on the rating plate and scan the QR code or search online for the official State manual to confirm the exact meaning of E150 for your unit.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screen** on the front or side of the unit, removing lint, dust, spider webs, or other debris that blocks combustion air.
3. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the gas valve is fully open, the gas line is not kinked or undersized, and other gas appliances in the home are working normally.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** where the cold water line enters the heater, flushing out sediment or debris that restricts flow.
5. **Examine the exhaust vent** for blockages, bird nests, or icing, and verify the vent run meets the manufacturer's length and elbow count limits.
6. **Reset the unit** by turning off power at the breaker for 30 seconds, then restoring power and attempting to run hot water to see if the fault clears.
7. **Call for service** if the E150 code returns after cleaning and resetting, as the fault likely requires component-level diagnosis of ignition, flame sensing, or gas valve hardware.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E150 code persists after you have cleaned the air intake, verified gas supply, flushed the inlet filter, and checked the exhaust vent. Ignition and flame-sensing systems involve live gas and high-voltage components that require proper tools, manufacturer service manuals, and safety training. A technician can perform combustion analysis, measure gas pressure, test flame sensor current, and replace faulty ignition hardware without risking gas leaks or unsafe operation. Also call for help if you are unfamiliar with gas appliance shutoff procedures or if your local code requires permitted work on gas-fired equipment.
