## State Water Heater E75 Error Code — What It Means

The E75 code on State tankless water heaters typically signals an ignition failure or combustion lockout. While the exact meaning can vary by model, State and A. O. Smith tankless units most commonly lock out when the burner cannot ignite or maintain flame. This usually traces back to restricted air supply, insufficient gas flow, or blocked water inlet components preventing proper operation.

The control board monitors ignition attempts and sets the fault code when the system cannot establish safe combustion. In most cases the underlying issue is mechanical blockage or restriction rather than a failed electronic component. Always confirm your specific model's fault code definition in the service manual before starting repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Dust, lint, and debris accumulate on intake filters and prevent adequate combustion air from reaching the burner, causing ignition failure.
- **Low gas pressure or gas line restriction** Partially closed shutoff valves, undersized gas pipe, or low utility supply pressure starve the burner and prevent ignition.
- **Dirty inlet water filter** Scale, sediment, or debris clog the cold water inlet screen and reduce flow below the minimum threshold needed to open the gas valve.
- **Blocked exhaust venting** Obstructed or improperly sized vent pipes create backpressure that prevents safe combustion and triggers lockout.
- **Failed igniter or flame sensor** A worn ignition electrode or fouled flame detection component cannot reliably start or confirm combustion.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number** and look up the exact E75 definition in your unit's service manual, since fault codes differ across State product lines.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the front or side of the unit, removing all dust, lint, and debris that block combustion air.
3. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the shutoff valve is fully open and measuring inlet gas pressure under firing conditions with a manometer.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** at the cold water connection, flushing out sediment and scale buildup.
5. **Inspect the exhaust vent** for blockages, proper pitch, and correct termination clearances, clearing any obstructions or condensate traps.
6. **Reset the unit** using the control panel or power cycle, then monitor the ignition sequence to see if the fault returns.
7. **Test ignition components** if the fault persists, checking the igniter for spark and the flame sensor for continuity and proper positioning in the flame path.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate or clear the blockage after inspecting intake and water filters, if you lack a manometer to measure gas pressure safely, or if the fault returns after cleaning and resetting. Any work on gas connections or internal combustion components requires proper tools and training. If you need to test or replace the igniter, flame sensor, or gas valve, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent unsafe trial-and-error repairs.
