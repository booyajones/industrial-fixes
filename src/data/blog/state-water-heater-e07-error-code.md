## State Water Heater E07 Error — What It Means

The E07 error code on State water heaters is not universally defined across all models. Different manufacturers and even different State product lines use E07 to indicate different faults. In some electric tank models, E07 points to a problem with the lower thermistor sensor or the lower heating element. In certain tankless or gas models, E07 may relate to an electrical control circuit fault, such as a solenoid harness or controller output issue. Because State produces both electric and gas water heaters under many model families, you must consult your specific model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm what E07 means for your unit.

Without the exact State model number from the rating plate, a technician will verify the code definition before replacing parts. The code alone is not enough to diagnose the fault. If your model uses E07 for a thermistor or element fault, the lower heating element is the more common failure. If the code relates to a control circuit, wiring and harness faults are typical. Always cross-reference the error code list in your owner's manual before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first. Test the lower thermistor resistance and the lower heating element continuity before replacing any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed lower heating element (~35%)** The lower element burns out or develops heavy scale buildup, causing an open circuit or improper temperature rise that triggers E07 on some electric models.
- **Failed lower thermistor sensor (~30%)** The thermistor drifts out of specification or breaks internally, sending incorrect temperature data to the controller.
- **Damaged or loose wiring harness (~20%)** Corroded pins, broken wires, or loose connectors in the sensor or element circuit interrupt continuity and produce a fault code.
- **Controller or control board output failure (~10%)** The board fails to deliver correct voltage to the harness or component, even when the wiring tests good.
- **Model-specific control circuit fault (~5%)** On some tankless or gas models, E07 may relate to a solenoid circuit or other control function unique to that product line.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off all power** at the circuit breaker and verify zero voltage at the heater terminals with a multimeter.
2. **Record the complete model number** from the rating plate on the water heater and download or request the service manual for that model.
3. **Look up E07** in the manual's error-code table to confirm whether it indicates a thermistor fault, element fault, or control-circuit fault for your specific model.
4. **Remove the access panel** for the lower thermostat and element. Inspect all wire connectors for corrosion, loose pins, or physical damage.
5. **Test the lower thermistor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance with an ohmmeter. Compare the reading to the manufacturer's resistance chart at the current water temperature.
6. **Test the lower heating element** for continuity across its terminals and for ground leakage from each terminal to the tank shell. Replace the element if it shows an open circuit or a short to ground.
7. **Check controller output voltage** at the harness connector if the harness and components test good. If voltage is absent or incorrect, replace the control board. If voltage is present but the component does not respond, replace the component.
8. **Restore power** and monitor the heater. If E07 returns immediately, recheck all connections and verify you replaced the correct part for your model. If the code clears, run hot water and confirm normal heating.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you do not have the service manual for your exact State model, if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt electric circuits, or if you lack an ohmmeter and the manufacturer's resistance charts. A professional will confirm the E07 definition for your model, test the thermistor and element with calibrated meters, and verify controller output voltage before replacing parts. If you replace a sensor or element and the code returns, stop and call for help. The fault may be in the control board or a wiring fault that requires tracing continuity through the entire harness. Gas models or integrated tankless units with E07 codes should always be diagnosed by a technician, because the code may relate to solenoid circuits, ignition systems, or exhaust monitoring that require gas-safe practices and manufacturer-specific training.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350.
