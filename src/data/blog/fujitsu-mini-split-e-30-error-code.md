## Fujitsu E:30 Error Code — What It Means

The E:30 error on Fujitsu mini-splits indicates a heat exchanger (pipe) thermistor error in the indoor unit. The system has detected that the temperature sensor attached to the indoor coil is either open-circuit or short-circuit at power-up. This sensor monitors refrigerant temperature to control cooling and heating cycles, and when the control board cannot read it correctly, the system shuts down and displays the fault.

This code appears on models such as the 30RLXEH and other Fujitsu wall-mounted and ducted indoor units. The fault can stem from a failed sensor itself, loose or broken wiring between the sensor and the control board, or a defective indoor controller PCB that is misreading a healthy sensor. Because the exact behavior and component locations vary by model and year, always confirm the fault code format and consult your specific service manual before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heat exchanger thermistor** The pipe sensor has drifted out of range or broken internally, causing an open or short reading at the control board.
- **Loose or disconnected wiring** The connector at the sensor or at the indoor PCB has backed out, corroded, or been damaged during installation or service.
- **Damaged thermistor harness** The sensor wire has been pinched, cut, or exposed to moisture, creating an intermittent or permanent open circuit.
- **Defective indoor controller PCB** The main control board is misreading a good sensor signal due to a failed input circuit or solder joint.
- **Incorrect indoor unit model or wiring mismatch** The indoor unit does not match the outdoor unit or was wired incorrectly, causing the board to flag a sensor fault at startup.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the entire system** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and wait two minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening the indoor unit cover.
2. **Locate the heat exchanger thermistor** on the indoor coil piping (usually clipped or strapped to the suction or liquid line near the evaporator) and inspect the connector and wiring for corrosion, looseness, or physical damage.
3. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** at the indoor PCB and measure the resistance across the sensor leads using a multimeter set to ohms, then compare the reading to the resistance-temperature table in your model's service manual.
4. **Check continuity in the harness** by measuring from the sensor-side connector pins to the board-side pins with the sensor unplugged to confirm there are no open wires or poor crimps.
5. **Inspect the indoor controller PCB** for burnt traces, bulging capacitors, or corrosion around the thermistor input terminals, and replace the board if the sensor and wiring both test good.
6. **Replace the failed component** (thermistor or PCB), reconnect all wiring, and secure the sensor to the pipe with a new clip or insulation tape to maintain thermal contact.
7. **Restore power and monitor** the system through a full cooling or heating cycle to verify the E:30 code does not return and that the unit operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical connections, if you cannot locate the thermistor on your specific indoor unit, or if the resistance reading does not match any value in the service manual. Professional diagnosis is also recommended when both the sensor and wiring test good but the fault persists, since PCB-level troubleshooting requires refrigerant-side knowledge and proper part identification. If your system is still under warranty, contact Fujitsu or your installing contractor before attempting any repairs to avoid voiding coverage.
