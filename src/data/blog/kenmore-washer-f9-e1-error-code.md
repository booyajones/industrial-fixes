## What this code means
The F9 E1 error code on a Kenmore washer indicates a long drain fault. The machine detected that water did not leave the tub within the expected drain time. On Whirlpool-platform Kenmore washers, this code is treated as a drain system fault rather than a generic electronic failure. The code is triggered when the washer does not see the water level drop as expected during the drain cycle.

This fault points to a blocked or restricted drain path, a failed drain pump, or an installation problem with the drain hose. Less commonly, oversudsing from non-HE detergent or too much detergent can slow draining on these Whirlpool-family washers.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump first when the real culprit is a kinked hose or a hose installed too far down into the standpipe. Always inspect the hose for kinks, blockage, and proper installation depth before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~35%)** A crushed, kinked, or debris-blocked drain hose prevents water from leaving the tub quickly enough.
- **Jammed or failed drain pump (~25%)** Coins, lint, or debris can jam the pump impeller, or the pump motor itself may fail electrically or mechanically.
- **Drain hose installed too far into standpipe (~20%)** Inserting the hose too deep into the standpipe can cause siphoning or slow drainage, triggering the code.
- **Clogged drain path in standpipe or utility sink (~10%)** A blockage downstream in the household drain line restricts flow and causes a long drain condition.
- **Oversudsing from non-HE detergent (~7%)** Excess suds from using too much detergent or the wrong type can slow the drain and confuse the water-level sensor.
- **Drain pump wiring or harness damage (~3%)** Broken wires or loose connectors in the pump circuit can prevent the pump from receiving power during the drain cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and spread towels or place a pan under the work area to catch any remaining water.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** from the washer to the standpipe for kinks, crush points, and blockage; straighten or clear any obstructions.
3. **Verify proper hose installation** by checking that the hose is not inserted more than a few inches into the standpipe (typical standpipe height is 30 to 96 inches).
4. **Access the drain pump and filter area** (usually behind a lower front panel or at the back) and remove any trapped coins, lint, or debris from the pump inlet and filter.
5. **Run a drain test** using the service diagnostic mode (consult your model's tech sheet) to command the pump on and observe whether it hums, moves water, or stalls.
6. **Check for voltage at the drain pump** during the drain command; if voltage is present but the pump does not run correctly, replace the pump.
7. **Test continuity and resistance** of the pump circuit and harness if the pump itself appears intact; repair or replace broken wiring or connectors as needed.
8. **Reassemble the washer** and run a drain-and-spin cycle or automatic test to confirm the fault clears and water drains within the expected time.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable unplugging the washer, accessing internal components, or using a multimeter to test voltage and continuity. A pro is also warranted if you have cleared the hose and filter, verified the pump runs and has voltage, yet the code persists. In that case the fault may involve the main control board or the pressure sensor, which require deeper diagnostics and possible control replacement. If you have already replaced the pump and hose but still see F9 E1, a trained technician can run the full service diagnostic sequence and check the pressure-switch circuit and wiring to isolate the issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
