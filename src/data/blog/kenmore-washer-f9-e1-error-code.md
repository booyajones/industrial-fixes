## Kenmore Washer F9 E1 Error Code — What It Means

On Kenmore washers that use the Whirlpool-derived fault system, F9 E1 indicates a long drain condition. The control detected that water did not drain fast enough within the allowed time window. The tub water level did not drop as expected during the drain phase, triggering a drain timeout fault.

This is not a generic error. It points to a specific drain-related problem: either a restricted drain path, a pump issue, or incorrect drain-hose installation. The washer is telling you it tried to drain but the water level stayed too high for too long.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board or pressure sensor first. Before buying any electrical parts, inspect the drain hose for kinks and clean the drain pump filter to eliminate mechanical blockages.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~35%)** The drain hose is pinched, collapsed, or has lint and debris blocking the interior, preventing water from flowing out of the tub.
- **Drain pump blockage or weak pump (~25%)** The pump impeller is jammed by coins, lint, or small items, or the pump motor is failing and cannot move water at full speed.
- **Drain hose installed incorrectly (~20%)** The hose is shoved too far down into the standpipe, which can cause siphoning or slow draining, or the hose form has been altered in a way that restricts flow.
- **Drain pump filter or trap blockage (~15%)** On models with an accessible filter, lint, hair, and debris accumulate in the filter housing and restrict water flow to the pump.
- **House drain or standpipe restriction (~5%)** The household standpipe or utility sink is slow or clogged, backing up water and preventing the washer from draining in time.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the drain hose visibly kinked, crushed, or collapsed?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Straighten or replace the hose and retest; a kinked hose is the most common cause of F9 E1.<br><strong>No:</strong> Move on to check the drain-hose installation depth at the standpipe and inspect the pump filter.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the drain hose inserted more than about 4-5 inches into the standpipe?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Pull it out to the correct depth per your manual; too-deep insertion can cause siphoning or slow drain.<br><strong>No:</strong> Check whether your model has a drain pump filter and clean it if accessible.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does water drain quickly when you run a rinse-and-spin cycle with no clothes?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The code may be intermittent or related to overload; use smaller loads and retest.<br><strong>No:</strong> The pump itself is likely blocked or weak; access the pump, clear debris, and test or replace it.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and lay down towels to catch any water that spills when you open panels or filters.
2. **Inspect the external drain hose** from the back of the washer to the standpipe or sink for kinks, collapse, or visible blockage.
3. **Verify drain-hose installation** at the standpipe: the hose should not be inserted too far (typically no more than 4-5 inches) and should form a smooth bend without sharp crimps.
4. **Check the household drain** by pouring a bucket of water into the standpipe or sink to confirm it flows freely and does not back up.
5. **Clean the drain pump filter** if your model has an accessible filter door at the front lower panel; remove lint, coins, and debris.
6. **Access the drain pump** by removing the lower front panel or tipping the washer; inspect the pump inlet and impeller for obstructions and check the pump connector for loose or corroded pins.
7. **Run a drain or spin cycle** after clearing any blockage to confirm the tub drains in a normal time and the F9 E1 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the hose, filter, and pump and the code persists, or if you are uncomfortable removing panels and working around water and electrical connectors. A pro can perform electrical continuity and voltage tests at the pump harness, verify the control's drain signal, and diagnose less-common pressure-sensor or wiring faults. Also call if you need to replace the drain pump and prefer not to tip or disassemble the washer yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (hose or pump filter), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 service call including labor and parts.
