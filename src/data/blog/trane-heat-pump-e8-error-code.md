## Trane E8 Error Code — What It Means

The E8 code on a Trane heat pump signals a discharge temperature sensor fault for system B, specifically the T5-B sensor. Trane defines this as the sensor being either open or shorted, which means the control board is not receiving a valid temperature signal from the discharge line. When this happens, the unit will lock out or flag the fault to prevent operation until the sensor circuit is restored.

## Common Causes

- **Failed T5-B thermistor** The discharge temperature sensor itself has failed and is reading open or shorted, which is the primary cause Trane lists for this code.
- **Broken or disconnected sensor wiring** Damaged, cut, or disconnected wires between the sensor and the control board will mimic an open sensor condition.
- **Loose or corroded connector at the sensor or board** A loose pin, corrosion, or poor contact at either the sensor plug or the board connector can interrupt the signal.
- **Shorted sensor circuit** Damaged insulation or pinched wires can create a short to ground or between conductors in the sensor harness.
- **Faulty main control board** If the sensor and wiring both test good, the control board's sensor input circuit may have failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the disconnect or breaker and confirm the fault code is still present after a reset if your model allows manual reset.
2. **Locate the T5-B discharge temperature sensor** on system B's discharge line (consult your unit's service manual for exact position and connector identification).
3. **Inspect the sensor and harness** for loose connectors, broken wires, damaged insulation, or visible corrosion at the pins.
4. **Test the sensor with a multimeter** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance across its terminals (Trane's guidance is to replace if open or shorted, but specific resistance values are not published and vary by model).
5. **Check wiring continuity** from the sensor connector all the way back to the control board to rule out breaks or high resistance in the harness.
6. **Replace the T5-B sensor** if it reads open, shorted, or out of expected range for your model.
7. **Replace the main control board** if the sensor and all wiring test good but the E8 code persists after reassembly and power-on.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable working with live electrical circuits, measuring sensor resistance, or interpreting wiring diagrams, call a licensed HVAC technician. Misdiagnosing sensor faults can lead to unnecessary part replacement or damage to the control board. A professional has the correct resistance and voltage specifications for your exact Trane model, as well as the tools to verify board inputs and rule out refrigerant-side issues that may cause abnormal discharge temperatures.
