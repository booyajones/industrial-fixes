## What this code means
The F31 error code on Whirlpool-built Kenmore electric dryers indicates a power-supply fault, specifically a low or missing L2 leg of the incoming 240-volt supply. The control has detected that the heater is not receiving proper voltage from one of the two hot legs feeding the dryer. This is not a venting, airflow, or temperature-sensor code. It is strictly an electrical supply issue.

Electric dryers require a full 240-volt supply from two separate hot legs (L1 and L2) on a dedicated 30-amp circuit. When the L2 leg is absent, too low, or not reaching the heater circuit properly, the control posts F31. The dryer may still tumble (since the motor can run on 120 volts from L1 alone), but it will not heat. Common triggers include one tripped breaker in a two-pole circuit, a loose or miswired power-cord connection at the terminal block, a damaged cord or outlet, or an upstream wiring fault in the home.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the heating element or thermal fuse when the dryer won't heat, but F31 explicitly points to a power-supply problem. Before buying any parts, verify full 240-volt supply at the outlet and the dryer terminal block with a multimeter.

## Common Causes

- **One tripped breaker in the two-pole dryer circuit (~50%)** Electric dryers use a pair of breakers feeding both L1 and L2, and if only one trips the control sees low L2 voltage and posts F31.
- **Loose or corroded terminal-block connections (~25%)** The power cord attaches to a terminal block inside the dryer, and loose screws or heat-damaged terminals can interrupt the L2 leg.
- **Damaged power cord or poor cord-to-outlet fit (~15%)** A frayed cord, bent prongs, or a loose receptacle can drop voltage on one leg, especially if the cord was recently replaced or the dryer was moved.
- **Faulty wall receptacle or house wiring (~8%)** An aging outlet, backstabbed connections, or a loose wire in the breaker panel can cause intermittent loss of the L2 supply.
- **Internal control-board power-input fault (~2%)** If the supply is verified good but F31 persists, the control board's voltage-sensing circuit or relay may be failing, though this is less common.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker panel.** Locate the two-pole breaker feeding the dryer circuit (usually labeled 30 amps) and check that both toggles are fully in the ON position. If one is tripped (middle position), turn both OFF then back ON together.
2. **Unplug the dryer power cord** from the wall outlet. Inspect the cord and the outlet for any visible burn marks, melted plastic, bent prongs, or loose fit. Replace the cord or have an electrician replace the outlet if damage is present.
3. **Remove the dryer's rear access panel** to expose the terminal block where the power cord connects. Check that all terminal screws (typically three or four, depending on three-wire or four-wire cord) are tight and that no wires show signs of overheating or corrosion.
4. **Measure voltage at the wall outlet** using a multimeter set to AC volts. With the breakers on and the dryer unplugged, measure hot-to-hot (should be approximately 240 volts) and each hot-to-neutral (each approximately 120 volts). If readings are low or zero on one leg, the problem is in the house wiring or panel.
5. **Reconnect the dryer and measure voltage at the terminal block** with the dryer plugged in but still powered off at the control. Confirm full 240 volts reaches the dryer. If voltage is good at the outlet but low at the terminal block, inspect the cord for internal breaks or poor crimps.
6. **Clear the error code** by pressing and holding the control's cancel or reset button (consult your model's manual), or by unplugging the dryer for one minute. Restart a heat cycle and observe whether F31 returns.
7. **If voltage is verified good and F31 persists,** contact a qualified appliance technician to inspect the control board's power-input circuitry and internal wiring harness for faults in the L2 path.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt circuits, if you cannot confidently use a multimeter to measure line voltage, or if your voltage checks reveal low or missing supply at the outlet (which requires an electrician to trace house wiring and the breaker panel). Also call a pro if the dryer supply measures good but F31 continues to appear, since that points to an internal control-board or wiring-harness fault that requires disassembly and component-level diagnosis. Any work inside the breaker panel or behind the wall outlet must be performed by a licensed electrician.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (power cord if damaged), 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for electrician or appliance tech service call and diagnosis.
