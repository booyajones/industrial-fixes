## Bosch Dishwasher E03 Error Code — What It Means

The E03 code on Bosch dishwashers does not have a single universal meaning across all models. Most commonly it indicates a control board relay failure, either in the main power module or the auxiliary heater relay circuit. Some Bosch models instead use E03 to flag a water inlet or fill problem, such as a faulty inlet valve or restricted water supply. Because the code interpretation varies by model, always confirm your exact model number and consult the wiring diagram or service manual before replacing parts.

When E03 appears, the dishwasher typically stops mid-cycle or refuses to start because the control board cannot energize the relay that powers the heater or other components. On models where E03 relates to water fill, the machine may fail to fill properly or show low water pressure symptoms. The safest diagnostic approach is to verify both the control circuit and the fill system before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the heating element or inlet valve first without testing the control board relay. Always perform a power reset and check for visible relay damage or burn marks on the main board before ordering other parts.

## Common Causes

- **Main control board relay failure (~55%)** The relay on the control board that switches power to the heater or other load has failed and cannot complete the circuit.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~25%)** On certain Bosch models, E03 signals that the inlet valve is not opening or is electrically open, preventing proper fill.
- **Restricted water supply (~12%)** Kinked hose, closed shutoff valve, clogged inlet screen, or low household water pressure can trigger E03 on models that interpret it as a fill fault.
- **Power module fault (~8%)** The integrated power module on the main control board has a component failure unrelated to the relay alone, requiring board replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the circuit breaker and wait at least 30 seconds to reset the control board.
2. **Restore power** and run a test cycle to see if E03 returns. If the code is gone, monitor the next few cycles for recurrence.
3. **Check the water supply** by verifying the shutoff valve is fully open, inspecting the inlet hose for kinks or damage, and cleaning the inlet screen or filter at the valve connection.
4. **Test the inlet valve** by disconnecting power, removing the valve, and using a multimeter to check the solenoid coil for continuity (consult your model's wiring diagram for the correct resistance range).
5. **Inspect the main control board** for visible damage, burn marks, or a stuck relay. Remove the lower access panel or control panel cover to view the board. Look for discoloration near the relay contacts or auxiliary heater relay.
6. **Replace the control board** if the relay is visibly damaged or if all other checks pass but the E03 code persists. Order the correct board using your exact model number.
7. **Reassemble and test** by reconnecting all harnesses, restoring power, and running a full wash cycle to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical components or removing the control panel to access the main board. Also call for help if the E03 code returns after replacing both the control board and the inlet valve, since that points to a wiring fault, faulty pressure sensor, or a model-specific component not covered in general troubleshooting guides. If your dishwasher is under warranty, contact Bosch service directly before opening the machine to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150-300 for control board, 45-90 min; $40-80 for inlet valve, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $200-450 including labor and part.
