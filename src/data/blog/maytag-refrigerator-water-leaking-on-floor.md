## Maytag Refrigerator Water Leaking on Floor — What's Happening

Water on the floor under a Maytag refrigerator is not an error code. It is a symptom that typically points to a blockage in the defrost system or a leak in the fresh-water supply. When the defrost drain in the freezer becomes clogged with ice or debris, meltwater from the evaporator coil backs up and overflows into the cabinet, eventually reaching the floor. If the leak comes from the water supply side, you will see water tracking from connections at the back of the unit, the filter housing, or the household supply valve.

Maytag's own troubleshooting for bottom-freezer models tells you to check the household water supply line, all fittings and connections, the saddle or shut-off valve, the water filter and its O-rings, and the filter housing for cracks or deterioration from water impurities. The filter should be replaced every six months or as indicated. Because the symptom can stem from either the defrost system or the fresh-water system, you need to trace the water source before replacing parts.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged or frozen defrost drain** Ice or debris blocks the drain hole under the evaporator coil, so meltwater backs up into the freezer and cabinet and spills onto the floor.
- **Damaged water filter, missing O-rings, or cracked filter housing** A cracked filter body, missing or worn O-ring seals, or a deteriorated housing allows water to drip from the filter area onto the floor.
- **Loose, cracked, or damaged household water supply line** Kinks, splits, or loose push-fit connections in the supply tubing leak water at the back of the refrigerator or under the unit.
- **Leaking shut-off or saddle valve** The household shut-off valve or saddle valve connection at the cold-water pipe leaks at the threads or seat.
- **Blocked or clogged drain tube or check valve** Food particles or mineral deposits restrict the drain tube between the evaporator and the drain pan, preventing meltwater from reaching the pan.
- **Cracked or overflowing drain pan** A split in the drain pan under the refrigerator lets condensate drip directly onto the floor, or the pan overflows if water does not evaporate normally.
- **Ice-maker water line or inlet-valve leak** Tubing or fittings that feed the ice maker develop cracks or come loose, dripping water inside the cabinet or at the rear connections.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the refrigerator and determine whether water is coming from the fresh-water system or the defrost system by checking for wet tracks inside the cabinet, water under crispers, or pooling near the back wall.
2. Inspect the household water supply line, all fittings at the shut-off valve, and the connection at the rear of the refrigerator for cracks, kinks, or loose push-fit couplings.
3. Remove the water filter and examine it for cracks, verify both O-rings are present and intact, and inspect the filter housing for splits or deterioration from mineral buildup.
4. Remove the rear freezer evaporator cover panel to access the drain hole under the evaporator coil, clear any visible debris or ice, and thaw the area with controlled heat if frozen.
5. Flush the defrost drain with hot water from a turkey baster or squeeze bottle until water flows freely into the drain pan under the refrigerator.
6. Pull the refrigerator forward and remove the drain pan from underneath, then inspect the pan for cracks and check that it is not overflowing.
7. If equipped with a drain tube or check valve between the evaporator and the pan, disconnect and clean or replace the tube if clogged with debris or mineral deposits.
8. Replace the water filter every six months or as indicated by the filter-change light, and confirm all supply fittings are fully seated and hand-tight before restoring power.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you cannot access the evaporator cover panel or drain area safely, if flushing the drain does not stop water from pooling inside the cabinet, or if you see active leaks at the inlet valve or internal tubing that require refrigerator disassembly. A technician can also diagnose less common causes such as a failing evaporator fan that alters frost patterns or a restricted capillary tube in the defrost system.
