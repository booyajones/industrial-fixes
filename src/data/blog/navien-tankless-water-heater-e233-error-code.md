## Navien Tankless Water Heater E233 Error — What It Means

The E233 error on a Navien tankless water heater signals that the exhaust temperature has exceeded safe limits or the exhaust temperature sensor has detected an abnormal condition. This code is a safety lockout designed to prevent damage to the unit and protect against potential carbon monoxide issues. The heater will typically shut down until the problem is resolved and the error is cleared.

Because exhaust temperatures directly relate to combustion and venting, this code often points to blocked or restricted venting, a failed exhaust temperature sensor, or improper combustion due to gas pressure or air supply problems. The exact threshold and sensor specifications vary by model, so consult your owner's manual for detailed troubleshooting steps specific to your unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust temperature sensor without first inspecting the venting system. Check all vent pipes for blockages, debris, or bird nests before ordering any parts, as restricted airflow is the usual culprit.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~45%)** Debris, bird nests, ice, or improper vent pipe installation restricts exhaust flow and causes overheating.
- **Failed exhaust temperature sensor (~25%)** The sensor may give false high readings or fail internally, triggering the code even when exhaust temperature is normal.
- **Improper gas pressure or combustion (~15%)** Low or high gas pressure, dirty burner, or insufficient combustion air can cause incomplete combustion and elevated exhaust temperatures.
- **Heat exchanger blockage or scaling (~10%)** Mineral buildup or soot inside the heat exchanger restricts flow and raises exhaust temperatures.
- **Faulty control board or wiring (~5%)** A damaged wire, corroded connector, or failing control board may misread the sensor signal and throw the code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the water heater at the service switches and shut-off valve.
2. **Inspect the exhaust vent termination** outside for blockages such as bird nests, leaves, ice, or snow. Remove any obstructions you find.
3. **Check all vent pipe sections** inside and out for proper connection, correct slope, and no crushing or kinking. Consult the installation manual for required vent length and clearances.
4. **Restart the unit** and observe whether the E233 code returns. If it clears, the issue was likely a temporary blockage.
5. **Test the exhaust temperature sensor** with a multimeter for proper resistance at room temperature, or swap it with a known-good replacement if available.
6. **Inspect the heat exchanger** for soot or scale buildup. If you see heavy deposits, arrange for a professional descaling and combustion tune-up.
7. **Check gas supply pressure** with a manometer to confirm it matches the specifications in your model's manual. Adjust the regulator if needed or call your gas utility if pressure is off.
8. **Clear the error code** using the unit's control panel or by cycling power, then monitor for recurrence over the next few days.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the venting system requires modification or repair, or if the error persists after clearing blockages and restarting. A professional can perform a combustion analysis, measure gas pressure, descale the heat exchanger, and safely test or replace the exhaust temperature sensor. Because this code involves combustion safety and potential carbon monoxide risks, professional diagnosis is the safest choice when the cause is not immediately obvious.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
