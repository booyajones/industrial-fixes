## Samsung Dryer t0 Error Code — What It Means

Samsung groups the t0 (or tO) error code in the temperature-error family. According to Samsung's support page, t0 can indicate that a firmware download to the dryer is in progress on certain models. In practice, however, field technicians and repair videos commonly identify t0 as a thermistor or temperature-sensor fault, and in some cases specifically an open thermistor condition. Because the exact meaning depends on your model and how the code is displayed, the safest approach is to treat t0 as a temperature-sensing or temperature-control fault until you confirm the specific behavior for your dryer.

The most common field repair for t0 is a failed thermistor that reads out of range or shows an open circuit. Poor airflow from a clogged lint screen or vent system can also trigger temperature-related codes by trapping heat. Power and installation issues, door-latch faults, or stuck control-panel buttons can confuse the dryer's logic and cause the error to persist. Start with a power cycle and airflow inspection, then move to thermistor measurement if the code returns.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or out-of-range thermistor** The temperature sensor reads open, shorted, or far outside the normal range (around 10 kΩ at room temperature), preventing the dryer from regulating heat correctly.
- **Clogged lint screen or exhaust vent** Restricted airflow traps heat inside the drum, causing the temperature sensor to report abnormal readings and trigger the error.
- **Power supply or installation fault** The dryer is on a shared circuit, connected via an extension cord, or an electric model has incorrect voltage or an improperly installed power cord.
- **Door latch or control-panel issue** A door that does not close or latch fully, or a stuck button on the control panel, can confuse the dryer's logic and cause persistent error codes.
- **Firmware download in progress** On certain models, t0 specifically indicates that the dryer is receiving a firmware update and the display will clear when the download completes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the dryer** by turning it off, unplugging the cord or switching off the breaker, waiting 60 seconds, and restoring power to clear transient faults.
2. **Verify the power supply and installation** by confirming the dryer is on its own dedicated circuit, not plugged into an extension cord, and that an electric model has the correct voltage and a properly installed power cord.
3. **Clean the lint filter and exhaust vent** by removing all lint from the screen, disconnecting the vent duct, and clearing any clogs or buildup in the vent system that can trap heat.
4. **Check the door and control panel** by making sure the door closes and latches fully with nothing trapped in the seal, and that no buttons on the control panel are stuck or jammed.
5. **Measure the thermistor resistance** by removing the access panel (often the lower or rear panel), locating the thermistor on the blower housing or duct, disconnecting the wires, and using a multimeter to check for roughly 10,000 ohms at room temperature.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the reading is open (infinite resistance), shorted (near zero), or far outside the normal range, then reconnect all wiring and reassemble the panel.
7. **Run a test cycle with a load** so the dryer can sense moisture and temperature normally, and monitor for the code to confirm the repair resolved the fault.
8. **Contact Samsung service** if the code returns after reset, airflow cleaning, and thermistor replacement, as model-specific diagnosis or a control-board fault may be needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the t0 code returns after you have power-cycled the dryer, cleaned all airflow paths, verified the power supply, and replaced the thermistor with a known-good part. Persistent codes can indicate a wiring harness fault, a failing main control board, or a model-specific firmware or sensor-calibration issue that requires diagnostic software and Samsung service training. Also call for help if you are not comfortable working with electrical components, removing panels, or using a multimeter to measure resistance. For electric dryers on 240-volt circuits, any work beyond cleaning the vent and resetting the machine should be handled by a qualified appliance technician to avoid shock hazards.
