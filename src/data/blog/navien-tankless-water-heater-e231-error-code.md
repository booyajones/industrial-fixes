## Navien Tankless Water Heater E231 Error — What It Means

The E231 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a flame detection issue. The unit has failed to maintain a stable flame after ignition or cannot sense the flame reliably. This typically occurs when the flame rod cannot confirm combustion is happening properly, or when the burner loses flame unexpectedly during operation.

The error is a safety feature that prevents gas from flowing without proper combustion. Common triggers include gas supply interruptions, dirty or fouled flame sensors, igniter problems, or venting issues that affect combustion stability. The unit will lock out and require a reset after addressing the underlying cause.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter assembly when the real issue is a dirty flame rod that needs cleaning or a gas supply problem. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and verify full gas valve opening before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame rod prevent it from properly sensing the flame, causing false flame-loss signals.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an empty propane tank prevents stable combustion and triggers flame loss.
- **Failed igniter (~15%)** A weak or cracked igniter cannot reliably light the burner, leading to repeated ignition failures and flame-loss detection.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructed exhaust or intake vents disrupt the air-fuel ratio and cause combustion instability that the flame sensor detects.
- **Faulty flame rod cable or connection (~5%)** Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring between the flame rod and control board interrupts the flame signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the mounting screws to access the burner chamber.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner assembly, and inspect it for carbon buildup, corrosion, or cracks.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a wire brush, removing all deposits without bending the rod, then wipe it clean with a dry cloth.
5. **Inspect the igniter** for cracks or white discoloration and check that the gap between igniter and burner is consistent, adjusting if necessary per the service manual.
6. **Check the flame rod cable** for secure connections at both the rod and the control board, looking for corrosion or damage.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and initiate a test heat cycle by opening a hot-water tap and observing whether ignition occurs and the flame remains stable.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances or if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and verifying gas supply. Tankless water heaters require precise combustion tuning, and incorrect gas pressure or venting can create safety hazards. A pro will use a manometer to measure gas pressure, test flame current with a microamp meter, and inspect the heat exchanger and venting system for blockages. Any work involving the gas valve, pressure regulation, or sealed combustion components should be left to qualified service personnel.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
