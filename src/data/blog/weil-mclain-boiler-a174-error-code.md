## Weil-McLain A174 Error Code — What It Means

A174 on a Weil-McLain boiler is a lockout or fault-history code, not a single universal part failure. The exact meaning depends on your boiler model and control generation. Weil-McLain directs technicians to retrieve the fault name from the boiler's diagnostics, errors, past errors, or lockout history screens and then follow the manual-specific corrective steps for that fault. The code itself is an indexed entry that must be decoded by model and control menu.

Because the code is not uniquely defined across all Weil-McLain models, the real repair starts by identifying the actual fault category tied to A174 in your unit's history. Common fault families that trigger lockout entries include flame-sensing or ignition problems, pressure or low-water issues, temperature-sensor faults, gas-train or combustion issues, and circulation problems. You must check your specific boiler's manual and diagnostics to confirm the root cause before attempting repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame sensor or gas valve without checking the boiler's fault history menu first. Enter the diagnostics screen to read the actual fault name tied to A174, then test the specific component indicated by that fault before buying parts.

## Common Causes

- **Flame-sensing or ignition fault (~30%)** Dirty or misaligned flame sensor, weak gas flow, dirty burner or pilot, or ignition failure triggers a lockout that may be logged as A174 in the fault history.
- **Low system pressure or leak (~25%)** System pressure below normal (typically below 12 psi), a leak in the piping, or a pressure-related safety trip causes the boiler to lock out and log a fault.
- **Temperature-sensor fault (~20%)** Failed thermistor, low-water-cutoff sensor reading falsely, or loose sensor wiring prevents the boiler from verifying safe operating conditions.
- **Gas train or combustion issue (~15%)** Faulty gas valve, pilot valve sticking, or combustion sequence error stops the burner from firing correctly and triggers a lockout.
- **Circulation problem (~10%)** Bad circulator pump, closed system valves, or air trapped in the system causes abnormal operation and a fault log entry.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Enter the diagnostics menu** on your boiler's control panel and navigate to Errors, Past Errors, or Lockout History to retrieve the exact fault name tied to A174.
2. **Consult your boiler's manual** for the model-specific troubleshooting tree and corrective steps for that fault name.
3. **Verify basic operating conditions** including call for heat, power, safeties, gas supply, valve position, system pressure (refill to about 15 psi if below 12 psi), and visible leaks.
4. **Inspect and test the component** indicated by the fault category. For flame-related faults, clean or replace the flame sensor and inspect burner and ignition components. For pressure or heat-sense faults, test thermistor resistance and wiring. For circulation faults, verify circulator operation, open valves, and purge air. For gas-train faults, test the gas valve and pilot valve per the manual.
5. **Repair the root cause** by cleaning or replacing the failed sensor, thermistor, low-water cutoff, gas valve, circulator, or by correcting water pressure, leaks, trapped air, or blocked venting.
6. **Reset the boiler** only after the underlying issue is corrected, then run the boiler through a full firing cycle and confirm stable operation with no repeat lockout.
7. **Monitor the boiler** for at least one full heating cycle to verify the fault does not return and the boiler maintains normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician for A174 diagnosis and repair. The code itself is a history index, not a definitive fault, so proper diagnosis requires navigating the control menu, interpreting model-specific fault tables, and testing gas-train components, flame-sensing circuits, and safety devices with calibrated tools. Gas valve, ignition, and combustion repairs involve working with natural gas or propane and must comply with local codes. Temperature sensors and thermistors require resistance testing and wiring checks that are easy to misinterpret without a manual and multimeter. If you lack experience with hydronic heating systems, gas appliances, or electronic controls, professional service will be faster, safer, and more reliable than trial-and-error part replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
