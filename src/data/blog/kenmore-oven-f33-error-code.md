## Kenmore Oven F33 Error Code — What It Means

The F33 code on a Kenmore oven (often displayed as F3 on Kenmore/Frigidaire-built models) indicates an open circuit in the oven temperature sensor system. The control board cannot read the RTD (resistance temperature detector) probe, which means the sensor itself, the wiring between the sensor and control, or the control board has failed. The oven will not heat or regulate temperature until this fault is cleared.

## Common Causes

- **Failed RTD temperature sensor probe** The oven temperature sensor has gone open-circuit or drifted far out of its normal 1,000 to 1,100 ohm resistance range at room temperature.
- **Broken or corroded sensor wiring** The two-wire harness running from the sensor to the control board has a break, corrosion, or poor connection that interrupts continuity.
- **Loose or damaged connector at the control board** The plug where the sensor wires attach to the electronic control has worked loose, corroded, or has a damaged pin.
- **Defective electronic oven control board** When the sensor and wiring both test good, the control board's sensor-input circuit has failed and the board must be replaced.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect electrical power** to the range at the circuit breaker or by unplugging the unit to make sure safe work.
2. **Access the rear or top** of the range to locate the oven temperature sensor, usually a thin metal probe inserted into the oven cavity with two wires leading to the control board.
3. **Measure the sensor resistance** by disconnecting the two-wire plug and using a multimeter set to ohms across the sensor terminals. A good probe reads approximately 1,000 to 1,100 ohms at room temperature.
4. **Check wiring continuity** from the sensor plug all the way to the control board connector if the sensor resistance is in range. Look for breaks, burnt insulation, or loose terminals.
5. **Inspect the control-board connector** for corrosion, bent pins, or a loose fit and clean or reseat it as needed.
6. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if its resistance is out of range or open. If the sensor and wiring both pass tests, replace the electronic oven control board.
7. **Restore power and test** the oven by running a bake cycle to confirm the error code is gone and temperature regulation is normal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt appliances, cannot safely access the back of the range, or do not own a multimeter to test resistance and continuity. A qualified appliance technician can diagnose the fault quickly, has the correct replacement parts on the truck, and will verify that the new sensor or control board solves the problem before leaving. If your oven is still under warranty or you have a service contract, always call for authorized repair to preserve coverage.
