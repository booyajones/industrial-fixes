## LG Oven F2 Error Code — What It Means

LG defines F2 as an upper oven thermistor error. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that tells the control board the current oven temperature. When the control sees a signal outside the expected range (open circuit, short, or out-of-spec resistance), it throws F2 and stops the upper oven from heating. LG recommends pressing CLEAR/OFF or power cycling the range. If the code returns, turn off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds and restore power. If F2 persists after these resets, the issue is real hardware and you need to diagnose the sensor circuit or schedule service.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the control board first because F2 sounds like an electronic fault. Before spending on a board, disconnect the sensor and measure its resistance. A bad sensor costs far less than a control and is much easier to swap.

## Common Causes

- **Defective thermistor (~60%)** The upper oven temperature sensor drifts out of range, reads open, or shorts internally, and the control rejects the signal.
- **Loose or corroded connector (~20%)** The two-pin plug at the sensor or at the control board works loose, or terminals corrode and create an intermittent or high-resistance connection.
- **Damaged wiring (~10%)** The sensor harness is pinched, melted by radiant heat near the cavity, or chafed against sheet metal, causing an open or short to ground.
- **Control board circuit failure (~10%)** The thermistor and wiring test normal but the control board's sensor input circuit is faulty, so it misreads a good sensor as an error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press CLEAR/OFF** or power the range off and back on. If F2 disappears and does not return, the fault may have been a transient glitch. Monitor the oven over the next few uses.
2. **Turn off the circuit breaker** to the range for 30 seconds, then restore power. This hard reset can clear a stuck error if the sensor circuit is actually healthy.
3. **If F2 returns immediately**, disconnect power at the breaker again and leave it off. Pull the range forward or remove the rear access panel to reach the upper oven cavity.
4. **Locate the thermistor** on the back wall of the upper oven (a small metal probe with two wires). Unplug the connector and inspect both the sensor terminals and the harness plug for corrosion, soot, or melted plastic.
5. **Measure the thermistor resistance** with a multimeter. At room temperature (around 70 °F) a healthy LG oven sensor typically reads about 1080-1100 ohms. If the reading is open (infinite), near zero, or wildly different, replace the sensor.
6. **Check the wiring harness** from the sensor to the control board for cuts, pinches, or chafe marks. Use the meter to verify continuity in each wire and confirm neither wire shorts to the oven frame.
7. **Install the new thermistor** if the old one tested bad, route the wires away from hot surfaces, plug in the connector, and push the range back. Restore power, clear any lingering code, and run a test preheat to verify normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working inside a 240 V appliance, if the sensor and wiring both test normal but F2 keeps coming back (pointing to a control board fault), or if your range is still under warranty. LG recommends scheduling service when the code persists after the power-cycle steps. A qualified tech can verify signal voltages at the control board, confirm the thermistor curve against factory specs, and replace the main control if the sensor input circuit has failed. Also call for help if you find evidence of arcing, melted insulation, or other damage that suggests a bigger electrical problem.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-60 for the thermistor, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for diagnosis and sensor replacement.
