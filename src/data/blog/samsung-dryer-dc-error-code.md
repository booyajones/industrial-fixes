## Samsung Dryer dC Error Code — What It Means

The dC code on a Samsung dryer tells you the control board is not detecting the door as properly closed or locked. Even if the door looks shut, the dryer thinks it is open or the latch circuit is not satisfied. The unit will refuse to start or will stop mid-cycle because the safety interlock requires a good door-closed signal before the motor and heater can run.

In practice Samsung documents this as a door switch or door circuit problem. The fault can be mechanical (a latch not seating, a hinge out of alignment, or lint blocking the strike) or electrical (a failed door switch, broken wire, or corroded connector). Either way the dryer sees an open door condition and displays dC to protect against running with the drum accessible.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board thinking the error is a logic fault. In fact the door switch or latch hardware is almost always the real culprit. Test the door switch for continuity with a multimeter before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Lint or debris in the latch or strike (~35%)** Built-up lint, small garments, or threads block the latch from seating fully so the switch never closes.
- **Garment caught in the door seal (~25%)** A sock, sleeve, or towel edge is pinched between the door gasket and cabinet, holding the door slightly open.
- **Failed door switch or sensor (~20%)** The switch contacts are worn, corroded, or mechanically broken so continuity is never made even when the latch is engaged.
- **Worn or damaged door latch or strike (~10%)** Plastic latch fingers are cracked, the strike is bent, or the latch spring has weakened so engagement is incomplete.
- **Loose or misaligned door hinges (~7%)** Hinge screws have backed out or the hinge is bent, causing the door to sit crooked and the latch to miss the strike.
- **Wiring fault or connector issue (~3%)** A broken wire, corroded spade terminal, or loose harness plug in the door switch circuit creates an intermittent open.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet to remove power before touching any door hardware or switch components.
2. **Open the door and inspect the seal** closely for any garment, thread, or lint trapped between the gasket and the cabinet; pull out anything you find.
3. **Clean the latch and strike** thoroughly using a vacuum crevice tool and a damp cloth to remove lint, fabric softener residue, and dust from both the door-mounted latch and the cabinet strike plate.
4. **Close the door firmly** and listen for a distinct click; visually confirm that the plastic latch fingers engage the strike completely and that the door sits flush against the cabinet.
5. **Check hinge tightness and alignment** by wiggling the door; tighten any loose hinge screws and verify the door does not sag or twist when closed.
6. **Test the door switch** by unplugging the switch connector (typically behind the front panel or inside the door frame), setting a multimeter to continuity, and checking across the switch terminals with the latch manually pressed; you should see continuity (zero ohms) when closed and open circuit when released.
7. **Replace the door switch or latch assembly** if testing shows no continuity when engaged, or if the latch is visibly cracked or the strike is bent beyond adjustment; consult your model's service manual for the correct part number and mounting hardware.
8. **Reconnect all wiring**, plug the dryer back in, and run a short test cycle to confirm the dC code does not reappear and the drum starts normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have tested the door switch and found good continuity, cleaned and aligned all latch hardware, and the dC code still appears. A pro can trace the wiring harness from the door switch back to the main control board, check for intermittent connector faults, and verify that the control board itself is correctly reading the switch signal. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable removing the front or top panel to access the switch, or if your model uses an electronic door lock assembly that requires special diagnostic mode entry to test.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220.
