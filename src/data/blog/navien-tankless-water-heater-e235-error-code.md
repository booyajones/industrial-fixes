## Navien E235 Error Code — What It Means

The E235 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a problem with the exhaust or venting system. The unit has detected an issue that prevents proper venting of combustion gases, which triggers a safety shutdown to prevent carbon monoxide buildup indoors.

This code typically appears when the air pressure switch detects abnormal pressure in the exhaust path, when the vent pipes are blocked, or when the venting configuration does not meet the manufacturer's installation requirements. The heater will not operate until the venting issue is resolved and the error is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the air pressure switch thinking it has failed, when the real problem is a blocked vent or improper venting configuration. Always inspect the entire vent run and test for blockages before replacing any sensors.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked exhaust vent (~40%)** Bird nests, ice, debris, or condensate buildup inside the vent pipe restricts airflow and triggers the pressure switch.
- **Improper vent installation (~25%)** Vent pipes that are too long, have too many elbows, lack proper slope, or use incorrect materials create back pressure.
- **Failed air pressure switch (~15%)** The sensor that monitors exhaust pressure can fail or drift out of calibration, reporting false blockage conditions.
- **Disconnected or cracked vent pipe (~10%)** A separated joint or crack in the vent system allows combustion gases to leak and disrupts the pressure balance.
- **Wind-induced backdraft (~7%)** Strong winds at the exterior termination can push air back into the exhaust system if the vent cap or location is inadequate.
- **Faulty venturi or exhaust fan (~3%)** The exhaust blower or venturi assembly may be obstructed, damaged, or running at reduced speed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Inspect the exterior vent termination** for any visible obstructions such as ice, bird nests, leaves, or snow buildup and clear them away.
3. **Check all accessible vent pipe joints** inside and outside the home for separation, cracks, or gaps and reseal or reconnect as needed.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the unit and locate the air pressure switch and its attached sensing tubes. Look for loose, kinked, or disconnected tubing.
5. **Inspect the exhaust blower** housing for debris or obstructions and verify the blower spins freely by hand when power is off.
6. **Measure or review the vent run** to confirm total length, number of elbows, and slope meet the specifications in your model's installation manual.
7. **Reset the error code** by cycling power to the unit, then attempt to fire the heater and observe whether the error returns or clears permanently.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you find no obvious blockages in the vent pipes but the error persists, if the venting configuration appears incorrect or overly complex, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. A professional can perform a combustion analysis, measure actual exhaust pressures, verify compliance with local codes, and safely diagnose sensor or blower failures. Any work that involves extending or reconfiguring the vent system should be done by a qualified installer to prevent carbon monoxide hazards and make sure warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
