## What this code means
The EA1 code on an Electrolux dryer indicates a power supply issue. The dryer's control board is detecting that the frequency of incoming power is outside the expected range, which should be 60 Hz on North American models. This is not a component failure inside the drum or vent system. It is a signal that the dryer cannot safely operate because the electricity it is receiving does not match specification.

The code can be triggered by problems with your home's utility power, incorrect installation or wiring at the terminal block, poor connections, or a faulty control board that is incorrectly reading good power. Because the fault points to the supply side first, always verify the power source and wiring before replacing internal electronics.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board immediately, but the code usually points to incoming power quality or terminal-block wiring. Verify supply frequency and connections with a meter before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Utility power frequency out of specification (~35%)** Your home's incoming power may be delivering voltage at an abnormal frequency or with unstable quality that the dryer control rejects.
- **Improper installation or terminal-block wiring (~30%)** Incorrect connections at the dryer's terminal block, loose wires, or the wrong supply configuration can cause the control to see abnormal power characteristics.
- **Faulty main control or electronic board (~20%)** If the incoming power and wiring are confirmed correct, the control board itself may be defective and falsely reporting EA1.
- **Extension cord or marginal outlet connection (~15%)** Running the dryer through an extension cord or a worn outlet can create voltage drop and power-quality problems that register as a frequency fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker and confirm the dryer is off.
2. **Verify the dryer is plugged into the correct outlet** for its model and is not using an extension cord or adapter, which can cause power-quality faults.
3. **Inspect the terminal block** at the rear of the dryer where the power cord connects, checking for loose, burned, or incorrectly installed wires.
4. **Check incoming power with a multimeter** capable of measuring frequency to confirm the supply is stable and at 60 Hz, or have an electrician perform this test if you lack the equipment.
5. **Power-cycle the dryer** by turning the breaker off for two minutes, then back on, and check if the code clears after verifying the supply and wiring.
6. **Replace the main control board** if the power supply and terminal-block wiring are confirmed correct and the EA1 code persists.
7. **Test the dryer** on a normal cycle after repair to confirm the code does not return and operation is normal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you lack a meter to verify incoming power frequency, or if the code persists after you have checked the basics. Because EA1 points to power-supply issues, an electrician may be needed first to rule out problems with your home's utility service or panel before a dryer technician replaces the control board. High-voltage work at the terminal block and control-board replacement require care and proper safety lockout. A qualified appliance technician will test the supply with calibrated equipment, inspect all wiring, and replace the board only after confirming the fault is not installation-related.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on whether the fix is wiring or board replacement.
