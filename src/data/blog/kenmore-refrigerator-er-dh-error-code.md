## What this code means
Er dH is a defrost failure code on Kenmore refrigerators that use an LG-style control system. It appears when the defrost sensor does not detect the expected temperature (around 46°F or 8°C) within the normal defrost cycle window. This means frost is building up on the evaporator coils and blocking airflow, which causes poor cooling and ice buildup in the freezer compartment.

The control board expects the evaporator to warm up during a defrost cycle. When the defrost heater fails to raise the temperature, the board logs the error. Common triggers include a burned-out defrost heater, an open thermal fuse in the defrost circuit, a failed defrost thermistor, or a control board that is not energizing the heater. Heavy ice accumulation or a blocked drain can also prevent normal temperature recovery after defrost starts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when they see this code. Test the defrost heater, thermal fuse, and thermistor for continuity before buying a new board, since those three components fail far more often.

## Common Causes

- **Open or bad defrost heater (~40%)** The defrost heater element burns out or develops an open circuit, so it cannot warm the evaporator coils during the defrost cycle.
- **Open thermal fuse or temperature fuse (~30%)** The thermal fuse in the defrost circuit has blown due to overheating or age, cutting power to the heater even when the control board calls for defrost.
- **Failed defrost sensor or thermistor (~20%)** The defrost thermistor on the evaporator has drifted out of range or failed open, so the control board cannot read the correct temperature and logs a fault.
- **Main control board not energizing the heater (~7%)** The control board relay or output circuit has failed and does not send voltage to the defrost heater during the scheduled defrost cycle.
- **Severe frost buildup or blocked drain (~3%)** Heavy ice on the evaporator or a clogged drain hole prevents normal temperature recovery after defrost starts, even if the heater is working.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** and remove all items from the freezer compartment.
2. **Remove the freezer rear panel or evaporator cover** to expose the evaporator coils, defrost heater, and sensor assembly.
3. **Inspect for frost buildup** and manually defrost the evaporator if heavy ice is present (use a hair dryer on low or let the unit sit unplugged for 24 hours).
4. **Disconnect the defrost heater leads** and test the heater element for continuity with a multimeter. If the reading is open (infinite resistance), replace the heater.
5. **Test the thermal fuse** in the defrost circuit for continuity. If it shows no continuity, replace the defrost sensor or controller assembly that includes the fuse.
6. **Test the defrost thermistor** by checking its resistance at ice-water temperature (consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected value). Replace if out of range or open.
7. **Reassemble the evaporator cover**, plug in the unit, and monitor the next defrost cycle to confirm the code clears and frost does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working inside the freezer compartment with the rear panel removed, or if you have tested the heater, thermal fuse, and thermistor and all show continuity but the code persists. A pro can verify the control board output voltage during a defrost cycle and diagnose board-level faults. Also call for help if the evaporator is severely iced over and you cannot safely or fully defrost it yourself, or if the repair requires refrigerant work due to a damaged evaporator coil.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
