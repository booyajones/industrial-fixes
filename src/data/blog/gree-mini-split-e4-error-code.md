## Gree E4 Error Code — What It Means

E4 on a Gree mini-split means compressor discharge temperature protection. The outdoor unit has detected that the compressor discharge pipe temperature is too high, so the system trips to protect the compressor from damage. This is not a generic fault. It is a real overheat condition at the compressor discharge line.

The system is protecting itself from a condition that can destroy the compressor if allowed to continue. The error usually points to a problem with heat rejection, airflow, refrigerant charge, or the sensor circuit that monitors discharge temperature.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted airflow at indoor or outdoor coil** Dirty filters, clogged coils, or blocked air intake and discharge raise system temperatures and trigger discharge overheat protection.
- **Outdoor fan failure or poor heat rejection** If the outdoor fan does not run or moves insufficient air, the condenser cannot reject heat and compressor discharge temperature climbs.
- **Low refrigerant charge or refrigerant leak** Undercharge or loss of refrigerant drives abnormal compressor temperatures and is a common field cause of discharge temperature faults.
- **Refrigerant circuit restrictions or installation errors** EEV faults, line-set problems, improper evacuation, or incorrect piping connections create flow issues that overheat the compressor.
- **Discharge temperature sensor or wiring fault** A failed thermistor, loose connector, or bad outdoor board input can falsely report high temperature or fail to read correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit and reset** by switching off the breaker for 60 seconds, then power back on and confirm whether the E4 code returns or clears.
2. **Clean the indoor filter and verify blower operation** to restore full airflow at the evaporator, then inspect and clean the outdoor coil fins and confirm nothing blocks the outdoor unit's air intake or discharge.
3. **Check the outdoor fan** runs when the compressor starts and moves adequate air across the condenser, and replace the fan motor or blade if the fan does not spin or runs weakly.
4. **Inspect the refrigerant circuit** for signs of leak, check oil residue at flare joints, verify proper evacuation was performed during installation, and confirm line sets and connections match the correct ports on multi-zone systems.
5. **Test the outdoor discharge temperature sensor with power off** using an ohmmeter and compare readings to your model's thermistor chart (a sensor in ice water should correspond to 32°F on the chart), then inspect the sensor plug and harness continuity.
6. **Check the outdoor main board** if the sensor and wiring test correctly but the error persists, and inspect all connectors and board traces for damage or corrosion.
7. **Replace only the failed component**, reassemble the system, evacuate and recharge the refrigerant circuit if it was opened, then run the unit under load and verify stable operation with no returning fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the error returns after you clean filters and coils, if the outdoor fan does not run, or if you lack refrigerant gauges and recovery equipment. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification and specialized tools. Testing sensors, diagnosing board faults, and opening the refrigerant circuit are not homeowner repairs. A tech will verify refrigerant charge, test all sensors against the factory chart, inspect the discharge line and compressor for mechanical faults, and replace boards or sensors as needed. If your unit is under warranty, contact Gree support before opening the system.
