## Navien Tankless Water Heater E178 Error — What It Means

The E178 error on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a problem with the condensate drain system. Condensing tankless units produce acidic water as a byproduct, and this condensate must drain freely. The error typically appears when the condensate drain line is blocked, the condensate trap is full or dirty, or the drain safety float switch is stuck or malfunctioning.

When the drain system cannot evacuate condensate properly, the float switch triggers a safety shutdown to prevent water damage or internal corrosion. The heater will not operate until the drain path is clear and the switch resets.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the condensate drain switch when the real issue is a simple blockage in the trap or drain line. Always clear and flush the entire drain path before ordering a new switch.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged condensate trap or drain line (~50%)** Mineral deposits, sludge, or debris accumulate in the trap or drain tubing, preventing condensate from draining and triggering the float switch.
- **Stuck or faulty float switch (~25%)** The condensate drain float switch may stick in the closed position due to scale buildup or mechanical failure, falsely signaling a drain blockage.
- **Blocked or improperly sloped drain line (~15%)** The external condensate drain line may be kinked, sagging, or blocked by ice or debris, preventing gravity drainage.
- **Failed condensate neutralizer cartridge (~7%)** If your system uses a neutralizer, a spent or clogged cartridge can restrict flow and cause the drain to back up.
- **Loose or corroded wiring to the float switch (~3%)** Poor electrical connections at the float switch can create intermittent signals that the control board reads as a drain fault.

## Quick Diagnosis

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker to work safely on the drain system.
2. **Locate the condensate trap** on the bottom or side of the unit, typically a clear or white plastic chamber with a drain outlet.
3. **Remove the trap** by unscrewing the retaining collar or releasing the clips, and catch any residual water in a shallow pan.
4. **Flush the trap** under warm running water, scrubbing away any white mineral deposits or dark sludge with a soft brush.
5. **Check the float switch** inside the trap chamber for free movement and remove any debris or scale that might cause it to stick.
6. **Inspect the drain line** from the trap outlet to the termination point, straightening any kinks and flushing with warm water if accessible.
7. **Reinstall the trap** securely, ensuring all seals are seated and the drain line is sloped continuously downward.
8. **Restore power** and run hot water at a tap to verify normal operation and confirm the E178 code does not reappear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you have cleared the trap and drain line but the error persists, if you lack confidence working with the internal plumbing connections, or if you suspect a wiring fault inside the control cabinet. Professionals can test switch continuity with a multimeter, trace drain line routing through walls or floors, and replace internal components under warranty. Also seek help if the heater shows signs of water leakage around the base or if other error codes appear alongside E178.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts if a trap or tubing needs replacement, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220 for a service call and drain clearing.
