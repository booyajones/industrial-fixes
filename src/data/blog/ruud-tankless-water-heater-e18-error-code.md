## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E18 Error — What It Means

The E18 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition sequence. The unit may be failing to detect a stable flame after ignition, or the flame sensor is not confirming combustion properly. This safety feature prevents gas from flowing without proper ignition. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for your specific model number. The heater will lock out and stop providing hot water until the issue is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the entire control board when the flame rod is simply dirty or corroded. Inspect and clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper or steel wool before spending hundreds on electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame sensing rod prevent it from detecting the flame signal properly.
- **Faulty flame rod sensor (~25%)** The flame rod itself may have failed internally and cannot conduct the ionization current needed to confirm flame presence.
- **Weak or inconsistent gas supply (~15%)** Low gas pressure or blockage in the gas line can cause an unstable flame that the sensor cannot reliably detect.
- **Igniter or ignition assembly failure (~10%)** A weak igniter may produce a delayed or partial flame that does not generate enough signal for the sensor.
- **Grounding or wiring issue (~7%)** Poor grounding or corroded wire connections to the flame rod can interrupt the flame-detection circuit.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The circuit that reads the flame signal on the control board may be damaged or malfunctioning.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the access panel fasteners.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a small metal probe near the burner assembly, and inspect it for soot, corrosion, or white calcium buildup.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or steel wool until the metal is shiny, taking care not to bend or damage the rod.
5. **Inspect the igniter and burner area** for debris, spider webs, or carbon deposits and clean with a soft brush or compressed air if needed.
6. **Check all wire connections** to the flame rod and igniter for corrosion or looseness and reseat any suspect connectors.
7. **Restore gas and power**, then initiate a heating cycle and observe whether the burner lights and stays lit without throwing the E18 code again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and checking connections, or if you suspect a control board or gas valve problem. Flame-sensing circuits involve both gas safety and low-voltage electronics, and misdiagnosis can lead to unsafe operation or expensive part replacements. A pro can test flame current with a microamp meter, verify gas pressure at the inlet, and confirm proper grounding of the unit.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
