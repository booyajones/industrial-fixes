## Electrolux Dryer EA3 Error Code — What It Means

The EA3 code on an Electrolux IQ-Touch dryer indicates that the control board has detected incoming line voltage below 90 VAC. This is not a heater or airflow problem. The dryer is seeing insufficient voltage to operate safely and will not run until the power supply issue is corrected.

The fault can be caused either by a genuine low-voltage condition at the outlet or supply circuit, or by a control board that is misreading normal voltage. Poor connections at the receptacle, power cord, or terminal block can also drop voltage under load and trigger this code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some owners replace the control board immediately without checking the actual supply voltage first. Always measure voltage at the outlet with a multimeter under load before replacing any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low supply voltage at the receptacle (~40%)** The outlet is delivering less than 90 VAC due to undersized wiring, overloaded circuit, or utility supply problems.
- **Loose or corroded connection at outlet, cord, or terminal block (~30%)** A poor connection creates resistance that drops voltage under load even when the supply is otherwise adequate.
- **Extension cord or temporary power setup (~15%)** Extension cords or undersized temporary wiring cannot maintain voltage when the dryer draws current.
- **Faulty electronic control board (~12%)** The control board is misreading line voltage and generating the EA3 fault even though supply voltage is normal.
- **Incorrect branch circuit wiring or breaker sizing (~3%)** The dryer is connected to a circuit that does not meet the manufacturer's electrical requirements.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and turn off the circuit breaker to make sure no power is present during inspection.
2. **Measure voltage at the wall outlet** using a multimeter set to AC volts. Record the reading with no load, then again with the dryer plugged in and attempting to start. Voltage must remain above 90 VAC under load.
3. **Inspect the power cord and terminal block** inside the dryer's rear panel. Look for loose, corroded, burned, or overheated wire connections. Tighten or replace any damaged terminals.
4. **Verify the dryer is on a dedicated circuit** and not sharing power with other high-draw appliances or using an extension cord. Consult the installation manual for the correct circuit size and wiring.
5. **If measured voltage is below 90 VAC**, have a licensed electrician evaluate the branch circuit, breaker, and receptacle. Do not proceed with dryer repairs until the supply is corrected.
6. **If measured voltage is normal** and connections are sound, replace the electronic control board. The board is generating a false EA3 fault.
7. **Restore power and reset the dryer** by pressing the appropriate button or cycling the breaker. Run a test cycle to confirm the EA3 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 120-volt or 240-volt electrical connections, or if you do not own a multimeter and cannot verify supply voltage yourself. If the outlet voltage is measured low, contact a licensed electrician to diagnose and repair the branch circuit, breaker, or service panel. Do not attempt to modify household wiring without proper training and permits. A technician will measure voltage under load, inspect all supply connections, and replace the control board only after confirming the electrical supply is correct.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150-250 for control board if DIY, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400 including diagnosis and parts.
