## Electrolux Dryer EA3 Error Code — What It Means

The EA3 code on an Electrolux IQ-Touch dryer means the control has detected incoming line voltage below 90 VAC at the dryer's supply. This is a power-supply-side fault, not a heating element or sensor problem. The dryer is reporting that it is not receiving enough voltage from your house electrical system to operate safely.

Electrolux groups EA3 under power supply issues and recommends checking the electrical supply first. The code fires when the measured voltage drops below the 90-volt threshold, whether from utility brownout, damaged wiring, poor connections at the terminal block, or a failing control board that misreads normal voltage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board without measuring the actual supply voltage first. Always use a multimeter at the terminal block and outlet to confirm whether voltage is truly low before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Low outlet voltage from utility or building supply (~35%)** Utility brownout, undersized branch circuit, or voltage drop in the panel feeds less than 90 VAC to the dryer and triggers EA3.
- **Loose or burned terminal-block connection (~30%)** Poor contact at the dryer's terminal block or power-cord terminals creates voltage drop under load, causing EA3.
- **Damaged power cord or wiring (~20%)** Broken conductors, heat-damaged insulation, or loose plug blades reduce the delivered voltage below the threshold.
- **Extension cord, generator, or inverter in use (~10%)** A nonstandard power source without adequate capacity drops voltage when the dryer tries to run, triggering EA3.
- **Failed electronic control board (~5%)** If measured supply voltage is normal but EA3 persists, the control board is misreading the input and needs replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the dryer (or disconnect the hard-wired supply if applicable).
2. **Measure outlet voltage** with a multimeter set to AC volts. For a 240 VAC dryer, you should see approximately 240 VAC between the two hot terminals and around 120 VAC from each hot to neutral.
3. **Remove the dryer's rear access panel** and locate the terminal block where the power cord or hard-wired supply attaches.
4. **Inspect the terminal block** for loose screws, burned terminals, discolored wires, or melted insulation. Tighten all screws and replace any damaged terminals or wiring.
5. **Test the power cord** (if cord-connected) for continuity on each conductor from plug to terminal. Replace the cord if any conductor is open or shows heat damage.
6. **Restore power and re-test** the dryer. If EA3 clears, the repair is complete. If EA3 returns and supply voltage is verified normal, the electronic control board is likely misreading the input.
7. **Replace the electronic control board** if all wiring and supply voltage tests are normal but the EA3 code persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician if measured outlet voltage is below 90 VAC or unstable, if you find burned or melted wiring in the wall box, or if the circuit breaker trips when the dryer runs. Call an appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240 VAC wiring, if you cannot access the terminal block safely, or if voltage tests normal but the EA3 code remains after you have checked all connections. High-voltage work and control-board diagnosis are best handled by a professional to avoid shock or further damage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150-250 in parts (control board or cord), 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
