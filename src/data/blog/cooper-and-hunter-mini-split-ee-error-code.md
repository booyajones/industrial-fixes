## Cooper & Hunter EE Error Code — What It Means

The EE error code on Cooper & Hunter multi-zone mini split systems indicates a water level float switch malfunction. The indoor unit's control board expects a valid signal from the condensate safety circuit and flags EE when that signal is missing or abnormal. The code specifically appears when the sampling voltage is not 5 V, which typically means the float switch is stuck, disconnected, or the drain path is blocked.

This is a condensate management fault, not a refrigerant or compressor issue. The float switch is a small safety device inside the drain pan that monitors water level. If condensate water cannot drain properly or the float mechanism jams in the raised position, the system throws EE and shuts down to prevent water overflow and damage.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain hose** A blockage in the condensate drain line prevents water from leaving the pan, raising the float and triggering the fault.
- **Jammed or improperly seated float switch** The float can stick in the upright position or become dislodged from its mount, sending a constant alarm signal to the board.
- **Broken float switch** The switch contacts or float arm can crack or fail electrically, preventing the board from reading the correct water level.
- **Faulty water pump** If the condensate pump is dead or weak, water accumulates in the pan and the float stays raised.
- **Faulty wiring or connections** Loose, corroded, or broken wires between the float switch and the indoor main PCB interrupt the signal and trigger EE.
- **Faulty indoor main PCB** If all mechanical and wiring checks pass, the control board itself may no longer read the float circuit correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the breaker or disconnect switch and wait about 2 minutes, then restore power to see if the code clears on its own.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the indoor unit and locate the drain pan and condensate drain hose, usually exiting the bottom or side of the unit.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** for kinks, clogs, or standing water. Flush the line with water or compressed air to confirm it is open and flowing freely.
4. **Locate the water level float switch** in the drain pan. Gently lift and lower the float arm by hand to verify it moves freely and is not stuck or jammed.
5. **Disconnect the two wires** from the float switch and use a multimeter to check continuity or resistance between the terminals. Move the float up and down and watch for the reading to change, confirming the switch operates correctly.
6. **Check the condensate pump** (if your model has one) by pouring water into the pan and watching the pump cycle. Replace the pump if it does not run or does not move water.
7. **If all mechanical and electrical checks pass**, reconnect everything, power up, and monitor. If EE returns immediately, replace the indoor main PCB or contact a qualified technician for board-level diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you cannot access the indoor unit safely, if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components, or if the code returns after you have verified the drain is clear and the float switch tests correctly. A professional can also perform refrigerant-side diagnostics to rule out secondary issues and has the tools to safely replace the indoor PCB if needed. If your system is under warranty, contact Cooper & Hunter support before opening the unit to avoid voiding coverage.
