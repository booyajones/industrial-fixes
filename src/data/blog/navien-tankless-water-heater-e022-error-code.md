## Navien Tankless Water Heater E022 Error — What It Means

The E022 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with water flow detection or the flow sensor system. The unit is not detecting the minimum flow rate needed to activate the burner, or the flow sensor itself is malfunctioning. This code prevents the heater from igniting because the control board needs to confirm adequate flow before allowing gas to fire.

The error can arise from restricted water flow due to clogged filters, low supply pressure, a faulty flow sensor, or scaling inside the heat exchanger. Because Navien units require a minimum flow rate to operate, even partial blockages or pressure drops can trigger this code. The heater will not produce hot water until the flow issue is resolved and the code clears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor when the real problem is a dirty inlet filter or scaled heat exchanger. Clean the filter and flush the system before buying a sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter (~40%)** Sediment and debris accumulate in the cold water inlet filter, reducing flow below the minimum threshold and preventing ignition.
- **Low incoming water pressure (~20%)** Municipal supply pressure below specification or a partially closed isolation valve starves the unit of flow.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~20%)** The Hall-effect flow sensor fails to generate the proper signal to the control board even when flow is adequate.
- **Scaled heat exchanger (~15%)** Mineral buildup inside the heat exchanger restricts water passages and reduces effective flow rate.
- **Wiring or connector issue at the flow sensor (~5%)** Corroded terminals or loose connectors interrupt the signal between the sensor and the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the isolation valves on the cold and hot water lines.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws at the bottom and lifting the panel away from the unit.
3. **Locate the inlet filter** on the cold water supply line where it enters the heater and unscrew the filter housing using a wrench.
4. **Clean the filter screen** thoroughly under running water, removing all sediment and debris, then reinstall the housing with a new rubber washer if the old one is damaged.
5. **Open the isolation valves** fully and restore power, then run a hot water tap at full flow to test whether the error clears.
6. **If the error persists**, disconnect power again and inspect the flow sensor connector near the inlet manifold for corrosion or looseness, cleaning and reseating as needed.
7. **Test the flow sensor resistance** with a multimeter if you have one, comparing the reading to your model's service manual, and replace the sensor if readings are out of spec or if cleaning did not resolve the code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if cleaning the inlet filter and checking connectors does not clear the error, if you lack the tools to test the flow sensor, or if the unit requires descaling due to hard water buildup. Technicians have the pumps and descaling chemicals to flush the heat exchanger safely and can test the sensor circuit with diagnostic equipment. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with the gas supply line or if the error returns frequently, which may indicate a more complex flow or control board issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 in parts if filter cleaning or descaling solves it, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350 for service call, descaling, and sensor replacement if needed.
