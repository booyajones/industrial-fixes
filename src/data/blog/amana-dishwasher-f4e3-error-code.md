## Amana Dishwasher F4E3 Error — What It Means

The F4E3 code on your Amana dishwasher signals a communication problem between the electronic control board and the circulation pump or wash motor. In practical terms, the control is not detecting that the wash motor is operating as expected. You will usually see this fault when the dishwasher fills with water but then fails to begin the wash cycle, or when the fault appears immediately at the moment the wash motor should start. Amana's official first service step is to disconnect power for 30 seconds to reset the control and see if the fault clears.

This code is specific to Whirlpool-family dishwashers (Amana is part of that platform). It does not mean the drain system is blocked. Instead, it points to a failure in the motor itself, a broken or corroded wire between the control and the pump, or a control board that can no longer drive or read the pump circuit correctly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the electronic control board first. Check all wiring connectors between the control and the pump for corrosion or loose pins, and listen for pump noise during a test cycle, before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed circulation pump or wash motor (~55%)** The pump motor may be mechanically seized, electrically open, or shorted, preventing it from starting or communicating with the control board.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring and connectors (~25%)** Broken conductors, corroded pins, or loose connectors between the control and the pump interrupt the signal the control needs to verify motor operation.
- **Electronic control board failure (~15%)** The control board may have a failed output driver or cannot read the pump feedback circuit, even when the pump and wiring are intact.
- **Harness or interface wiring damage (~5%)** Wiring between the user interface and the main control, or within the control harness itself, can develop breaks or shorts that mimic a pump fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-reset the dishwasher.** Unplug the unit or switch off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds, then restore power and attempt a new wash cycle to see if the fault clears.
2. **Verify the symptom.** Run a cycle and confirm whether the dishwasher fills but does not circulate water, or whether the fault appears immediately when the wash motor should start.
3. **Inspect all wiring connections.** Open the lower access panel and check the connectors at the control board, at the circulation pump, and at any inline harness plugs for looseness, corrosion, bent pins, burn marks, or broken wires.
4. **Test and inspect the circulation pump.** Disconnect power, remove the pump assembly, and check for mechanical seizure by rotating the impeller by hand. Look for signs of an open or shorted motor winding if you have a multimeter.
5. **Replace the circulation pump** if it is jammed, shows burn marks, or does not pass basic continuity and resistance checks. Reinstall the pump and secure all connectors.
6. **Inspect the electronic control board** for heat damage, charred traces, or failed relay or output components. Replace the board only after confirming the pump and wiring are good.
7. **Reassemble and retest.** Button up the machine, restore power, and run a full wash cycle to verify that the motor starts, water circulates, and the F4E3 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with 120 V wiring, removing the lower spray arm and sump assembly, or handling the circulation pump. A qualified service tech can quickly isolate whether the fault is in the pump, the wiring, or the control board using the model-specific test values on the tech sheet. Also call for help if you have already replaced the pump and all wiring tests good but the code persists, since that points to a control board issue that may require programming or firmware checks beyond typical DIY scope.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–180 for a circulation pump, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $180–350 including pump and labor.
