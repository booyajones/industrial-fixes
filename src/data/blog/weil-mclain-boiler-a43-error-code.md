## Weil-McLain Boiler A43 Error Code — What It Means

A43 is not a single universally defined fault code across all Weil-McLain product lines. The exact meaning depends on the specific boiler series and control board installed. Weil-McLain expects technicians to identify the failed part and part number through model-specific troubleshooting and the boiler's own fault-history screen or service manual.

For any Weil-McLain fault, the correct diagnostic flow is to confirm the exact model and control from the boiler nameplate, access the diagnostics or fault history in the installer menu, and read the stored lockout or alarm details. The control will log the specific condition that triggered A43 for that model. Most Weil-McLain boiler faults in general point to ignition or flame-proving issues, gas supply problems, sensor faults, control module faults, or circulation and temperature-limit issues depending on the model.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor or failed pressure switch. Access the boiler's fault-history menu to identify the exact input or output that failed before ordering any part.

## Common Causes

- **Ignition or flame-proving fault (~30%)** The control did not detect flame during the ignition sequence, often due to a dirty or failed flame sensor, weak ignitor, or gas supply interruption.
- **Pressure switch or inducer fault (~25%)** The air-proving pressure switch did not close or the inducer motor failed to establish the required draft before ignition.
- **Control module or board fault (~20%)** The main control board logged an internal error or failed to complete the start sequence due to a memory or relay fault.
- **Temperature limit or sensor fault (~15%)** A high-limit or temperature sensor opened or reported an out-of-range value, triggering a lockout to protect the boiler.
- **Low water cutoff or circulator fault (~10%)** The LWCO interrupted the circuit due to low water level, or the circulator pump failed to prove flow before the burner was allowed to fire.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the boiler's service switch and wait 30 seconds before accessing the control panel.
2. **Access the fault-history menu** by pressing the installer or contractor button sequence documented in your boiler's manual and write down the full fault message displayed with A43.
3. **Record the boiler model number** from the nameplate (usually inside the jacket or on the front panel) and locate the matching service manual online or from the installer.
4. **Look up A43** in the fault-code table for your exact model and control board to identify which sensor, switch, or sequence step triggered the code.
5. **Inspect the identified component** visually for loose wires, corrosion, or physical damage, then test it according to the manual's test points and resistance or voltage specifications.
6. **Replace only the failed part** verified by testing, using the OEM part number from the manual or a cross-referenced equivalent from a boiler-supply house.
7. **Restore power and initiate a call for heat**, then observe the full start sequence (inducer, ignition, flame proving, circulator) to confirm the fault does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician if you cannot access the fault-history menu, do not have the service manual for your exact model, or are uncomfortable working with gas piping and 120 V control circuits. A qualified technician will use the model-specific fault table to identify the exact input or output that triggered A43, test each component with a multimeter, and replace only the verified failed part. Gas ignition, flame-proving, and control-board diagnostics require training and proper test equipment. Repeated resets without correcting the root cause can damage the control or create a safety hazard.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-500.
