## Navien Tankless Water Heater E242 Error — What It Means

The E242 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a combustion or flame-sensing issue. The unit has detected a condition where the flame is not stable, the flame rod is not sensing properly, or exhaust gases are not venting correctly. The heater will lock out to prevent unsafe operation.

This code can appear when the system cannot verify proper combustion. The flame rod sensor may be fouled with carbon deposits, the air intake or exhaust vent may be blocked or undersized, or the gas pressure and air mixture may be incorrect. Because the exact definition can vary slightly across Navien model families, consult your owner's manual for model-specific troubleshooting tables.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or igniter when the real problem is a flame rod covered in soot or a partially blocked vent pipe. Clean the flame rod and inspect venting before ordering expensive components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame-sensing rod prevent it from detecting the flame signal, causing the control board to shut down and display E242.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~25%)** Obstructions in the intake or exhaust vent, or undersized vent piping, disrupt combustion airflow and cause unstable flames or incomplete combustion readings.
- **Incorrect gas pressure or air mixture (~15%)** Gas supply pressure outside the specified range or a faulty gas valve modulation can create a weak or irregular flame that the sensor cannot verify.
- **Faulty flame rod wire or connection (~10%)** A loose, corroded, or broken wire between the flame rod and the control board interrupts the flame signal and triggers a combustion fault.
- **Control board sensing circuit failure (~5%)** The flame-detection circuit on the main control board can fail and report an error even when the flame rod and flame are functioning correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power to the heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the unit.
2. Remove the front cover panel by unscrewing the fasteners and lifting the panel away from the unit.
3. Locate the flame rod, a thin metal probe extending into the burner chamber near the igniter.
4. Disconnect the wire lead from the flame rod and unscrew the mounting bracket or nut holding the rod in place.
5. Clean the flame rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a wire brush to remove all carbon deposits and corrosion, then wipe it with a clean cloth.
6. Inspect the flame rod wire for cracks, corrosion, or loose connections and repair or replace as needed.
7. Reinstall the flame rod, reconnect the wire lead, and check that all connections are secure and properly seated.
8. Inspect the intake and exhaust vent pipes for blockages, condensation buildup, or improper slope and correct any issues.
9. Restore gas and power, then run a test cycle and observe the burner flame through the viewing window to confirm stable combustion.
10. If the error persists after cleaning, measure gas inlet pressure with a manometer and consult your model's specifications, or call a qualified technician to test the control board and gas valve.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the flame rod and venting are clean but the error continues, or if you lack a manometer to verify gas pressure. Combustion problems can involve gas valve calibration, control board diagnostics, and safety testing that require specialized tools and training. A pro can also perform a full combustion analysis to verify proper air-fuel ratios and check for heat exchanger damage or other internal faults that are not visible during a simple flame rod cleaning.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
