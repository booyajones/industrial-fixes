## Navien Tankless Water Heater E316 Error — What It Means

The E316 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically signals a problem with the flow sensor or communication between the sensor and the main control board. The unit may fail to detect water flow correctly, preventing the heater from firing or causing intermittent operation. Because error code definitions can vary between Navien models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the unit's wiring diagram for the exact meaning on your specific model. In general, E316 points to either a failed flow sensor, loose wiring connections, or a control board that cannot read the sensor signal.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a corroded sensor connector or debris on the flow sensor paddle. Check and clean the sensor connections and paddle before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty flow sensor (~50%)** The flow sensor paddle or Hall-effect sensor inside can fail due to mineral buildup, wear, or electrical failure, preventing accurate flow measurement.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~25%)** Connections at the flow sensor or main board can oxidize or vibrate loose over time, breaking the signal path.
- **Debris blocking the flow sensor paddle (~15%)** Scale, sediment, or foreign objects can jam the flow sensor paddle so it cannot spin freely and generate a signal.
- **Control board communication fault (~10%)** A failed or corrupted main control board may misread or fail to process the flow sensor signal, triggering the error code.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error code clear after a power-cycle and return only when hot water is demanded?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The flow sensor is likely intermittent or the wiring is loose; inspect connections and test the sensor.<br><strong>No:</strong> The code persists even after reset, suggesting a failed sensor or board; proceed to sensor removal and testing.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you access the flow sensor and spin its paddle freely by hand with the unit off?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The paddle moves, so debris is not the issue; suspect sensor electronics or wiring.<br><strong>No:</strong> The paddle is stuck or stiff; clean or replace the flow sensor.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Do you see corrosion or moisture inside the sensor connector or on the pins?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Clean the connector with contact cleaner, let it dry, and reseat; retest before replacing parts.<br><strong>No:</strong> Wiring looks good; the sensor itself or the control board is likely at fault.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water isolation valves on the inlet and outlet of the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners to access the flow sensor and control board.
3. **Locate the flow sensor** on the cold water inlet side of the heat exchanger; it is a cylindrical housing with a wiring harness.
4. **Disconnect the sensor connector** and inspect both the sensor pins and the board-side socket for corrosion, moisture, or bent contacts; clean with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Unscrew and remove the flow sensor** from its mounting port using a wrench, taking care not to damage the O-ring or threads.
6. **Inspect the sensor paddle** for debris or scale buildup; spin it by hand to verify it rotates freely, and clean any deposits with white vinegar or descaling solution.
7. **Test the sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode; consult your model's service manual for the expected ohm range (resistance readings vary by model); replace if out of spec or if no continuity is present.
8. **Install the new or cleaned sensor** with a new O-ring if provided, torque to the manufacturer's specification (consult your manual), and reconnect the wiring harness.
9. **Restore water flow** by opening the isolation valves slowly and checking for leaks around the sensor body.
10. **Restore power** at the breaker, open a hot water tap, and verify the unit fires without displaying the E316 code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or heating technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the unit continues to show E316 after replacing the flow sensor and verifying all wiring, or if you suspect the main control board needs replacement. A pro can run advanced diagnostics with manufacturer software, check gas valve operation, and safely handle any sealed-system or gas-line work. Also call a pro if your unit is still under warranty, since DIY repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-180 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
