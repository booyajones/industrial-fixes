## Maytag F9 E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 E1 code signals a 'Long Drain' fault. The control board has detected that water in the drum did not drain completely within the allowed time, typically 8 minutes. The pressure sensor monitors the water level and if it does not register 'empty' by the time limit, the machine halts the cycle and displays the error. The lid may lock and the spin cycle will not start until the condition is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump or control board when the real problem is a kinked hose or improper standpipe installation. Check the hose for obstructions and verify the hose is not inserted too deeply into the standpipe before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged or kinked drain hose (~45%)** Lint, small objects, or a physical kink in the drain hose restricts water flow and prevents the tub from emptying in time.
- **Improper drain hose installation (~25%)** The hose is inserted too deeply (more than 6 to 8 inches) into the standpipe, creating a siphon effect or trapping air and slowing the drain.
- **Blocked drain pump filter (~15%)** On front-load models the filter traps lint, hair, coins, and debris and if not cleaned regularly will block the pump intake.
- **Household drain restriction (~8%)** A slow or clogged utility sink or standpipe on the home plumbing side prevents the washer from emptying fast enough.
- **Faulty drain pump (~5%)** The pump motor is seized, the impeller is broken, or the pump is mechanically jammed by debris and cannot move water.
- **Wiring or connector issue (~2%)** A loose connection, corroded pin, or broken wire between the control board and the drain pump interrupts power or signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait one to two minutes to reset the control board, then replug and attempt a spin or drain cycle to see if the error clears on its own.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** for kinks or bends by tracing it from the washer to the standpipe or utility sink. Straighten any bends you find.
3. **Disconnect the drain hose** from both the washer and the standpipe. Look inside with a flashlight and remove any lint, coins, or debris. Flush the hose with water from a garden hose or faucet.
4. **Check the standpipe installation**. The hose should hang over the edge of the standpipe with the end no more than 6 to 8 inches deep. If the hose is submerged or inserted too far, pull it back to create an air gap and prevent siphoning.
5. **Clean the drain pump filter** (front-load models). Open the small access panel at the bottom front of the washer. Place towels and a shallow pan underneath. Twist the filter cap counterclockwise and pull out the filter. Remove all lint, hair, and debris. Rinse the filter and reinstall.
6. **Test the household drain** by pouring a bucket of water into the standpipe or utility sink. If it drains slowly, the clog is in the home plumbing and not the washer.
7. **Inspect the drain pump** by accessing it through the lower front or rear panel (depending on model). Disconnect power. Check the pump inlet and impeller for obstructions. Spin the impeller by hand to confirm it moves freely. If the pump is jammed or the impeller is broken, replace the pump assembly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared the hose, cleaned the filter, confirmed the household drain is flowing freely, and the error persists. The problem may be a failed pump, a wiring harness fault, a pressure sensor issue, or a control board failure. These require multimeter testing and part-level diagnosis. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable disconnecting hoses, opening panels, or handling wet components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-90 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
