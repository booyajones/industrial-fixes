## Bosch E36 Error Code — What It Means

The Bosch E36 or E36-10 error code signals a drainage fault. The washer is not emptying water within the expected time frame. Bosch owner support identifies this as a blocked drain pipe or water outlet hose preventing normal water evacuation.

Technician guidance treats E36-10 as a failure to drain or empty water, not a spin cycle or door lock issue. The fault typically points to a blockage in the hydraulic path (filter, hose, or external drain) or a failure in the drain pump circuit that is supposed to move water out of the machine.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump without checking the hydraulic path first. Clean the pump filter, inspect the sump hose for debris, and verify the external drain outlet is clear before buying any pump.

## Common Causes

- **Pump filter or coin trap blockage (~40%)** Coins, lint, hair ties, buttons, and other debris collect in the filter housing and stop water flow.
- **Sump hose blockage (~25%)** Debris traps in the hose between the tub and drain pump, or the anti-siphon ball gets stuck and blocks the path.
- **External drain restriction (~15%)** A kinked, collapsed, or clogged household waste hose, sink spigot, U-bend, or drain outlet prevents water exit.
- **Failed drain pump or jammed impeller (~12%)** The pump motor has failed electrically or the impeller is mechanically jammed and cannot spin.
- **Control board not powering the pump (~6%)** The PCB does not supply voltage to the pump connector during the drain command even when the hydraulic path is clear.
- **Damaged or corroded pump wiring (~2%)** Heat damage, corrosion, or a poor connection at the pump connector interrupts power delivery.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply. Move the machine away from the wall for access to the rear and bottom panels.
2. **Drain standing water** by opening the pump filter access door at the front lower corner and using the emergency drain hose or slowly unscrewing the filter cap into a shallow pan.
3. **Remove and clean the pump filter** completely. Inspect the filter housing for coins, buttons, lint, hair ties, and other debris. Check that the filter cap threads smoothly and is not cracked or cross-threaded.
4. **Inspect the sump hose** from the tub to the drain pump. Release the hose clamp, pull the hose free, and look inside for trapped objects or an anti-siphon ball that is stuck in the path.
5. **Check the external drain hose** routing. Verify the hose is not kinked, collapsed, or installed too high. Disconnect the hose at the sink spigot or standpipe and flush water through to confirm it is clear.
6. **Test the drain pump impeller** by hand with power removed. Reach into the pump inlet from the filter housing and verify the impeller spins freely without grinding or jamming.
7. **Run a drain command** (on some models you can select Spin or Drain cycle) and use a multimeter to check for voltage at the pump connector. If voltage is present and the pump does not run, replace the pump. If no voltage is present, inspect wiring and connectors or replace the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have tested for voltage at the pump connector during a drain command and found no voltage, which points to a control board fault or internal wiring damage. Also call if you have cleared all blockages, confirmed the pump runs and the impeller spins freely, yet the code persists and water still does not drain. A technician can perform circuit-level diagnostics on the board and pressure tests on the hydraulic path that are difficult without service tools. If the machine is still under warranty, contact Bosch support before attempting any repair to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-90 in parts if a pump is needed, 45-75 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 service call.
