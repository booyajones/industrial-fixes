## Bosch Washer E15 Error Code — What It Means

The E15 error code on a Bosch dishwasher indicates that the anti-flood leak protection system has detected water in the base pan beneath the tub. A float switch in the base rises when water accumulates and triggers a safety shutdown to prevent flooding. The machine will not run until the water is removed and the leak source is repaired.

This is not a simple drain fault. The code signals that water has escaped the normal circulation path and pooled in the undertray, pointing to a leak from a hose, seal, valve, or connection somewhere inside the unit. The error will persist until the base is completely dried and the underlying leak is fixed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board thinking it is faulty when E15 appears. The real cause is almost always physical water in the base pan from a leak. Always inspect the base, hoses, and inlet valve before ordering electrical parts.

## Common Causes

- **Water inlet valve leak (~35%)** The inlet valve body or internal seal weeps water into the base pan, especially when the machine is off and water pressure remains on the supply line.
- **Loose or failed hose clamp (~25%)** Clamps on fill, circulation, or drain hoses loosen over time or the hose itself cracks, allowing water to drip into the base during a cycle.
- **Door gasket or tub seal failure (~20%)** A torn, displaced, or debris-clogged door seal lets water escape the tub and run down into the base pan during wash or rinse.
- **Sump or pump seal leak (~12%)** The seal around the circulation pump or sump housing deteriorates and allows water to seep into the base instead of staying in the recirculation path.
- **Excess detergent or rinse aid causing foam overflow (~5%)** Too much detergent or rinse aid generates foam that spills over the tub edge or through vents, pooling in the base and triggering the float.
- **Drain hose backflow or installation error (~3%)** A missing high loop or incorrect drain connection allows waste water to siphon back into the tub and overflow into the base pan.

## Quick Diagnosis

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** and shut off the water supply valve under the sink or at the wall to make the workspace safe.
2. **Remove the lower front kick plate** by pulling it forward or unscrewing retaining clips to access the base pan and float area.
3. **Tilt the dishwasher back about 30-45 degrees** onto a stack of towels to drain any trapped water from the base pan, then dry the pan and float completely with towels or a shop vacuum.
4. **Inspect all hose connections, clamps, and the inlet valve** for drips, cracks, or looseness; also check the sump area, door gasket, and drain hose routing for signs of leakage or foam residue.
5. **Repair or replace the failed component**: tighten or replace loose clamps, swap a leaking inlet valve, replace a torn door seal, or correct the drain hose high loop if missing.
6. **Disconnect power for five minutes** to allow the control to reset, then reconnect and observe the base area during a short test cycle with the kick plate off.
7. **Reinstall the kick plate** only after confirming no new leaks appear and the E15 code does not return during the test run.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you cannot locate the leak source after inspecting all visible hoses and connections, if the sump or pump area shows damage that requires disassembly of the tub, or if the E15 code returns immediately even after the base is completely dry and all accessible parts are secure. A pro has the tools to pressure-test internal seals, access hidden clamps behind the tub liner, and replace pump or sump components that require specialized disassembly. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working with water supply plumbing or tilting a heavy appliance safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (valve, clamp, or seal), 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for diagnosis, leak repair, and part.
