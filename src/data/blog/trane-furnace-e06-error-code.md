## Trane Furnace E06 Error Code — What It Means

The E06 error code on many Trane furnaces typically relates to an airflow or pressure issue detected during startup or operation. The exact meaning can vary by model, so check your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific definition on your unit. In general, this code appears when the furnace control board does not receive the expected signal from a pressure switch, or when venting or combustion air problems prevent safe ignition. The furnace will lock out to prevent unsafe operation until the underlying issue is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the pressure switch first when the real problem is a blocked vent pipe, disconnected hose, or clogged drain line. Inspect all vent terminations and condensate drains before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked intake or exhaust vent (~35%)** Snow, ice, bird nests, or debris at the outdoor termination restrict airflow and prevent the pressure switch from closing.
- **Disconnected or cracked pressure switch hose (~25%)** The small rubber or silicone tube that connects the pressure switch to the inducer assembly can crack, kink, or pull off, breaking the vacuum signal.
- **Clogged condensate drain line (~15%)** Water backs up in the collector box or pressure port, blocking airflow and preventing the switch from sensing proper draft.
- **Failed pressure switch (~15%)** The switch diaphragm may stick or the internal contacts may fail, even when proper vacuum is present.
- **Weak or failed inducer motor (~10%)** An aging motor may not spin fast enough to develop the required draft pressure, or it may fail to start at all.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the furnace at the service disconnect or breaker to work safely.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vents** for both intake and exhaust pipes, removing any snow, ice, leaves, or nests blocking the terminations.
3. **Check the pressure switch hose** inside the furnace cabinet, following the small tube from the inducer assembly to the switch, and reconnect or replace any cracked or loose sections.
4. **Clear the condensate drain** by disconnecting the line and using a wet-dry vacuum or hand pump to pull out any standing water or debris from the collector box and trap.
5. **Test the inducer motor** by restoring power and listening for the motor to spin up when the thermostat calls for heat, and check that the blower wheel turns freely by hand when power is off.
6. **Replace the pressure switch** if all hoses and vents are clear, the inducer runs normally, and you measure proper vacuum at the switch port but the furnace still locks out on E06.
7. **Restore power and monitor** the startup sequence, watching for the inducer to run, the pressure switch to close, and the burners to ignite without further error codes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if the inducer motor is not running or makes grinding noises, or if you have cleared all vents and drains but the error persists. A pro can measure draft pressure with a manometer, test switch operation under load, and diagnose control board or gas valve issues that may mimic a pressure switch fault. Gas furnace work also requires proper combustion analysis and carbon monoxide testing to maintain safety, especially after any repair that affects airflow or venting.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
