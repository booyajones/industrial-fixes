## Coleman Furnace 4 Flashes — What It Means

A Coleman furnace flashing **4 blinks** on the LED status light indicates the **high limit switch has opened** — the furnace detected an overtemperature condition in the heat exchanger or supply plenum and shut down for safety. The high limit switch is a thermal safety device that trips when supply air temperature exceeds approximately 170–200°F. Coleman furnaces use the same control board logic as York and other Bosch brand equipment, so this diagnostic applies across the product family. The furnace will attempt to restart after cooling, but will lockout after repeated trips.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty air filter** — A clogged filter is the most common cause; restricted return airflow causes heat to build up in the heat exchanger until the limit trips.
- **Blocked supply or return registers** — Closed registers in multiple rooms, blocked return grilles, or furniture covering vents reduces airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
- **Failed blower motor or capacitor** — If the blower doesn't run at full speed, air isn't moved across the heat exchanger fast enough to control temperature.
- **Cracked heat exchanger** — A cracked exchanger can alter airflow dynamics enough to cause hot spots and repeated limit trips, even with good filter and blower operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Replace the air filter** — This is step one, every time. A dirty 1" filter can restrict airflow enough to trip the high limit on its own. Replace with a fresh filter of the same MERV rating (stay at MERV 8 or lower for standard residential furnaces).
2. **Check all supply and return registers** — Walk through the house and confirm every supply register is open and unobstructed. Return registers must be completely clear — furniture, rugs, and curtains are frequent blockers.
3. **Test the blower** — Listen for the blower starting after the burners light. If you can feel minimal airflow at registers, the blower capacitor may have failed. Test the run capacitor with a capacitor meter.
4. **Check the high limit switch** — With power off, use a multimeter set to continuity mode and test across the high limit switch terminals. A healthy, cool limit switch should show continuity. If it reads open when cool, the switch has failed.
5. **Reset and monitor** — After repairs, restore power and run a full heat cycle. If 4 flashes returns within one cycle, the heat exchanger needs inspection by a licensed HVAC tech.

## When to Call a Pro

A cracked heat exchanger is a carbon monoxide risk. If 4 flashes returns repeatedly after correcting the filter and blower, have a licensed HVAC tech perform a heat exchanger inspection before running the furnace again.
