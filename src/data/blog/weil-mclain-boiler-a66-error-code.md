## Weil-McLain A66 Error Code — What It Means

The A66 error code is not documented in the available Weil-McLain manufacturer materials for common models like the Aqua Balance series. The closest verified manufacturer code is A01, which signals no burner ignition on Aqua Balance controls. This means the boiler calls for heat but flame does not prove within the expected time window.

If your display shows A66, consult your model's installation or service manual for the exact meaning. Many ignition-fault codes share similar root causes: the gas supply is shut off or low, the igniter or flame sensor is dirty or failed, the gas valve is stuck, or the condensate trap is blocked. Until you confirm the code definition in your manual, treat it as a general ignition or burner-lightoff failure.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or control board first. Before ordering expensive parts, verify that all gas shutoff valves are open, the igniter glows, and the flame sensor is clean-these account for most ignition faults.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or partially closed gas shutoff valve (~30%)** If the manual or ball valve at the meter or appliance is off or only partly open, no fuel reaches the burner and ignition fails immediately.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~25%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame-proving electrode prevent the control from detecting flame even when the burner lights, so it shuts down and logs a fault.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition electrode (~20%)** A cracked ceramic insulator, broken wire, or weak spark prevents the gas from lighting in the first place.
- **Low or insufficient gas supply pressure (~15%)** Weil-McLain specifies 3.5 to 11 inches water column for some models; pressure below that range causes incomplete combustion or no light.
- **Blocked condensate trap or drain line (~10%)** Standing water or ice in the trap creates back-pressure that trips a pressure switch or floods the heat exchanger, preventing safe ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the boiler service switch and close the manual gas shutoff valve.
2. **Locate the error code** on the display panel and cross-reference it in your model's installation or service manual to confirm whether A66 applies to your unit.
3. **Check all gas shutoff valves** at the meter and at the boiler inlet to make sure they are fully open, then reopen the manual valve and restore power.
4. **Press and hold the reset button** for 1.5 seconds to clear the fault and initiate a new ignition cycle.
5. **Observe the ignition sequence** through the sight glass or status LED. If the igniter glows but no flame appears, proceed to the next step.
6. **Remove and inspect the flame sensor** (typically a stainless-steel rod near the burner). Clean it with fine-grit emery cloth or steel wool until shiny, then reinstall.
7. **Check the condensate trap and drain line** for blockage. Remove the trap, flush it, and refill to the proper level before reconnecting.
8. **Measure gas supply pressure** at the inlet with a manometer. For models specifying 3.5 to 11 inches water column, pressure below that range requires utility or regulator service.
9. **If the fault persists**, call a licensed HVAC technician to test the gas valve, igniter continuity, flame-proving circuit, and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician immediately if you smell gas, if you are not comfortable working around gas piping, or if the boiler has been modified or installed recently and may have air in the gas line. A professional should also handle gas-pressure testing, gas-valve replacement, control-board diagnostics, and any work inside the combustion chamber. Because the A66 code is not verified in manufacturer literature, a technician can pull the service manual for your exact model, retrieve the fault history from the control, and follow the manufacturer's diagnostic tree to pinpoint the root cause without guessing.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350.
