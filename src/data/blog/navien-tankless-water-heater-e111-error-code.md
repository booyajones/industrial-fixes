## Navien E111 Error Code — What It Means

The E111 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a flame sensing or ignition issue. The unit is having trouble detecting flame after ignition, or the flame is not stable enough to be properly sensed by the flame rod. This is a safety feature that prevents the unit from running gas without proper combustion.

The code may appear during startup or while the unit is running. It will shut down the heater until the issue is resolved. Common triggers include a dirty or corroded flame sensor, poor gas supply, or a failing flame rod assembly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is simply a dirty flame sensor that needs cleaning with fine sandpaper or emery cloth.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~50%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents it from sensing the flame properly, triggering a false shutdown.
- **Improper grounding or loose wiring (~20%)** Poor electrical grounding or a loose connection at the flame sensor can interrupt the flame-sensing signal.
- **Low or inconsistent gas pressure (~15%)** Insufficient gas flow results in weak or unstable flame that the sensor cannot reliably detect.
- **Failed flame sensor rod (~10%)** The flame rod itself can crack or degrade over time, losing its ability to conduct the sensing current.
- **Blocked or dirty burner assembly (~5%)** Dust or debris in the burner chamber can disrupt flame shape and prevent proper sensing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the unit at the breaker and turn the gas supply valve to the off position.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the Navien unit according to your model's instructions to access the burner chamber.
3. **Locate the flame sensor rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner assembly, and note its wiring connection.
4. **Disconnect the flame sensor wire** and unscrew the mounting bracket or fastener holding the sensor in place.
5. **Clean the sensor rod** thoroughly with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth, removing all carbon deposits and corrosion until the metal is bright and shiny.
6. **Reinstall the sensor**, reconnect the wire firmly, and check that the ground wire connection to the unit chassis is secure.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then run a hot water tap to trigger the unit and observe whether it ignites and stays running without the E111 code returning.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified plumber or gas technician if cleaning the flame sensor does not resolve the E111 code, if you smell gas or suspect a gas leak, or if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances. A professional should also be consulted if gas pressure testing is required, if the wiring and grounding appear damaged, or if the error persists after sensor replacement. Any work involving gas lines, combustion testing, or internal gas valve adjustments should be left to licensed professionals to avoid safety hazards and maintain warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if sensor replacement needed, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for service call and cleaning or sensor replacement.
