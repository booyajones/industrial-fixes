## Mitsubishi P5 Error Code — What It Means

The P5 code on Mitsubishi mini-split systems signals a condensate-drain or water-disposal safety fault. The indoor unit has detected that condensate water is not being removed normally, so the float safety or drain-pump circuit has tripped and the unit has entered a water-protection shutdown. This code points to a problem in the drain pump, float switch, drain pan, or drain path rather than a refrigerant or communication issue. Because Mitsubishi uses model-specific fault tables, the exact wording can vary by series. Always consult your indoor unit's service manual for the authoritative definition, but field experience ties P5 to the condensate system across most residential models.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain line or trap** Algae, sludge, or debris builds up in the drain hose or trap and blocks the path, so water backs up and triggers the float switch.
- **Failed or weak condensate pump** The pump motor has failed or is too weak to lift water out of the drain pan, leaving water in place and tripping the safety.
- **Stuck or contaminated float switch** The float assembly is stuck in the triggered position, mispositioned, or fouled with debris so it signals high water even when the pan is dry.
- **Drain pan overflow or poor pitch** The pan is not tilted correctly toward the drain outlet, or blockage and excessive condensate load cause water to pool and overflow.
- **Improper drain piping** Air locks, sags, kinks, or disconnected discharge lines prevent the pump from priming or moving water to the outlet.
- **Control board or wiring fault** The board is not sending power to the pump, or a wiring or connector issue interrupts the pump or float-switch circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power cycle the unit** by turning it off at the thermostat and the breaker for two minutes, then restore power and check if the P5 alarm returns to rule out a transient fault.
2. **Inspect the entire condensate path** starting at the indoor drain pan, looking for sludge, algae, kinks, sags, ice, or standing water in the pan, hose, and outlet.
3. **Test the condensate pump** by pouring a small amount of water into the drain pan or triggering the float and confirming the pump runs and moves water out through the discharge line.
4. **Check the float switch** for contamination, sticking, or incorrect height, and verify that it opens and closes properly as the water level rises and falls in the pan.
5. **Measure voltage at the pump** if the pump does not run during your test. If voltage is present and the pump is silent or does not move water, replace the pump. If voltage is absent, trace the wiring back to the control board.
6. **Correct installation issues** such as bad slope in the drain pan, air pockets in the discharge line, or a disconnected or improperly routed hose.
7. **Clean all components**, reassemble the drain path, and run the unit through a full cooling cycle to confirm condensate flows freely and the P5 code does not reappear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the P5 code returns after you have cleared the drain line and verified the pump runs, or if you measure correct voltage at the pump but the pump does not operate and you are not comfortable replacing it. Also call if you suspect a control board output fault, if the float switch behaves erratically and you cannot source the correct replacement, or if the drain pan itself is cracked or misaligned and requires removal of the indoor unit. Refrigerant work and electrical troubleshooting beyond basic voltage checks should be left to qualified professionals.
