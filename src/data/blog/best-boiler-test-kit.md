## Quick answer

A proper hydronic boiler test kit isn't a single product — it's four instruments that work together. Start with the **Testo 320** combustion analyzer, the **Fieldpiece SDMN6** dual-port manometer, a quality **IR thermometer or IR + contact thermometer combo** (Fluke 561), and a **digital pH + total hardness test kit** (LaMotte BoilerCare or Hach AquaChek). That four-tool set covers everything from combustion tuning to water-side chemistry on a residential or light-commercial boiler. Skip the all-in-one consumer "boiler kits" sold at home centers; the individual instruments are better and you'll never replace them.

## What goes in a hydronic boiler test kit

After 16 years on hydronic boilers — cast iron through fire-tube condensing — here's what you actually pull out at every service call:

- **Combustion analyzer** — O2, CO, CO2, stack temperature, efficiency calculation. Required for tuning any modulating condensing boiler and for annual inspection on most jurisdictions' commercial boilers.
- **Differential manometer** — gas manifold pressure, gas inlet pressure under load, draft pressure, system static pressure on closed-loop hydronic systems.
- **Temperature measurement** — supply water temp, return water temp, flue gas temp, ambient combustion air temp. IR for quick non-contact reads on insulated lines; contact probe for accurate water temp on bare metal.
- **Water chemistry test kit** — pH, total hardness, conductivity, iron content. Required for warranty compliance on most condensing boilers; required for closed-loop system longevity regardless.
- **Voltage/current/continuity meter** — for ignition control diagnostics, flame current measurement, pump and damper testing.
- **Inspection mirror + flashlight** — for visual inspection of burner heads, heat exchanger surfaces, draft inducer.

I run a four-instrument boiler kit on every service call. The Testo 320 has been with me five years across roughly 600 boiler commissionings and annual services. The SDMN6 came in three years ago and replaced two single-port manometers I was carrying for inlet and manifold pressure. The Fluke 561 I bought 8 years ago and the contact thermocouple is still in service — though I've replaced the K-type tip twice.

The LaMotte BoilerCare kit I started using when a Lochinvar regional rep showed me the difference between strip-test pH (±1.0 accuracy) and drop-titration pH (±0.1 accuracy). On a boiler warranty claim, the difference between "pH was 7.2" and "pH was 6.5" can be the warranty denial. The drop kit pays for itself the first time you save a heat exchanger claim.

Selection criteria: instruments must be accurate enough for warranty compliance on modern condensing boilers, must come from brands with sensor swap and parts availability for 5+ years, and must work in unconditioned mechanical room environments (cold winters, hot summers, dusty).

Verification: combustion analyzer bench-checked against a Fluke 5500A reference at annual cal day. Manometer bench-checked against a Druck DPI 612 reference. Thermometer bench-checked against a calibrated ice bath (32.0°F) and boiling water (212.0°F at sea level). pH kit bench-checked against NIST-traceable buffer solutions (4.00, 7.00, 10.00).

## What to skip

**Skip "all-in-one" homeowner boiler test kits.** $200–400 kits sold at home centers bundle a cheap combustion analyzer, an even cheaper manometer, and a "thermometer" that's actually a kitchen instant-read thermocouple. The combustion analyzer in these kits is rated 0–500 ppm CO (modern boilers can hit 4000+ ppm in a misadjust). Skip the bundle and buy quality instruments individually.

**Skip pH-only paper strips.** Roll-paper pH strips read ±1.0 — useless for boiler water that requires ±0.2 accuracy. Hach AquaChek 5-in-1 strips are ±0.5 and acceptable for go/no-go; LaMotte drop titration is the right tool for documented service.

**Skip $40 IR thermometers for boiler water temp.** They read insulation surface temperature, not water temperature, and the emissivity is wrong for bare metal anyway. Use a contact thermocouple for water temps. IR is fine for stack temp (which is high enough that surface effects don't matter) and flue gas spot checks.

## FAQs

**Why measure CO2 instead of just O2 on a boiler?**
You don't have to — they're calculated from each other. O2 is the directly measured value on a Testo 320 combustion analyzer. CO2 is calculated from O2 and the fuel's stoichiometric ratio. Most manufacturers publish target CO2 windows (e.g., "8.5–9.5% CO2 for natural gas at full fire") because the number is more meaningful to legacy combustion techs.

**What's the right supply-return temperature differential on a hydronic system?**
Conventional: 20°F ΔT (e.g., 180°F supply, 160°F return). Low-temperature (radiant floor): 10°F ΔT. Modern modulating-condensing: aim for 30–40°F ΔT to maximize condensing operation. ΔT lower than 10°F suggests excess flow or pump too big; higher than 25°F (conventional) suggests low flow, air binding, or a partially closed valve.

**Why does my boiler keep tripping on high CO during annual service?**
Three common causes: misadjusted gas:air ratio (combustion analyzer tells you immediately), partially blocked burner (visual inspection plus reduced O2 reading), or poor combustion air supply (manometer on the air inlet shows below-spec pressure drop, indicating restriction). Tune with a combustion analyzer.

**How often should I test boiler water chemistry?**
Annual minimum for closed-loop hydronic. For glycol-protected systems, twice per year (glycol degrades and produces acids). For open systems (uncommon residentially), quarterly. After any system fill, leak repair, or pump replacement, test within 30 days.

**Can I tune a boiler without a combustion analyzer?**
Not legally and not safely on any modern modulating condensing boiler. Every manufacturer's installation manual requires combustion analyzer commissioning. Setting input on gas pressure alone leaves CO production unverified — and a misadjusted modcon can produce CO in the 1000+ ppm range that ages the heat exchanger and risks occupant safety.
