## What this code means
The F6 E5 error on a KitchenAid wall oven or range indicates a control-panel or control-board communication fault. KitchenAid's official product help identifies this as a problem with the Control Panel Assembly or its associated wiring. In the broader KitchenAid oven error-code system, F6 codes relate to the oven control, electronic control, and related wiring.

Because model-specific documentation varies, the safest interpretation is that the control system is not communicating correctly or is not configured or programmed correctly. This can be a physical wiring issue, a failed control panel, a failed control board, or a mismatch between a replacement board and the oven's configuration.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but the real culprit is often a loose or damaged ribbon cable or wire harness between the panel and board. Always inspect and reseat all connectors before ordering a new control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~40%)** The interconnect harness, ribbon cable, or connectors between the UI panel and control board can become loose, corroded, moisture-damaged, or have bent pins.
- **Failed control panel assembly (~30%)** The control panel, keypad, or UI board itself can fail and lose the ability to communicate with the main control board.
- **Failed or mismatched control board (~20%)** The oven control board may have failed, or a replacement board may be the wrong model or incorrectly programmed for your oven size and configuration.
- **Cracked or degraded ribbon cable (~10%)** The flat ribbon cable that carries signals between the panel and board can develop cracked traces or broken conductors over time, especially near flex points.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** for at least one minute, then restore power and observe the display for one minute to see if the error clears.
2. **If the code returns immediately**, turn off the breaker again and leave the oven disconnected for all remaining work.
3. **Remove the control panel** by taking out the screws that secure it to the oven frame (location varies by model, often along the top edge or behind the door).
4. **Inspect all wire harnesses and ribbon cables** that connect the control panel to the main control board for loose seating, bent pins, moisture, heat damage, or cracked traces.
5. **Reseat every connector** firmly, checking that each plug is fully inserted and latched, then reassemble the panel and restore power to test.
6. **If the error persists**, replace the control panel assembly first (KitchenAid's named suspect component) and test again.
7. **If replacing the panel does not fix it**, replace the oven control board, making sure the new board matches your exact model number and is programmed correctly for your oven configuration.
8. **If both panel and board are new and the error still appears**, replace the wire harness or ribbon cable between them, as it is the remaining common failure point.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working around high-voltage connections inside the oven, if the error returns after a power reset and you cannot safely access or inspect the control panel wiring, or if you have already replaced the control panel or board and the fault persists. Technicians have model-specific service manuals, programming tools for control boards, and the experience to trace intermittent wiring faults that can be difficult for a homeowner to diagnose. Because this error can involve multiple expensive parts, professional diagnosis can save money by identifying the exact failed component before you order replacements.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200–$450.
