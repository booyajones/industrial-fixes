## State Water Heater E54 Error Code — What It Means

E54 is not a standard published error code in State or A. O. Smith manufacturer documentation, but it falls into the ignition and flame-proving fault family common to State-branded tankless water heaters. In practical terms, this means the unit attempted to ignite but could not prove a stable flame, or it lost flame shortly after lighting. The control board locks out to prevent unburned gas from accumulating. Most often the root cause is restricted combustion air, low gas pressure, or a dirty flame sensor that cannot detect the flame reliably.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, or debris covering the combustion air inlet prevents adequate airflow for ignition and is the most common culprit on State tankless models.
- **Insufficient gas supply or pressure** A partially closed gas valve, undersized supply line, or low inlet pressure stops the burner from lighting or maintaining flame.
- **Dirty or oxidized flame sensor** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame rod prevent the control from detecting flame, triggering a false lockout even when the burner is lit.
- **Restricted water flow through the inlet filter** Sediment or debris in the cold-water inlet screen reduces flow below the minimum required to trigger ignition on demand.
- **Blocked exhaust vent or improper venting** Obstructions in the flue, excessive vent length, or too many elbows create back pressure that disrupts combustion and flame proving.
- **Failed gas valve or burner contamination** A defective gas valve may deliver inconsistent fuel, or dirt in the burner orifices produces a weak, unstable flame the sensor cannot confirm.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the gas** supply at the manual shutoff valve upstream of the heater and switch the unit's power or mode switch to off.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the exterior of the unit, removing any lint, dust, or debris that restricts airflow to the burner chamber.
3. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the upstream manual valve is fully open and checking that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
4. **Remove and clean the flame sensor** or flame rod by unscrewing it from the burner assembly and gently polishing the probe tip with fine sandpaper or a clean cloth until shiny.
5. **Check and clean the cold-water inlet filter** by closing the inlet isolation valve, removing the filter screen, rinsing it under running water, and reinstalling it.
6. **Inspect the burner and exhaust vent** for soot, rust, or blockages, and confirm the vent termination outside is clear of obstructions like bird nests or ice.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then initiate a hot-water draw to test ignition; if the unit lights and runs without faulting, monitor for 10 minutes to confirm stable operation, or call a technician if the code reappears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the fault persists after cleaning the air intake, flame sensor, and inlet filter and confirming full gas supply. Ignition problems that return after these steps usually require gas-pressure testing with a manometer, combustion analysis, vent-system inspection, or control-board diagnostics that fall outside safe DIY scope. Always call a pro immediately if you smell gas, hear abnormal burner noise, or see soot around the vent termination.
