## State Tankless Water Heater E11 Error Code — What It Means

E11 on a State tankless water heater indicates an ignition failure or flame-not-established fault. The unit attempted to ignite but either did not produce a spark, did not light the burner, or did not sense flame after startup. This is the same fault class as error code 111 (flame failure) documented in related A. O. Smith and State platform units.

The control board expects to prove flame within a few seconds of opening the gas valve. When the flame rod does not detect combustion or the ignition system does not fire, the sequence aborts and the error appears. Common root causes include blocked venting, low gas supply, dirty ignition components, and wiring or sensor contamination.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted air intake or exhaust vent** Lint, debris, ice, or improper installation can prevent proper combustion airflow and cause ignition failure.
- **Inadequate gas supply or pressure** Closed gas valve, undersized line, low pressure, or empty propane tank will prevent ignition or stable flame.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (flame rod)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame rod prevents the control from sensing flame even when the burner lights.
- **Failed igniter or ignition transformer** A weak or broken igniter will not produce spark, and a faulty transformer will not supply voltage to the ignition circuit.
- **Damaged ignition wiring or poor ground** Cracked ignition cord, loose connections, or corroded flame-rod wire will interrupt spark generation or flame signal.
- **Burner contamination or scale buildup** Debris, scale, or sediment in the burner chamber or heat exchanger can interfere with combustion or sensor operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check and clear the air intake and exhaust vent** for blockage, lint, nests, ice, or improper termination. Verify that both pipes are free, level or sloped correctly, and meet code clearances.
2. **Confirm gas supply is on and adequate.** Open the manual gas shutoff, check that the gas meter or propane tank has fuel, and verify that other gas appliances in the home operate normally.
3. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor and igniter.** Remove the burner cover or access panel, locate the flame rod and ignition electrode, and gently clean both with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon and oxidation.
4. **Check ignition wiring and connections.** Examine the ignition cord and flame-rod wire for cracks, corrosion, or looseness. Verify that the flame rod is properly grounded and that all connectors are seated.
5. **Test for spark and flame.** Initiate a hot-water call and observe the ignition sequence. Listen for spark clicking and watch for burner ignition. If there is no spark, suspect the igniter or transformer. If flame appears but the error persists, suspect the flame sensor or wiring.
6. **Inspect the burner and combustion chamber** for debris, scale, or soot. Remove any contamination and vacuum the area. Replace gaskets if disturbed during cleaning.
7. **Clear the error and test.** Reset power to the unit, open a hot-water tap, and verify that the unit ignites and runs without faulting. Repeat the test several times to confirm stable operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas connections, if you do not observe spark during ignition, if the unit continues to fault after cleaning and wiring checks, or if gas pressure testing or control-board diagnostics are required. A technician can measure gas inlet pressure, test ignition transformer output voltage, verify flame-sensor microamp signal, and safely replace control or valve components. Professional service is always recommended when the fault involves gas delivery, electrical safety, or repeated ignition failures.
