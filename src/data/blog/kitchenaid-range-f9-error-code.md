## KitchenAid Range F9 Error Code — What It Means

KitchenAid defines F9 or F9 E0 on electric ranges and wall ovens as a detected incoming power supply problem. The control has sensed either miswired home electrical supply at the hard-wire connection or an incorrect voltage or line noise condition, often during or after a power interruption. KitchenAid specifically warns that the electrical outlet or terminal block may be miswired, with L2 and N swapped on the connection.

This is not a temperature sensor fault. The code points to the supply side: branch circuit wiring, the terminal block at the back of the range, or transient voltage events from the utility. If the range was recently installed, KitchenAid advises calling a qualified electrician to verify the electrical supply before any appliance repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when F9 appears, but KitchenAid's own guidance says to verify supply wiring and the terminal block connection first. An electrician can confirm correct L2 and N landing in minutes and avoid a needless $200+ board swap.

## Common Causes

- **Miswired terminal block or hard-wire connection (~40%)** L2 and neutral conductors are swapped or incorrectly landed at the appliance's terminal block, the most common cause KitchenAid cites for F9.
- **Miswired home outlet or branch circuit (~25%)** Incorrect wiring at the breaker panel or receptacle feeds the wrong voltage phase to the range, triggering the fault.
- **Power outage or line noise event (~20%)** Transient voltage spikes or utility line noise during a power interruption are interpreted by the control as an incorrect voltage condition.
- **Defective main control board (~7%)** If all wiring checks pass, the control board itself may have failed and is falsely reporting a supply fault.
- **Keys pressed during control startup (~5%)** Pressing buttons within the first 10 seconds of power-up before the control initializes can trigger F9.
- **Damaged wiring harness or connectors (~3%)** Loose or corroded connections between the terminal block and control board can create intermittent voltage readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker.** Flip both breakers (if a double-breaker 240 V circuit) to the full OFF position and wait one full minute to allow the control to discharge and reset.
2. **Restore power and wait.** Turn the breakers back on and do not touch any buttons on the range for at least 10 seconds. Observe whether F9 reappears within the first minute of power-up.
3. **Verify the supply connection at the terminal block.** If the code returns, turn power off again. Remove the rear access panel and inspect the terminal block where the home supply conductors land. Confirm that L1, L2, and neutral are landed per the installation instructions and that L2 and N are not swapped.
4. **Check for loose or burned terminals.** Tighten every terminal screw at the block. Look for discoloration, heat damage, or corrosion on lugs and conductors. Replace any damaged conductors or terminal hardware.
5. **Measure incoming voltage.** If you are a qualified electrician or technician, measure L1 to N, L2 to N, and L1 to L2 with a multimeter. You should see approximately 120 V on each leg to neutral and 240 V leg to leg. Significant deviation indicates a utility or panel fault.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness inside the range.** Trace the wires from the terminal block to the main control board. Check every connector for tight seating, corrosion, or pinched insulation.
7. **Replace the main control board if wiring is correct.** When all supply and appliance wiring has been verified and the fault persists, the control board is the remaining likely cause. Order the board by your range's full model number and swap it per the service sheet.
8. **Monitor operation after repair.** Run the oven through a full bake cycle and observe for 24 hours to confirm the fault does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician first if the range was recently installed, if you see any burned or discolored wiring at the terminal block, or if you are not comfortable working inside a 240 V electrical panel. Miswired supply connections can create shock and fire hazards. Once the electrician confirms correct supply voltage and landing, call an appliance service technician if the F9 code persists. The technician will perform appliance-side diagnostics, measure harness continuity, and replace the control board if needed. Do not attempt to replace the control board or open the range back panel while power is connected.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $100-250 for electrician wiring correction, $250-400 if control board truly needed.
