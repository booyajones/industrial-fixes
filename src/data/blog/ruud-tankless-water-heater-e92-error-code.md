## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E92 Error — What It Means

The E92 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame sensing or ignition issue. The unit has detected a problem with establishing or maintaining a flame, or the flame sensor is not reading the flame correctly. This safety feature prevents the heater from operating when combustion conditions are not safe or properly verified.

Because error code meanings can vary by specific model and firmware version, consult your owner's manual or the diagnostic chart on the inside of the service panel for the exact definition for your unit. The general pattern points to flame detection circuits, but the exact trigger condition may differ across Ruud's tankless lineup.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve when the real culprit is a dirty flame rod that costs nothing to clean with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensing electrode prevents the control board from detecting the flame, even when the burner is firing correctly.
- **Insufficient or interrupted gas supply (~20%)** Low gas pressure, a closed valve, or air in the gas line prevents the burner from igniting or maintaining a stable flame.
- **Failed or weak flame rod sensor (~15%)** The flame rod itself has failed and cannot generate the small current needed to signal the control board that a flame is present.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition assembly (~10%)** The igniter does not spark reliably or the spark gap is incorrect, so the burner fails to light even when gas is present.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The main PCB has a failed flame-sensing circuit or corrupted firmware that misreads normal flame signals as errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve upstream of the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** and locate the burner assembly and flame rod (a thin metal probe near the burner, usually with a white ceramic insulator).
3. **Disconnect the flame rod wire** from the terminal and unscrew the flame rod from its mounting bracket.
4. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper (320-grit or finer) or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits, soot, and corrosion. Wipe the ceramic insulator clean as well.
5. **Inspect the flame rod insulator** for cracks or chips. If damaged, replace the entire flame rod assembly.
6. **Reinstall the flame rod** and reconnect the wire, making sure the rod sits in the correct position relative to the burner flame path (consult your model's diagram).
7. **Open the gas valve** and restore electrical power. Open a hot water tap and observe whether the burner lights and the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you smell gas, if the burner does not light after confirming gas supply and cleaning the flame rod, or if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances. A pro should also handle control board replacement, gas valve testing, and any situation where the error persists after replacing the flame rod and verifying adequate gas pressure. If the unit is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
