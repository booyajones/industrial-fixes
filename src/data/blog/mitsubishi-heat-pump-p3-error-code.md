## Mitsubishi P3 Error Code — What It Means

The P3 error on Mitsubishi Electric heat pumps indicates an outdoor unit pipe temperature sensor fault. The outdoor control board is detecting an abnormality in the pipe thermistor circuit, meaning the sensor may be open, shorted, disconnected, miswired, or reporting an implausible temperature reading. This is not a refrigerant pressure code but a specific sensor circuit problem on the outdoor unit.

[Jump to Fix](#fix)

## Common Causes

- **Failed pipe temperature sensor** The thermistor itself has degraded internally, reading open, shorted, or out of specification.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector** The plug at the sensor or control board has backed out, become corroded, or lost contact.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** The harness has been pinched, cut, or has insulation breakdown causing an intermittent or false reading.
- **Outdoor control board fault** The PCB input circuit that reads the sensor is faulty, producing a false code even when the sensor and wiring are good.
- **Poor sensor mounting or thermal contact** The sensor has come loose from the pipe or lost its insulation wrap, causing abnormal temperature readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the P3 code** on the indoor unit display or remote using the Mitsubishi error retrieval procedure for your model.
2. **Power off the system** at the outdoor disconnect and wait 60 seconds before working on any components.
3. **Inspect the outdoor pipe thermistor and harness** for loose connectors, corrosion, pinched wires, broken conductors, or poor seating at both the sensor and the control board.
4. **Measure the sensor resistance** with a multimeter and compare the reading to the manufacturer's resistance curve for that thermistor type. An open circuit, short, or out-of-range value means replace the sensor.
5. **Check the outdoor PCB input** if the sensor and wiring test within specification. A board fault can produce the same code even when the sensor is good.
6. **Replace the failed component** (sensor, harness, or board), reassemble all connections, and verify the sensor is properly clipped and insulated to the pipe.
7. **Clear the fault code** per your service manual, restore power, and verify normal operation through a full heating or cooling cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with line voltage, if you do not have a multimeter and the manufacturer's resistance specifications for your specific model, or if the fault persists after you have verified the sensor and wiring. Outdoor control board diagnosis and refrigerant-circuit work require EPA certification and specialized gauges. If your system is under warranty, contact a Mitsubishi-authorized service provider to avoid voiding coverage.
