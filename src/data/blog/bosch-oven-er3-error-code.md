## Bosch Oven ER3 Error Code — What It Means

ER3 on a Bosch oven indicates an overheating or excessive-temperature fault. The oven's control system has detected that internal temperature has climbed too high and has triggered a protective shutdown to prevent damage or a fire hazard. This code is not always documented in official Bosch consumer manuals, but third-party repair references consistently describe it as an overheating condition.

The fault will not clear until the oven has cooled completely and the underlying cause has been corrected. In most cases the problem is related to restricted airflow, a failed cooling fan, or a temperature sensor that is reporting incorrectly or failing to regulate the heating element properly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or temperature sensor before checking the cooling fan and airflow. Always verify the fan runs and vents are clear before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked ventilation or poor airflow (~35%)** Dust, foil, pans, or cabinet clearance issues obstruct the oven's cooling vents and internal airflow path, trapping heat and triggering the overtemp protection.
- **Failed or weak cooling fan (~30%)** The cooling fan motor does not run, runs intermittently, or spins too slowly to move enough air through the oven cavity, allowing temperature to rise unchecked.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermostat (~20%)** The oven temperature sensor is out of calibration, has failed open or shorted, or has loose wiring, causing the control to misread cavity temperature or fail to shut off heating at the correct setpoint.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors (~10%)** Electrical connections to the fan, sensor, or control board have worked loose or been damaged by heat or vibration, creating intermittent faults or false overtemp signals.
- **Door seal problems (~5%)** A torn or degraded door gasket allows heat to escape or creates uneven temperature distribution, which can worsen overheating symptoms in combination with other faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power at the breaker** and let the oven cool completely before opening panels or touching any components. Do not attempt diagnostics while the unit is hot.
2. **Inspect all ventilation openings** on the top, rear, and sides of the oven. Remove any foil, pans, dust buildup, or debris blocking the vents. Confirm cabinet clearances match the installation manual if the unit is built-in.
3. **Access the cooling fan** by removing the rear service panel or lower kick panel (consult your model's service manual for location). Spin the fan blade by hand to confirm it rotates freely with no grinding or binding.
4. **Test cooling fan operation** by restoring power and turning the oven on. The fan should start within a few seconds or once the cavity reaches a threshold temperature. If the fan does not run, check for voltage at the fan connector and inspect the wiring harness for damage or looseness.
5. **Locate and inspect the oven temperature sensor**, typically a metal probe mounted in the upper rear or side wall of the cavity. Check that the sensor wiring is intact, connectors are seated firmly, and there is no visible heat damage or corrosion.
6. **Test the temperature sensor resistance** if you have a multimeter. At room temperature the sensor should read a stable resistance (consult your model's wiring diagram for the specification, often around 1,080 ohms at 70 °F for many Bosch models, though this varies). Replace the sensor if the reading is open, shorted, or drifts erratically.
7. **Replace the failed component** (fan motor, temperature sensor, or wiring harness). Reassemble all panels, restore power, and run the oven through a complete preheat and bake cycle to verify ER3 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240 V appliance, if the cooling fan and temperature sensor both test good but the code persists, or if diagnostics point to a control board or user interface fault. A technician has the Bosch service software and wiring diagrams to perform component-level testing and can safely handle high-voltage circuits. Also call a pro if the oven is still under warranty, since DIY repairs may void coverage. If you smell burning plastic, see arcing, or the oven trips the breaker repeatedly, shut off power immediately and do not attempt further diagnostics yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts (fan or sensor), 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150–300 including diagnosis and parts.
