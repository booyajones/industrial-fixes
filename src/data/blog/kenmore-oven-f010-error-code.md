## Kenmore Oven F010 Error Code — What It Means

The F010 error code on Kenmore ovens typically indicates a runaway oven condition, meaning the oven temperature has exceeded safe limits or the control has detected an abnormal heating cycle. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the oven cabinet for model-specific details.

This code is a safety feature that prevents overheating and potential fire hazards. The oven will usually shut down heating elements and lock out operation until the fault is cleared. The problem may involve the temperature sensor, control board, or a relay that has stuck closed and allowed continuous heating.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a simple resistance test of the oven temperature sensor with a multimeter often reveals the real problem and costs much less.

## Common Causes

- **Oven temperature sensor failure (~50%)** The sensor probe may have drifted out of spec or developed an open or short circuit, sending incorrect temperature readings to the control board.
- **Stuck relay on control board (~30%)** A relay that controls power to the bake or broil element can weld closed and allow continuous heating even when the control signals off.
- **Control board fault (~15%)** The electronic oven control may have developed a software glitch or component failure that misreads sensor input or fails to regulate temperature properly.
- **Wiring or connector issue (~5%)** Loose or corroded connections at the sensor or control board can cause intermittent signals that the control interprets as a runaway condition.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error appear immediately after turning on the oven or only after heating for several minutes?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> An immediate error suggests a sensor that is reading out of range or a control board fault rather than an actual overheat condition.<br><strong>No:</strong> An error after heating suggests the oven is truly overheating, which points to a stuck relay or failed sensor that is not reporting high temperature accurately.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you measure the oven sensor resistance with a multimeter at room temperature?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Compare the reading to the resistance table in your service manual (typically around 1,080 to 1,100 ohms at room temperature for most Kenmore models). If it is far outside that range or shows infinite resistance, replace the sensor.<br><strong>No:</strong> If you do not have a multimeter or cannot access the sensor terminals, you may need to call a technician or proceed with a trial replacement of the sensor as the most common part.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After replacing the sensor, does the F010 error still appear?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The control board is the next suspect, especially a stuck relay. Replacement or repair of the board is needed.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor was the problem and the oven should now operate normally.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and verify the oven is completely off before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the oven racks** and locate the temperature sensor probe at the upper rear wall of the oven cavity. It is a metal tube about 1/4 inch in diameter extending a few inches into the oven.
3. **Disconnect the sensor** by removing the rear access panel or lower kick panel (depending on your model) to reach the back of the sensor mounting bracket and unplug the wire connector.
4. **Test the sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance (ohms). At room temperature, it should read close to the value in your model's specification table. If the reading is far off or shows an open circuit, the sensor has failed.
5. **Install a new sensor** by threading it through the mounting hole, securing the bracket with the screw, and reconnecting the wire harness.
6. **Restore power** at the breaker and run a test bake cycle to verify the error does not return.
7. **If the error persists**, inspect the control board for signs of relay damage (burn marks or a relay that rattles when shaken) and consider replacing the board or calling a technician for further diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if you have replaced the temperature sensor and the F010 error continues to appear. A technician can perform relay and board-level diagnostics that require specialized tools and experience. Also call a pro if you see any signs of arcing, burning, or melted wiring inside the oven, as this indicates a more serious electrical fault that should not be tackled without training.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
