## LG Range F9 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 code on an LG range means the upper oven is not heating properly. The control board expects the oven to reach roughly 150°F within about five minutes of starting a bake cycle. When the temperature sensor does not report that rise, the board flags a heating fault and displays F9.

This code appears on both electric and gas models. On electric ranges the problem is usually a failed bake element, relay board, or temperature sensor. On gas models a weak or failing igniter is the most common cause. The igniter may still glow but not draw enough current to open the gas valve.

## Common Causes

- **Failed bake element (electric models)** The lower or upper bake element burns out, breaks, or develops an open circuit so the oven cannot generate heat.
- **Weak or failing igniter (gas models)** The igniter glows but does not draw enough current (typically 3.0–3.6 amps) to prove the gas valve open, so no flame lights.
- **Faulty oven temperature sensor** The thermistor reads open, shorted, or far out of range (normal room temperature is around 1080–1100 Ω), so the board cannot measure true oven temperature.
- **Open thermal fuse or limit switch** A safety fuse has blown due to overheating or age, breaking the heating circuit and preventing the element or igniter from energizing.
- **Failed relay or main control board** The board relay that switches power to the bake element or igniter has failed, or the control logic itself is faulty.
- **Loose or burned wiring connections** Corroded terminals, heat-damaged connectors, or loose plugs at the element, sensor, or board interrupt the circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset the range** by pressing CLEAR/OFF, then turn the circuit breaker off for one minute and restore power to clear transient faults.
2. **Run a bake cycle** at 350°F and watch whether the oven heats at all. If it stays cold or barely warms after five minutes, the fault is real.
3. **Inspect the heating hardware** with power off. On electric models, look at the bake element for breaks, blistering, or loose terminals. On gas models, check the igniter for cracks and verify all wire connections are tight and free of char.
4. **Test the oven temperature sensor** with a multimeter. Disconnect the sensor, measure resistance at room temperature, and compare to your model's service data. Replace the sensor if it reads open, shorted, or far outside the typical 1080–1100 Ω range.
5. **Check continuity through thermal fuses** and any other inline safety devices in the bake circuit. Replace any fuse that reads open.
6. **Measure igniter current on gas models** by clamping an ammeter around one igniter lead during a bake cycle. If the igniter glows but draws less than about 3.0 amps, replace it.
7. **Evaluate the control or relay board** if all heating components, the sensor, fuses, and wiring test good but the oven still will not heat. Verify the board is sending voltage to the element or igniter. If not, replace the board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt electric circuits or natural-gas connections. Gas work requires safe disconnection of the supply line, proper igniter current testing, and leak-checking after reassembly. Electric oven repairs involve high-voltage wiring behind the control panel and inside the oven cavity. A pro can also access the full wiring diagram and service calibration data for your specific LG model, which speeds diagnosis when multiple components test marginal or the fault is intermittent.
