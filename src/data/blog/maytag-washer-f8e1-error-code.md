## Maytag Washer F8E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F8E1 code (sometimes displayed as Lo FL on front-load models) tells you the control board expected the water level to rise but did not detect enough incoming water. This is a no-fill, low-fill, or slow-fill fault. When the code appears, the washer will run a drain routine for about 8 to 10 minutes and ignore button presses during that time.

The code applies to both top-load and front-load Maytag washers. It does not mean the inlet valve or pressure sensor has failed outright. It means something in the water supply chain is restricting flow so the tub cannot reach the target level in the time the control allows.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve assembly first. Before ordering parts, disconnect the hoses and inspect the small mesh screens at the valve inlets for sediment or mineral crust and verify wall valves are turned fully counterclockwise.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens (~40%)** Sediment and mineral deposits collect on the fine mesh filters where the hoses connect to the washer, choking flow.
- **Supply valves not fully open (~25%)** Wall shut-off valves left partially closed after maintenance or moving the machine restrict incoming pressure.
- **Kinked or pinched inlet hoses (~15%)** Hoses crushed behind the washer, frozen in an unheated space, or routed with sharp bends reduce flow below what the control expects.
- **Low household water pressure (~10%)** Weak supply pressure at the wall connection (below approximately 20 PSI in field practice) cannot fill the tub fast enough.
- **Faulty water inlet valve assembly (~8%)** A bad solenoid coil or stuck diaphragm inside the valve prevents it from opening fully even when commanded.
- **Hot and cold hoses reversed (~2%)** On models that require color-to-color connection, swapped hoses can confuse fill logic or reduce available flow if only one side energizes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Wait for the drain routine to finish.** The washer will drain for 8 to 10 minutes and ignore button presses. Do not unplug the machine during this time.
2. **Turn off both hot and cold wall valves** and unplug the washer from the wall outlet.
3. **Pull the washer forward** enough to reach the back panel and locate the two fill hoses (hot and cold). Inspect each hose for kinks, crushing, or ice damage and straighten or replace if needed.
4. **Disconnect each hose at the washer inlet** (where it threads onto the water valve). You will see a small mesh screen inside each inlet port. Use needle-nose pliers or a small pick to gently remove each screen and rinse it under running water, brushing away sediment with a soft toothbrush. Do not tear or distort the mesh.
5. **Reinstall the clean screens** and reconnect the hoses, making sure hot goes to hot and cold to cold if your model uses color-coded inlets. Turn the wall valves fully counterclockwise.
6. **Plug in the washer and run a rinse or fill test cycle.** If the tub fills normally and the code does not return, the repair is complete.
7. **If the code persists**, disconnect power and use a multimeter to check continuity across each inlet valve solenoid coil. Consult your model's wiring diagram for connector locations. Replace the water inlet valve assembly if a coil is open or if you find loose or corroded wiring at the valve harness.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the screens, confirmed strong wall flow, and verified correct hose routing but the code returns. The problem may be a failed pressure sensor, damaged wiring harness, or a fault in the main control board. A pro can measure household water pressure with a gauge, test the inlet valve solenoids in circuit, and check the analog pressure switch or pressure transducer signal. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working around 120 V wiring connections or if your washer requires disassembly of the cabinet to access the valve and harness.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (inlet screens, hoses, or valve), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250 service call including labor and valve if needed.
