## What this code means
The F8E1 code (or LO FL on some front-load models) means your Maytag washer is not detecting the correct amount of incoming water. The control board expects the water level to rise normally during the fill cycle, but it does not see the expected increase. On some top-load models, the door or lid may remain locked while the machine handles the error or runs a brief drain routine.

This is a fill problem. The machine is either getting no water or only a slow trickle. The code does not mean a drain issue or a pump fault. It is telling you the washer cannot complete its fill within the expected time.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve first, but most F8E1 codes are caused by closed supply valves, kinked hoses, or clogged screens at the valve inlet. Check water supply and clean the screens before buying a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Water supply valves not fully open (~30%)** One or both shutoff valves behind the washer are partially or fully closed, preventing adequate water flow into the machine.
- **Clogged inlet screens (~25%)** Mineral deposits, sediment, or debris block the small mesh screens at the water inlet valve connection points.
- **Kinked or pinched inlet hoses (~20%)** The rubber supply hoses are bent, crushed, or routed incorrectly, restricting water flow.
- **Low household water pressure (~10%)** The home's water supply delivers insufficient pressure or flow to the washer connection point.
- **Faulty water inlet valve assembly (~10%)** The valve coils or internal ports fail to open properly even when power and water supply are good.
- **Anti-flood device or inline flow restrictor (~5%)** An aftermarket flood-prevention device installed on the hoses reduces water pressure below what the washer needs.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the washer.
2. **Shut off both hot and cold water supply valves** behind the washer and pull the machine forward enough to access the connections.
3. **Inspect the inlet hoses** for kinks, pinches, or damage. Straighten any kinks and replace any hose that feels stiff or cracked inside.
4. **Disconnect both hoses** from the back of the washer and check for inline anti-flood devices. Remove any device that restricts flow.
5. **Remove and clean the inlet screens** on the water inlet valve ports using a small pick or brush. Rinse them under running water and reinstall.
6. **Reconnect the hoses** firmly to the correct ports (hot to red, cold to blue) and turn the supply valves fully open.
7. **Restore power and run a rinse or fill test cycle** to confirm the washer fills normally and the code does not return.
8. **If the error persists**, replace the water inlet valve assembly and retest. If the code still appears, inspect wiring connections and consult the service manual for pressure-sensor diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you've confirmed both supply valves are open, cleaned the inlet screens, checked the hoses, and replaced the water inlet valve but the F8E1 code still appears. At that point the fault may involve wiring, the pressure sensor, or the main control board. Also call a plumber if your household water pressure is low at multiple fixtures, since that is a supply-side issue the washer cannot fix. If the washer is still under warranty, contact Maytag service before replacing any parts yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
