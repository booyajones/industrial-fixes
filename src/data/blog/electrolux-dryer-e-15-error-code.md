## Electrolux Dryer E15 Error — What It Means

The E15 error code on Electrolux dryers typically indicates a problem with the heating system or temperature monitoring circuit. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consulting your owner's manual or wiring diagram is important. The code usually appears when the control board detects a fault in the temperature sensor, thermistor, heating element circuit, or associated wiring. In some models, it may also point to a control board communication error with the heating components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first when the real issue is a loose or corroded connector at the thermistor or a broken wire. Test the sensor resistance with a multimeter before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~50%)** The sensor that monitors drum or exhaust temperature has drifted out of range or failed open, sending incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~25%)** The plug or spade connector at the thermistor or heating element has oxidized or vibrated loose, breaking the signal path.
- **Broken wire in the harness (~15%)** A wire between the control board and the heating assembly has fractured from heat cycling or mechanical stress, creating an open circuit.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** The main electronic control has developed a fault in the temperature monitoring circuit or internal relay, though this is less common.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** (or top/front panel, depending on your model) using a screwdriver to expose the heating assembly and drum area.
3. **Locate the thermistor** or temperature sensor, usually mounted on the blower housing, heating duct, or near the heating element canister.
4. **Inspect all wire connectors** on the thermistor, heating element, and thermal fuses for corrosion, looseness, or burn marks, and reseat each firmly.
5. **Test the thermistor** by disconnecting its plug and measuring resistance across its terminals with a multimeter set to ohms; consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance range at room temperature.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the reading is open, shorted, or significantly out of specification; if the reading is correct, trace the wiring harness back to the control board for breaks or damage.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the error has cleared and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if testing shows the thermistor and wiring are intact but the error persists. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board and heating relay circuits. Also call a pro if the dryer uses a gas burner assembly, as gas appliance work requires specialized knowledge and licensing in many areas.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-60 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
