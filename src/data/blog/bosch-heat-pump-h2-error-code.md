## Bosch Heat Pump H2 Error Code — What It Means

The H2 error code on Bosch heat pump systems indicates a fault condition detected by the control board. The exact meaning of H2 varies by model and installation configuration, so you should consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit cover to confirm what system parameter triggered the alarm. In many cases, alphanumeric codes like H2 point to temperature sensor problems, refrigerant pressure issues, or communication errors between control components.

Because Bosch uses different code definitions across product lines, do not assume H2 means the same thing as on another brand or even another Bosch model. The fault may be intermittent or persistent. Record any other symptoms such as unusual noises, failure to heat, or simultaneous error codes, and note whether the system attempts to restart or locks out completely.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real culprit is a dirty or failed temperature sensor. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter and compare readings to the model's specification table before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Temperature sensor fault (~35%)** A thermistor on the coil, discharge line, or return air may have drifted out of range, failed open, or developed a short.
- **Wiring or connector problem (~25%)** Corrosion, vibration, or moisture can loosen connectors or break stranded wire at crimp points, sending erratic signals to the board.
- **Low refrigerant charge (~20%)** If the system is undercharged due to a slow leak, pressure or temperature readings will fall outside safe operating windows and trigger protection codes.
- **Control board calibration or firmware issue (~12%)** Occasionally a software glitch or aging capacitor on the board causes false alarms even when sensors and refrigerant levels are correct.
- **Blocked or frozen coil (~8%)** Ice buildup or debris on the outdoor coil can skew temperature readings and prevent normal defrost cycles, leading to fault codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the disconnect switch or breaker serving the heat pump and wait two minutes to allow capacitors to discharge and the control board to reset.
2. **Record the error code** and any other displayed codes or flashing LED patterns, then consult your owner's manual or the legend printed inside the service panel to confirm the H2 definition for your model.
3. **Inspect all wiring connectors** at the outdoor unit, indoor air handler, and thermostat for loose pins, corrosion, or moisture. Reseat any suspect connectors firmly.
4. **Check and clean air filters** at the indoor unit and verify that all supply and return grilles are open. Restricted airflow can cause temperature anomalies that trigger faults.
5. **Examine the outdoor coil** for dirt, leaves, or ice. Gently rinse debris from the inside out with a garden hose on low pressure. Do not use a pressure washer.
6. **Test temperature sensors** if you have a multimeter and the service manual. Measure resistance at room temperature and compare to the published table. Replace any sensor reading open, shorted, or far out of range.
7. **Call a licensed HVAC technician** if the error persists after these checks. The technician will verify refrigerant pressures, test control board outputs, and update firmware if a service bulletin applies.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional immediately if you smell refrigerant (a sweet chemical odor), see oil stains around pipe joints, or hear hissing from the refrigerant lines. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification, specialized gauges, and vacuum equipment. Also call a pro if the error returns after you have cleaned coils, replaced sensors per the manual, and verified all connections. A technician can perform a full system checkout, measure superheat and subcooling, check compressor performance, and update control board firmware if a known bug matches your symptoms. Because heat pumps are complex and codes vary widely by model, professional diagnosis often saves money compared to trial-and-error part replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-500.
