## Navien Tankless Water Heater E164 Error — What It Means

The E164 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a flame safety fault. The unit has detected a condition where the flame is not igniting properly, is unstable, or the flame sensor is not reading correctly. This code triggers a lockout to prevent unsafe operation.

The heater's control board monitors the flame signal through a flame rod or sensor. When the signal falls outside the expected range during ignition or operation, the system shuts down and displays E164. This can stem from issues with gas delivery, ignition components, sensor contamination, or venting problems that affect combustion quality.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flame sensor or ignition assembly without first checking gas pressure or venting. Confirm adequate gas flow to the unit and inspect the vent termination for blockages before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Insufficient gas supply or pressure (~35%)** Low inlet gas pressure, partially closed valve, undersized gas line, or shared line with other appliances can starve the burner and prevent stable flame establishment.
- **Contaminated or misaligned flame sensor (~30%)** Carbon deposits, corrosion, or physical misalignment of the flame rod prevent accurate flame detection even when combustion is occurring.
- **Venting obstruction or improper termination (~20%)** Blocked intake or exhaust vents, ice accumulation, bird nests, or incorrect vent pipe installation disrupt airflow and combustion stability.
- **Igniter or ignition assembly failure (~10%)** A weak or cracked igniter may produce intermittent spark or insufficient heat to reliably light the gas, triggering the safety lockout.
- **Control board sensor input drift (~5%)** The main control board's flame detection circuit can develop faulty readings due to age, moisture intrusion, or component degradation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the main disconnect switch and close the gas shutoff valve at the unit.
2. **Inspect the vent terminations** outside the building for obstructions, ice buildup, lint, or debris blocking intake or exhaust pipes.
3. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve to the heater is fully open and checking that other gas appliances operate normally.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the unit according to the service manual and locate the flame sensor rod inside the combustion chamber.
5. **Clean the flame sensor** with fine sandpaper or emery cloth to remove carbon and corrosion, then reposition it so the rod sits in the flame path without touching the burner.
6. **Check vent pipe connections** inside the unit for proper sealing, correct slope, and absence of condensate pooling that could obstruct airflow.
7. **Restore gas and power**, run a hot-water tap to initiate a call for heat, and observe the ignition sequence through the viewing window to confirm stable flame and no error code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas connections, if gas pressure testing is required, or if the error persists after cleaning the flame sensor and verifying venting. Tankless water heaters require specific gas-pressure and combustion-air measurements that need calibrated gauges and training. A technician will test inlet and manifold gas pressure, measure flame signal microamps, inspect the heat exchanger for scale or blockage, and verify all safety interlocks are functioning. Attempting repairs beyond basic sensor cleaning without proper tools and knowledge can lead to unsafe operation or carbon-monoxide hazards.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
