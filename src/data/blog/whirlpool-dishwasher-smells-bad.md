## Whirlpool Dishwasher Smells Bad — What's Happening

When your Whirlpool dishwasher smells bad, it's not a fault code but a symptom of trapped organic material or moisture. The most common sources are a dirty filter, food residue on tub walls and racks, mold or mildew in the door gasket, or a restricted drain that leaves stagnant water behind.

If the smell is sulfur-like or eggy, the issue often traces to debris in the air gap, a clogged drain hose, or food buildup in the garbage disposal inlet where the dishwasher drains. Machines that don't air out properly after the cycle can also hold odors from moisture and leftover food particles.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Dirty or clogged filter** Food grease and debris collect in the filter, creating the most common source of dishwasher odor.
- **Food residue on tub walls or spray areas** Film from food particles builds up on the interior surfaces and holds odor over time.
- **Mold or mildew on door gasket** Moisture trapped in the gasket crevices allows mold and mildew to grow and smell.
- **Restricted drain or standing water** A kinked or clogged drain hose or sump area leaves stagnant water that develops odor.
- **Dirty air gap or garbage disposal inlet** When the dishwasher drains through an air gap or disposal, debris in those connections causes smell.
- **Poor ventilation after cycle** If the tub stays damp and closed after washing, stale odor persists inside the machine.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm where the odor is coming from by checking the dishwasher tub, the sink drain, the air gap if installed, and the garbage disposal connection.
2. Power down the dishwasher and empty it, then pull out the lower rack to access the filter and sump area.
3. Remove the filter assembly per your model's instructions, wash it in warm soapy water with a soft brush, and reinstall only after it is clean and seated correctly.
4. Inspect the tub bottom and sump for trapped food or grease and wipe accessible debris out by hand or with a damp cloth.
5. Inspect the door gasket for mold or residue, wipe it clean with warm soapy water, and dry it thoroughly.
6. Run a cleaning cycle with a dishwasher-safe bowl of white vinegar placed on the top rack and use an air-dry or energy-saving dry option if available.
7. Check the drain hose for kinks or blockages, flush the air gap if present, and run the garbage disposal to clear any debris in the disposal inlet.
8. After the cycle finishes, leave the door slightly ajar to allow the tub to air out and prevent moisture buildup.

## When to Call a Pro

If the odor persists after cleaning the filter, gasket, and drain path, call a technician to inspect for hidden sludge in the sump, a failing drain pump, or a recirculation defect. Persistent smells can indicate a mechanical drainage issue or contamination deep in the wash system that requires disassembly and professional cleaning.
