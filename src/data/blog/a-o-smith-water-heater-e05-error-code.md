## A.O. Smith Water Heater E05 Error Code — What It Means

The E05 error code on A.O. Smith water heaters indicates a fault detected by the control system, but the exact meaning varies by model and product line. On some models it refers to a temperature sensor issue, while on others it may point to a control board communication problem or a different system fault. Because A.O. Smith uses different control platforms across residential tank, tankless, and hybrid heat-pump models, the same code can have different definitions.

Always consult your specific model's owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit to verify what E05 means for your heater. Do not assume the code definition from one model applies to another. The error will typically prevent normal heating operation until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real fault is a loose connector or a failed temperature sensor. Check all sensor wiring and measure sensor resistance before buying a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty temperature sensor (~40%)** A failed or out-of-range thermistor sends invalid readings to the control board, triggering the error.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~25%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen connectors or corrode terminals at the sensor or control board, breaking the signal path.
- **Control board fault (~20%)** The main control module may have a failed input circuit or firmware glitch that misreads sensor data.
- **Transient power surge (~10%)** A brief voltage spike can lock the controller in an error state even though no component has actually failed.
- **Incorrect sensor or wiring installation (~5%)** After service, a technician may have connected the wrong sensor type or reversed polarity, confusing the controller.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply (if applicable) or the water-heater disconnect switch.
2. **Locate the error-code definition** in your owner's manual or on the wiring diagram label affixed to the unit to confirm what E05 means for your specific model.
3. **Power-cycle the heater** by leaving the breaker off for two full minutes, then turn it back on and observe whether the error reappears.
4. **Remove the access panel** covering the control board and identify all temperature-sensor connectors (often small two-pin plugs).
5. **Inspect each connector and wire** for corrosion, moisture, or looseness; clean terminals with electrical contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
6. **Disconnect one sensor lead** and use a digital multimeter set to ohms to measure the sensor's resistance; compare the reading to the resistance table in your manual.
7. **Replace the sensor** if the resistance is out of range or if the sensor shows physical damage; use only the OEM part number listed for your model to avoid compatibility issues.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections, if the error persists after replacing sensors and checking wiring, or if you cannot locate a wiring diagram or resistance chart for your model. Professionals have diagnostic tools to read live sensor values and can quickly isolate controller faults. If your heater is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage. Gas-fired units also benefit from a combustion-safety inspection whenever the control system is serviced.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-400.
