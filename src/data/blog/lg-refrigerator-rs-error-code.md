## LG Refrigerator rS Error Code — What It Means

The rS code on an LG refrigerator indicates a fault in the refrigerator-side temperature detection system. LG identifies this as a problem with the fresh-food compartment sensor (the R1 thermistor in the upper refrigerator section). The fault means the main control board has detected either a short circuit or an open circuit in the sensor wiring or the sensor itself. When the control board loses valid temperature feedback from the refrigerator compartment, it cannot regulate cooling properly and appliance performance drops.

This code points specifically to the sensor circuit rather than the compressor, fan, or defrost system. The sensor may be physically intact but disconnected, or the thermistor element inside may have failed and reads out of range. Less commonly, the circuit fault can be at the control-board connector or in a damaged wire along the harness.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real fault is a $15 sensor or a loose connector pin. Always test the sensor harness for continuity and inspect both ends of the connector before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~40%)** The refrigerator sensor plug at the evaporator housing or at the control board has backed out, corroded, or lost contact due to vibration or moisture.
- **Open or broken wiring harness (~25%)** The wire between the sensor and the control board is pinched, cut, or has an internal break that opens the circuit.
- **Failed refrigerator thermistor (~20%)** The thermistor element inside the sensor has shorted or gone open and no longer reads within the valid resistance range.
- **Shorted sensor due to moisture or ice (~10%)** Water or frost has penetrated the sensor housing and created a short across the thermistor leads.
- **Faulty control-board sensor input (~5%)** The sensor circuit traces or input stage on the main control PCB have failed, even though the sensor itself is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** for a full 60 seconds to reset the control board, then plug it back into a direct wall outlet (not a power strip or extension cord).
2. **Wait five minutes** for the control to complete its startup sequence and internal monitoring cycle, then check whether the rS code reappears on the display.
3. **Locate the refrigerator sensor** by removing shelves and any covers on the rear wall of the fresh-food compartment or near the evaporator housing (consult your model's service manual for exact placement).
4. **Inspect the sensor connector** at both the sensor end and the control-board end for loose pins, corrosion, moisture, or frost buildup, and disconnect and reconnect each plug firmly.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter across the sensor leads if you have access to the service data for your exact model, looking for an open (infinite ohms) or short (near zero ohms) condition.
6. **Replace the refrigerator thermistor** if the connector is intact but resistance is out of range or if the code returns immediately after reseating the connector.
7. **Reassemble the interior panels**, restore power, and verify that the rS code clears and the refrigerator compartment begins to cool normally within 30 minutes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the refrigerator sensor behind interior panels, if you are uncomfortable working with a multimeter to test resistance, or if replacing the sensor does not clear the rS code. A technician can trace the harness for hidden breaks, verify control-board inputs with diagnostic software, and address any related defrost or airflow issues that may contribute to sensor failures. Also call a pro if the refrigerator compartment shows heavy frost buildup or airflow blockage that suggests a secondary defrost fault requiring evaporator removal.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 including service call and sensor.
