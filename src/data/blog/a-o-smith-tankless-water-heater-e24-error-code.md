## A.O. Smith Tankless E24 Error — What It Means

The E24 error code on A.O. Smith tankless water heaters can indicate different conditions depending on the specific model. Without manufacturer documentation for this exact code, the precise meaning may vary. Many tankless water heater error codes in this range relate to temperature sensor faults, control board communication problems, or safety limit issues. Consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover to confirm what E24 means for your particular model number.

Because error code definitions are not standardized across all A.O. Smith tankless models, attempting repairs without knowing the exact cause can waste time and money. The code may point to a failing component or simply a transient electronic glitch that clears with a power cycle. Always verify the meaning in your documentation before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a loose sensor connection or a failing thermistor. Check all sensor plugs and measure thermistor resistance before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Temperature sensor fault (~35%)** A thermistor or sensor may have failed, drifted out of calibration, or lost electrical contact at its plug.
- **Control board communication error (~25%)** The main control board may have a firmware glitch or a solder joint failure that interrupts internal signals.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~20%)** Sensor or board connectors can oxidize or back out over time, breaking the circuit and triggering an error.
- **Low water flow or pressure (~10%)** Insufficient flow can confuse the control logic and cause the unit to flag a sensor or safety error.
- **Transient power surge (~10%)** A brief voltage spike or brownout may have locked the controller into an error state that clears with a reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and shut the gas valve to the closed position.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws at the bottom and lifting the panel clear of the unit.
3. **Locate the control board** and inspect every wire connector for tight contact, corrosion, or burn marks.
4. **Disconnect each temperature sensor** plug one at a time and examine the pins for oxidation or moisture.
5. **Measure thermistor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms and compare readings to the chart in your manual.
6. **Replace any sensor** that reads open, shorted, or far outside the expected range for room temperature.
7. **Reassemble the unit** and restore power, then call for hot water and observe whether the error returns or clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the error persists after replacing sensors and checking wiring, or if you lack a multimeter and the skills to test thermistor resistance. A technician can cross-reference the exact error code in the service manual, measure voltages at the board, and confirm whether the heat exchanger or gas valve is contributing to the fault. Professional diagnosis saves money when the code meaning is ambiguous or when multiple components may be involved.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $180-400.
