## Speed Queen Washer EI:09 Error Code — What It Means

The EI:09 code on your Speed Queen washer is not a widely published factory code, but it falls into the same fault family as Er, OF or Er, oF overfill and water-level errors. It means the control detected an unsafe high water level or the machine could not lower the water level fast enough during a drain cycle. The washer has shut down to prevent flooding or damage. In most cases, either the inlet valve is leaking water into the tub when it should be closed, the pressure hose that senses water level is blocked or kinked, or the drain path is restricted so water cannot exit quickly enough. The machine is protecting itself and your floor, so the fault needs to be cleared before you can resume normal operation.

## Common Causes

- **Leaking inlet valve** The water inlet valve is not sealing fully when power is off, allowing water to seep into the tub continuously and trigger an overfill fault.
- **Blocked or kinked pressure hose** The small air hose that connects the tub to the pressure sensor is clogged, kinked, or has an air leak, so the control cannot read the true water level.
- **Slow or restricted drain** A clogged drain pump, restricted drain hose, or plumbing backup prevents water from exiting the tub fast enough, and the control sees the level staying too high.
- **Drain hose installation problem** The drain hose is inserted too far into the standpipe or laundry sink, creating a siphon block or backflow that slows drainage.
- **Air trap or pressure-switch issue** The air trap chamber or pressure switch has debris, a loose connection, or a crack that prevents accurate water-level sensing.
- **Control or sensor interpretation fault** If all mechanical checks pass, the main control or water-level sensing circuit may have a fault that misreads the tub level.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off the water supply** at the hoses behind the washer, then unplug the machine from power to prevent further filling or damage.
2. **Drain the tub manually** if water is still present by setting the dial to drain/spin or by using the drain hose at the bottom front of the cabinet on some models.
3. **Reset the fault** by unplugging the washer for two minutes, then plug it back in and check whether the code returns immediately or after starting a new cycle.
4. **Test the inlet valve for seepage** by leaving the machine off and the water supply on, then watching the empty tub for five to ten minutes to see if water creeps in on its own.
5. **Inspect the pressure hose** from the air trap at the bottom of the tub to the pressure switch or control, looking for kinks, blockages, loose fittings, or cracks, and clear or reconnect as needed.
6. **Run a drain-and-spin cycle** to confirm the drain pump activates and water exits the tub quickly without backing up at the standpipe or laundry sink.
7. **Check drain hose installation** by verifying the hose end is inserted no more than four to six inches into the standpipe and that the plumbing drain is not clogged or slow.
8. **Replace the inlet valve** if you saw seepage in step four, or replace the pressure hose if you found damage in step five, then test a short cycle to verify the fault clears.

## When to Call a Pro

If you have replaced the inlet valve and cleared the pressure hose but the EI:09 code returns immediately on every cycle, or if you are not comfortable working with water connections and testing electrical components, call a qualified appliance technician. Persistent faults after mechanical repairs usually point to a control-board or sensor-circuit issue that requires diagnostic software or component-level troubleshooting. Also call for help if the washer continues to fill uncontrollably even with the water supply off, as that may indicate a stuck valve or a more complex plumbing cross-connection that needs professional isolation.
