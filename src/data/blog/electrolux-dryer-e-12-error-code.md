## Electrolux Dryer E12 Error Code — What It Means

The E12 error code on Electrolux dryers indicates a fault in the heating system or control circuit, though the exact definition can vary by model. Some versions point to a problem with temperature sensing or thermostat signals, while others indicate a communication error between the control board and heating elements. Because Electrolux uses different diagnostics across its model lines, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cabinet to confirm the precise meaning for your dryer.

When this code appears, the dryer typically stops heating or shuts down mid-cycle to prevent damage. The fault may involve thermostats, wiring connections, the heating element circuit, or the main control board itself. Start with the simplest checks and work toward component replacement only after ruling out loose connections and visible damage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board when the real issue is a loose or corroded plug at the thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat. Test continuity through each safety device and inspect every connector before ordering electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~40%)** Vibration and heat cycles can loosen plugs or corrode terminals at the thermostats, thermal fuse, or heating element, breaking the signal path the control board monitors.
- **Failed high-limit thermostat or thermal fuse (~30%)** Either safety device can open due to restricted airflow or age, cutting power to the heating circuit and triggering the error.
- **Faulty heating element or grounded coil (~15%)** A broken or partially shorted heating element changes circuit resistance and confuses the control board's diagnostics.
- **Defective control board (~10%)** A failed relay, capacitor, or microprocessor on the main board may misread sensor signals or lose communication with the heating circuit.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~5%)** Chafed or melted insulation anywhere in the harness can create intermittent shorts or open circuits that the board interprets as a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and turn off the gas supply if applicable, then pull the unit away from the wall for access.
2. **Remove the top or rear panel** by unscrewing the sheet-metal screws (locations vary by model) to expose the heating assembly and control board.
3. **Inspect and reseat every wiring connector** on the thermal fuse, high-limit thermostat, heating element, and control board, looking for corrosion, burn marks, or loose pins.
4. **Test the thermal fuse and high-limit thermostat** with a multimeter set to continuity or resistance; each should read near zero ohms when cool and intact.
5. **Check the heating element** for continuity and verify it reads the resistance specified in your model's service manual or wiring diagram (typically 8-12 ohms for 240V units, but consult your table).
6. **Examine the wiring harness** along its entire length for chafed insulation, melted spots, or pinched wires, and repair or replace any damaged sections with high-temperature wire and crimp connectors.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (thermostat, fuse, or element) if testing confirms an open or out-of-spec reading, or install a new control board if all other parts test good and connections are secure.
8. **Reassemble the panels**, plug in the dryer, restore gas if applicable, and run a timed-dry cycle to verify the error is gone and heat is restored.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if the heating element or gas valve assembly requires soldering or specialized tools, or if you have tested every component and connector without finding a fault. Technicians have factory diagnostic software and voltage-test equipment that can pinpoint intermittent board failures and communication errors not visible with a basic multimeter. Also seek help if the dryer shares a dedicated circuit with other appliances and you suspect a broader electrical issue at the panel.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-280.
