## State Water Heater E122 Error Code — What It Means

E122 is documented as a Truma Combi fault code, not a standard State Water Heaters code. On Truma Combi gas systems, E122 or E 122 H indicates the combustion air inlet or exhaust outlet is blocked or closed. The code can also appear when the gas shut-off valve is closed, gas pressure regulation is defective, overheating protection has responded, the electronics are defective, or the control panel cable has a fault. If you have a State-branded water heater showing E122, verify the exact model number on the rating plate, because this code does not appear in typical State documentation and may point to a Truma-supplied component or a misread display.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked combustion air inlet or exhaust outlet** Ice, slush, leaves, debris, or other obstructions can close off the terminals and trigger the fault.
- **Gas shut-off valve closed** The valve may have been left closed or inadvertently turned off, stopping propane flow to the burner.
- **Defective gas pressure regulation** A fault in the regulator or pressure control prevents correct gas delivery and triggers a safety lockout.
- **Overheating protection responded** The unit's safety system tripped due to high temperature, blocked airflow, or low water level inside the heater.
- **Electronics or control panel cable defect** A failed control board or damaged cable connection between the panel and burner assembly can generate the error even when all mechanical components are clear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the heater at the control panel** and wait five minutes before beginning inspection.
2. **Inspect the exterior combustion air intake and exhaust terminals** for ice, leaves, slush, or physical blockage and remove any obstructions you find.
3. **Verify the propane gas shut-off valve is fully open** and that the tank or line has adequate supply pressure.
4. **Check for overheating signs** by feeling the heater case for excessive heat, and if present, allow the unit to cool completely and make sure the water reservoir is filled with cold water.
5. **Examine warm-air outlets and circulated-air intakes** (if your model has forced-air circulation) and clear any dust, lint, or blockages.
6. **Power the unit back on** and attempt a normal start cycle to see if the fault clears after addressing blockages and gas supply.
7. **If E122 persists**, suspect gas pressure regulation, control electronics, or panel cable fault and contact manufacturer service or a qualified RV/gas-appliance technician for diagnostic testing and board replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician or RV service center if the fault returns after you have cleared all visible blockages, confirmed gas supply, and allowed the unit to cool. Gas pressure regulation faults, control board diagnostics, and combustion-system testing require specialized gauges and manufacturer training. Because E122 is primarily documented for Truma Combi systems rather than standard State residential water heaters, a technician familiar with RV or marine gas appliances will have the correct diagnostic charts and replacement parts. Do not attempt to disassemble gas valves, regulators, or control boards yourself if you are unfamiliar with propane appliances.
