## Frigidaire F3 Error Code — What It Means

On Frigidaire electric ranges and ovens, F3 typically indicates an oven temperature sensor (RTD) circuit problem or an over-temperature condition detected by the control. The electronic control cannot get a valid signal from the sensor probe that monitors oven temperature. On some Frigidaire electric range models, F3 (sometimes displayed as F3/F20) can mean a communication error between control boards. Because Frigidaire uses model-dependent fault codes, always confirm the exact meaning against your specific model number and service manual.

The temperature sensor is a resistive thermal device mounted inside the oven cavity. When it fails, reads out of range, or loses connection, the control throws F3 and disables oven operation to prevent runaway heating. The fault may be the sensor itself, damaged wiring between the sensor and control board, or less commonly the electronic control board when sensor and wiring test good.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first when the real culprit is a failed sensor or corroded connector. Always test the sensor resistance (should be about 1,000–1,100 ohms at room temperature) and inspect the wiring before replacing the control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~50%)** The RTD probe reads out of specification (not 1,000–1,100 ohms at room temperature) or has an open or shorted element.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~25%)** Oxidized terminals, pushed-back pins, or a loose plug at the control board interrupt the sensor circuit and mimic a sensor failure.
- **Damaged sensor harness (~15%)** Pinched, melted, or broken wires between the sensor and the control board create an open or intermittent circuit.
- **Defective electronic control board (~8%)** When the sensor and all wiring test good, the control board's sensor input circuit has failed and cannot read the RTD signal.
- **Communication error (F3/F20 models) (~2%)** On certain Frigidaire ranges that display F3 or F3/F20, the fault is a loss of communication between control boards rather than a sensor issue.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Reset the control** by leaving power off for at least 30 seconds, then restore power and observe whether F3 returns immediately or only when the oven is commanded to heat.
3. **Access the oven temperature sensor** by removing the rear access panel or lower kick panel (model-dependent) to reach the sensor wiring at the control board.
4. **Measure sensor resistance** by disconnecting the two sensor wires at the control board and placing multimeter probes across the sensor terminals. A good sensor reads approximately 1,000–1,100 ohms at room temperature.
5. **Inspect wiring and connectors** for burn marks, corrosion, pushed-back pins, or breaks in the harness between the sensor and control board.
6. **Replace the failed component**: install a new oven temperature sensor if resistance is out of range, repair or replace damaged wiring if the sensor tests good, or replace the electronic control board if both sensor and wiring are intact but F3 persists.
7. **Reassemble and test** by restoring power, setting the oven to 350°F, and running a complete preheat cycle to confirm the code does not return and the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range with the rear panel removed, if multimeter testing is unfamiliar, or if the sensor and wiring both test good and you need the control board diagnosed and replaced. A qualified appliance repair tech has the service manual, wiring diagrams, and replacement control boards specific to your Frigidaire model. Also call for help if F3 returns after you have replaced the sensor, since that points to a wiring fault or failed control board that requires expert troubleshooting to avoid replacing parts unnecessarily.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
