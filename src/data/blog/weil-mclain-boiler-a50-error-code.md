## Weil-McLain Boiler A50 Error — What It Means

The A50 code on a Weil-McLain boiler indicates an ignition or flame-proving failure. The control board attempted to start the burner, but the burner did not reliably establish or prove flame, so the boiler locked out for safety. This is a critical safety interlock that prevents raw gas from entering the combustion chamber without ignition.

The code means the boiler tried to light but failed to maintain or prove a stable flame signal during the ignition trial period. The control then went into lockout mode to protect the system. Because the exact code definition can vary by model family, always confirm the code meaning in your specific boiler's installation and service manual.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the gas valve when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor or a simple closed manual gas shutoff. Always verify gas supply and clean the flame sensor before ordering a new valve.

## Common Causes

- **No gas supply or closed gas valve (~35%)** The manual gas shutoff is closed, the utility has interrupted service, or gas pressure is too low to support ignition.
- **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor (~30%)** The flame-proving electrode is fouled with carbon, soot, or scale, or it is positioned too far from the flame path to detect combustion.
- **Failed ignitor or ignition electrode (~20%)** The ignitor is cracked, broken, or not generating spark, or the electrode is corroded or disconnected.
- **Blocked intake or exhaust vent (~10%)** Ice, debris, or a bird nest in the combustion air intake or flue exhaust prevents proper airflow and stable ignition.
- **Faulty gas valve (~5%)** The gas valve coil or internal mechanism has failed and will not open to deliver fuel during the ignition trial.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the boiler at the disconnect switch and close the manual gas shutoff valve before any inspection.
2. **Verify gas supply** by confirming that other gas appliances in the home operate, then open the boiler's manual gas valve fully and check for any inline sediment traps or filters that may be clogged.
3. **Inspect the venting system** for blockages at both the intake and exhaust terminations, and clear any ice, debris, or nests that restrict airflow.
4. **Remove and clean the flame sensor** using fine sandpaper or emery cloth to remove carbon buildup, then reinstall it at the correct position relative to the burner flame path (consult your model's service manual for the exact gap).
5. **Inspect the ignitor and electrode** for cracks, corrosion, or misalignment, and verify that the wire connection is secure and not grounded against metal.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then initiate one start attempt while observing the ignition sequence: inducer fan, ignition spark, and flame establishment.
7. **If the fault returns** after one reset, do not cycle the power repeatedly. Pull the installation and service manual for your exact model number and follow the manufacturer's diagnostic flowchart, or call a qualified heating technician to complete the troubleshooting.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician immediately if you smell gas, if you are not confident working with gas piping and electrical controls, or if the boiler continues to lock out after you have confirmed gas supply and cleaned the flame sensor. Gas ignition systems require precise electrode gaps, proper grounding, and control-board diagnostics that are beyond typical homeowner tools. Repeated failed ignition attempts can flood the combustion chamber with unburned gas, creating a dangerous condition. A qualified technician will use a combustion analyzer, multimeter, and the model-specific service manual to diagnose ignition timing, flame signal strength, and gas valve operation. Because Weil-McLain emphasizes that only qualified installers and service technicians should perform these checks, professional service is the safest and most reliable path to a correct repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
