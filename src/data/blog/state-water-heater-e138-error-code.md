## State Water Heater E138 Error Code — What It Means

E138 is an ignition or combustion-start failure fault on State tankless water heaters. The unit called for heat, but the burner did not light because one of the startup conditions was not met. This code is part of the A. O. Smith/State error family and indicates the heater cannot establish stable ignition due to missing air, gas, or water flow prerequisites.

In practice, this means something is blocking the startup sequence. The heater needs clean combustion air, adequate gas pressure, and sufficient water flow to ignite safely. When any of these is restricted or out of range, the control board aborts ignition and displays E138.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or intake screen** Lint, dust, or debris accumulates on the intake screen and starves the burner of combustion air during startup.
- **Low gas supply pressure or flow** Insufficient gas delivery from a closed valve, undersized line, or low upstream pressure prevents ignition.
- **Clogged inlet water filter** A dirty filter restricts flow below the minimum threshold needed to trigger the burner sequence.
- **Venting or combustion air restriction** Blocked exhaust vent or improperly installed vent piping limits airflow and causes failed ignition.
- **Faulty ignition or flame-sensing components** Worn igniter or dirty flame rod can prevent the control board from confirming successful ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** at the shutoff valves before inspection or cleaning.
2. **Check the gas shutoff valve** and confirm it is fully open, then verify gas is flowing to other appliances on the same line.
3. **Inspect and clean the air intake screen** by removing any lint, dust, or blockage from the intake opening and filter.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** located at the cold water inlet, flushing debris and reinstalling securely.
5. **Verify adequate water flow** by opening a hot tap and confirming strong flow, then check that the heater calls for heat without error.
6. **Inspect venting for blockages** or improper installation, ensuring exhaust and intake pipes are clear and meet the installation manual specifications.
7. **Reset the unit** by cycling power off for 30 seconds, then restore power and gas and test for normal ignition.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the code returns after cleaning the air intake and inlet filter, or if you cannot verify adequate gas pressure at the unit. If you are uncomfortable working with gas connections or venting systems, stop and get professional help. A technician can measure gas pressure, test ignition components, and verify combustion air and venting are within specification. Persistent ignition failures may indicate control board or gas valve issues that require diagnostic tools and manufacturer training.
