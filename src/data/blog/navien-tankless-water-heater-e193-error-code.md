## Navien E193 Error Code — What It Means

The E193 code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a communication error between control boards or sensors within the unit. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit for details specific to your heater. In general, this code appears when the main PCB cannot reliably exchange signals with a secondary board, a sensor module, or another internal component. The fault may be intermittent or persistent depending on whether a connector has loosened, corrosion has built up on terminals, or a board has developed a defect.

Because communication faults often stem from physical connections rather than failed electronics, many E193 cases resolve with simple steps like reseating connectors or cleaning contacts. If physical checks find nothing obvious, the problem typically lies in a control board or sensor interface that will need professional diagnosis and replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board immediately, but a loose ribbon cable or oxidized connector is the usual culprit. Inspect and reseat every plug on the boards before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded connector (~40%)** Vibration, thermal cycling, or moisture can loosen ribbon cables and wire-harness plugs between the main PCB and secondary boards, breaking the signal path.
- **Main control board fault (~25%)** A failed component or solder joint on the main PCB can prevent it from sending or receiving communication packets correctly.
- **Secondary board or interface module failure (~20%)** Some models use a separate display board or sensor interface that can develop communication faults if a chip or trace fails.
- **Transient power surge or low voltage (~10%)** Brief electrical disturbances can corrupt the firmware state or damage communication circuits, especially if the heater shares a circuit with heavy loads.
- **Water intrusion on circuit boards (~5%)** Leaking condensate or spray from service work can seep onto boards and create corrosion or short-circuits that disrupt signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover panel** using a screwdriver to access the control boards and internal wiring.
3. **Photograph the board layout** with your phone so you can reference connector positions later.
4. **Inspect every wiring connector** on the main PCB and any secondary boards, looking for loose fits, corrosion, or melted plastic.
5. **Reseat each connector** by gently pulling it free and then pushing it back on until you hear or feel a positive click.
6. **Clean any oxidized pins or sockets** with electronics contact cleaner and a soft brush, then let them dry completely.
7. **Restore power and gas**, wait for the heater to boot, and check whether the E193 code has cleared; if not, document which connectors you checked and call a qualified technician for board-level diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working around circuit boards or if your free checks and connector reseating do not clear the code. A pro will use multi-meter tests and diagnostic software to pinpoint whether the main PCB, a secondary board, or a sensor interface has failed. Because gas and electrical safety interlocks are managed by these boards, incorrect repairs can create hazards. Technicians also have access to OEM replacement boards with the correct firmware and can update the unit if a known communication bug exists for your model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-400.
