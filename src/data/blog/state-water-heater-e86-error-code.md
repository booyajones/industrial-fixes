## State Water Heater E86 Error Code — What It Means

E86 is not a standardized code across all State water heater models, and the exact meaning depends on your specific unit's model number. State requires users to identify the model and serial number from the rating plate and consult the model-specific manual for accurate code definitions. In the broader tankless water heater field, codes in the 80s range often indicate airflow, venting, or combustion-safety lockouts. If your State unit is a tankless gas model, E86 likely points to a problem that prevents safe combustion, such as blocked intake air, obstructed exhaust venting, or a flame-detection fault. The unit locks out to prevent unsafe operation until the issue is corrected.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screen or filter** Dust, lint, or debris on the intake restricts combustion air and triggers a safety lockout.
- **Obstructed exhaust vent or termination** Bird nests, ice, or vent condensate buildup block exhaust gases and prevent proper draft.
- **Dirty or fouled flame sensor** Scale, carbon deposits, or corrosion on the flame rod prevent the control from confirming ignition.
- **Insufficient gas supply or pressure** A partially closed gas valve, undersized line, or low line pressure starves the burner and causes ignition failure.
- **Failed combustion fan or pressure switch** A weak or stalled fan cannot establish proper airflow, or the pressure switch fails to close and signal the control board.
- **Scale or debris in the heat exchanger** Heavy mineral buildup affects combustion air paths or heat transfer and can mimic airflow faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the full error code** exactly as displayed on the screen and note your model and serial number from the rating plate before resetting or powering down the unit.
2. **Locate the rating plate** on the front or side of the water heater and use the QR code or model number to download the model-specific manual and error-code chart from State's support site.
3. **Inspect and clean the air intake screen** by removing the cover, pulling the filter or screen, and washing or vacuuming away dust, lint, and debris.
4. **Check the exhaust vent and termination** outside for obstructions such as bird nests, ice, leaves, or condensate ice dams, and confirm the vent pipe is intact and properly sloped per the installation manual.
5. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve upstream of the unit is fully open and checking that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
6. **Reset the unit** using the control-panel reset button or by cycling power off for thirty seconds, then back on, and observe whether the code returns immediately or after an ignition attempt.
7. **If the code persists**, measure gas inlet pressure and test the combustion fan and flame sensor per the model-specific service manual values, or call a qualified technician for component-level diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the error returns after you have cleaned the intake and vent, confirmed gas supply, and reset the unit. Combustion and gas-control diagnostics require calibrated pressure gauges, multimeters, and familiarity with your model's wiring and test points. Any work on gas piping, the gas valve, or the control board should be performed by a qualified service professional to avoid fire, explosion, or carbon-monoxide hazards. If you cannot locate your model-specific manual or the code definition does not match the general guidance here, contact State customer support or a factory-authorized service provider for model-specific troubleshooting.
