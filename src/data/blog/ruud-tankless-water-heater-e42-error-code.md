## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E42 Error — What It Means

The E42 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a flame or ignition problem. The unit may be failing to ignite, losing flame after ignition, or detecting an unsafe combustion condition. Because exact meanings can vary by model, consult your owner's manual or the diagnostic chart inside the unit's cover for your specific series.

This code often appears when the gas supply is interrupted, the igniter is weak, or the flame sensor is dirty or failing. It may also trigger if there is insufficient air for combustion or if the venting is blocked. The heater will shut down to prevent unsafe operation until the condition is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame sensor when the real problem is low gas pressure at the unit or a stuck gas valve. Check that other gas appliances are working normally and verify the inlet pressure before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Insufficient gas supply or low pressure (~35%)** The gas valve to the heater may be partially closed, the meter regulator may be failing, or demand from other appliances is reducing pressure below the minimum required for ignition.
- **Dirty or faulty flame sensor (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents it from detecting flame presence, causing the unit to shut down even when ignition occurs.
- **Weak or failed igniter (~20%)** The igniter may not generate enough spark to light the gas, or it may be cracked or grounded out against the burner assembly.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Exhaust vent blockage or insufficient combustion air starves the burner and triggers a safety shutdown during or after ignition.
- **Failed gas valve (~10%)** The gas valve solenoid may be stuck closed or opening too slowly, preventing gas from reaching the burner when the igniter fires.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve on the supply line to the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the heater to access the burner chamber and control components.
3. **Inspect the flame sensor rod** for carbon deposits or corrosion. Remove it (typically one screw) and gently clean with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth, then reinstall.
4. **Check the igniter** for cracks or heavy carbon buildup. If damaged, replace it. Verify the igniter gap to the burner matches the specification in your model's service manual.
5. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the gas valve to the heater is fully open. If you have a manometer, check inlet pressure against the data plate requirements (typically 4 to 10 inches water column depending on fuel type).
6. **Inspect venting** by going outside and confirming the exhaust termination is clear of nests, leaves, or ice. Check that the intake is not blocked and that vent pipes are properly supported and free of sag or condensate pooling.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then call for hot water and observe the ignition sequence. If the error persists or you see no spark, call a licensed technician to test the gas valve and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you smell gas at any point, or if the free checks and sensor cleaning do not resolve the E42 code. Professionals have manometers to measure gas pressure, multimeters to test igniters and flame sensors, and the training to safely diagnose gas valve and control board failures. Any work involving gas line connections, pressure regulation, or sealed combustion components should be left to a pro to avoid carbon monoxide hazards and code violations.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
