## Frigidaire Refrigerator SH Error Code — What It Means

The SH code on a Frigidaire refrigerator is a sensor high or high-temperature alarm. It appears when the control board detects that the freezer section, the refrigerator section, or both have exceeded the safe temperature threshold. Frigidaire's own documentation identifies SH as a temperature-threshold fault, meaning either the compartment truly warmed up or the control believes the temperature sensor is reading abnormally high.

This code is often triggered by everyday events like a door left open, a heavy load of warm groceries, a power outage, or repeated door openings. It can also signal a real cooling problem such as a failed fan, blocked airflow, ice buildup from a defrost failure, or a faulty temperature sensor. The key is to determine whether the cabinet truly warmed or the control is misreading the sensor.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but most SH faults are caused by a door seal, blocked vent, or failed fan motor. Check door closure, airflow, and fan operation before buying any board.

## Common Causes

- **Door not fully closed or damaged gasket (~35%)** Warm air leaks in when the door does not seal all around, or bins and food packages block closure.
- **Recent power loss or heavy warm food load (~25%)** The unit is still recovering from a temporary warm-up event and the alarm will clear once temperature drops.
- **Evaporator or condenser fan failure (~20%)** A stopped or intermittent fan reduces airflow so the compartment cannot cool properly.
- **Blocked air vents or poor circulation (~10%)** Food stacked against vents or ice buildup in the evaporator section restricts airflow.
- **Failed temperature sensor or thermistor (~8%)** A sensor with an open circuit or out-of-range resistance causes the control to read an abnormally high value.
- **Defrost system fault causing ice buildup (~2%)** Ice on the evaporator coil blocks airflow and prevents cooling, triggering the high-temperature alarm.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check door closure and gasket seal.** Open and close each door slowly, watching for gaps or resistance. Inspect the rubber gasket all the way around for tears, warping, or food debris. Clean the gasket with warm soapy water and make sure no bins or packages are preventing the door from seating fully.
2. **Clear all interior vents.** Move food away from the back wall and ceiling of both compartments so cold air can flow freely. Verify that the air channels between the freezer and fresh-food section are not blocked by ice or packages.
3. **Listen for fan operation.** With the doors closed, listen for the evaporator fan inside the freezer and the condenser fan near the compressor. If either is silent or makes grinding noise, locate the motor and check for ice buildup or a failed bearing.
4. **Perform a hard reset.** Unplug the refrigerator from the wall outlet or turn off the circuit breaker for 2 to 3 minutes, then restore power. Frigidaire recommends this step to clear transient alarms and allow the control to re-initialize.
5. **Inspect for frost or ice buildup.** Remove the evaporator cover inside the freezer and look for thick ice on the coil. If present, the defrost system has failed and you will need to manually defrost or replace the defrost heater, thermostat, or timer.
6. **Test the temperature sensor with a multimeter.** Disconnect power, locate the thermistor in the fresh-food or freezer section, unplug the connector, and measure resistance across the sensor leads. Compare the reading to the model-specific table in your service manual. Replace the sensor if the value is out of range or the circuit is open.
7. **Monitor compartment temperature.** Place an appliance thermometer in both sections and check after 4 to 6 hours. If the freezer is above 10°F or the fresh-food is above 42°F and the alarm persists, you have a real cooling fault and should inspect the sealed system or call a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the SH code returns after you have verified door seals, cleared vents, confirmed both fans are running, and performed a power reset. A technician has the tools to test the sealed refrigeration system, check refrigerant charge, measure compressor performance, and diagnose control-board faults that require specialized equipment. Also call if you find heavy ice buildup and are not comfortable removing panels or testing defrost components, or if the compartment temperature remains high despite all airflow and sensor checks. Any work involving the compressor, refrigerant lines, or soldered connections requires EPA certification and should not be attempted by a homeowner.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–80 in parts (sensor, gasket, or fan motor), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on diagnosis and part.
