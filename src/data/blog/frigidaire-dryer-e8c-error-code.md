## Frigidaire Dryer E8C Error Code — What It Means

The E8C error code on a Frigidaire dryer typically indicates a communication fault between the main control board and another component, or a sensor reading that falls outside expected parameters. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for your specific dryer.

Because this code involves electronic communication rather than a mechanical failure, the problem usually lies in wiring, connectors, or one of the circuit boards rather than belts or motors. The dryer may refuse to start or stop mid-cycle when this code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first, but a loose harness connector or a failed thermistor is often the real cause. Inspect all connectors and test sensors with a multimeter before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wire harness connector (~40%)** Vibration over time can work connectors loose, interrupting communication between boards.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** A thermistor that reads out of range or open will trigger a fault code on many Frigidaire models.
- **Main control board fault (~20%)** A failed processor or corrupted firmware on the control board can generate communication errors.
- **User interface board failure (~10%)** The touch panel or display board may fail to respond to the main control, logging an error.
- **Damaged wire or harness (~5%)** A pinched or chafed wire near the drum can short or break, stopping signals between components.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off the gas supply if you have a gas model.
2. **Remove the top panel or control console** by taking out the screws along the back edge or under the lint screen, then lifting or tilting the panel forward to access the control boards.
3. **Inspect and reseat every wire connector** on the main control board, user interface board, and any sensor harnesses, pressing each firmly into place.
4. **Check for damaged wires** by tracing harnesses from the control area down to the drum and blower housing, looking for pinched, burned, or frayed insulation.
5. **Test thermistors and temperature sensors** with a multimeter set to resistance mode, comparing readings to your model's service manual (typical room-temperature readings range from 10k to 50k ohms, but consult your model's table).
6. **Replace any sensor or board** that tests out of range or shows visible burn marks, then reassemble the dryer and restore power.
7. **Run a test cycle** and monitor the display to confirm the E8C code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with control boards or if you have tested connectors and sensors without finding a fault. A pro has the diagnostic software and wiring diagrams to pinpoint communication errors quickly. Also call if you find evidence of repeated electrical arcing, burn marks on boards, or if the dryer has a history of multiple error codes, since the root cause may be a failing power supply or a complex wiring fault that requires advanced troubleshooting.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
