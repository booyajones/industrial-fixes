## Gree P8 Error Code — What It Means

The P8 code on a Gree mini-split signals IPM or PFC over-temperature protection. Your outdoor unit has shut down because the inverter drive module (the IPM or PFC section) is running too hot. This is a hardware-level safety lock triggered by the power electronics that drive the compressor.

The fault lives on the outdoor inverter board and its heatsink assembly, not in the indoor unit or refrigerant system directly. Gree service documentation groups P8 with module temperature sensor and power module diagnostics, and the troubleshooting flow focuses on thermal transfer and the drive board itself.

## Common Causes

- **Poor heat transfer between the IPM or PFC module and its heatsink** Loose mounting screws, degraded thermal compound, or a poorly seated module prevent the power electronics from cooling properly.
- **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil and condenser airflow** Dust, leaves, or obstructions around the outdoor unit reduce air movement across the heatsink and raise internal temperatures.
- **Weak or failed outdoor fan motor** A slow or stopped condenser fan cuts cooling to the outdoor board and its heatsink, letting the inverter module overheat.
- **Failed IPM or PFC module on the inverter drive board** Internal component degradation in the power module creates excess heat even when airflow and thermal interface are correct.
- **Burnt or damaged drive board components** Heat damage, discolored board traces, or loose connectors on the outdoor mainboard can cause localized overheating.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off all power** at the disconnect and circuit breaker, then wait five minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening the outdoor unit electrical compartment.
2. **Inspect outdoor airflow.** Clean the condenser coil with coil cleaner and rinse from inside-out, remove any leaves or debris blocking the fan discharge, and verify the outdoor fan spins freely by hand.
3. **Check outdoor fan operation.** Restore power briefly and confirm the outdoor fan motor runs at normal speed when the unit calls for cooling. Replace the fan motor or capacitor if weak or stopped.
4. **Open the outdoor electrical cover and locate the inverter drive board.** Find the large IPM or PFC module mounted to the board heatsink, usually a black rectangular block with fins or a metal backing plate.
5. **Check module mounting and thermal contact.** Tighten the module mounting screws snugly if loose. If thermal compound is dry, cracked, or missing, power down, remove the module, apply fresh thermal paste to the mating surface, and re-seat the module firmly.
6. **Inspect the drive board for heat damage.** Look for burnt components, discolored solder joints, or loose connectors. If the board shows visible damage or P8 returns after correcting airflow and module mounting, replace the outdoor mainboard or the IPM module per Gree service flow.
7. **Restore power and monitor.** Run the system through a full cooling cycle and watch for stable operation without repeated P8 faults. If the error clears and does not return, the repair is complete.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside live electrical enclosures or if P8 persists after you have cleaned the outdoor coil, verified fan operation, and checked module mounting. Drive board and IPM replacement require safe handling of high-voltage DC bus capacitors, proper torque on module fasteners, and functional testing of the inverter section. A technician will also check refrigerant charge and compressor current draw to rule out load-related contributions to module overheating. If your outdoor board shows burn marks or the IPM module is physically damaged, professional replacement and system checkout are the safest path.
