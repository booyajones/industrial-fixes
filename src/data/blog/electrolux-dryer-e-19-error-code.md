## Electrolux Dryer E19 Error Code — What It Means

The E19 error code on Electrolux dryers typically indicates a heating system or temperature sensor problem. The exact definition can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for your specific unit. In most cases, the control board has detected a fault in the heater circuit, a temperature sensor out of range, or a safety device that has opened to prevent overheating. The dryer may run but produce no heat, or it may not start at all.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board when the real problem is a blown thermal fuse or broken heating element. Use a multimeter to test continuity on the thermal fuse and element before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Blown thermal fuse (~40%)** A thermal fuse on the blower housing or heater assembly opens permanently when the dryer overheats, cutting power to the heating circuit.
- **Failed heating element (~30%)** The coil inside the heating element can break or short, triggering a fault code when the control board detects abnormal resistance.
- **Defective thermistor or thermal sensor (~15%)** The temperature sensor that monitors heater operation may drift out of range or fail open, causing the control to flag an error.
- **Faulty high-limit thermostat (~10%)** A cycling thermostat or high-limit safety switch can stick open or fail, preventing the heater from receiving power.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The main electronic control board may have a failed relay or firmware glitch that incorrectly reports a heating error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and move it away from the wall so you can access the rear panel or lower front access panel, depending on your model.
2. **Remove the cabinet panels** by taking out the screws that secure the top, front, or rear cover, then lift or slide the panel free to expose the blower housing and heater assembly.
3. **Locate the thermal fuse** on the blower housing or near the heating element canister, then disconnect the wire terminals and test continuity with a multimeter set to ohms.
4. **Check the heating element** by removing the wire leads and testing across the element terminals for continuity, typically a few tens of ohms; an open circuit or very low resistance indicates a fault.
5. **Inspect the thermistor** or temperature sensor, usually a small disc or probe mounted on the heater duct, and measure its resistance at room temperature; consult your model's table for the expected value.
6. **Replace any failed component** by installing the new thermal fuse, element, or sensor, reconnecting all wire terminals securely, and reassembling the cabinet panels.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to confirm the error code clears and the dryer heats properly; clean the entire vent system if overheating was the trigger.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if multimeter testing is unfamiliar, or if you have replaced the thermal fuse and heating element but the E19 code persists. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board, wiring harness, and sensor circuits to isolate intermittent faults. Also call a pro if the dryer uses a gas burner instead of an electric element, since gas valve and igniter work requires special training and safety precautions.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
