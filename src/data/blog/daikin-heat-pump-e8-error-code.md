## Daikin E8 Error Code — What It Means

The E8 error on a Daikin heat pump signals overcurrent of the inverter compressor. The outdoor unit's inverter drive has detected that the compressor is drawing more current than allowed and has shut down the system to protect itself. This fault originates in the outdoor unit and indicates either a problem with the compressor itself, the inverter PCB that powers it, or operating conditions that are forcing the compressor to work too hard.

In practical terms, something is causing the compressor motor to pull excessive amperage. That can be a mechanical or electrical fault inside the compressor, a drive circuit problem on the inverter board, poor heat rejection at the outdoor coil, or refrigerant-side conditions that overload the compressor. The system will not run until the root cause is identified and corrected.

## Common Causes

- **Failed compressor windings or bearings** A compressor with shorted windings, grounded phases, or seized bearings will draw far more current than normal and trip the inverter's overcurrent protection.
- **Faulty inverter PCB or drive circuit** The outdoor control board or inverter module may have failed transistors, capacitors, or drive components that cause erratic current delivery or false overcurrent detection.
- **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil** A condenser coil clogged with dirt, grass, or debris restricts heat rejection and forces the compressor to work harder, driving up current draw.
- **Outdoor fan motor failure** If the outdoor fan is not running or running slowly, the unit cannot reject heat properly and compressor load increases.
- **Refrigerant system abnormalities** Overcharge, restriction, or abnormal pressures from airflow problems can all increase compressor load and current beyond safe limits.
- **Unstable or low input voltage** Power supply issues such as voltage sag, phase imbalance, or loose connections can contribute to overcurrent faults even when the compressor is mechanically sound.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm the fault code by checking the indoor controller or outdoor unit display and verify it reads E8 for your specific Daikin model.
2. Inspect the outdoor unit for visible problems: clean the condenser coil, check that the outdoor fan spins freely and runs when the unit is powered, and look for damaged wiring or burned connectors at the compressor and inverter board.
3. Measure compressor winding resistance phase-to-phase with the unit off and disconnected. Compare readings to each other (they should be balanced) and check insulation resistance to ground with a megohmmeter.
4. Check the inverter PCB and outdoor control board for burn marks, swollen capacitors, or damaged traces. Inspect all connectors and harnesses for overheating or corrosion.
5. Verify input power at the outdoor unit with the system under load. Confirm voltage is stable and within the nameplate range for your model.
6. Check refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling if compressor and electrical components pass initial tests. Look for restrictions, airflow problems, or charge issues that could overload the compressor.
7. Clear the fault code, restart the unit, and monitor compressor current draw and operating pressures. If the E8 returns immediately or current remains abnormally high, replace the failed component identified in testing.

## When to Call a Pro

E8 is a serious fault that involves high-voltage inverter circuits, compressor diagnostics, and refrigerant system work. If you are not a licensed HVAC technician with inverter drive experience, call a Daikin-trained professional. Misdiagnosis can lead to expensive part replacement without solving the problem, and working on energized inverter boards or compressors without proper tools and safety procedures is dangerous. A qualified tech will have the correct service literature, electrical test equipment, and refrigerant gauges to pinpoint the cause and perform the repair safely and correctly.
