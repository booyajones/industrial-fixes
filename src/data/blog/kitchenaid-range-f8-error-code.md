## What this code means
The F8E4 code appears on KitchenAid dishwashers when the machine detects a water-level fault related to overfill or leak detection. The dishwasher will not run normally until the condition is corrected and the code is cleared. This fault is triggered by the float switch, which sits in a drip tray beneath the tub and rises when water accumulates there, or by issues with the water supply connection or excessive suds.

KitchenAid troubleshooting guidance identifies F8E4 as an overfill condition and directs you to check for water in the drip tray, verify the water supply connection is properly seated, and confirm the float switch connector is fully locked in. The code prevents operation to protect against flooding and must be addressed before the dishwasher will resume normal cycles.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or inlet valve first. Before ordering parts, remove and inspect the drip tray for water and verify the supply line connection is properly tightened and not cross-threaded.

## Common Causes

- **Water in the drip tray (~45%)** A small leak or previous overfill leaves water in the drip pan under the tub, lifting the float switch and triggering the fault.
- **Loose or cross-threaded water supply connection (~25%)** The 90° elbow, hose fitting, rubber washer, or valve connection at the dishwasher inlet is improperly seated or cross-threaded, allowing water to escape into the base.
- **Float switch connector not fully seated (~15%)** The wiring harness to the float switch was dislodged or damaged during service or drip-tray removal, breaking the circuit or creating a false overfill signal.
- **Excess suds or foam (~10%)** Using the wrong detergent type or too much detergent creates foam that triggers the overfill sensor even when the tub level is normal.
- **Faulty inlet valve or control circuit (~5%)** The water inlet valve sticks open or the control board misreads the float-switch circuit, causing genuine overfill or repeated false codes after all mechanical checks pass.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Silence and clear the code** by pressing Cancel once to mute the alarm, then pressing Cancel a second time to clear F8E4 from the display.
2. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the water supply valve under the sink before opening the dishwasher base or removing any panels.
3. **Remove and inspect the drip tray** by sliding out the lower access panel and carefully pulling the drip pan assembly forward without yanking the attached float-switch wire.
4. **Empty and dry the tray** completely if any water is present, then check the float switch connector to confirm it is fully seated and locked into the housing.
5. **Inspect the water supply connection** at the 90° elbow, 3/8 inch compression fitting, and 3/4 inch hose fitting for cross-threading, missing rubber washers, or loose threads, and re-tighten or replace hardware as needed.
6. **Check for foam or suds** inside the tub and dispenser area. If present, stop the cycle, allow suds to dissipate, and use only detergent labeled for automatic dishwashers in the correct dose.
7. **Restore power and water**, clear the code again if it reappears, then run a short test cycle and watch for water under the unit or a repeat fault code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if water continues to accumulate in the drip tray after correcting all visible leaks and connection issues, or if the F8E4 code returns immediately after clearing with no water present and the float switch connector fully seated. A pro will perform electrical diagnosis of the float-switch circuit, measure inlet-valve behavior, and check for control-board misinterpretation of sensor signals. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working around electrical connections or removing the drip-tray assembly while the float-switch harness is still attached. If the dishwasher has flooded the floor or cabinet repeatedly, shut off water and power and schedule service right away to prevent further damage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (float switch or supply connection hardware), 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call.
