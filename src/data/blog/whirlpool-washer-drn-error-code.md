## Whirlpool drn Error Code — What It Means

The drn code on Whirlpool top-load HE washers indicates the machine is not draining properly or is taking too long to remove water. Whirlpool groups this code with the F9 E1 fault, which signals a drain pump system problem or long drain condition. The washer has detected that water remains in the tub beyond the expected time window, so it halts the cycle and displays the code.

In many cases the code is triggered by an installation or drain-path issue rather than a failed component. Whirlpool calls out incorrect drain hose setup, kinked hoses, and siphoning as the leading causes. If the hose is installed correctly and the drain path is clear, then a blockage in the pump inlet or outlet, or rarely a faulty drain pump, may be responsible.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the drain pump when the real problem is a kinked or incorrectly installed drain hose. Always verify hose height, insertion depth, and kinks before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose inserted too far into standpipe (~30%)** Whirlpool specifies the hose must not extend more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe or it restricts airflow and slows draining.
- **Drain hose end too low (siphoning) (~20%)** If the hose outlet is below 36 inches from the floor, water can siphon out during the fill cycle and cause a continuous fill-and-drain loop.
- **Drain hose kinked or clogged (~20%)** A sharp bend, pinch, or lint buildup inside the hose restricts flow and triggers the long-drain fault.
- **Blockage in pump inlet, outlet, or coin trap (~20%)** Socks, lint, coins, or debris can lodge in the hose connections or pump body and slow water removal.
- **Drain pump faulty or obstructed internally (~10%)** If the impeller is jammed or the pump motor has failed, the washer cannot move water through the drain path at the required rate.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves.
2. **Clear the error** by pressing the power button, then start a drain/spin cycle to confirm the long-drain symptom.
3. **Pull the drain hose** out of the standpipe and measure insertion depth. Trim or reposition so no more than 4.5 inches of hose enters the pipe.
4. **Check hose height** by measuring from the floor to the hose end. Secure it at or above 36 inches using the drain hose form or a bracket to prevent siphoning.
5. **Inspect the entire drain hose** for kinks, pinches, or clogs. Straighten any bends and flush the hose with water if needed.
6. **Remove the rear access panel** (on applicable models) and disconnect the drain hose from the pump. Check both the pump inlet and outlet for lint, coins, or other debris and clear any blockage.
7. **Reassemble all connections**, plug the washer back in, and run a full drain/spin cycle to verify the code does not return and water drains completely.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have corrected the drain hose installation, cleared the entire drain path, and the drn code returns after a full test cycle. Also call if you are uncomfortable removing the rear panel or accessing the pump, or if the washer is still under warranty. A pro can perform a voltage test on the pump motor, check the control board signals, and access model-specific wiring diagrams to isolate electronic faults that may mimic a drain problem.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (if hose or clamp needed), 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220 service call.
