## Whirlpool drn Error Code — What It Means

The drn code (often displayed with F9 E1) means the washer's control has timed the drain cycle and determined that water is not leaving the tub fast enough. The machine flags a drain-system fault and stops the cycle to prevent overflow or damage.

This code does not usually mean the pump motor is dead. Instead, the washer has detected restricted water flow somewhere between the tub and the standpipe. Whirlpool flags incorrect drain hose installation and kinked or clogged hoses as the leading causes.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump assembly before checking the external drain setup. Verify that the hose end is higher than 36 inches from the floor and that the hose does not extend more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose set too low or inserted too far (~40%)** Whirlpool specifies the hose end must be higher than 36 inches from the floor and must not extend more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe, or flow is restricted and the code appears.
- **Drain hose kinked or clogged (~30%)** A pinch point behind the washer or lint buildup inside the hose slows the flow enough to trigger the long-drain timer.
- **Foreign object in the drain pump impeller (~20%)** Nails, screws, coins, or small garments lodge in the pump housing and prevent the impeller from turning freely, blocking water discharge.
- **Failed drain pump motor (~8%)** The pump motor itself has burned out or the impeller blades are broken, so the pump runs but moves little or no water.
- **Partial blockage in the standpipe or house drain (~2%)** A slow household drain can back-pressure the washer's discharge and cause the control to time out even when the machine's own components are working correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait five minutes to reset the control and clear the fault code from memory.
2. **Inspect the external drain hose setup** against Whirlpool's installation requirements: confirm the hose end is higher than 36 inches from the floor, the hose does not extend more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe, and the hose has no kinks or pinch points.
3. **Disconnect the drain hose** from the standpipe and check inside the hose for lint, soap buildup, or foreign objects that may restrict flow.
4. **Access the drain pump** by removing the lower front panel or tipping the washer back (consult your model's service manual for the exact method), then remove the pump from the housing.
5. **Inspect the pump impeller** for coins, nails, screws, socks, or other debris, and rotate the impeller by hand to confirm it spins freely without binding.
6. **Remove any blockage**, rinse the pump housing, and reinstall the pump, reconnect all hoses, and secure the access panel.
7. **Restore power and run a drain or spin test cycle** to verify that water evacuates quickly and the code does not return. If the code persists and the hose is clear, replace the drain pump assembly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified the drain hose installation meets Whirlpool's height and insertion specs, cleared all visible blockages from the hose and pump, and the drn code still appears after a power reset. A pro can test the pump motor for correct voltage and resistance, confirm that the control board is sending the right signals, and rule out a slow household drain or vent problem. Also call if you are uncomfortable tipping the washer or removing panels to reach the pump, or if your model requires special tools or a cabinet disassembly you do not have.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts (pump or hose), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250 service call.
