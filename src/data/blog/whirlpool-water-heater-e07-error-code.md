## Whirlpool Water Heater E07 Error — What It Means

E07 on Whirlpool Energy Smart electric water heaters indicates a lower thermistor sensor failure. The diagnostic system has detected that the lower temperature sensor (located above the lower heating element) is not reading correctly or has lost communication with the electronic thermostat. This code appears on the display with a flashing alert icon. The water heater will not heat properly until the fault is corrected.

[Jump to Fix](#fix)

## Common Causes

- **Loose or disconnected wiring** The most common cause is a loose connector or detached wire at the electronic thermostat or lower sensor harness.
- **Failed lower thermistor sensor** The lower thermistor sensor itself can fail internally and stop sending valid temperature readings to the control board.
- **Corroded or damaged connectors** Moisture or age can corrode the electrical connections at the electronic thermostat, creating an open circuit.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** Any break, short, or damage in the lower sensor circuit wiring will trigger E07.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Shut off both dedicated breakers to the water heater at your electrical panel and verify power is off with a non-contact voltage tester.
2. Remove the control panel cover to access the electronic thermostat and wiring connections underneath.
3. Inspect all wiring and connectors at the electronic thermostat for looseness, corrosion, or visible damage, and re-seat any loose plugs.
4. Repair or replace any damaged wiring or corroded connectors in the lower sensor circuit before replacing any parts.
5. Replace the lower thermistor sensor if all wiring and connections are sound, following the Whirlpool service procedure for your model.
6. Restore power at both breakers and monitor the display to verify the E07 code has cleared and the unit begins heating.
7. If the code returns after wiring repair and sensor replacement, consult the service manual to verify the electronic thermostat circuit condition.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician or appliance technician if you are not comfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance or if the error persists after you have checked the wiring and replaced the lower thermistor sensor. A pro can perform additional circuit diagnostics, verify the electronic thermostat is functioning correctly, and make sure all repairs meet local electrical code. Water heaters involve both high voltage and scalding water, so professional help is worth the cost if you have any doubt.
