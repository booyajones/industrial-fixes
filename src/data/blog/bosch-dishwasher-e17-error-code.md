## Bosch Dishwasher E17 Error — What It Means

Bosch defines E17 as an issue with water flowing into the dishwasher or the filling system. The error is triggered when the water level sensor detects that water is entering too slowly, too quickly, or at an incorrect level. In many models the code fires when water enters too fast and trips the internal flood-detection float, or when a blockage prevents normal fill. Either way, the control board sees the fill cycle is out of tolerance and stops the machine to protect against overflow or incomplete wash.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve or even the control module (part 12008382, often discontinued) when the real problem is a kinked hose or clogged screen filter at the inlet connection. Always check the hose routing and clean the mesh screen before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or damaged inlet hose (~40%)** The hose behind the dishwasher can be pinched against the cabinet wall or kinked at the connection when the unit is pushed back into place.
- **Clogged inlet filter or screen (~25%)** Sediment, sand, and debris collect in the mesh filter at the inlet connection or inside an Aquastop hose, restricting water flow.
- **Low household water pressure (~15%)** Insufficient supply pressure from the home plumbing prevents the dishwasher from filling at the expected rate.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~12%)** The internal restrictor can blow out (causing overfilling and a flood trip) or the solenoid can fail (causing underfilling).
- **Restricted float chamber tube (~5%)** Grease buildup in the pipe from the float chamber causes the float to rise falsely and trigger the flood-protection circuit.
- **Control module communication error (~3%)** In rare cases a fault in the control board (part 12008382) can send a false E17 signal, though this part is often discontinued.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water shutoff valve under the counter.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** enough to access the inlet hose and connections at the back of the unit.
3. **Inspect the inlet hose** for kinks, sharp bends, or crushing against the cabinet. Straighten any kinks and reroute the hose if needed.
4. **Disconnect the inlet hose** from the dishwasher and remove the mesh screen filter from the inlet port. Rinse the screen under running water to clear sediment and debris.
5. **Check household water pressure** by turning the shutoff valve fully open and observing flow. If pressure seems low, verify the main house supply or call a plumber.
6. **Reconnect everything**, restore power, and run a short rinse cycle to verify normal fill. If the error persists, test for 120 V AC at the inlet valve solenoid during a fill cycle.
7. **Replace the inlet valve** if voltage is present but water does not flow, or if water flows too fast and trips the flood float. If the valve is fine, suspect the control module or float-chamber tube restriction.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the filter, straightened the hose, verified good household water pressure, and the error still appears. The repair may require testing the inlet valve solenoid with a multimeter, diagnosing the float-chamber tube for grease restriction, or replacing the control module (which is often discontinued and hard to source). A pro can also check for communication faults between the control board and the water-level sensor that are not visible to a homeowner.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
