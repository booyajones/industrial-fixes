## Bosch Dishwasher E17 Error — What It Means

The E17 error code on a Bosch dishwasher indicates a water inlet or filling fault. Bosch support documentation confirms this code means the dishwasher is not receiving water at the expected rate or the filling system has failed. The machine detects that water is not flowing into the unit normally during the fill cycle.

This code is grouped by Bosch with E16 as a water-flow or filling issue, not a drain problem. The dishwasher's control board has timed out waiting for the expected water level and has stopped the cycle to prevent damage. The root cause is almost always in the external water supply path, the inlet hose, the inlet valve, or a blockage in the fill system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the inlet valve first without checking the water supply or inlet screen. Always verify the shutoff valve is fully open and clean the inlet screen filter before condemning the valve.

## Common Causes

- **Water supply shutoff valve closed or restricted (~35%)** The valve under the sink or behind the dishwasher is not fully open, blocking or limiting water flow into the machine.
- **Kinked, crushed, or damaged inlet hose (~25%)** The flexible supply hose behind the dishwasher is bent, pinched during installation, or has internal collapse that restricts flow.
- **Clogged inlet screen or filter (~20%)** Sediment, mineral scale, or debris has accumulated in the mesh screen at the hose connection or valve inlet and is blocking water entry.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~15%)** The solenoid valve is stuck closed, electrically open-circuit, or mechanically blocked and cannot open to allow water into the tub.
- **Low household water pressure (~5%)** Insufficient supply pressure from the home plumbing system prevents the dishwasher from filling quickly enough to satisfy the timer.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The supply valve is open. Move on to inspect the inlet hose and screen.<br><strong>No:</strong> Turn the valve fully counterclockwise to open it completely, then reset the dishwasher and run a test cycle.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>When you disconnect the inlet hose at the dishwasher, does water flow freely from the house-side coupling into a bucket?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> House supply and hose are good. </details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After cleaning the inlet screen, does the dishwasher fill normally when you start a cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The screen blockage was the cause. No further parts are needed.<br><strong>No:</strong> The inlet valve itself is likely faulty and should be tested with a multimeter or replaced.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water shutoff valve under the sink or behind the dishwasher.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** slightly to access the rear and check the inlet hose for kinks, crushing, or damage. Straighten or replace the hose if restricted.
3. **Remove the lower front kick panel** (usually two screws at the top edge) to expose the inlet valve and connections at the bottom of the unit.
4. **Disconnect the inlet hose** from the valve and inspect the inlet screen or mesh filter at the valve connection. Rinse the screen under running water or use a small brush to remove sediment and scale.
5. **Test the inlet valve electrically** using a multimeter set to resistance mode. Disconnect the valve's wire harness and measure across the solenoid terminals. If the valve reads open-circuit or does not click when 120 V AC is briefly applied (with proper safety precautions), replace the valve.
6. **Inspect the valve body and internal fill path** for debris, corrosion, or mineral buildup that could block water flow. Clean or replace the valve if obstructed.
7. **Reassemble all connections**, restore water and power, and run a short test cycle to confirm the dishwasher fills normally and the E17 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified the water supply is fully open, the inlet hose is clear, and the inlet screen is clean but the E17 code persists. A technician can perform electrical diagnostics on the inlet valve solenoid, trace wiring for open circuits or damaged connectors, and inspect internal fill-path components like the sump chamber and any float-related sensors that may be contributing to the fault. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V AC connections or if the dishwasher is still under warranty and you want to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts (valve or hose), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 service call and parts.
