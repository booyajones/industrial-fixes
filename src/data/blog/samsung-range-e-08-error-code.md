## Samsung Range E-08 Error — What It Means

The E-08 error on a Samsung range or oven signals that the control board has commanded the oven to heat but is not detecting the expected temperature rise. In the field this typically appears as an oven that will not heat at all, even though the control panel accepts commands. The fault may be in the heating circuit (elements or igniter), the temperature sensing circuit (sensor or wiring), or the control logic itself.

On electric models the control looks for current flow through the bake or broil elements and monitors the temperature sensor to confirm heating. On gas models the control expects the igniter to draw sufficient current and the burner to light. When any part of that chain fails, the board throws E-08 and stops the cycle to prevent damage or unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Test the bake element for continuity and the temperature sensor for correct resistance (approximately 1080 ohms at room temperature) before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Burned-out bake or broil element** Electric ovens rely on resistance elements to generate heat, and a break or blister in the element stops current flow and triggers the error.
- **Out-of-range temperature sensor** The oven temperature probe reports resistance to the control board, and if the reading drifts more than about 200 ohms from specification the board assumes a fault.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections** Heat and vibration can loosen spade terminals or corrode connectors in the element or sensor circuit, breaking the electrical path.
- **Failed igniter on gas models** A weak igniter may glow briefly but fail to draw enough current to open the gas valve, so the oven never lights and the control sees no heat.
- **Control board relay or output failure** When elements and sensors test good but the oven still will not heat, the relay or solid-state output on the main board may have failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely before opening any panels or touching internal components.
2. **Remove the oven racks** and inspect the bake element (bottom) and broil element (top) for visible cracks, blisters, or burn marks that indicate an open circuit.
3. **Test the bake element** with a multimeter set to resistance. A good element shows continuity (usually 15–50 ohms). No reading means the element is open and must be replaced.
4. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** at the back or side wall of the oven cavity. Disconnect the two-wire plug and measure resistance across the sensor terminals. Compare the reading to approximately 1080 ohms at room temperature. If the sensor reads more than 200 ohms off, replace it.
5. **Inspect all wiring connectors** on the elements, sensor, and control board for loose spade terminals, heat discoloration, or corrosion. Push connectors fully onto terminals and check for burned relay sockets on the control board.
6. **Reassemble and restore power.** Start a bake cycle and confirm that the element glows and the oven begins to heat. If the error returns immediately, move to control board or relay diagnosis.
7. **On gas ranges**, watch the igniter during a bake cycle. It should glow bright orange for several seconds and then the burner should light. If the igniter glows but the burner does not light, replace the igniter assembly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if you have a gas range and smell gas during troubleshooting, or if you have replaced both the element and the sensor but the error persists. Diagnosing control board relay outputs or tracing broken wiring harnesses often requires a schematic and experience with appliance electronics. A qualified service technician can also verify gas pressure, igniter current draw, and control board outputs that are difficult to measure with basic tools.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
