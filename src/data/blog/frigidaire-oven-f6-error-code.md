## Frigidaire Oven F6 Error Code — What It Means

The F6 error code on Frigidaire ovens does not have a single universal meaning across all models. On some Frigidaire ranges it indicates a problem with a specific button or relay on the control board, while on other models it may point to a failed internal component or wiring issue. Because Frigidaire uses different control platforms across its product lines, the exact cause can vary. Always consult your model's wiring diagram or owner's manual to confirm what F6 means for your specific unit.

In general, F6 errors are tied to the electronic control board or the touchpad assembly. The code appears when the control detects an abnormal signal or a stuck relay. Power cycling the oven may temporarily clear the code, but if it returns the control board or touchpad usually needs replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the touchpad overlay first, but the touchpad itself rarely causes F6. Test the control board by disconnecting the touchpad ribbon cable and powering on the oven. If the error persists, the board is at fault.

## Common Causes

- **Failed electronic control board (~60%)** A relay or component on the main control board has shorted or failed, triggering the F6 fault.
- **Defective touchpad or membrane switch (~25%)** A stuck or shorted button in the touchpad assembly sends a continuous signal that the control interprets as a fault.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~10%)** Poor contact at the ribbon cable or harness connector between the touchpad and control board can generate an intermittent F6 code.
- **Power surge damage (~5%)** A voltage spike from the household supply can damage sensitive circuits on the control board, causing persistent error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by switching off the circuit breaker dedicated to the oven for at least three minutes to allow the control to reset fully.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether the F6 code reappears immediately or after starting a bake cycle. If it does not return, monitor the oven and stop here.
3. **Pull the oven** forward from the wall or remove the rear access panel to reach the control board, which is typically mounted behind the back panel or inside the console.
4. **Disconnect the touchpad ribbon cable** from the control board connector. Power the oven back on. If the F6 error persists, the control board is at fault.
5. **Inspect the control board** for burn marks, swollen capacitors, or damaged traces. Replace the board if you find physical damage.
6. **Order the correct replacement** using your model number. Control boards are model-specific, so verify the part number against your wiring diagram or service label.
7. **Install the new control board** by transferring wire connectors one at a time, reconnecting the touchpad cable, and securing the board in its bracket. Restore power and test all oven functions.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring or if the oven is still under warranty. A technician can confirm the diagnosis with a multimeter and service manual, verify that the replacement part matches your model, and handle the live electrical connections safely. If the error persists after replacing the control board, a pro can trace wiring faults or check for problems in the oven's relay board or sensor circuits that are not obvious during a visual inspection.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-220 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-380.
