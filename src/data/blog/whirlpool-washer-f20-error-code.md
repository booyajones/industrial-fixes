## Whirlpool Washer F20 Error Code — What It Means

F20 on a Whirlpool front-load washer signals a water inlet problem. The machine has detected no water or insufficient water supply entering the drum. Whirlpool defines this code as the washer not seeing the expected fill condition within its control logic.

In everyday terms, the washer tried to fill but timed out because too little water arrived. The fault can be as simple as a closed faucet or as involved as a failed inlet valve or pressure-sensing system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve immediately. Before ordering parts, verify both hot and cold faucets are fully open, clean the inlet screens, and check for kinked hoses. Most F20 codes clear once the water supply is restored.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or partially closed supply faucets (~35%)** Hot or cold valve at the wall not turned fully open, blocking flow.
- **Clogged inlet screens (~25%)** Sediment or debris trapped in the mesh filters at the washer valve ports.
- **Kinked or restricted inlet hoses (~15%)** Hose crushed behind the washer or twisted during installation.
- **Faulty water inlet valve assembly (~15%)** Valve solenoids fail to open even when power is applied.
- **Low house water pressure (~5%)** Municipal supply or well pump delivers insufficient flow to fill in time.
- **Pressure hose or switch fault (~5%)** Air tube to the pressure sensor is blocked, kinked, or the switch has failed, preventing the washer from confirming fill.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off both hot and cold water faucets** at the wall and unplug the washer from power.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the back panel and inspect the inlet hoses for kinks, crushing, or tight bends.
3. **Disconnect the inlet hoses** from the washer valve ports (keep a towel ready for residual water) and remove the small mesh screens inside each port with needle-nose pliers.
4. **Rinse the screens** under running water and use a soft brush to clear any sediment, then reinstall them and reconnect the hoses hand-tight plus a quarter turn with pliers.
5. **Unplug the washer for 5 minutes** to reset the control board, then plug it back in and turn both faucets fully open.
6. **Run a rinse or normal cycle** and watch for steady fill. If F20 reappears, isolate hot versus cold by running cycles with only one hose connected at a time to identify which side is faulty.
7. **If flow is good from the faucets but the washer still will not fill**, check the small air/pressure hose running from the tub to the pressure switch for holes or blockage, or test the inlet valve for correct voltage and mechanical opening. Replace the valve or pressure switch as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified strong water flow from both faucets, cleaned the inlet screens, straightened the hoses, and reset the control but F20 still appears. A pro will test the inlet valve for proper voltage and mechanical operation, inspect the pressure-sensing hose and switch for blockage or failure, and check control-board outputs. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connectors or pulling the washer away from the wall. If the main control board is suspect after all water-supply components test good, replacement and reprogramming often require a service visit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (screens or inlet valve), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call.
