## Whirlpool Dryer F4E3 Error Code — What It Means

F4E3 on a Whirlpool dryer is a dryer airflow fault. On standard Whirlpool dryers, the code indicates restricted or low airflow caused by a clogged lint screen, blocked exhaust duct, crushed vent hose, excessive vent length, or an exterior hood that cannot open freely. On Whirlpool all-in-one washer/dryer models, Whirlpool Product Help identifies F4E3 as a possible dryer blower motor issue, and the dry function may not operate even though the wash function does.

In practice, the code appears when the control board detects insufficient air movement through the dryer. Most cases trace to lint buildup or vent restrictions rather than a failed component. On the all-in-one platform, however, Whirlpool specifically calls out the blower motor as the likely failed part when other airflow checks pass.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or heating element first. Instead, disconnect the vent and run a test cycle to confirm the fault clears when airflow is unrestricted.

## Common Causes

- **Lint screen restriction (~35%)** Lint buildup or residue from dryer sheets and fabric softener blocks airflow through the screen.
- **Blocked exhaust duct (~30%)** Lint accumulation inside the vent run from the dryer to the exterior hood restricts air volume.
- **Crushed or kinked flex vent (~15%)** The flexible duct behind the dryer is compressed, kinked, or disconnected at a joint.
- **Exterior vent hood restriction (~10%)** The flap on the exterior hood is blocked or does not open freely during operation.
- **Excessive vent length or too many elbows (~5%)** The vent run is too long or has too many turns, reducing air volume below the threshold.
- **Dryer blower motor failure (all-in-one models) (~5%)** On all-in-one washer/dryer platforms, Whirlpool identifies the blower motor as the likely failed component when airflow paths are clear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** for at least 5 minutes to reset the control board, then restore power and retest.
2. **Remove and inspect the lint screen** for lint buildup or residue. If a film is present, wash the screen with warm water and a small amount of dish soap, rinse thoroughly, and dry completely before reinstalling.
3. **Pull the dryer away from the wall** and disconnect the vent hose from the rear exhaust port. Inspect the hose for kinks, crushing, lint blockage, or disconnected joints. Clean or replace as needed.
4. **Inspect the entire vent run** from the dryer to the exterior hood. Remove lint or debris, check for excessive length or too many elbows, and confirm all joints are secure.
5. **Check the exterior vent hood** to confirm the flap opens freely. Clean away any lint, bird nests, or other obstructions.
6. **Reconnect the vent** and run a Timed Dry or heated test cycle to confirm the fault clears.
7. **If the code persists on an all-in-one model**, call for service. Whirlpool guidance identifies the dryer blower motor as the likely failed component on that platform.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the fault persists after you have cleaned the lint screen, inspected and cleared the entire vent run, and confirmed the exterior hood opens freely. On all-in-one washer/dryer models, Whirlpool specifically recommends service when F4E3 appears, because the fault may involve the dryer blower motor. A technician can test the blower motor, airflow sensor, and control board with proper instruments. Also call for service if you are uncomfortable disconnecting the vent, working behind the dryer, or diagnosing electrical components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
