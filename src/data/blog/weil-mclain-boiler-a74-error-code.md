## Weil-McLain A74 Error Code — What It Means

The A74 code on a Weil-McLain boiler is commonly reported as an ignition or flame-proving fault. The boiler attempted to light, but the control did not successfully prove that flame was established. Because Weil-McLain uses different controls and fault nomenclature across product lines (Ultra, Evergreen, CGa, GV90+, WM97+, and others), the exact meaning must be confirmed by matching the code to your specific boiler model and control using the installation and service manual.

In real-world service, A74 typically means the ignition sequence started, the gas valve may have opened, but the flame sensor did not detect flame within the allowed time window. The boiler then locks out for safety. The fault can be caused by no flame actually forming, or by flame forming but the sensor failing to prove it to the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or control board first, when a dirty flame sensor is usually the culprit. Pull and inspect the flame sensor for soot, corrosion, or poor grounding before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~40%)** Soot, carbon, or mineral deposits coat the flame rod and prevent the microamp flame signal from reaching the control, even when flame is present.
- **Failed or fouled ignitor (~25%)** A cracked, dirty, or weak hot-surface igniter cannot generate enough heat to ignite the gas-air mixture during the ignition window.
- **Gas valve not opening (~15%)** The gas valve may be energized but stuck closed, or there is no gas pressure at the inlet due to a closed manual shutoff or supply problem.
- **Combustion air blower or pre-purge fault (~10%)** The inducer motor or pressure switch does not complete the pre-purge cycle, preventing the ignition sequence from advancing to flame proving.
- **Control board or wiring issue (~10%)** Loose pins, corroded connectors, poor grounding, or a failed board can interrupt the flame-sensing circuit even when all components check good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the boiler at the service switch and close the manual gas shutoff valve upstream of the unit.
2. **Locate your boiler's model and control number** on the rating plate or data label, then download or reference the correct installation and service manual from Weil-McLain to confirm the A74 fault definition and component locations for your specific model.
3. **Inspect the flame sensor** by removing the sensor rod from the burner assembly, checking for soot, white corrosion, cracks, or bending, and cleaning it gently with fine sandpaper or emery cloth until the metal is bright.
4. **Check the hot-surface ignitor** for cracks, chips, or heavy carbon buildup, and confirm it is positioned correctly in relation to the burner per the manual's diagram.
5. **Verify gas supply and pressure** by confirming the manual gas cock is open, other gas appliances operate normally, and measuring inlet gas pressure at the test port if your model provides one and the manual gives the spec.
6. **Observe a full startup cycle** with power and gas restored, watching for blower pre-purge, ignitor glow or spark, gas valve click, flame establishment, and whether the code returns, taking note of each step that completes or fails.
7. **Replace the failed component** (flame sensor, ignitor, gas valve, or control board) only after confirming which part is at fault through observation and testing, and consult the manual for part numbers and wiring diagrams.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician for an A74 fault if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, high-voltage wiring, or combustion systems. Gas boilers require precise diagnosis of ignition timing, flame proving circuits, and gas pressure, and mistakes can create safety hazards including gas leaks, carbon monoxide, or fire. A qualified technician will use the model-specific manual, measure flame signal in microamps if applicable, test gas valve operation and pressure, and verify venting and airflow per code. Professional diagnosis is also essential if you have already cleaned the flame sensor and inspected the ignitor but the fault returns, or if the boiler cycles repeatedly without establishing stable flame.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
