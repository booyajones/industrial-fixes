## A.O. Smith Tankless E30 Error Code — What It Means

The E30 error code on A.O. Smith tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame-sensing problem. The unit either fails to detect a flame after ignition or detects flame when it should not be present. This safety interlock prevents unsafe operation and shuts down the burner.

Because error code meanings can vary across different A.O. Smith tankless models, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover to confirm the exact definition for your model. The underlying issue usually involves the flame rod sensor, gas supply, or ignition sequence components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas control valve or igniter when the real culprit is a dirty flame rod that costs nothing to clean with fine sandpaper.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing rod prevents it from detecting current through the flame, triggering a false no-flame shutdown.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or air in the gas line can cause weak or unstable flame that the sensor cannot reliably detect.
- **Faulty flame rod or wiring (~15%)** A cracked ceramic insulator, broken wire, or corroded connection on the flame rod circuit prevents proper flame detection current flow.
- **Failed igniter or ignition transformer (~10%)** If the igniter does not produce a strong spark or is intermittent, the burner may not light consistently, leading to flame-fault errors.
- **Control board flame-sensing circuit failure (~5%)** A defective component on the main control board can misread the flame signal even when the rod and wiring are intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or disconnect switch and turn off the gas supply valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners (usually Phillips-head screws) and setting the panel aside.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe near the burner assembly, and inspect it for soot, carbon deposits, or corrosion.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit emery cloth or sandpaper, taking care not to bend or break the ceramic insulator.
5. **Check the flame rod wire connection** at the terminal and at the control board, looking for corrosion, looseness, or damage.
6. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve is fully open and checking that other gas appliances in the home operate normally.
7. **Reassemble the cover**, restore gas and power, and run a test cycle to see if the E30 code clears and the unit fires normally.
8. **If the error persists**, measure gas inlet pressure with a manometer (consult your model's table for required pressure) or call a licensed technician to inspect the igniter, gas valve, and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician whenever you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, lack a manometer to measure gas pressure, or cannot safely access internal components. Professional service is required if cleaning the flame rod and checking the gas supply do not resolve the E30 code, if you suspect a faulty gas valve or control board, or if you smell gas at any time. Technicians can test the ignition transformer output, measure flame rectification current, and verify proper venting and combustion air, all of which are beyond typical DIY scope.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
