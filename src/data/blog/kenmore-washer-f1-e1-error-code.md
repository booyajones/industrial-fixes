## Kenmore Washer F1 E1 Error Code — What It Means

F1 E1 on most Kenmore washers built on Whirlpool platforms signals a main control failure or fault in the electronic control board. The central control unit has detected an internal error or is receiving improper input from one of its circuits. Whirlpool service documentation defines F1E1 as a main control fault and recommends a power reset followed by board replacement if the error returns.

Because Kenmore washers are built by multiple OEMs, the exact meaning can vary slightly by model. If your manual shows a different definition or if you see a different code such as F7 E1 (basket speed sensor), consult your model's wiring diagram. For the majority of top-load Kenmore washers showing F1 E1, the fault points to the main electronic control board or a false signal reaching that board from the pressure sensor, drain system, or wiring harness.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the control board without first checking the air dome hose and drain pump. Inspect the pressure hose for kinks or clogs and clear any debris from the pump before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed electronic control board (~35%)** Internal failure of the main CCU that prevents normal cycle operation and requires board replacement.
- **Transient control glitch (~30%)** A temporary software or power fault in the main control that clears after unplugging the washer for five minutes.
- **Kinked or clogged air dome hose (~15%)** The pressure-sensing hose between the tub and control is bent, disconnected, or blocked, sending false water-level signals to the board.
- **Drain pump obstruction (~10%)** Debris, detergent buildup, or calcium deposits in the pump or drain path create abnormal sensing or prevent proper drainage.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness (~10%)** Damaged connectors or intermittent opens in the harness between the control board and motor control or sensors feed false inputs to the CCU.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and wait a full five minutes to allow the control board to reset and clear any transient fault.
2. **Plug the machine back in** and start a normal wash cycle to see if the F1 E1 error returns.
3. **Locate the air dome hose** running from the back of the control panel down to a fitting on the outer tub. Inspect the entire length for kinks, disconnections, clogs, or water inside the tube.
4. **Remove and blow through the hose** to confirm it is fully open. Reconnect it securely at both ends and retry a cycle.
5. **Access the drain pump** (usually behind a front lower panel or by tipping the washer back). Remove the filter or pump cover and clear any debris, coins, detergent buildup, or calcium deposits from the impeller and housing.
6. **Inspect all wiring harnesses** at the control board and motor-control connectors for loose pins, corrosion, or damaged insulation. Re-seat any suspect connectors.
7. **If the error persists** after these checks, order and install a replacement main electronic control board (CCU) for your exact Kenmore model number. Follow the installation guide to transfer connectors and mount the new board, then verify normal fill, wash, drain, and spin operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable removing panels or working near 120-volt wiring inside the control console. A pro can quickly verify whether the fault is a bad board, a wiring short, or a sensor issue and will have the correct replacement part on the truck. If your washer is still under warranty or a service plan, contact Kenmore or Sears Home Services for an authorized repair. If the machine continues to show F1 E1 even after a new control board is installed, the problem may lie in the motor control unit or a deeper harness fault that requires diagnostic equipment and schematic tracing.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $100-200 for control board, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-350 including labor.
