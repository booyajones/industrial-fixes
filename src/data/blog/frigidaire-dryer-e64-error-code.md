## What this code means
On Frigidaire and Frigidaire Affinity electric dryers, E64 signals a fault in the heater or heating element circuit. The control board has detected an open circuit in the heating system, which prevents the dryer from producing heat. This is not a performance or airflow issue. It is an electrical fault that typically points to a broken heating coil, damaged wiring to the heater assembly, or a failed safety device in the heating circuit.

The code appears when continuity is lost somewhere between the control board and the heating element. The dryer may still tumble and run cycles, but it will not heat. Repair involves identifying which component in the heating circuit has failed and restoring electrical continuity.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some people replace the control board first when E64 appears. Test the heating element and its wiring harness for continuity before ordering any electronic control.

## Common Causes

- **Broken heating element coil (~55%)** The metal coil inside the heater assembly cracks or burns open, creating an infinite-resistance open circuit that triggers E64.
- **Loose or damaged heater wiring (~20%)** Connectors at the heater assembly corrode, burn, or pull loose, interrupting the circuit and mimicking a failed element.
- **Failed thermal fuse (~15%)** The one-time thermal safety fuse in the heater circuit blows open during an overheat event, cutting power to the heating element.
- **Defective high-limit or cycling thermostat (~8%)** A stuck-open thermostat in the heating circuit prevents continuity and stops the heater from completing its electrical path.
- **Control board fault (~2%)** If all heater-side components test good, a relay or detection circuit on the main control board may be setting E64 incorrectly.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the dryer tumble normally but produce no heat?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor and belt are fine. The problem is isolated to the heating circuit, so proceed to heater testing.<br><strong>No:</strong> You may have multiple faults. Clear E64 first, then diagnose the motor or belt separately.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>With power off and the heater disconnected, does the heating element measure infinite resistance (OL) on a multimeter?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The element is open and must be replaced. A good element typically reads around 20 to 50 Ω.<br><strong>No:</strong> The element has continuity. Check the thermal fuse, thermostats, and wiring harness for the open circuit.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are any wire terminals at the heater assembly discolored, loose, or burned?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Clean or replace the damaged connector and retest. Heat damage here can open the circuit without a failed element.<br><strong>No:</strong> The wiring is intact. Focus on testing the element, thermal fuse, and limit thermostats with a meter.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off the circuit breaker to make sure zero electrical hazard during service.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** (or lower front panel on some models) to expose the heater assembly, which is usually a rectangular metal housing with a wire harness.
3. **Inspect the heating element coils** through any vent slots or after removing the housing cover for visible breaks, burn spots, or separated coils.
4. **Disconnect the heater wiring harness** and use a multimeter set to resistance (Ω) to test the heating element terminals for continuity. An open or infinite reading (OL) confirms a broken element.
5. **Test the thermal fuse and thermostats** mounted on or near the heater housing for continuity. A blown thermal fuse or stuck-open thermostat will also read infinite resistance.
6. **Replace the failed component** (heating element assembly, thermal fuse, or thermostat). If the element is open, replace the entire heater assembly or coil as your model allows.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, reconnect power, and run a timed-dry cycle to confirm heat returns and E64 does not reappear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if the wiring diagram is unclear, or if you lack a multimeter and do not want to test electrical components yourself. Also call for help if you replace the heating element and all safety thermostats yet E64 persists, since that points to a control-board relay fault or a wiring harness issue deeper in the machine. Professional diagnosis costs less than buying multiple parts on speculation.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
