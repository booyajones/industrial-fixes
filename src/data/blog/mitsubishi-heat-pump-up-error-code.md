## Mitsubishi UP Error Code — What It Means

The UP error code on a Mitsubishi heat pump indicates compressor overcurrent interruption. The outdoor unit has detected compressor current higher than expected and has stopped operation to protect the compressor and drive components. On some model families this code is also described as a compressor lock or protection fault, so you should verify the exact wording against your unit's service manual or the Mitsubishi Electric error lookup for your specific model.

This is not a transient fault. If the code appears, the system has detected a real overcurrent condition that requires diagnosis and correction before the unit can safely resume normal operation.

## Common Causes

- **Wiring or terminal faults** Loose, damaged, overheated, or incorrectly landed conductors at the indoor, outdoor, or interconnect terminals can create resistance or poor contact that drives overcurrent conditions.
- **Power supply issues** Incorrect voltage, wrong phase on applicable models, or improper breaker and fuse sizing can contribute to compressor overcurrent trips.
- **Mechanically locked compressor** A seized or locked compressor will draw excessive current on startup and trigger the protection circuit.
- **Failing inverter or compressor drive board** A faulty inverter PCB or compressor drive circuit can incorrectly energize the compressor or fail to regulate current properly.
- **Refrigerant or system stress** Abnormal operating conditions such as improper charge, restriction, or extreme ambient temperature can overload the compressor and trip overcurrent protection.
- **Improper installation** Incorrect power configuration, phase reversal, or undersized wiring during installation can create conditions that trigger UP.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the code in service history** at the controller or service menu because Mitsubishi systems can store prior faults even if the live display has cleared.
2. **Power down the system** at the breaker and open both indoor and outdoor disconnect switches before beginning any inspection.
3. **Inspect all wiring and terminals** at the indoor unit, outdoor unit, and interconnect for loose connections, corrosion, burned contacts, or damaged insulation.
4. **Verify supply voltage and phase** at the outdoor unit disconnect using a multimeter and confirm breaker sizing matches the unit nameplate requirements.
5. **Check the outdoor unit mechanically** for signs of compressor seizure, fan obstruction, overheating, or physical damage to components.
6. **Review refrigerant operating conditions** if your model's service manual calls for it, checking pressures and temperatures against the operating range for your ambient conditions.
7. **Reset the system only after correcting the root cause** by restoring power at the breaker and using the remote or service menu. If UP returns immediately or after a short run, the fault requires component-level diagnosis and likely board or compressor replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician immediately if you are not trained in heat pump diagnostics or if you are uncomfortable working with high-voltage electrical components. Compressor overcurrent faults require methodical diagnosis of power supply, control circuitry, and mechanical condition. Technicians use the fault history, wiring diagrams, voltage and current measurements, and model-specific service documentation to isolate the root cause. If the fault returns after a reset or if you see burned terminals, hear unusual compressor noise, or smell overheated components, do not attempt further resets and call for service.
