## Navien E326 Error Code — What It Means

The E326 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically signals a problem with the combustion fan system or air intake and exhaust. The unit's control board has detected that the fan is not spinning at the correct speed, is failing to start, or that air flow is restricted. Because combustion safety depends on proper ventilation, the unit will lock out and refuse to fire until the fault is cleared.

This code can appear due to a failing fan motor, broken or loose wiring to the fan, a blocked air intake or exhaust vent, or debris obstructing the fan itself. In some cases a failing control board may incorrectly report the fault. Always consult your model's service manual for the exact definition, as error codes can vary slightly across different Navien models and firmware versions.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first, assuming electronics are at fault. Check the fan motor's wiring connections and listen for unusual noise or failure to spin before replacing any board.

## Common Causes

- **Combustion fan motor failure (~45%)** The fan motor's bearings wear out or the windings fail, preventing the fan from reaching the speed the board expects.
- **Loose or corroded fan wiring (~25%)** Vibration or moisture causes connections at the fan harness to loosen or corrode, interrupting the power or feedback signal.
- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent (~15%)** Debris, lint, insect nests, or ice block the intake or exhaust pipe, restricting air flow and causing the fan to labor or stall.
- **Debris in the fan housing (~10%)** Dust, cobwebs, or small objects lodge in the fan blades or housing, preventing free rotation.
- **Failed control board (~5%)** The main PCB incorrectly reads the fan speed signal or fails to supply proper voltage to the motor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the breaker and close the gas isolation valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners and lifting the panel away.
3. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vents** outside the building for blockages, nests, or debris and clear any obstructions.
4. **Locate the combustion fan** inside the unit (usually a round blower assembly near the burner chamber) and check that the blades spin freely by hand with no grinding or resistance.
5. **Examine the wiring harness** at the fan motor for loose plugs, broken wires, or corrosion and reseat or clean connections as needed.
6. **Restore power** and observe the fan during a call for hot water. Listen for unusual noise or failure to spin.
7. **If the fan does not run**, use a multimeter to verify voltage at the fan connector during startup. If voltage is present but the fan is silent, replace the fan motor. If no voltage is present, inspect the control board and its fuse.
8. **Clear the error code** by cycling power or following the reset procedure in your manual, then test hot water flow to confirm normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are not comfortable working around gas appliances, if you cannot safely access the fan or wiring, or if you lack a multimeter to test voltage. Combustion system repairs require knowledge of venting codes and gas safety. A pro will quickly isolate whether the fan motor, wiring, or control board is at fault and will have factory parts on the truck. If your unit is under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $250-500.
