## Frigidaire Oven F2 Error Code — What It Means

On most Frigidaire oven models, F2 indicates an over-temperature condition or a fault in the oven temperature sensor (RTD). The control sees the oven as too hot or the sensor is reporting out-of-range resistance. On some model families, Frigidaire uses F2 as a communication fault between the main control board and the user interface or auxiliary board, making diagnosis model-dependent.

Because the code meaning is not uniform across all Frigidaire ovens, always verify your exact model number and symptom pattern before replacing parts. The over-temperature interpretation is the most common in the field, and in that case the oven temperature sensor or its wiring is the usual culprit. If your model uses the communication-fault interpretation, you will need to inspect ribbon cables and control boards instead.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board when F2 appears, but a faulty or out-of-range oven temperature sensor is the usual fix. Measure the sensor resistance at room temperature (typically about 1050–1100 Ω) and inspect the harness before buying any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (RTD) (~50%)** The sensor's resistance drifts out of range, is open, or shorted, causing the control to read an impossible temperature and trip F2.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wiring or connectors (~20%)** Intermittent contact at the sensor harness, control connector, or ribbon cable (on communication-fault models) creates a fault signal.
- **Stuck bake or broil relay on the control board (~15%)** A relay fails closed, leaving a heating element energized and creating a real overheat condition that triggers F2.
- **Failed main control board or user interface board (~10%)** On communication-fault variants, a defective main ERC or UI/touchpad board cannot exchange data and logs F2 or related codes like F21 and F23.
- **Temporary power glitch (~5%)** A voltage interruption or surge leaves the control in a fault state that may clear with a full power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait two minutes, then restore power to see if F2 clears (rules out a transient glitch).
2. **Look up your model number** on the Frigidaire service site or in your owner's manual to confirm whether F2 is an over-temperature/sensor fault or a communication fault on your specific range.
3. **Access the oven temperature sensor** (usually mounted through the rear wall of the oven cavity), unplug the two-wire connector, and use a multimeter to measure resistance at room temperature (typically about 1050–1100 Ω for most models).
4. **Inspect the sensor harness and connectors** for burns, corrosion, loose pins, or damaged insulation from the sensor back to the control board.
5. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if resistance is out of range, open, or shorted, or if the wiring shows damage at the sensor end.
6. **Check for a stuck relay** on the main control board if the sensor tests good but the oven still overheats or F2 returns immediately (you may hear a relay click or see scorch marks near the relay).
7. **Inspect ribbon cables and UI board connections** if your model uses F2 as a communication fault, verifying that the harness between the main control and the user interface is firmly seated and undamaged.
8. **Run a test bake cycle** after the repair to confirm the oven heats normally and F2 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt electric range, if the control board shows signs of arcing or burnt relays, or if the F2 code persists after you have replaced the oven temperature sensor and verified all wiring. A technician has the service manual, voltage test points, and board-level diagnostic tools to isolate a stuck relay, a failed control output, or a communication-fault board pairing. Also call if your model uses the communication-fault interpretation of F2 and you cannot determine which board (main control or user interface) is at fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–60 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
