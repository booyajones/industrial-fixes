## LG Oven H5 Error Code — What It Means

The H5 error code on LG ovens typically indicates a problem with the heating system, though the exact meaning can vary by model. On many LG ranges and wall ovens, this code points to a fault with the bake element circuit, a temperature sensor reading outside normal range, or a communication issue between the control board and heating components. Because LG uses different control platforms across its oven lineup, always consult your specific model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm what H5 means for your unit.

In general, the oven will not heat properly or will shut down when this code appears. The control may display the code continuously or show it intermittently during a preheat or bake cycle. The fault is usually in the lower bake element circuit, the oven temperature sensor (RTD), or the main control board that monitors these components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but a failed bake element or temperature sensor is far more common. Use a multimeter to test element continuity and sensor resistance before spending money on a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed bake element (~40%)** The lower bake element develops an open circuit or short, preventing current flow and triggering a heating fault code.
- **Faulty oven temperature sensor (~30%)** The RTD sensor drifts out of spec or fails open, sending incorrect temperature readings to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~15%)** Wire terminals at the element, sensor, or control board become loose or oxidized, breaking the circuit intermittently.
- **Main control board fault (~10%)** The electronic oven control (EOC) board relay or monitoring circuit fails, misreading sensor input or not energizing the element.
- **Tripped thermal fuse or cutoff (~5%)** An internal thermal fuse in the element circuit opens due to overheating, cutting power to the bake element permanently.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven to make sure safe work conditions.
2. **Remove the oven racks** and locate the lower bake element, typically secured by two screws at the rear wall of the oven cavity.
3. **Unscrew and pull the element forward** to expose the wire terminals, then disconnect the two wire leads by pulling off the spade connectors.
4. **Test the element with a multimeter** set to ohms; a good bake element will show continuity and a resistance reading (typically 15-50 ohms depending on wattage), while a failed element shows infinite resistance or zero if shorted.
5. **Inspect the oven temperature sensor**, a thin metal probe usually mounted at the upper rear of the cavity, and disconnect its wire plug; measure resistance (typically 1000-1200 ohms at room temperature) and replace if out of range.
6. **Check all wire terminals and connectors** for signs of burning, corrosion, or looseness; clean or crimp as needed, or replace damaged wire harness sections.
7. **Install the new bake element or sensor** by reversing removal steps, ensuring all connectors are firmly seated and screws are snug.
8. **Restore power and run a test bake cycle** to verify the code clears and the oven heats to the set temperature without error.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt appliance wiring, if the error persists after replacing both the bake element and temperature sensor, or if you suspect the main control board or internal thermal fuse. A technician has the service manual, wiring diagrams, and board-level diagnostic tools to pinpoint control-board faults and safely replace high-voltage components. Also call if you see evidence of arcing, burnt wire insulation, or damage inside the control-panel area, as these issues require experience with appliance electronics and fire-safety protocols.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
