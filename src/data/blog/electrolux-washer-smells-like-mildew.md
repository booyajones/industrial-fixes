## Electrolux Washer Smells Like Mildew — What's Happening

An Electrolux washer that smells like mildew is not displaying a fault code. This is a maintenance symptom caused by mold or bacteria growth from trapped moisture and detergent residue, especially after low-temperature washing or infrequent cleaning. The odor is typically strongest around the door seal, detergent drawer, or drum.

Electrolux support materials for washer odors point to cleaning the seal, detergent drawer, drum, and drain filter rather than replacing electronic components. The smell comes from organic buildup in areas that stay damp between cycles, not from a failed sensor or control board.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Moisture trapped in the door gasket or door seal** Water and debris sit in the folds of the rubber seal after cycles, creating a breeding ground for mold and mildew if the door is kept closed or the seal is not wiped dry.
- **Detergent residue buildup in the dispenser drawer or housing** Excess detergent or use of the wrong type leaves sticky residue in the drawer and cavity, which traps moisture and grows bacteria over time.
- **Infrequent hot maintenance washes** Running only low-temperature cycles allows soap scum and biofilm to accumulate inside the drum and hoses because they are not regularly sanitized by hot water.
- **Dirty or clogged drain pump filter** Lint, hair, and debris in the filter prevent complete drainage, leaving residual water in the pump area that stagnates and smells.
- **Poor ventilation or door kept closed between loads** Closing the door and drawer immediately after use prevents the tub and seals from air-drying, keeping them damp and encouraging mold growth.
- **Incomplete drainage or residual water in the drum** A restricted drain path or pump issue can leave standing water in the tub or hoses, which turns foul between cycles.
- **Damaged or torn door seal holding contamination** Cracks or tears in the gasket allow water and debris to hide in pockets that cannot be cleaned, requiring seal replacement by a service engineer.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the complaint by smelling the door seal, dispenser drawer, drum interior, and drain filter area to locate the strongest odor source.
2. Inspect the door gasket by pulling back the rubber folds and checking for slime, mold, debris, tears, or trapped items that hold moisture.
3. Remove the detergent drawer completely, wash it under running water, and clean the cavity behind it with a damp cloth to remove residue and biofilm.
4. Access the drain pump filter at the lower front of the machine, unplug the washer, capture residual water with a shallow pan, remove the filter, clear lint and debris, rinse it, and reinstall securely.
5. Run the hottest available empty cycle with an approved washer cleaner to sanitize the drum, hoses, and internal surfaces.
6. Wipe the door seal dry with a clean cloth and leave the door and detergent drawer open after the cycle so the tub and seals can air-dry completely.
7. If odor persists after cleaning, re-check the drain path for incomplete drainage, a restricted filter, or a seal that remains contaminated or shows physical damage.
8. Escalate to component inspection only if you find evidence of abnormal drainage, a leaking pump, or a door seal that is cracked or torn and cannot be cleaned.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the odor persists after thorough cleaning of the seal, drawer, filter, and drum, or if you find the door gasket is cracked or torn and needs replacement. A qualified service engineer should handle seal replacement and any inspection of the drain pump or drain path if you suspect incomplete drainage or residual water retention. If the machine shows signs of poor draining or you are uncomfortable accessing the filter or internal components, schedule a service call rather than forcing parts or guessing at electrical issues.
