## Frigidaire Range F91 Error Code — What It Means

F91 is a door lock issue on Frigidaire ranges. The electronic oven control (EOC) has tried to unlock the oven door more times than allowed and still cannot confirm an unlocked condition. Frigidaire groups F90, F91, F92, F93, and F94 under the general heading of door lock faults, and F91 specifically means repeated unlock attempts have failed.

The range senses that the lock mechanism has not returned to the unlocked position even after multiple cycles. This fault typically appears after a self-clean cycle or when you try to open the door and the control cannot release the lock. The oven will usually remain locked and inoperable until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the EOC (control board) first. Check the door lock assembly, switch, and wiring connections before replacing the board, because a jammed lock or loose connector is far more common than a failed EOC.

## Common Causes

- **Binding or obstructed door lock mechanism (~40%)** Debris, grease, or a bent linkage prevents the lock cam or rod from returning to the unlocked position, so the EOC cannot confirm unlock.
- **Failed door lock motor or assembly (~35%)** The lock motor wears out or the internal gears strip, leaving the assembly unable to drive the lock cam back to the unlocked state.
- **Faulty door lock switch (~15%)** The switch that signals locked or unlocked status to the EOC fails to change state, so the control thinks the door is still locked even when it is not.
- **Damaged or loose wiring between the EOC and lock assembly (~10%)** Broken wires, corroded terminals, or a loose connector interrupt the signal or power to the lock motor and switch.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds, then restore power and check if the error clears.
2. **Open the oven door** and locate the door lock assembly (usually mounted on the front frame above the door or on the side of the cavity).
3. **Inspect the lock mechanism** for debris, grease buildup, or bent linkage that would prevent smooth movement of the cam, rod, and motor.
4. **Manually cycle the lock cam** (with power off) to verify it moves freely through the full lock and unlock range without binding.
5. **Check the door lock switch** by observing or testing continuity as you move the cam; the switch should change state between locked and unlocked positions.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness and connectors** between the EOC and the lock assembly for loose pins, broken wires, or corrosion.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor does not operate, the mechanism binds, or the switch does not change state correctly; if all components test good and the fault persists, replace the EOC.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt connections or if the door lock assembly is difficult to access on your model. A technician has the tools to test the lock motor circuit, measure switch continuity under load, and safely diagnose the EOC. Also call for service if you have replaced the lock assembly and inspected all wiring but F91 persists, because the fault may lie in the control board or a hidden harness issue that requires a wiring diagram and multimeter work.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
