## State Water Heater E06 Error Code — What It Means

E06 is not a universally documented code across all State water heater models, so you must verify the exact meaning in your unit's service manual using the model number from the data plate. On some tankless water heaters from other manufacturers, E06 indicates that outlet water temperature has exceeded 140°F for three seconds or longer, triggering an overheat shutdown. On other product families, an E6 code can mean the heater cannot maintain the set temperature without overheating, often due to low flow or high inlet water temperature. Because State does not publish a single E06 definition that covers all models, the troubleshooting steps below address both overtemperature faults and sensor circuit problems that commonly produce similar codes on tankless and electronic tank-type heaters.

If your State unit is displaying E06 and shutting down, start by measuring the actual outlet water temperature with a probe thermometer and comparing it to the setpoint. If the water is genuinely too hot, you have a real overtemperature condition that may stem from restricted flow, scale buildup, or a faulty temperature sensor sending incorrect data to the control board. If the outlet is near setpoint but the code appears anyway, suspect a failed thermistor or wiring problem that makes the board see an out-of-range resistance value.

## Common Causes

- **Outlet water exceeds the high-limit threshold** On some tankless models, outlet temperatures above 140°F for three seconds will trip E06 and shut down the burner or element to prevent scalding.
- **Low water flow rate through the heat exchanger** Restricted flow forces the same amount of heat into less water, raising outlet temperature and triggering overheat protection or preventing the unit from maintaining setpoint without overshooting.
- **Failed outlet temperature sensor or thermistor** A shorted sensor showing zero ohms or an open sensor reading infinite resistance will send invalid data to the control board, which then displays a fault code and locks out operation.
- **Scale or mineral buildup inside the heat exchanger** Hard-water deposits narrow flow passages and create hot spots, raising local temperatures above the sensor threshold even when bulk outlet temperature is acceptable.
- **High incoming cold-water temperature** Inlet water warmer than design conditions reduces the margin between setpoint and high limit, making it easier for the heater to overshoot and fault out during normal firing.
- **Control board misreading sensor circuit** Corroded connectors, damaged wiring, or board-level failures can make the controller see a sensor short or open when the thermistor itself is still good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the model and serial number** on the data plate and download the service manual from State's website or call technical support to confirm that E06 on your specific model means overtemperature or sensor fault.
2. **Measure outlet water temperature** with a calibrated probe thermometer while the code is active or immediately after shutdown, and compare the reading to your setpoint and the high-limit threshold listed in the manual.
3. **Check water flow rate** at a nearby tap by timing how many seconds it takes to fill a gallon jug, then compare to the minimum flow requirement in your manual (typically 0.5 to 0.75 gallons per minute for tankless units).
4. **Inspect and clean inlet screens and filters** at the cold-water inlet valve and at any inline filters, flushing out sediment and checking for kinked or undersized supply lines that restrict flow.
5. **Test the outlet thermistor resistance** by unplugging the sensor connector at the board, measuring across the thermistor leads with a multimeter set to ohms, and comparing the reading to the temperature-resistance table in the service manual (typical range is 8,000 to 12,000 ohms at room temperature).
6. **Examine wiring and connectors** between the sensor and control board for corrosion, loose pins, or melted insulation, cleaning contacts with electrical contact cleaner and reseating all plugs firmly.
7. **Perform a flush or descale procedure** if the manual recommends it and you have hard water, circulating manufacturer-approved descaling solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve mineral deposits and restore full flow area.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you cannot find an E06 definition in your State manual, if the outlet temperature is dangerously high and you smell gas or see steam, or if thermistor resistance falls within specification but the code reappears immediately after reset. Professional help is also warranted when descaling or heat-exchanger disassembly is needed on a tankless unit, when you lack a multimeter and temperature-resistance table to test the sensor correctly, or when local code requires permitted work for any gas or electrical connections. A qualified tech can verify actual fault conditions with calibrated instruments, cross-reference your serial number to the correct parts list, and replace sensors or boards under warranty if your unit is still covered.
