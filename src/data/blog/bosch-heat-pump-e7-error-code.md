## Bosch Heat Pump E7 Error — What It Means

E7 on Bosch heat pump and air conditioning equipment indicates a discharge temperature sensor error. The system has detected that the sensor monitoring compressor discharge temperature is out of range, disconnected, or sending faulty readings to the control board. This safety feature prevents compressor damage from overheating. Note that E7 has a different meaning on Bosch gas furnaces (ignition lockout), so confirm you have a heat pump or air conditioner before following this guide.

## Common Causes

- **Failed discharge temperature sensor** The thermistor has drifted out of specification or failed internally, sending incorrect resistance values to the board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring** Connector pins at the sensor or control board are corroded, backed out, or the harness has been damaged by vibration or service work.
- **Sensor dislodged from mounting** The sensor bulb has pulled away from the discharge line or lost thermal contact, reading ambient air instead of refrigerant temperature.
- **Control board input failure** The board's sensor input circuit has failed even though the sensor itself tests within range.
- **Wrong code interpretation** The unit is actually a Bosch furnace where E7 means ignition lockout, leading to misdiagnosis if heat-pump troubleshooting is applied.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model family** by checking the rating plate and confirm the unit is a heat pump or air conditioner, not a gas furnace, since E7 has different meanings across Bosch product lines.
2. **Power off the system** at the disconnect and wait two minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening the outdoor unit cabinet.
3. **Locate the discharge temperature sensor** on the compressor discharge line near the service valve and inspect the connector and wiring for corrosion, damage, or loose pins.
4. **Measure sensor resistance** with a multimeter after disconnecting the sensor leads. Bosch IDS and IDP models use 55,000-ohm thermistors, so compare your reading to the temperature-resistance chart in your model's service manual.
5. **Replace the sensor** if resistance is out of specification or if the sensor shows an open or short circuit.
6. **Check harness continuity** from the sensor plug to the control board connector if the sensor tested good, looking for broken wires or bad crimps in the outdoor harness.
7. **Restore power and monitor** the system for at least one full cycle. If E7 returns immediately with a known-good sensor and wiring, the outdoor control board input circuit has likely failed and the board needs replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical components or refrigeration equipment. Misdiagnosing E7 by confusing heat pump and furnace code definitions wastes time and money. A technician has the manufacturer temperature-resistance charts, the correct replacement sensor part numbers for your serial number range, and the tools to verify that the repair has restored safe compressor operation. If the sensor and wiring check out but the fault remains, board-level diagnosis and refrigerant-circuit checks require certification and specialized instruments.
