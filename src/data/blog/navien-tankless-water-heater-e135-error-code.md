## Navien E135 Error Code — What It Means

The E135 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates the unit has detected a problem during the ignition or combustion process. The heater either failed to ignite when demanded, lost flame during operation, or sensed an unsafe condition in the burner chamber. The unit shuts down to protect itself and your home.

This code typically involves the flame detection system, which uses a flame rod to confirm combustion is occurring safely. When the sensor cannot verify proper flame, the control board throws the E135 code and locks out the unit. The underlying issue may be related to fuel delivery, air supply, or the sensing circuit itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or gas valve first. Check and clean the flame rod and burner orifices before ordering parts, since buildup usually causes the sensing failure.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Carbon or mineral buildup on the flame rod insulates it and prevents proper flame sensing, so the board cannot confirm ignition.
- **Blocked or dirty burner orifices (~25%)** Dust, debris, or corrosion in the burner ports disrupts the flame pattern and prevents stable combustion.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or flow (~15%)** Low inlet gas pressure or a partially closed valve starves the burner and prevents reliable ignition.
- **Restricted air intake or exhaust vent (~10%)** Blockage in the combustion air path or exhaust pipe creates poor airflow and incomplete combustion.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition cable (~7%)** A weak or failed igniter does not produce enough spark to light the gas, or a damaged cable prevents the spark from reaching the burner.
- **Failed flame rod or wiring (~3%)** A broken flame rod or loose wire in the sensing circuit prevents the board from detecting flame even when present.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve upstream of the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws and lifting the panel away to access the burner chamber.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner. Disconnect the wire connector and unscrew the mounting hardware.
4. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a ScotchBrite pad until the metal is shiny and free of carbon or oxidation. Do not bend the rod.
5. **Inspect the burner orifices** for dust, rust, or blockage. Use a soft brush or compressed air to clean the ports without damaging them.
6. **Check the igniter** for cracks or carbon tracking. Wipe the ceramic insulator clean with a dry cloth.
7. **Reassemble the flame rod** and igniter, ensuring tight electrical connections and proper clearance from the burner.
8. **Restore gas and power**, then open a hot-water tap to call for heat. Observe ignition through the inspection window and verify the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod and burner does not resolve the E135 code, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a failed gas valve. Professionals have the meters to measure inlet gas pressure, the tools to disassemble the burner chamber safely, and the training to test ignition circuits. They can also inspect the heat exchanger for scale buildup or corrosion that may interfere with combustion. Any work involving gas piping, pressure regulation, or internal control boards should be left to a qualified technician to avoid safety hazards and maintain your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
