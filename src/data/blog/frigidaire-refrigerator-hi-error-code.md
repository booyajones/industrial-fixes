## Frigidaire HI Error Code — What It Means

The H or HI error code on a Frigidaire refrigerator is a high-temperature alarm. The control has detected that one or both compartments (refrigerator or freezer) are warmer than the expected setpoint. The unit will illuminate a High Temp indicator and sound an alarm every 60 seconds until you acknowledge it with the CLEAR ALERTS button on supported models.

This code does not identify a single failed part. It simply reports that the cabinet is too warm, which can happen for usage reasons (door left open, temperature settings changed, poor door seal) or mechanical problems (blocked airflow, dirty condenser coils, failed evaporator fan, defrost system fault, or faulty temperature sensor). The code can appear after a power interruption, extended door opening, or any event that allowed the compartments to warm up.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the temperature sensor or control board first, but most HI alarms are caused by airflow or door-seal problems. Before ordering parts, check that doors close fully, gaskets seal without gaps, and interior vents are not blocked by food.

## Common Causes

- **Door left open or door not closing fully (~35%)** When a door stays open or does not latch, warm room air floods the compartment and the control reports high temperature.
- **Poor door seal or torn gasket (~25%)** A damaged, dirty, or misaligned door gasket allows warm air infiltration even when the door appears closed.
- **Blocked air vents or airflow obstruction (~15%)** Food packages or bins blocking interior vents prevent cold air from circulating, causing warm spots and triggering the alarm.
- **Dirty condenser coils (~10%)** Dust and debris on the condenser coils reduce heat rejection, forcing the cabinet to run warmer and eventually trip the high-temp alarm.
- **Evaporator fan or damper failure (~8%)** A stopped evaporator fan or stuck air damper cuts off cold air delivery to the refrigerator compartment, especially on side-by-side models.
- **Defrost system fault causing ice buildup (~5%)** When the defrost system fails, ice accumulates on the evaporator coil and blocks airflow, starving the cabinet of cold air.
- **Faulty thermistor or temperature sensor (~2%)** A failed sensor can report an incorrect high temperature even when the actual compartment temperature is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the alarm** by pressing CLEAR ALERTS on models that have the button, or by unplugging the refrigerator for 30 seconds to 5 minutes and restoring power.
2. **Check both doors** to confirm they close fully and are not blocked by shelves, bins, or food packaging that prevents a complete seal.
3. **Inspect the door gasket** on all edges for tears, folds, debris, or visible gaps; clean the gasket with warm soapy water and wipe dry, then test the seal by closing the door on a dollar bill and pulling it out (you should feel resistance).
4. **Verify temperature setpoints** on the control panel; Frigidaire recommends 37°F for the refrigerator compartment and 0°F for the freezer as standard kitchen settings.
5. **Check interior airflow** by removing food items that block vents at the back or top of the refrigerator compartment and confirming cold air can circulate freely.
6. **Clean the condenser coils** by unplugging the unit, locating the coils (usually at the rear or beneath the unit behind a grille), and vacuuming or brushing away dust and debris.
7. **Test the evaporator fan** by listening for fan noise when the compressor is running; if you hear no fan or the refrigerator compartment has no airflow, inspect the fan motor, damper, and evaporator area for ice buildup that signals a defrost problem.
8. **Measure actual compartment temperatures** with an independent thermometer after waiting 4-6 hours; if the display shows HI but the thermometer reads normal, test the thermistor with a multimeter and compare its resistance to the temperature chart in your service manual.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have confirmed the doors and gaskets are good, the vents are clear, the coils are clean, and the HI alarm persists after a full day. A pro can test the evaporator fan, damper, defrost system, thermistor, and control board with proper tools and diagnose refrigerant or sealed-system issues if the compressor is running but the cabinet will not cool. Also call if you see heavy ice buildup on the evaporator coil or if the fan has stopped and you are not comfortable removing panels or testing electrical components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 20-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
