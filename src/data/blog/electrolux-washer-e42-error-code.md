## Electrolux Washer E42 Error Code — What It Means

The E42 error code on Electrolux front-load washers generally indicates a problem with the door lock mechanism. The control board detects that the door is not locking properly or that the lock circuit is not completing as expected. The washer will not start a cycle until the door is securely locked for safety reasons.

This code can result from mechanical issues with the latch itself, electrical problems in the lock assembly, or wiring and connector faults. Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary between model series, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel to confirm the code definition for your specific washer.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the control board assuming it is at fault when the door lock assembly or a loose connector is the real problem. Test the lock mechanism and inspect the wiring harness before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock assembly (~55%)** The electric lock solenoid or switch inside the door lock mechanism wears out or burns out, preventing the control board from detecting a secure lock.
- **Obstructed or misaligned door latch (~20%)** Lint, detergent residue, or a bent strike plate prevents the door from closing completely or the latch from engaging the lock.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness (~15%)** The connector at the door lock or along the harness becomes loose, corroded, or damaged, breaking the electrical circuit to the lock.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** The main control board fails to send voltage to the door lock or does not recognize the lock signal, though this is less common than a bad lock assembly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and turn off the water supply valves to make sure safety during the repair.
2. **Clean the door latch and strike** with a damp cloth to remove lint, detergent buildup, or foreign objects that might prevent full engagement.
3. **Locate the door lock assembly** by opening the door and removing the screws that secure the inner door boot clamp at the front of the tub opening, then peel back the rubber boot to expose the lock on the door opening frame.
4. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the door lock assembly and inspect the connector pins for corrosion, burn marks, or looseness, cleaning or reseating as needed.
5. **Test the door lock** with a multimeter set to continuity mode by checking across the lock's common and normally open terminals while manually engaging the latch, looking for a closed circuit when locked and an open circuit when unlocked.
6. **Replace the door lock assembly** if it fails the continuity test or shows visible damage, securing the new unit with the mounting screws and reconnecting the wiring harness firmly.
7. **Reassemble the door boot clamp** and any panels removed, restore power and water, then run a short test cycle to verify the door locks and the error code does not reappear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if testing reveals the control board itself is faulty and requires replacement. A technician can also diagnose intermittent wiring faults that are difficult to trace without specialized tools. If the door lock replacement does not resolve the error and the wiring tests good, the control board may need to be replaced, a job best handled by a qualified service provider who can confirm the diagnosis and program the new board if required.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
