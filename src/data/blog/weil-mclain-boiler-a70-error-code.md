## Weil-McLain Boiler A70 Error — What It Means

The A70 code is not documented in Weil-McLain's published error-code lists for Ultra, Aqua Balance, or CGa series boilers. Weil-McLain codes are platform-specific and vary by control family, so you must identify your exact boiler model and consult that model's service manual or control diagnostic menu to confirm what A70 means on your unit. If your boiler is displaying A70 and locking out, treat it as a fault condition and retrieve the fault history from the contractor menu before resetting.

Because the code is not standard, the most common underlying issues when a Weil-McLain boiler locks out are gas supply problems, ignition or flame-detection electrode faults, blocked condensate traps on condensing models, or low system water pressure. Do not reset repeatedly without diagnosing the root cause, and do not assume the meaning from another boiler brand or model.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or control board when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor or blocked condensate trap. Check and clean the ignition and flame-sensing electrodes and clear the condensate trap before replacing any expensive components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or damaged ignition or flame-sensing electrode (~30%)** Carbon buildup, corrosion, or a cracked ceramic insulator prevents reliable spark or flame detection.
- **No gas flow or closed shutoff (~25%)** Manual gas valve is closed, gas meter is off, or supply has been interrupted, preventing ignition.
- **Blocked condensate trap or drain (~20%)** On condensing boilers, a clogged trap or improper drain routing can lock out the ignition sequence.
- **Low gas supply pressure (~15%)** Inlet gas pressure below the boiler's specification (typically 3.5–10.5 in. w.c. for many Weil-McLain models) prevents ignition.
- **Faulty gas valve (~10%)** Internal valve failure or stuck operator prevents gas from reaching the burner even when commanded open.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the displayed code** and boiler model number, then consult your model's service manual or error-code chart to confirm the exact meaning of A70 for your control platform.
2. **Access the fault history** through the control's diagnostic or contractor menu before resetting, and write down all stored fault codes to help identify the pattern.
3. **Verify gas supply** by checking that the manual gas shutoff is fully open, the meter or LP tank is delivering gas, and other gas appliances are operating normally.
4. **Inspect the ignition and flame-sensing electrodes** for carbon buildup, cracks, corrosion, or misalignment; clean with fine emery cloth or replace if damaged.
5. **Check the condensate trap and drain line** (condensing models only) for blockage, standing water, or improper installation; clear and refill the trap per the manual.
6. **Test inlet gas pressure** with a manometer at the gas valve inlet tap; compare to the boiler's specification (often 3.5–10.5 in. w.c.) and correct supply issues before proceeding.
7. **Clear the fault and reset** only after correcting the underlying issue, then observe a full ignition cycle to confirm the boiler lights, proves flame, and runs without relocking.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician immediately if you smell gas, if the boiler repeatedly locks out after reset, or if you cannot identify your boiler model and retrieve the correct error-code definition. Gas supply testing, gas-valve diagnosis, control-board troubleshooting, and combustion analysis all require specialized tools and training. A qualified technician will retrieve fault history, measure gas pressure and electrical signals, inspect the burner and heat exchanger, and verify proper venting and condensate drainage. Do not bypass safeties or reset the boiler more than once without finding and fixing the root cause.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350.
