## LG Washer CE Error Code — What It Means

The CE error code on an LG washer indicates an over-current condition in the motor electrical circuit. LG defines this as a drive-system electrical fault caused by a short circuit, loose connection, ground fault, or power surge. It is not a normal operation issue.

When the washer detects excessive current flowing to the drive motor, the control board triggers CE and stops the cycle to prevent further damage. The fault lies somewhere in the electrical path between the main control board and the motor, including the wiring harness, connectors, the motor itself, or the control electronics that drive it.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drive motor first, but a failed main control board (specifically the motor control section) causes CE just as often. Check all wiring and connectors first, then test the motor windings for shorts or opens before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed main control board (~40%)** The motor control circuitry on the main PCB can short or fail, sending over-current to the motor and triggering CE.
- **Defective drive motor (~35%)** A shorted or grounded motor winding draws excessive current and trips the over-current detection in the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~15%)** Poor contact at the motor connector or control board can create resistance, arcing, or intermittent shorts that appear as over-current.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~8%)** Chafed or pinched wires between the control board and motor can short to ground or to each other, causing the fault.
- **Power surge damage (~2%)** A recent lightning strike or utility voltage spike can damage both the control board and motor, leaving residual over-current paths.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and wait 10 seconds, then plug it back in and start a test cycle to see if the CE code returns.
2. **Remove the rear or top panel** to access the main control board, drive motor, and wiring harness (consult your model's service manual for panel removal).
3. **Inspect all connectors** at the motor and control board for looseness, corrosion, burn marks, or pushed-back pins, and reseat any suspect connections.
4. **Examine the wiring harness** from the control board to the motor for chafing, pinch points, or exposed copper that could short to the cabinet.
5. **Check the drive motor** for physical damage, burnt terminals, or a seized rotor (spin the drum by hand with the belt off to feel for binding).
6. **Test motor winding resistance** using a multimeter if you have access to the motor terminals (consult your model's wiring diagram for expected values, as the research does not provide specific ohm readings).
7. **Replace the failed component** (main control board, drive motor, or damaged harness section) and reassemble the washer, then run a full test cycle to confirm the CE code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the CE code returns after a power reset, or if you are not comfortable working with live AC circuits and the washer's internal wiring. Diagnosing CE requires tracing the motor electrical path, testing control board outputs, and sometimes checking motor winding resistance under load. If both the control board and motor appear intact but the code persists, a technician with a service manual and multimeter can isolate the fault faster and avoid replacing the wrong part. Any work involving the main control board or motor replacement also carries risk of further damage if connectors are mishandled or the wrong part is installed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150-350 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200-450.
