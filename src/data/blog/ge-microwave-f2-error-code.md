## GE Microwave F2 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 error code on GE appliances indicates an over-temperature or runaway temperature condition. The control board detects that the cavity temperature has exceeded safe limits, typically around 615°F (343°C) in normal cooking mode or around 915°F (491°C) during self-clean cycles. This code is most commonly documented on GE ovens and ranges rather than standalone microwave units. When F2 appears, the control is responding to either a genuine overheat event or a false signal from a faulty sensor or wiring.

The fault triggers a lockout to prevent damage or fire risk. In most cases the root cause is a failed oven temperature sensor, damaged wiring between the sensor and the control board, or a defective electronic range control board itself. Heavy debris in the cavity or blocked ventilation during self-clean can also cause legitimate overheating that trips the code.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor** The sensor measures cavity temperature and sends resistance values to the control board, but if it drifts out of range (typically below 1,050 Ω or above 1,150 Ω at room temperature) the board sees a false overheat signal and trips F2.
- **Damaged wiring harness or connectors** Pinched, melted, or corroded wiring between the sensor and the control board can create open circuits or intermittent high-resistance faults that the board interprets as an over-temperature condition.
- **Defective electronic range control board** The control board itself can fail due to burned solder joints, failed components, or power surges, causing it to falsely trigger F2 even when the sensor and wiring are good.
- **Actual overheating during self-clean** Blocked vents, heavy grease buildup, or running self-clean cycles too frequently can cause the cavity to exceed safe temperature thresholds and legitimately trip the F2 code.
- **Loose or corroded sensor terminals** Poor contact at the sensor connector or control board plug increases resistance in the circuit and can make the control board read artificially high temperature values.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power at the circuit breaker** and leave the unit de-energized for at least five minutes to reset the control board and clear any false lockup condition.
2. **Inspect the oven cavity and vents** for heavy debris, grease buildup, or any signs of overheating such as discoloration or melted plastic, especially if the fault occurred during or after a self-clean cycle.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor**, usually a metal probe extending into the rear of the cavity, and visually inspect the sensor, wiring harness, and connectors for damage, melted insulation, pinched wires, or corrosion.
4. **Disconnect the sensor from its harness** and use a multimeter to measure its resistance at room temperature, which should typically read between 1,050 Ω and 1,150 Ω (commonly around 1,080 to 1,100 Ω depending on the model).
5. **Check wiring continuity** from the sensor connector to the control board if the sensor resistance is in spec, looking for open circuits or high-resistance joints in the harness.
6. **Inspect the control board** for visible signs of failure such as burned spots, cracked solder joints, or bulging capacitors, and replace the board if the sensor and wiring both test good but the F2 code persists.
7. **Reassemble all components**, restore power at the breaker, and run a test bake cycle at moderate temperature to verify the code does not return and the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with live 240 V circuits, if you cannot safely access the rear of the unit to reach the sensor or control board, or if the F2 code returns after you have replaced both the sensor and verified all wiring. A technician has the tools and experience to safely diagnose intermittent faults, test the control board under load, and make sure all high-temperature and high-voltage connections meet safety standards. If your unit is still under warranty, contact GE or an authorized service provider before opening any panels to avoid voiding coverage.
