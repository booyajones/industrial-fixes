## Maytag Oven F7 Error Code — What It Means

The F7 error code on a Maytag oven or range signals a keypad or touchpad fault. The electronic control detects a function key that is stuck, shorted, or sending a continuous signal. This can result from a physically stuck button, moisture or residue under the membrane, a damaged touchpad assembly, or a failure in the electronic range control (ERC) board that misreads the keypad circuit.

Because Maytag uses different control platforms across models, the exact interpretation of F7 can vary. Some wall ovens use F7 E1 and other F7 variants with distinct meanings. Always check your model's tech sheet or owner's manual for the precise definition. The most common technician interpretation of a standalone F7 code is a stuck or shorted function key or a touchpad/electronic control malfunction.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the expensive electronic control board first. Before ordering a board, disconnect power for one minute, then inspect the touchpad for stuck keys and check all wiring connections between the keypad and control. Many F7 faults resolve with a simple power reset or touchpad replacement.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or depressed function key (~40%)** Physical sticking from residue, grease, moisture intrusion, or a worn membrane causes a continuous key closure that the control reads as a fault.
- **Shorted or damaged touchpad membrane (~30%)** A cracked or worn membrane switch panel can create false key closures or short circuits that trigger the F7 code.
- **Loose or disconnected keypad wiring (~15%)** A loose harness connector or corroded pins between the touchpad and the electronic control can interrupt or distort the keypad signal.
- **Failed electronic range control (ERC) board (~15%)** Internal logic failure or a damaged keypad input circuit on the control board can misread normal keypad signals as a stuck key.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power at the breaker** for a full minute to allow the control board to reset, then restore power and check if the F7 code clears.
2. **Record your model number** from the label inside the oven door or on the frame, and locate the tech sheet or wiring diagram (usually taped inside the control console or under the storage drawer) to confirm the F7 definition for your platform.
3. **Inspect the touchpad** for any stuck, mushy, or unresponsive keys, and wipe away grease, moisture, or residue with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly.
4. **Check wiring connections** by removing the control console cover and inspecting the harness connector between the touchpad and the electronic control for loose, corroded, or damaged pins.
5. **Test the touchpad in isolation** by unplugging the touchpad connector from the control board and restoring power. If F7 disappears, the touchpad or its wiring is shorted and should be replaced.
6. **Replace the touchpad assembly** if the keypad shows stuck or shorted behavior and the wiring is sound, or replace the electronic control board if the touchpad tests good and all connections are secure.
7. **Restore power and verify** all function keys respond normally without sticking and that the F7 code does not return during a full operational test of the oven.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if the control console disassembly is beyond your skill level, or if the fault persists after you have replaced the touchpad and checked all connections. A technician can pull the exact fault history from the control board's memory, test keypad circuits with a multimeter, and confirm whether the ERC board or touchpad is at fault. Professional diagnosis is also recommended if your model uses a complex touchscreen interface or if the tech sheet lists multiple F7 variants that require pinpoint testing.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–150 in parts (touchpad or control board), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350 for service call and part replacement.
