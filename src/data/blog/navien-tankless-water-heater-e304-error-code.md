## Navien Tankless Water Heater E304 Error — What It Means

The E304 error on a Navien tankless water heater signals an exhaust overheat condition. The unit has detected temperatures in the exhaust system that exceed safe operating limits and has shut down to prevent damage or unsafe operation. This code typically appears when something is preventing proper ventilation or combustion air flow.

The heater's exhaust system must expel combustion gases quickly and draw in fresh air for the burner. When venting is restricted, exhaust gases accumulate and temperatures rise beyond the threshold monitored by the unit's safety sensors. The heater will not resume normal operation until the overheat condition is resolved and the code is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust temperature sensor or flame rod when the real problem is a simple blockage in the vent pipe. Always inspect the entire vent run for obstructions, nests, or ice buildup before ordering sensors.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked exhaust vent (~40%)** Debris, bird nests, ice, or snow blocking the outdoor vent termination prevents exhaust gases from escaping and causes dangerous temperature buildup.
- **Restricted combustion air intake (~25%)** A clogged intake air filter or blocked intake vent starves the burner of oxygen, leading to incomplete combustion and overheating.
- **Improper vent pipe installation (~15%)** Vent pipe runs that are too long, have too many elbows, or use incorrect diameter piping can restrict flow and trap heat.
- **Exhaust temperature sensor failure (~10%)** A failing or damaged exhaust temperature sensor may report false high readings even when actual temperatures are normal.
- **Heat exchanger scale buildup (~7%)** Mineral scale deposits on the heat exchanger reduce heat transfer efficiency and can cause localized overheating in the exhaust path.
- **Weak or failing exhaust fan (~3%)** An exhaust fan running below proper speed cannot evacuate combustion gases quickly enough, allowing heat to accumulate.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vent termination** for visible blockages such as bird nests, leaves, ice, or snow and remove any obstructions you find.
3. **Remove and clean the intake air filter** located inside the unit (consult your owner's manual for exact location), rinsing it with water and allowing it to dry completely.
4. **Check all vent pipe joints and connections** from the unit to the outdoor termination, ensuring nothing has come loose, separated, or collapsed.
5. **Reset the error code** by following the procedure in your owner's manual (typically holding a reset button or cycling power) and restore gas and electrical power.
6. **Run hot water** at a fixture for several minutes and observe whether the unit fires and operates normally without the error returning.
7. **If the code reappears immediately**, turn off the unit and call a qualified technician to inspect the exhaust temperature sensor, fan operation, and heat exchanger condition.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared all visible blockages and cleaned the filter but the E304 error returns immediately, or if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances. A technician can test the exhaust temperature sensor, measure fan performance, inspect the heat exchanger for scale buildup, and verify that the vent pipe installation meets manufacturer specifications. Professional service is also necessary if the vent pipe run needs to be reconfigured or if internal components such as the fan or gas valve require replacement. Do not attempt to bypass safety sensors or operate the unit with the error active, as overheat conditions pose a fire and carbon monoxide risk.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 if clearing blockage or filter cleaning, $100-180 if replacing vent pipe section. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
