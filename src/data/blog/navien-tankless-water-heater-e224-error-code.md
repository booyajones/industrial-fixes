## Navien Tankless Water Heater E224 Error — What It Means

The E224 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with combustion, airflow, or flame sensing. This code may appear when the unit detects abnormal combustion conditions, restricted air supply or exhaust, or an issue with the flame rod that monitors ignition. Because tankless water heaters require precise air-to-gas ratios and proper venting to operate safely, the control board will shut down the unit and display this code to prevent unsafe operation.

The exact meaning can vary slightly between Navien models, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover for model-specific details. In most cases, the error points to a blockage in the intake or exhaust system, a dirty or failing flame sensor, or a problem with the gas valve or burner assembly. Addressing ventilation and cleaning the flame rod are the most common first steps.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame rod or igniter when the real problem is a blocked exhaust termination or bird nest in the vent pipe. Always inspect and clear all venting before ordering sensors.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted intake or exhaust vent (~40%)** Snow, ice, leaves, bird nests, or debris at the vent termination can starve the burner of air or prevent exhaust gases from escaping, triggering the error.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing rod prevents the control board from detecting the flame, causing the unit to shut down.
- **Faulty flame rod or igniter assembly (~15%)** A worn or failed flame rod or igniter can no longer sense or create a stable flame, leading to repeated error codes.
- **Gas supply or gas valve issue (~10%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed manual gas valve, or a failing gas valve can produce weak or unstable combustion.
- **Improper venting or vent configuration (~7%)** Venting that is too long, has too many elbows, or does not meet manufacturer specifications can cause poor draft and combustion problems.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~3%)** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a failing control board can misread sensor signals and trigger false error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the breaker or disconnect switch and close the manual gas shutoff valve.
2. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vent terminations** outside your home for blockages, snow, ice, bird nests, or debris, and clear any obstructions.
3. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the fasteners and locate the burner assembly and flame rod inside.
4. **Remove the flame rod** by disconnecting the wire lead and unscrewing the mounting hardware, then clean the rod with fine sandpaper or a cloth to remove carbon and corrosion.
5. **Inspect the burner assembly** for dust, soot, or debris and vacuum or brush it clean if needed.
6. **Reinstall the flame rod** and reconnect the wire lead, making sure the connection is snug and corrosion-free.
7. **Check that all vent pipes** inside the unit are secure, properly sealed, and free of gaps or disconnections.
8. **Restore power and gas**, open a hot water tap, and observe startup. If the error persists, call a qualified technician for gas pressure testing and control board diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after clearing vents and cleaning the flame rod, or if you suspect a gas pressure or gas valve problem. Professionals have the tools to measure combustion efficiency, test gas pressure, and diagnose control board and sensor faults safely. Because tankless water heaters involve both gas and electrical systems, improper repairs can create carbon monoxide hazards or void your warranty. If you see soot, smell gas, or notice the unit cycling on and off repeatedly, shut it down and call for service immediately.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
