## What this code means
The tE error code on an LG washer indicates a heating or temperature-sensing fault. LG's published error list states the code is usually caused by a faulty thermistor. In practical terms, the washer cannot read water temperature correctly, so the control board stops the cycle to prevent incorrect heating or damage.

The fault can stem from a failed thermistor (temperature sensor), loose or damaged wiring in the heater and sensor circuit, a failed heater element, or less commonly a main control board that is not interpreting the sensor signal correctly. LG's official first response is to unplug the washer for 10 seconds, restore power, and restart the cycle. If the code persists, close the water taps, drain the washer if needed, and call for service.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board when the real problem is a failed thermistor or damaged wiring. Test the thermistor with a multimeter and inspect all connectors before replacing any electronic boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The temperature sensor has failed and no longer provides a valid resistance reading to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~25%)** Connectors at the thermistor, heater, or harness are loose, corroded, or have broken insulation, interrupting the signal.
- **Failed heater element (~15%)** The heater assembly is open, shorted, or otherwise damaged, causing the control board to register a fault.
- **Faulty main control board (~10%)** The control board is not correctly interpreting the thermistor signal even though the sensor and wiring test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault.** Confirm the display shows tE (not another code). Unplug the washer for 10 seconds, restore power, and restart the cycle to rule out a temporary glitch.
2. **Disconnect power.** Unplug the washer or turn off the circuit breaker before opening any panels to make sure safe access to internal components.
3. **Access the thermistor.** Remove the rear panel (on most LG front-load models) and locate the thermistor mounted near the heater assembly at the bottom of the tub.
4. **Inspect wiring and connectors.** Check for loose plugs, broken wires, damaged insulation, or corrosion at the thermistor, heater, and harness connections. Repair or replace damaged wiring.
5. **Test the thermistor.** Disconnect the thermistor connector and measure resistance across its terminals with a multimeter. Technician field guidance reports approximately 38–60 kΩ at room temperature (values are model- and temperature-dependent, consult your service manual for exact specs). Replace the thermistor if the reading is out of range or infinite.
6. **Test the heater element.** If the thermistor tests good, check the heater assembly for continuity and correct resistance. Replace the heater if it is open, shorted, or shows signs of physical damage.
7. **Evaluate the control board.** If the thermistor, heater, and all wiring test normal, the main control board may not be reading the sensor correctly. Replace the board or contact a professional for further diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical components, do not own a multimeter, or if the fault persists after a power cycle. Professional diagnosis is especially important if wiring and the thermistor both appear normal, because the heater element and control board require safe disassembly, accurate testing, and correct reassembly. Technicians have model-specific service manuals, the correct replacement parts, and the tools to test the heater circuit without risk of shock or further damage. If your washer is under warranty or a service plan, contact LG or your retailer first to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–300.
