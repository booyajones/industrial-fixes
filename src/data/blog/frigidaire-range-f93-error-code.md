## What this code means
Frigidaire treats F93 as part of the F90–F94 door lock issue family. The code appears when the electronic oven control sends the lock command (typically during self-clean) but the door does not lock within the allowed time. The control expects confirmation from the lock switch that the door latched, and F93 indicates that signal never arrived.

Frigidaire's official support guidance recommends resetting power first by unplugging the range or switching the breaker off and on for 30 seconds. If the code persists after the reset, the manufacturer directs users to contact service because the problem lies in the lock motor, switch, wiring, or control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first. Before buying electronics, inspect the lock mechanism for debris or binding and test the lock motor and switch, since mechanical failure is far more common.

## Common Causes

- **Obstructed lock mechanism (~35%)** Debris, a shifted latch, or mechanical binding prevents the lock rod or cam from moving freely and completing the lock cycle.
- **Defective lock motor (~30%)** The motor does not drive the cam or rod properly, so the door never reaches the locked position.
- **Faulty door lock switch (~20%)** The switch does not report locked status back to the control, even if the mechanism moves.
- **Damaged wiring or loose connections (~10%)** Broken or loose conductors between the control board and lock assembly interrupt the signal.
- **Failed electronic oven control (~5%)** The control board itself is faulty, though this is rare when the lock hardware and wiring check out.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker for at least 30 seconds, then restore power and test whether the code clears.
2. **Open the oven door** and visually inspect the lock latch, cam, and rod for food debris, grease buildup, or visible damage that could block movement.
3. **Gently cycle the lock mechanism by hand** (if accessible) to feel for binding or excessive resistance, and clean any obstructions.
4. **Access the lock assembly** by removing the range top or control-panel shield according to your model's service manual, and disconnect the wiring harness.
5. **Test the lock motor** by applying the voltage specified in your model's tech sheet (consult the wiring diagram) and watching for smooth movement of the cam and rod.
6. **Test the door lock switch** for continuity in both the locked and unlocked positions using a multimeter, and compare readings to your model's table.
7. **Replace the lock motor assembly** if the motor does not run or the mechanism binds, and replace the switch if it does not change state correctly.
8. **Reconnect all wiring**, reassemble the range, restore power, and run a short self-clean cycle to verify the lock engages and the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable disconnecting power at the breaker, disassembling the range top, or testing components with a multimeter. If you have already replaced the lock assembly and checked all wiring but the F93 code returns, the control board may need diagnosis with specialized test equipment. Frigidaire's official guidance recommends professional service whenever a power reset does not clear a door lock code, and for newly installed units the installer is responsible for resolving the issue under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
