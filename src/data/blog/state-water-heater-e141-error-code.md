## State Water Heater E141 Error — What It Means

The code E141 does not appear in official State Water Heaters documentation. You are most likely seeing code E14 (or 14), which indicates the water has overheated beyond safe limits (typically above 190°F) or a thermal fuse has blown. The main control board shuts down the unit to prevent damage or injury. E141 may be a misread of E14 or confusion with codes from other brands.

Code 14 or E14 on State (and related Rheem/Rinnai tankless models) means the safety system detected excessive temperature or a failed thermal circuit. The unit will not reignite until the fault is cleared and the system is reset.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic thermostat or entire control board first. Check the vents for blockages and test the thermistor resistance before replacing any circuit boards.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked exhaust vent or air inlet (~35%)** Dust, debris, or nests prevent proper combustion and cause heat to build up inside the unit.
- **Low water flow through the unit (~25%)** Insufficient water circulation causes heat to concentrate and triggers the overheat sensor.
- **Closed hot water taps during operation (~15%)** If all taps are closed while the unit is running (especially with recirculation systems), heat cannot dissipate.
- **Failed thermistor sensor (~15%)** The sensor monitoring water temperature sends incorrect high-temperature readings to the control board.
- **Blown thermal fuse (~10%)** A safety fuse designed to open at high temperatures has permanently opened due to a previous overheating event.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the unit's switch or breaker and wait 2 minutes, then restart to attempt a reset.
2. **Inspect the exhaust vent and air inlet** for blockages such as dust, lint, debris, or bird nests and clear any obstructions you find.
3. **Open a hot water tap** and verify that water flows freely through the unit; if you have a recirculation pump, turn it off temporarily to test.
4. **Test the thermistor sensor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance with a multimeter; compare the reading to your model's specification table (typically a few thousand ohms at room temperature).
5. **Check the thermal fuse** for continuity using a multimeter; if it shows infinite resistance (open circuit), the fuse has blown and must be replaced.
6. **Verify gas supply pressure** is within the manufacturer's range (often 5 to 7 inches water column for natural gas) using a manometer.
7. **Replace the electronic thermostat** if the code persists after all other checks, as a faulty control board can send incorrect signals.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the code persists after clearing vents and resetting, if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, or if you need to test or replace the thermistor, thermal fuse, or control board. Gas water heater work requires knowledge of combustion safety, gas pressure testing, and electrical diagnostics. A pro can also verify proper venting and make sure the unit meets local code requirements.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
