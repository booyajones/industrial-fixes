## Kenmore Dishwasher F2 Error — What It Means

The F2 error code on Kenmore dishwashers typically indicates a problem with the heating system or temperature sensing circuit. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the door for confirmation. In most cases, the control board has detected that water is not heating properly, the thermistor is reading out of range, or there is a wiring fault in the heating circuit. The dishwasher may stop mid-cycle or refuse to start when this code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a loose or corroded wire connector at the thermistor or heating element is often the real problem. Check all connections and measure thermistor resistance before buying a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty thermistor (~40%)** The temperature sensor may have drifted out of specification or failed open, causing the control board to read an incorrect temperature and throw the code.
- **Loose or corroded wire connector (~25%)** Moisture can corrode the connector pins at the thermistor or heating element, creating an intermittent open circuit that triggers the error.
- **Failed heating element (~20%)** An open or shorted heating element will prevent the water from reaching the correct temperature and may cause the control to set the F2 code.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main electronic control board may have a failed relay or sensing circuit that misreads the temperature input.
- **Wiring harness damage (~5%)** Chafed or pinched wires in the harness between the control board and the heating components can create intermittent faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or turning off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the lower access panel** or toe kick by unscrewing the fasteners along the bottom edge of the door.
3. **Locate the thermistor** near the heating element or sump area and disconnect its wire connector.
4. **Measure the thermistor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. Compare the reading to your model's specification table (typically a few thousand ohms at room temperature).
5. **Inspect all wire connectors** in the heating circuit for corrosion, burnt pins, or loose fit. Clean and reseat any suspect connectors.
6. **Test the heating element** by disconnecting its wires and measuring resistance across its terminals. An open circuit or a very low resistance indicates a failed element.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (thermistor, heating element, or control board) and reassemble the panels. Restore power and run a test cycle to verify the error is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with electrical components or using a multimeter to test resistance. A technician can quickly isolate whether the fault is in the thermistor, heating element, wiring, or control board. Also call a pro if you have replaced the thermistor and inspected all wiring but the code persists, since the control board may require specialized diagnostics or the error definition for your specific model may differ from the general case.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
