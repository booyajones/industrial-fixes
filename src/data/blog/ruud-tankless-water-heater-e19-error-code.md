## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E19 Error — What It Means

The E19 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a fault in the ignition or flame sensing system. The heater is attempting to light but either cannot establish a flame or cannot confirm the flame is present. This may occur during startup or during operation when the unit tries to modulate.

Because error code definitions can vary between model series, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cover to confirm the exact meaning for your unit. In general, ignition and flame-sensing faults point to interruptions in gas delivery, dirty flame sensors, or faulty ignition components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the entire gas valve when the real problem is a dirty flame rod that costs nothing to clean or a few dollars to replace.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame-sensing rod prevents the control board from detecting the flame, causing a shutdown.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or closed valve (~20%)** Low inlet pressure, a partly closed shut-off valve, or an empty propane tank will prevent ignition or cause flame instability.
- **Faulty igniter (~15%)** A cracked or weak igniter cannot create a reliable spark to light the gas, leading to repeated ignition failures.
- **Blocked burner or heat exchanger (~10%)** Dust, debris, or scale in the burner assembly or on the heat exchanger can disrupt the flame pattern and sensor signal.
- **Failed gas valve (~5%)** An internal fault in the gas valve may prevent proper modulation or opening, though this is less common than sensor issues.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Perform a full descale and burner cleaning, as buildup commonly causes false flame signals.<br><strong>No:</strong> Look for loose wiring, recent power surges, or a defective new component if the unit is still under warranty.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shut-off valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the retaining screws and lifting or sliding the panel away from the unit.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe extending into the burner chamber, and disconnect its wire connector.
4. **Remove the flame rod** by unscrewing the mounting nut or bracket, taking care not to bend the rod.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth until bright metal is visible, wiping away all residue with a dry cloth.
6. **Reinstall the flame rod** in the same position, reconnect the wire, and make sure all burner chamber seals are seated properly.
7. **Restore gas and power**, then run hot water at a fixture and observe the unit through the viewport to confirm ignition and steady flame, checking that the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and verifying gas supply, or if you suspect a faulty gas valve or control board. Professionals have manometers to measure inlet and manifold gas pressure, combustion analyzers to check air-fuel ratio, and the training to diagnose wiring faults safely. Any time you smell gas or hear hissing, shut off the gas valve immediately and call for service.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts if cleaning or replacing flame rod, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for a service call including diagnostics and cleaning or replacement.
