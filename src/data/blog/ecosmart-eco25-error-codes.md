## EcoSmart ECO25 Error Codes — What It Means

EcoSmart tankless water heaters display error codes when the unit detects a fault that prevents normal operation. Without the exact code shown on your ECO25 display, a precise diagnosis is not possible. Most faults trace back to electrical supply issues, insufficient water flow, or internal component failures like heating elements or the flow sensor. The unit requires 240 volts to operate correctly, and voltage below 220 volts will reduce heating capacity or prevent startup altogether.

Before attempting repairs, record the exact error code from the display and consult your owner's manual for model-specific definitions. The general troubleshooting sequence for EcoSmart units starts with verifying power and voltage at the breaker, then checking water flow and pressure, and finally testing internal components. A reset often clears transient faults, but recurring codes point to a component that needs replacement or a supply condition that must be corrected.

## Common Causes

- **Insufficient line voltage** Voltage below 220 volts at the unit prevents proper heating or causes shutdown errors.
- **Tripped breaker or loose wiring** One or more breakers may have tripped, or a connection at the unit or panel may be loose or corroded.
- **Low water flow** Flow below the minimum activation threshold (typically 0.3 to 0.5 GPM) prevents the unit from turning on or triggers a flow fault.
- **Failed heating element** One or more heating elements can burn out or develop high resistance, reducing output and triggering an error.
- **Faulty flow sensor** A clogged or broken flow sensor cannot detect water movement and causes the unit to remain off or display a fault.
- **Mineral buildup on internal components** Hard water deposits on the heating chamber or sensor interfere with operation and may mimic component failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Shut off power at all breakers serving the unit. Confirm zero voltage at the unit terminals with a multimeter before proceeding.
2. Inspect the breaker panel for tripped breakers. Reset any that have tripped and verify they hold under load.
3. Measure line voltage at the unit with a multimeter. Confirm you have close to 240 volts between the two hot legs. If voltage is below 220 volts, identify and correct the supply problem before testing further.
4. Check water flow by opening a hot-water tap fully. Verify flow rate meets the minimum activation threshold listed in your manual. Clean or replace inlet filter screens if flow is restricted.
5. Perform a reset by turning off power for 30 seconds, then restoring it. Observe whether the error clears and the unit operates normally.
6. If the error persists, shut off power and water. Remove the cover and visually inspect heating elements and wire connections for burns, corrosion, or loose terminals.
7. Test heating-element resistance with a multimeter according to your model's specification table. Replace any element that reads open or far from spec. Inspect and test the flow sensor for continuity and clean any debris from the turbine or hall-effect sensor housing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician or plumber if you measure low or unstable voltage at the unit and cannot identify the cause, if you are not comfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, or if the error returns immediately after replacing a heating element or flow sensor. Recurring faults after component replacement often indicate a control-board issue or a supply problem that requires diagnostic tools and experience to isolate safely.
