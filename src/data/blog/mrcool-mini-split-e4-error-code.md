## MRCOOL E4 Error Code — What It Means

On most MRCOOL mini-split systems, E4 indicates an indoor fan motor or fan speed feedback fault. The control board is not receiving the expected signal from the blower motor, or the fan is not reaching the commanded speed. Some MRCOOL product lines define E4 as a condenser coil sensor failure instead, so always verify the exact meaning in your unit's installation or service manual before you start troubleshooting. The indoor-fan interpretation is more common in field reports and tech forums.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or disconnected fan motor harness** The feedback wire or connector between the indoor fan motor and the control board has backed out, corroded, or broken.
- **Faulty indoor fan motor** The blower motor itself has failed or its internal feedback circuit (hall sensor or tachometer) no longer sends a valid signal.
- **Jammed or obstructed blower wheel** Debris, ice, or a seized bearing prevents the fan from spinning freely, so it cannot reach target speed and triggers a feedback fault.
- **Bad indoor control board** The PCB that reads the fan speed signal has a failed input stage or damaged traces, even when the motor and wiring test good.
- **Condenser coil sensor failure (model-specific)** If your model's manual lists E4 as a coil sensor error, the thermistor may be open, shorted, or poorly seated on the condenser coil.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power** at the breaker or outdoor disconnect and wait at least five minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening any panels.
2. **Look up your exact model number** in the MRCOOL installation or service manual to confirm whether E4 is a fan-motor fault or a sensor fault, since definitions vary.
3. **Open the indoor unit's front cover** and locate the fan motor connector on the main control board, then verify that all pins are fully seated, clean, and free of corrosion or damage.
4. **Spin the blower wheel by hand** to check for binding, obstructions, or rough bearings, and remove any debris from the fan housing or evaporator coil.
5. **Restore power and run a test cycle** for a few minutes to see if the code clears after reseating connections and clearing obstructions.
6. **Test or replace the indoor fan motor** if the code persists and you have confirmed good wiring, since a failed motor or feedback circuit is the most common root cause.
7. **Replace the indoor control board** if a known-good motor still throws E4, or if your model's E4 is sensor-based, check the coil sensor's resistance with a multimeter and replace the sensor if it reads open or shorted.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line voltage or refrigerant systems, if the code returns after you have reseated all connectors and verified free fan rotation, or if you lack a multimeter and the skills to test motor feedback signals and board inputs. A pro can quickly isolate whether the fault is in the motor, the harness, or the control board and can also verify refrigerant charge and airflow if a secondary issue is masking the real problem.
