## GE Oven F97 Error Code — What It Means

GE lists F97 under fault codes that usually mean an internal component needs to be replaced. In field reports for GE double wall ovens, F97 is most commonly associated with a lower cooling fan problem. The lower fan is either not spinning, spinning too slowly, or its speed signal is not being read correctly by the control board.

The code triggers when the oven expects the lower cooling fan to run at a certain speed during operation but does not detect the correct feedback. This can result from a failed fan motor, mechanical blockage, a bad speed sensor built into the fan assembly, damaged wiring between the fan and control board, or a control board failure.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Test the lower cooling fan mechanically and electrically before spending money on a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed lower cooling fan motor (~50%)** The blower motor itself has burned out or its windings are open, so it cannot spin or reach expected speed.
- **Fan mechanically restricted (~20%)** Grease, debris, or a seized bearing prevents the fan wheel from turning freely or reaching full speed.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors (~15%)** Heat-affected terminals, loose pins, or broken harness wires interrupt power or feedback signal between the fan and control board.
- **Fan speed sensor or tachometer feedback fault (~10%)** The sensor integrated into the fan assembly is failing to send a valid speed signal back to the control board.
- **Control board failure (~5%)** The board is not supplying voltage to the fan or is misinterpreting a valid speed signal, even when the fan is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** and unplug the oven or flip the dedicated breaker to prevent shock during diagnosis.
2. **Access the lower oven cavity** by removing the oven door and any interior panels or shields that expose the lower cooling fan assembly, typically mounted near the bottom or rear.
3. **Inspect the fan wheel and motor for blockage** by looking for grease buildup, debris, or damage, and try spinning the wheel by hand to check for free movement or a seized bearing.
4. **Restore power and command a heat cycle** to observe whether the lower fan starts and runs at full speed without unusual noise or slow rotation.
5. **Test voltage at the fan connector** using a multimeter while the oven is calling for the fan to run, confirming the control board is supplying power to the motor.
6. **Replace the lower cooling fan assembly** if the motor does not run with voltage present, if the fan is mechanically damaged, or if it runs but the speed signal is not reaching the control board.
7. **If the fan tests good, replace the control board** after confirming all wiring and connectors are intact and the fan operates correctly when powered directly.
8. **Reassemble the oven, restore power, and run a test cycle** to verify the lower fan starts normally and the F97 code does not return during operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working inside a hardwired double wall oven with 240 V power, if you cannot safely access the lower fan assembly, or if voltage testing and component replacement are outside your skill level. A qualified appliance repair tech can isolate whether the fan, wiring harness, or control board is at fault and has the model-specific service manual and wiring diagrams to test feedback signals and board outputs accurately.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-400 depending on part and labor.
