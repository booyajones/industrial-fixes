## Weil-McLain A182 Error Code — What It Means

The A182 code is not universal across Weil-McLain boilers. Different models and control platforms use A-series codes to mean different things, so you cannot decode A182 without knowing your exact boiler model and controller type. The code appears in the fault history menu on many newer controllers, which means the boiler logged a fault event and you need to match it to your model's specific fault table.

Because Weil-McLain documents fault codes by model, your next step is to locate the installation or service manual for your boiler and read the fault-history section. Common faults that appear as alphanumeric codes include ignition lockout (no flame detected), limit circuit open (a safety switch tripped), or sensor failure (bad temperature reading). Until you confirm the exact meaning for your model, treat A182 as a placeholder that tells you the boiler shut down for a reason that needs diagnosis.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when the real fault is a dirty flame sensor, open limit switch, or low system pressure. Record the fault code, then test gas flow, flame sensing, and limit devices before buying a controller.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~30%)** Carbon buildup on the flame sensor prevents it from proving ignition, triggering a lockout fault in the boiler's history.
- **Open limit or safety switch (~25%)** A high-limit, low-water cutoff, blocked-vent switch, or other safety device opened and stopped the boiler, logging a fault code.
- **No gas flow or ignitor failure (~20%)** The gas valve is closed, the supply is interrupted, or the ignitor is cracked and cannot light the burner.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or wiring (~15%)** The boiler controller reads an out-of-range or missing signal from an outlet, inlet, or return sensor and logs a fault.
- **Failed circulator pump (~10%)** Water does not circulate, outlet temperature climbs, and the high-limit switch opens or a no-flow fault is recorded.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the boiler at the breaker or service switch before any inspection or testing.
2. **Record the exact fault** from the display or fault-history menu by consulting your boiler's installation manual for the code table that matches your model and controller.
3. **Check gas supply and ignition components** if the fault suggests ignition lockout. Confirm the gas valve is open, inspect the ignitor for cracks, and clean the flame sensor with fine emery cloth or steel wool.
4. **Verify system water pressure** and refill if the gauge reads below the recommended range. Look for leaks in piping, radiators, or zone valves and repair before proceeding.
5. **Test the circulator pump** by listening for hum or vibration when the boiler calls for heat. Check voltage at the pump terminals and replace the pump if it is not running.
6. **Isolate the open limit or safety device** by inspecting the limit circuit wiring. Use a multimeter to test continuity across each switch (high limit, low water cutoff, blocked-vent switch) and replace the device that shows open when it should be closed.
7. **Inspect venting and combustion air** for blockage, sag, deterioration, or disconnection. Clear obstructions, resupport sagging vent pipe, and replace damaged sections as needed.
8. **Reset the boiler** only after correcting the underlying fault, then observe a full startup cycle to confirm normal ignition, flame proving, and circulation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician for any A182 code you cannot decode from your manual, for all gas-supply or combustion testing, and whenever a limit switch has opened. A technician has the model-specific fault tables, combustion analyzers, and multimeters to trace limit circuits, measure flame-sensor microamps, test gas-valve operation, and verify safe venting. If you have refilled the system multiple times or the fault returns after a reset, a professional must find the leak or intermittent sensor failure before the boiler can run safely. Do not bypass safety switches or repeatedly reset the controller without correcting the root cause.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
