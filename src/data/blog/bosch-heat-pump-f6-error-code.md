## Bosch Heat Pump F6 Error Code — What It Means

Bosch does not publish a single universal meaning for F6 across all heat pump models. In available documentation, Bosch uses different model-specific fault codes rather than a standard F6 designation. Based on common HVAC industry practice and Bosch-related equipment behavior, F6 typically indicates a fault in one of three areas: a temperature sensor that has failed or lost connection, a communication problem between the indoor and outdoor control boards, or a compressor or inverter protection trip caused by overheating or abnormal operating conditions.

Because the exact definition depends on your specific model, always consult your unit's service manual or the display's full error message before replacing parts. The code may point to anything from a simple sensor connector issue to a refrigerant-side protection event.

## Common Causes

- **Temperature sensor failure** The sensor may be open, shorted, disconnected, or damaged by moisture or corrosion at the terminals.
- **Communication loss between boards** Wiring faults, loose connectors, or a failed control board can interrupt data exchange between indoor and outdoor units.
- **Compressor or inverter protection trip** High pressure, thermal overload, or abnormal electrical conditions can trigger a safety shutdown.
- **Blocked airflow or restricted heat exchange** Dirty filters, blocked coils, or fan failure reduce heat transfer and can cause overheating protection faults.
- **Power or relay problem** The outdoor unit may not be receiving correct voltage, or a relay on the indoor board may have failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact model** and look up the fault-code table in the service manual or installation guide to confirm what F6 means for your unit.
2. **Reset power** by turning off the breaker or disconnect switch for at least one minute, then restore power and check whether the fault clears.
3. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** at the indoor board, outdoor board, and any sensor harnesses for loose plugs, corrosion, or physical damage.
4. **Test temperature sensors** with a multimeter by measuring resistance and comparing to the manufacturer's specification table for that sensor type.
5. **Check airflow and heat exchange** by replacing or cleaning filters, clearing any debris from coils, and verifying that both indoor and outdoor fans operate normally.
6. **Verify power delivery** to the outdoor unit and measure voltage at the board to confirm correct supply and relay output.
7. **Inspect refrigerant-circuit operation** if the fault persists after electrical and airflow checks, looking for abnormal pressures, icing, or signs of refrigerant loss that could cause a protection trip.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if the fault returns after a power reset, if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage electrical connections, or if diagnostic steps point to refrigerant-circuit issues such as abnormal pressures or compressor protection. Bosch heat pumps require model-specific fault-code interpretation, and misdiagnosis can lead to unnecessary part replacement. A technician has access to the correct service literature, refrigerant gauges, and board-level testing equipment to isolate the root cause safely and efficiently.
