## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E60 Error — What It Means

The E60 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater generally indicates a problem with the flame sensing system. The unit is detecting that the flame is not igniting properly, or the flame rod sensor is failing to confirm combustion. This safety feature prevents the heater from operating when it cannot verify a stable flame.

Because flame detection involves both the ignition system and gas flow, the code can point to several components. The heater will lock out to prevent gas from flowing without reliable ignition. In most cases, the flame rod or its wiring is the culprit, though gas supply and igniter issues also appear. Always consult your model's manual for the exact definition, as code meanings can vary across product lines.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter when the real problem is a dirty flame rod or a loose wire connection. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and check all wire terminals before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame rod prevent it from sensing the flame current, causing the heater to shut down.
- **Loose or corroded flame-rod wiring (~25%)** A poor connection at the flame rod terminal or control board interrupts the flame-sensing signal.
- **Low gas pressure or restricted gas valve (~15%)** Insufficient gas flow produces a weak or unstable flame that the sensor cannot reliably detect.
- **Failed flame rod (~10%)** The flame-rod sensor itself can crack or fail internally, losing the ability to conduct the flame signal.
- **Control board issue (~5%)** The board that interprets the flame signal may fail, misreading a good flame as absent.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shut-off valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front access panel** by unscrewing the retaining screws and lifting the cover away.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a small metallic probe positioned near the burner assembly, and disconnect its wire lead.
4. **Clean the flame rod** by gently rubbing the sensing tip with fine-grit sandpaper or an emery cloth until the metal is bright and free of carbon.
5. **Inspect all wire terminals** on the flame rod, igniter, and control board for corrosion or looseness, and reseat or clean connections as needed.
6. **Check gas supply pressure** with a manometer if available, or verify that the inlet valve is fully open and the supply line is not kinked.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and call for hot water to test whether the error clears and the burner ignites normally.
8. **If the code persists**, measure the flame signal current with a microamp meter or replace the flame rod, and consider calling a licensed technician if the problem continues.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the flame rod and connections are clean but the error persists, or if you suspect the control board or gas valve has failed. Gas-fired water heaters involve combustion safety and require proper testing equipment to diagnose flame-sensing circuits and gas pressure. A professional can measure flame current, verify gas-valve operation, and test the control board without risking carbon monoxide hazards or voiding your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
