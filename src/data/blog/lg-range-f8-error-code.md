## LG Range F8 Error Code — What It Means

The F8 error code on LG ranges indicates an oven meat probe error. The control board has detected a fault in the meat probe circuit, typically a short, open, or out-of-range reading from the probe sensor itself or its wiring. This code appears when the probe is plugged in and the control sees an abnormal signal, or when the probe circuit is damaged even if no probe is connected.

Because LG error codes can be model-dependent across different range platforms and regions, verify the exact meaning in your owner's manual or wiring diagram if your unit is a dual-fuel, slide-in, or international variant. For most LG freestanding and slide-in electric and gas ranges sold in the U.S., F8 consistently points to the meat probe circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when F8 appears, but the probe itself or its harness connector is almost always the problem. Disconnect the probe first and clear the code; if the error does not return, replace only the probe.

## Common Causes

- **Shorted or open meat probe (~55%)** Internal sensor wire damage, moisture intrusion, or a failed thermistor inside the probe tip causes the control to read a short or infinite resistance and throw F8.
- **Loose or corroded probe connector (~25%)** The plug-in jack at the oven cavity wall or behind the control panel develops corrosion, bent pins, or a poor connection that mimics a probe fault.
- **Damaged probe harness or wiring (~12%)** Insulation melted by oven heat, pinched wires during a prior repair, or rodent damage between the probe jack and the control board interrupts or shorts the circuit.
- **Control board input circuit failure (~8%)** A failed analog-to-digital converter or damaged trace on the main PCB misreads the probe signal even when the probe and harness test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** and wait 30 seconds to reset the control, then restore power and observe whether F8 reappears immediately or only when the probe is connected.
2. **Press CLEAR/OFF or the power button** to clear the error from the display, and note whether the code returns during a short bake cycle without the probe plugged in.
3. **Unplug the meat probe** from the oven cavity jack (usually on the left or right interior wall), then clear the code again and run a test bake to confirm the fault follows the probe.
4. **Inspect the probe plug and jack** for bent pins, black carbon deposits, corrosion, or melted plastic caused by oven heat or moisture, and clean or replace the connector if damaged.
5. **Check the probe harness continuity and insulation** by tracing the wire from the cavity jack to the control board, looking for pinch points, heat damage, or breaks in the insulation.
6. **Replace the meat probe** if the error clears when unplugged and the connector looks good, then test the new probe by plugging it in and running a cook cycle to verify F8 does not return.
7. **Replace the control board** only if the probe, harness, and connector all test good, the error persists without the probe connected, and you have verified wiring continuity from jack to board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the F8 code returns after you have replaced the meat probe and inspected the connector, or if you are not comfortable working with 240 V household power and testing control board inputs. Technicians have model-specific resistance tables and board-level diagnostic tools to isolate input-circuit failures without guessing. Also call a pro if your range uses dual fuel (gas cooktop, electric oven) and you see any signs of gas odor or burner control issues alongside the F8 fault, since combination appliances require coordinated safety checks.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (probe or harness), 15–30 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call plus parts.
