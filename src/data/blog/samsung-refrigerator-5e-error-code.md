## What this code means
In most Samsung refrigerators the 5E or SE code indicates the control board is not receiving a valid signal from the defrost sensor (thermistor) in the evaporator circuit. Samsung's own documentation sometimes also lists 1E, 5E, and SE as a power-interruption or system-reset condition, so technicians first verify whether the code clears with a reset or returns. Because Samsung uses different code families across model lines, the exact meaning must be confirmed from your model's service manual or tech sheet. In the field the most common interpretation is a defrost thermistor problem in the fresh-food or evaporator section.

If the code persists after a power reset, the fault is usually in the sensor itself, its harness connector, or conditions around the sensor such as moisture, frost buildup, or ice interference rather than a one-time power glitch. The control expects a specific resistance range from the thermistor to monitor defrost cycles, and when the signal is missing, shorted, or open the 5E/SE code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real problem is a bad sensor or corroded connector. Always test the defrost thermistor with a multimeter for open or shorted readings before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed defrost thermistor (~45%)** The evaporator defrost sensor reads open (infinite resistance) or shorted (zero resistance) on a multimeter.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~25%)** Oxidation, moisture, or a loose plug at the sensor harness prevents a valid signal from reaching the control board.
- **Frost or ice accumulation around the sensor (~15%)** Heavy ice buildup on the evaporator or sensor body causes erratic readings or physically damages the thermistor.
- **Damaged wiring between sensor and board (~10%)** A pinched, cut, or chewed wire in the harness creates an open circuit or intermittent fault.
- **Temporary power interruption or system glitch (~5%)** A brief outage or board reset condition triggers the code but clears after a full power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model and code behavior.** Check your owner's manual or the tech sheet behind the lower grille to confirm that 5E or SE corresponds to a defrost sensor fault on your specific Samsung refrigerator model, as code meanings vary by platform.
2. **Perform a full power reset.** Unplug the refrigerator or switch off its circuit breaker for at least five minutes, then restore power and observe whether the code returns. Some Samsung models also support a button reset (such as Energy Saver plus Lighting held together) but the specific combination depends on your control panel layout.
3. **Inspect for frost and ice buildup.** Remove any food and shelves blocking the rear panel, then check the evaporator cover area for heavy frost or ice. If iced over, turn off the refrigerator and allow it to thaw completely (usually 6 to 12 hours) or use a hair dryer on low heat to speed defrosting before further testing.
4. **Locate and inspect the defrost sensor.** The defrost thermistor is typically clipped to the evaporator coil or mounted near the evaporator outlet. Examine the sensor body, its two-wire harness, and the connector for corrosion, moisture, loose pins, or physical damage.
5. **Test the sensor with a multimeter.** Disconnect the thermistor plug and measure resistance across the sensor terminals on the kiloohm scale. An open (infinite) or shorted (zero) reading indicates a failed sensor. Consult your model's service table for the expected resistance range at room temperature if available, but open or shorted readings are always failures.
6. **Check related airflow and defrost components.** If the sensor tests good, verify that the evaporator fan spins freely, the defrost heater is not burned out, and the drain is not blocked by ice. Abnormal frost patterns or restricted airflow can create conditions that trigger sensor faults.
7. **Replace the failed part and reassemble.** Install a new defrost thermistor if testing confirmed failure, reconnect the harness, and secure the evaporator cover. Restore power and run the refrigerator through a full cycle to verify the 5E or SE code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable accessing the evaporator compartment or removing the rear panel, if the wiring harness shows extensive damage that requires tracing back to the main control board, or if the code persists after replacing the sensor and you suspect a board-level fault. Technicians have model-specific service manuals and can confirm the exact code meaning for your platform. Also call a pro if you discover a major defrost-system failure (burned heater, failed timer, or compressor issue) that goes beyond a simple sensor swap, or if heavy ice buildup points to a sealed-system refrigerant leak that requires EPA-certified recovery and repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 including service call and sensor.
