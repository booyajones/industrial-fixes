## LG Dishwasher HE Error Code — What It Means

The HE error code on an LG dishwasher indicates a heater-related condition. The control has detected a problem with water heating, typically described as a failure to recognize an expected temperature change during the wash cycle. In practical terms, the dishwasher is not seeing the heating response it expects within about 10 minutes, pointing to a fault in the heater circuit, the thermistor (temperature sensor), or related control logic.

LG frames HE as a temporary heater error in some cases, so a brief power interruption or control glitch can occasionally clear it. However, when the code repeats after a reset, service is required to address a genuine hardware or wiring fault in the heating system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board first, but the heater element and thermistor are far more common causes. Test both components with a multimeter for continuity and resistance before buying any board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element or heater assembly (~50%)** The element itself has burned out or broken internally, so the control sees no temperature rise and triggers HE.
- **Bad thermistor (~30%)** The temperature sensor is reading incorrectly or open, preventing the control from detecting the actual water temperature.
- **Corroded or loose heater connections (~10%)** Terminals at the heater connector on the pump case assembly have corroded or come loose, interrupting the heater circuit.
- **Low incoming water temperature (~5%)** Supply water is excessively cold (well below 120 °F), making it difficult for the dishwasher to reach target temperature in time.
- **Control board or control logic fault (~5%)** When the heater, thermistor, and wiring all test good, the electronic control itself may be failing to process the heating signals correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or unplug the dishwasher completely before opening any panels or touching wiring.
2. **Reset the dishwasher** by leaving it unpowered for at least two minutes, then restore power and run a test cycle to confirm the code is persistent and not a temporary glitch.
3. **Verify incoming water temperature** at the nearest faucet by running hot water and measuring with a thermometer (target is around 120 °F), and check that household water pressure is above 20 PSI.
4. **Access the heater and thermistor** by removing the lower front panel and toe-kick, then inspect all heater wiring and connectors at the pump case assembly for corrosion, burn marks, or loose terminals.
5. **Test the heater element** with a multimeter set to continuity or resistance mode (consult your model's service manual for the expected ohm value). If the heater is open or reads infinite resistance, replace it.
6. **Test the thermistor** with a multimeter for resistance (again, refer to your model's specification). If the sensor is out of range or open, replace it.
7. **Check the control board** only after confirming the heater, thermistor, and all wiring are intact and test within specification. If those components are sound, the electronic control may need replacement.
8. **Reassemble and retest** once the faulty component is replaced, then run a full wash cycle to verify normal heating and confirm the HE code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable removing panels, working with live electrical connections, or using a multimeter to test resistance and continuity. The heater and thermistor are located in the sump area beneath the tub, requiring partial disassembly and safe electrical isolation. A technician will also have access to your model's exact service manual specifications for heater resistance and thermistor values, ensuring accurate diagnosis. If the heater and thermistor both test good and you suspect the control board, a pro can confirm the diagnosis and handle the board replacement without risking damage to other components.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–$300.
