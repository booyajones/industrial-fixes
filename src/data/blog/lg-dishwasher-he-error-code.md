## LG Dishwasher HE Error Code — What It Means

The HE code on an LG dishwasher stands for heating error. LG identifies this as a heater-related fault, meaning the machine is not seeing the expected heating behavior during a cycle. LG support describes HE as a temporary heater error in some cases, so the code can appear when the dishwasher does not recognize a change in water temperature within about 10 minutes of starting the heating phase.

The error can be triggered by a failed heating element, a bad thermistor (temperature sensor), corroded or loose wiring at the heater connector, a control board relay fault, or incoming water that is too cold for the dishwasher to boost within the expected time. The heater assembly and thermistor are the most common components involved in field repairs.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first without testing the heater or thermistor. Use a multimeter to check heater continuity and thermistor resistance before replacing any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element (~40%)** The heater assembly has burned out or lost continuity, so the dishwasher cannot raise water temperature and throws HE.
- **Bad thermistor or temperature sensor (~30%)** The sensor no longer reads temperature correctly, so the control board does not see a temperature rise even if the heater is working.
- **Corroded or loose heater connector (~15%)** Corrosion, heat damage, or a loose terminal at the heater harness connector prevents power from reaching the element.
- **Incoming water too cold (~10%)** If household supply water is significantly below 120 °F, the heater may not raise the temperature fast enough and the unit times out.
- **Control board or relay fault (~5%)** The control board fails to energize the heater circuit even though the heater and sensor test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dishwasher** by unplugging the unit or opening the breaker, wait five minutes, then restore power and run a test cycle to see if the HE code was temporary.
2. **Verify the symptom** by running a normal heated cycle and confirming that the code returns and that dishes remain cold or the cycle stops early.
3. **Access the heater circuit** by removing the lower access panel or kick plate and locating the heating element and thermistor at the bottom of the tub.
4. **Inspect the heater connector and wiring** for corrosion, heat damage, loose terminals, or burned insulation, and clean or replace any damaged connectors.
5. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter set to resistance mode, checking for continuity through the element and comparing the reading to your model's service manual if available.
6. **Test the thermistor** by measuring its resistance and comparing it to the temperature-resistance table in your service data, or replace it if the reading is open or far out of range.
7. **Check incoming water temperature** at the kitchen sink to confirm household supply is near 120 °F, and adjust your water heater if needed.
8. **Replace the failed component** (heater assembly, thermistor, or connector) and run a full heated cycle to confirm the error is cleared and the dishwasher heats properly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with 120-volt appliance wiring, if multimeter tests are outside your skill level, or if both the heater and thermistor test good but the error persists (which points to a control board or relay problem). A pro can quickly isolate whether the control board is commanding heater power, test the full circuit under load, and replace the sump assembly or board as needed. If the heater connector is part of a larger pump case assembly and you do not want to disassemble the base of the dishwasher, a service call will save time and avoid the risk of damaging hoses or seals during reassembly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $60-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
