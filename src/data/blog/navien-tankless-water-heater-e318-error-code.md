## Navien Tankless E318 Error — What It Means

The E318 error on a Navien tankless water heater indicates an exhaust overheat condition. The unit has detected that exhaust gases are hotter than the acceptable range, which triggers a safety shutdown to prevent damage or hazardous operation. This code typically points to restricted airflow, a problem with venting, or a sensor reading incorrect temperature data.

The heater monitors exhaust temperature continuously during operation. When the reading exceeds the programmed limit, the system interprets this as a potential fire hazard or incomplete combustion issue and stops the burner. Common triggers include blocked or undersized venting, a failing exhaust thermistor, carbon buildup inside the heat exchanger, or inadequate combustion air supply.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the exhaust thermistor first, but the real culprit is usually a vent blockage or heat exchanger fouling. Inspect the entire vent run and combustion chamber for debris or carbon buildup before ordering sensors.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~40%)** Debris, bird nests, ice dams, or improper vent termination prevent hot gases from escaping, causing temperature to spike.
- **Carbon buildup in heat exchanger (~25%)** Incomplete combustion leaves soot and scale deposits that insulate the exhaust path and trap heat inside the unit.
- **Failing exhaust thermistor (~20%)** The sensor that monitors exhaust temperature drifts out of calibration or develops a short, sending false overheat signals to the control board.
- **Undersized or improperly installed venting (~10%)** Too many elbows, excessive length, or incorrect pipe diameter create back pressure that holds exhaust heat in the unit.
- **Insufficient combustion air (~5%)** Blocked intake vent or negative-pressure installation starves the burner of oxygen, leading to incomplete combustion and high exhaust temps.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shut-off valve to make the unit safe for inspection.
2. **Inspect the exhaust vent termination** outside. Remove any bird nests, ice, leaves, or debris blocking the opening. Check that the vent cap is intact and properly oriented.
3. **Trace the entire vent run** from the heater to the exterior, checking for separated joints, sagging pipe, or condensation pooling that could restrict flow.
4. **Examine the intake air vent** for blockages such as mulch, snow, or lint. Confirm the intake is drawing air freely and is located per manufacturer spacing requirements.
5. **Remove the front cover** of the water heater and visually inspect the heat exchanger and burner area for soot, carbon deposits, or white scale buildup. Heavy fouling requires professional cleaning.
6. **Locate the exhaust thermistor** mounted on or near the exhaust manifold. Disconnect the two-wire plug and measure resistance with a multimeter. Compare the reading to your model's service manual. Out-of-spec readings indicate sensor failure.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore power and gas, and run a hot-water demand cycle. Monitor for the E318 code. If it reappears after clearing vents and verifying the sensor, call a qualified technician for combustion analysis and heat-exchanger service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you find heavy carbon deposits in the combustion chamber, if the error persists after clearing all vent obstructions, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. Professional service includes combustion analysis, heat-exchanger descaling, and verification of proper venting per local codes. Any work involving gas connections, burner adjustment, or sealed-combustion components should be performed by a qualified technician to maintain safety and warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
