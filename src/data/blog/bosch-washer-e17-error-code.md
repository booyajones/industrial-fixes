## Bosch Washer E17 Error Code — What It Means

The E17 (or F17) error code on a Bosch washer means the machine has exceeded the allowed water supply time. In practical terms, the washer is not filling fast enough or not filling at all. This is a fill and inlet issue, not a drain fault.

The control board monitors how long it takes for water to enter the drum. When the expected fill time is exceeded, it throws E17 and stops the cycle to prevent a wash attempt with insufficient water.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many users replace the inlet valve or pressure sensor before checking the basics. Always verify the wall tap is fully open, inspect the hose for kinks, and clean the inlet screen first. These fixes cost nothing and solve the majority of E17 codes.

## Common Causes

- **Water supply tap closed or partly open (~35%)** The wall shutoff valve is not turned fully open, restricting flow to the washer.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hose (~30%)** The hose is bent sharply or pinched behind the machine, blocking water flow.
- **Clogged inlet screen or filter (~20%)** Sediment or debris has accumulated in the small mesh filter at the hose connection to the washer.
- **Faulty inlet solenoid valve (~10%)** The inlet valve coil is open, the plunger is stuck, or the valve does not energize when commanded.
- **Defective pressure sensor or control wiring (~5%)** The pressure switch or its wiring to the control board is damaged or out of specification, causing false timeout signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and close the wall water supply tap before beginning any disassembly or hose work.
2. **Verify the wall tap is fully open.** Turn it all the way counterclockwise and confirm household water pressure is present at the supply point.
3. **Inspect the inlet hose** from wall to washer for kinks, crushing, or sharp bends. Straighten or replace the hose if any restriction is found.
4. **Remove the inlet hose** at the washer connection and pull out the small mesh screen or filter. Rinse it under running water to clear sediment and debris.
5. **Reconnect the hose** firmly, open the supply tap, plug the machine back in, and run a short rinse or test cycle to confirm normal fill and verify the code clears.
6. **Test the inlet valve** if the supply, hose, and screen are all verified good. Disconnect power, access the valve behind the hose connection, and use a multimeter to check coil continuity. Replace the valve if it reads open or does not energize during a fill command.
7. **Inspect wiring and pressure sensor** only if the inlet valve tests good but the error persists. Look for damaged connectors, corroded terminals, or a faulty pressure switch, and consult the service manual for model-specific test points.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified the supply tap is fully open, the hose is clear, the inlet screen is clean, and you have tested or replaced the inlet valve, yet the E17 code returns. At that point the fault is likely in the pressure sensing circuit, control board, or internal wiring harness. Diagnosing those components requires the service manual, specialized test equipment, and experience tracing low-voltage control signals. A qualified appliance technician can run the manufacturer diagnostic mode, read live sensor values, and identify whether the pressure switch, wiring, or control module is at fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (hose or inlet valve if needed), 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call.
