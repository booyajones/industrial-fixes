## Bosch Washer E17 Error Code — What It Means

The E17 code on a Bosch washing machine signals that the appliance is not receiving enough incoming water or that the water supply is restricted. The machine detects insufficient fill flow, which can stop the cycle early or prevent it from starting at all.

This is a supply-side fault, not a drain or flood issue. The washer expects a certain flow rate during fill, and when it does not arrive quickly enough the control throws E17. Most cases trace to a simple restriction somewhere between the household plumbing and the inlet valve.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the inlet valve when E17 appears, but the majority of cases are caused by a blocked inlet screen or kinked hose. Always clean the mesh filters and verify full tap flow before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Water tap not fully open (~30%)** A tap turned only partway or bumped closed will starve the machine of flow and trigger E17 immediately.
- **Clogged inlet mesh filter (~25%)** Sediment and mineral deposits collect on the small screens inside the hose coupling and valve inlet, choking water flow over time.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hose (~20%)** Hoses pushed tight against a wall or pinched during installation restrict flow even when the tap is wide open.
- **Low household water pressure (~15%)** Supply pressure below 10 psi or a building-wide pressure drop can prevent the washer from filling at the expected rate.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~8%)** A valve that sticks partially closed or has a failed solenoid coil will block flow even after all external restrictions are cleared.
- **Pressure sensor or control issue (~2%)** A pressure switch or control board that misreads the fill level can throw E17 even when water is entering normally.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does water flow freely when you disconnect the inlet hose at the washer and point it into a bucket?</summary>

</details>

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The screens were the problem. No parts needed.<br><strong>No:</strong> The inlet valve or pressure sensor is suspect. Test the valve electrically or call a technician.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the water tap behind the washer.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to reach the supply connection at the back panel.
3. **Disconnect the inlet hose** at both the tap and the machine inlet, then inspect for kinks, crushing, or visible debris inside.
4. **Remove and clean the mesh filter screens** using a small brush or toothpick to clear sediment from the hose coupling and the valve inlet port.
5. **Reconnect the hose** snugly at both ends, open the tap fully, and check for leaks.
6. **Restore power and run a rinse or short cycle** to verify that the washer fills normally and the E17 code clears.
7. **If E17 persists**, test the inlet valve with a multimeter for continuity across each solenoid coil, replace the valve if open or shorted, and inspect the pressure sensor if the valve tests good.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the filters, verified full tap pressure, replaced the inlet hose, and the washer still throws E17. At that point the inlet valve or pressure sensor likely needs electrical testing and replacement, which requires safe disassembly of the top or rear panel and familiarity with solenoid circuits. A pro can also diagnose less common control-board faults that mimic a supply restriction. If your household water pressure measures below 10 psi at the tap, you may need a plumber to address the building supply before any washer repair will succeed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
