## State Water Heater E73 Error Code — What It Means

E73 does not appear in State Water Heater fault code documentation. This code belongs to Google Nest thermostats and means no power is detected on the RC wire. The RC terminal receives 24 VAC control power from your heating system's transformer. When the Nest loses this power feed, it cannot control your equipment and displays E73.

If you saw E73 on a Nest thermostat that controls a State-branded heating system, the issue is in the low-voltage control circuit between your furnace or boiler and the thermostat. The water heater itself is not generating this code. Check your heating system's power supply, control board, and wiring first.

## Common Causes

- **Tripped breaker or system power loss** The furnace or boiler circuit breaker has tripped, cutting 24 VAC transformer output to the thermostat RC wire.
- **Loose or corroded thermostat wiring** The RC wire connection at the thermostat base or the equipment terminal board is loose, corroded, or disconnected.
- **Blown control board fuse** A fuse on the furnace or boiler control board has blown, interrupting 24 VAC power to the thermostat circuit.
- **Condensate float switch triggered** A full condensate drip pan or clogged drain tube has activated the float switch, shutting down low-voltage power as a safety measure.
- **Clogged air filter reducing airflow** A dirty filter can cause system shutdown or safety lockout that cuts power to the thermostat.
- **Failed transformer** The 24 VAC transformer inside the heating equipment has failed and no longer supplies power to the RC circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check the main circuit breaker** for your furnace, boiler, or air handler at the electrical panel and reset it if tripped.
2. **Inspect thermostat wiring** by removing the Nest display and examining the RC wire connection at the base for looseness, corrosion, or damage.
3. **Replace the air filter** in your heating system if it appears dirty or clogged, then wait five minutes and check if the code clears.
4. **Examine the condensate drip pan and drain tube** below the air handler or furnace for standing water or blockage, and clear any obstruction or reset the float switch if present.
5. **Locate and test the control board fuse** inside the furnace or boiler cabinet, replacing it with an identical amperage fuse if blown.
6. **Verify transformer output** by measuring 24 VAC between the R and C terminals at the equipment using a multimeter, and replace the transformer if voltage is absent.
7. **Reboot the thermostat** by reconnecting the display to the base and allowing it to power up fully, then test system operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you cannot locate the source of the power loss, if the transformer tests at zero volts and you are not comfortable working inside the furnace cabinet, or if fuses blow repeatedly after replacement. A pro should also handle any scenario where you find burnt wiring, a scorched control board, or persistent condensate problems that suggest a refrigerant leak or drain system failure. If your State equipment uses a complex zone control or integrated boiler controller, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent misdiagnosis.
