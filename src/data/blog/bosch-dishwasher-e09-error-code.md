## Bosch E09 Error Code — What It Means

The E09 error code on a Bosch dishwasher indicates that the appliance has detected a fault in the heating system. Bosch states that this code means the heating element is defective. In practice, the heater is commonly the flow-through heater or heater-pump assembly integrated with the circulation pump, not just a standalone element.

When E09 appears, the dishwasher typically fills with water but does not heat, or the error shows early in the cycle. The fault may also be caused by damaged wiring, poor water circulation, or in some cases a dirty salt sensor if the salt LED is also illuminated. A technician will verify power, inspect the heater-pump assembly, check connectors and wiring, and confirm water flow before proceeding with replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first. Instead, inspect the heater-pump assembly and connectors for burn marks, loose terminals, or water damage before replacing any control module.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heater-pump assembly (~60%)** The integrated flow heater or heater-pump unit has burned out or developed an internal open circuit, preventing water from heating.
- **Dirty or corroded wiring and connectors (~20%)** Loose, oxidized, or damaged wire terminals at the heater or pump assembly interrupt the heating circuit and trigger the fault.
- **Poor water circulation (~10%)** Clogged filters or spray arms restrict flow through the heater assembly, causing overheating or preventing proper heating detection.
- **Dirty salt sensor (when salt LED is also on) (~5%)** Bosch notes that if the salt indicator is illuminated at the same time, the salt sensor may be dirty and should be checked or cleaned.
- **Intermittent fault or transient error (~5%)** A temporary glitch in the heater circuit or control module can set the code, which may clear after a power reset and retest.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dishwasher, wait 30 seconds, then restore power and run a short test cycle to see if E09 returns.
2. **Check the salt LED** on the control panel. If it is on at the same time as E09, inspect and clean the salt sensor according to your owner's manual before proceeding.
3. **Clean the filter and spray arms** by removing the lower rack, lifting out the filter assembly, rinsing under hot water, and checking that spray-arm holes are clear to make sure proper circulation.
4. **Disconnect power again** and access the heater-pump assembly under the tub. Look for burn marks, loose or corroded connectors, water damage, or any obvious physical damage to the unit.
5. **Inspect wiring and terminals** at the heater-pump harness. Clean or reseat any corroded or loose connections, and check for pinched or melted wires along the harness.
6. **Test the heater circuit** if you have a multimeter. Disconnect the heater terminals and check for continuity across the heating element. Consult your model's wiring diagram for expected values, as published specifications are not standardized.
7. **Replace the heater-pump assembly** if the element tests open, if connectors are burned, or if the error returns after all other checks. Use the model and serial number from the rating plate to order the correct Bosch replacement part.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or disassembling the dishwasher's base. The heater-pump assembly is located under the tub and requires disconnecting the drain and water lines, removing the lower panel, and handling 120-volt wiring. A technician will also have the correct ohm and voltage specifications for your specific model, can confirm that the control board is not at fault, and will warranty the replacement assembly. If E09 persists after a simple reset and basic cleaning, professional diagnosis is the fastest path to a reliable repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200–$400.
