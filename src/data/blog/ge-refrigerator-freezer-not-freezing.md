## GE Refrigerator Freezer Not Freezing — What's Happening

When a GE refrigerator freezer stops freezing, you may see code FF on the display, which means the freezer temperature is rising too high and frozen food may be thawing. Other related codes include dE (defrost system has not operated properly in the last 24 hours), CC (temperature controls are incorrect), PF (power interruption), and DE (demo mode, which disables cooling). If no code appears, the symptom is the same: the freezer compartment is not maintaining freezing temperatures.

The root issue is almost always that cold air is not reaching the freezer space or the appliance is not generating cold air in the first place. Most often, ice has built up on the evaporator coil because the defrost cycle failed, blocking airflow through the fins. Less commonly, a failed evaporator fan, dirty condenser coils, a bad door seal, or control and sensor faults prevent proper cooling.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Defrost system failure causing ice buildup on evaporator** The defrost heater, defrost thermostat, or control board defrost output has failed, allowing frost to accumulate on the evaporator coil and block airflow through the freezer.
- **Dirty condenser coils** Dust and debris on the condenser coils reduce heat rejection, which prevents the refrigerant cycle from cooling efficiently.
- **Evaporator fan failure or restricted airflow** The evaporator fan motor has failed or is obstructed, stopping cold air circulation from the evaporator to the freezer compartment.
- **Blocked air ports or airflow paths** Ice or food items are blocking the air ducts between the freezer and fresh-food sections, preventing cold air from reaching the freezer.
- **Faulty door gasket allowing warm air intrusion** A torn or worn door seal lets warm, moist air into the freezer, causing frost accumulation and temperature rise.
- **Thermistor or temperature sensor problem** The temperature sensor is giving false readings to the control board, so the system does not call for cooling when needed.
- **Control board or compressor relay issue** The main control board or compressor start relay has failed, preventing the compressor or defrost cycle from running properly.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the symptom and check the control panel for any error codes (FF, dE, CC, PF, or DE), then power-cycle the refrigerator briefly if the code appears transient.
2. Check for Demo mode first on applicable GE models, because cooling is disabled even though lights and controls work.
3. Remove the freezer rear panel and inspect the evaporator coil for heavy frost or ice buildup, which strongly points to a defrost or airflow problem.
4. Check the condenser coils (usually behind or beneath the unit) for dust and debris, and clean them if restricted.
5. Check the evaporator fan for free rotation and proper operation by spinning the blade by hand and listening for motor hum when the door is closed.
6. Run GE service diagnostics if available (model-specific diagnostic mode) to test loads and control outputs rather than guessing from symptoms alone.
7. Test the defrost components if frost is present: check the defrost heater for continuity, verify the defrost thermostat closes when cold, and confirm defrost timer or control board output depending on model.
8. Test the temperature sensor or thermistor against the correct resistance chart for your model if readings are suspect, and check the compressor start relay if the compressor is not running.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the evaporator remains warm and the cabinet never gets cold after you have verified airflow, defrost operation, and fan function. That points to a sealed-system issue such as a refrigerant leak, restriction, or compressor failure, which requires specialized equipment and EPA-certified handling. Also call a pro if you are not comfortable working with live voltage during diagnostics, if the control board needs replacement and you cannot access service mode, or if the defrost system tests good but heavy frost keeps returning.
