## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E35 Error Code — What It Means

The E35 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition. The unit either cannot establish a flame or the flame sensor is not confirming that combustion is occurring properly. This is a safety feature that prevents gas from flowing without proper ignition.

Because the exact meaning can vary slightly between different Ruud tankless models, always consult your owner's manual or the label inside the service panel for model-specific diagnostics. Common causes range from interrupted gas supply and dirty flame sensors to failed ignition components or venting problems that affect combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is a flame rod coated with carbon deposits. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper or a cloth before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod sensor (~45%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensor prevents it from detecting the flame signal, triggering a false fault even when ignition occurs.
- **Interrupted gas supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a closed valve, or an empty propane tank prevents the burner from lighting or maintaining a stable flame.
- **Failed igniter or ignition system (~15%)** A weak or broken igniter cannot create the spark needed to light the gas, or the ignition control module has failed.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructed intake or exhaust vents disrupt the air-fuel mixture, causing incomplete combustion or preventing flame establishment.
- **Faulty gas valve (~5%)** The gas valve may not open fully or at all, starving the burner of fuel even when the ignition sequence is correct.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve at the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners; locate the burner assembly and the flame rod sensor near the burner.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon buildup or white corrosion; gently clean the rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a clean cloth until the metal is shiny.
4. **Check the igniter** for cracks or heavy deposits; if damaged, mark it for replacement.
5. **Verify gas pressure** by confirming the gas valve is open and listening for gas flow when the unit calls for heat (if you are trained to do so safely).
6. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside for blockages, nests, or ice; clear any obstructions and make sure terminations meet clearance requirements.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and run a hot-water tap to initiate a heating cycle; observe whether the burner lights and the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician for any work involving gas connections, pressure testing, or disassembly of the gas valve and burner assembly. Tankless water heaters require precise combustion tuning and safe handling of natural gas or propane. A professional can measure gas inlet pressure, test flame current with a microamp meter, and verify proper venting and combustion air supply. If cleaning the flame rod and resetting the unit do not resolve the E35 code, a technician should diagnose the ignition control board, gas valve operation, and exhaust system to prevent unsafe conditions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
