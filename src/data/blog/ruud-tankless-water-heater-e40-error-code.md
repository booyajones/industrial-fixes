## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E40 Error — What It Means

The E40 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a flame detection or ignition failure. The unit has attempted to ignite the burner but either failed to establish a flame or could not detect it once lit. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent gas buildup or unsafe operation.

The code can be triggered by several conditions: insufficient gas supply, a dirty or corroded flame sensor, blocked venting, incorrect gas pressure, or a faulty ignition component. Because the exact definition of error codes can vary between Ruud models and firmware versions, always consult your unit's installation and service manual for the precise meaning and recommended troubleshooting steps for your specific model number.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the gas valve or igniter when the actual problem is a flame sensor covered in soot or mineral deposits. Clean the sensor with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth before replacing any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~40%)** Carbon buildup or mineral deposits on the flame rod prevent the sensor from detecting the flame, even when ignition occurs.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure from the meter, a partially closed valve, or demand from other appliances can prevent reliable ignition.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Obstructed intake or exhaust vents cause poor combustion and prevent the flame from stabilizing.
- **Faulty igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked ceramic insulator, corroded electrode tip, or broken wire prevents spark generation.
- **Low water flow rate (~5%)** Flow below the minimum threshold keeps the unit from firing, especially if inlet screens or filters are clogged.
- **Failed gas valve or control board (~5%)** A malfunctioning gas valve or main control board may fail to deliver gas or send the proper ignition signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater before opening the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** and locate the flame sensor rod, a thin metal probe near the burner assembly.
3. **Clean the flame sensor** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove any soot, corrosion, or mineral deposits, then wipe it dry.
4. **Inspect the igniter electrode** for cracks, corrosion, or improper gap. Consult your model's service manual for the correct electrode gap and adjust if necessary.
5. **Check all venting** by walking outside and confirming the intake and exhaust terminations are clear of debris, ice, bird nests, or lint buildup.
6. **Verify gas pressure** at the inlet to the unit using a manometer. Compare the reading to the specifications in your installation manual.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, then run a hot water tap to initiate a heat call and observe whether the flame ignites and the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame sensor does not resolve the error, or if you lack the tools to measure gas pressure. A pro should handle any repairs involving the gas valve, control board replacement, or venting modifications. If the unit repeatedly faults after cleaning and you have confirmed adequate gas supply and clear vents, the issue may require specialized diagnostic equipment and training to pinpoint. Always follow local codes, which often require permitted work and inspections for gas-fired equipment.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
