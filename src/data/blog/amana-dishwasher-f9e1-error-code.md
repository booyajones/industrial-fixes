## Amana Dishwasher F9E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F9E1 code on an Amana dishwasher means the control has detected that the unit is not draining within the expected time or power cycle. In plain terms, the dishwasher filled with water but could not empty it quickly enough, so it stopped and threw the error to protect itself from overflow or pump damage.

This is a drain-failure code specific to Amana dishwashers. It does not apply to Amana washing machines, which use the same code letters for a completely different fault. When you see F9E1 on the display, the machine is telling you that water is still sitting in the tub or that the drain cycle timed out before the pump could finish its job.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump first without checking the disposal knockout plug or drain hose. Always verify the drain path is open and the hose is not kinked before spending money on a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Garbage disposer knockout plug left in (~35%)** If the dishwasher drains into a new or replaced disposal and the installer forgot to punch out the drain knockout plug, water cannot exit the machine and the F9E1 code appears.
- **Drain hose kinked or improperly routed (~25%)** A hose that is crimped under the cabinet, looped too high, or pinched at the connection will restrict flow and cause the control to time out before draining is complete.
- **Clogged sump or drain path (~20%)** Food debris, broken glass, or foreign objects in the sump, filter area, or drain line can block water flow and prevent the pump from emptying the tub.
- **Weak or failing drain pump (~15%)** A pump that runs but moves water slowly, is clogged internally, or has worn impeller blades will not finish draining within the cycle time and triggers the error.
- **Blocked air gap or drain connector (~5%)** If the drain connects through an air gap or standpipe that is clogged with grease or debris, backpressure will stop the drain cycle and set the code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait one full minute, then restore power and try running a cancel/drain cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Check the garbage disposal knockout plug** by disconnecting the drain hose from the disposer inlet and shining a light into the port. If you see a plastic disc blocking the hole, use a hammer and screwdriver to knock it into the disposer, then fish it out and discard it.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** from the dishwasher pump outlet all the way to the disposal or air gap. Straighten any kinks, make sure the high loop or air gap is installed correctly, and confirm the hose is not pinched behind the machine.
4. **Remove and clean the sump and filter** inside the dishwasher tub. Pull out the lower spray arm if necessary, lift out the filter assembly, and rinse away food particles, broken glass, or any debris that could block the drain inlet.
5. **Test the drain pump** by running a cancel/drain cycle and listening for motor noise. If the pump hums but does not move water, remove it from the sump housing, check for jammed objects or a broken impeller, and replace the pump if it is damaged or weak.
6. **Reconnect and secure all hoses**, replace the filter and spray arm, then run a full wash cycle to verify the machine drains completely at each stage without triggering F9E1 again.
7. **Document the repair** by noting which component was at fault so you can track recurring issues and confirm the dishwasher drains within normal cycle time.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have removed the knockout plug, cleared the drain path, cleaned the sump, and the F9E1 code still appears after a power reset. A pro can test the drain pump with a multimeter, verify that the control board is sending the correct signals, and check for wiring faults or a faulty pressure switch that might falsely report standing water. Also call if you are uncomfortable working under the sink, disconnecting hoses, or pulling the dishwasher out of the cabinet. A service visit typically includes diagnosis, the drain pump or other failed part, labor, and a warranty on the repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–90 in parts (pump or hose), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call.
