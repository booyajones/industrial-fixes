## State Water Heater E116 Error Code — What It Means

State Water Heaters uses model-specific error codes to help diagnose faults in their electric and heat-pump units. The E116 code does not have a single universal definition across all State models. Your exact model and control board determine what E116 represents, whether it is a sensor fault, communication error, or component failure. To find the precise meaning, locate the rating plate on your water heater (usually on the front or side panel), scan the QR code if present, and download the troubleshooting section of your model's manual from State's support site. Without the model-specific code table, attempting repair may waste time or damage components.

## Common Causes

- **Sensor or probe failure** A temperature sensor, thermistor, or other monitoring probe may be open, shorted, or drifted out of range.
- **Wiring or connector issue** Loose, corroded, or damaged connections between the control board and sensors can trigger diagnostic codes.
- **Control board fault** The main control board may have failed internally or lost calibration data, misreporting component status.
- **Communication error (heat-pump models)** Heat-pump water heaters use separate boards that talk to each other, and a dropped signal can log an error.
- **Power supply or grounding problem** Low voltage, incomplete grounding, or a recent power surge can cause erratic code behavior.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and confirm zero voltage at the unit with a multimeter before touching any wires.
2. **Locate the rating plate** on your water heater and write down the full model number and serial number.
3. **Download your model's service manual** from the State Water Heaters support website or call State technical support with your model and serial to request the error code definition for E116.
4. **Inspect all visible wiring and connectors** for corrosion, burn marks, or loose pins, paying special attention to sensor leads and board-to-board cables on heat-pump units.
5. **Measure sensor resistance** if your manual provides test values for thermistors or probes associated with E116, replacing any sensor that reads open, shorted, or outside the specified range.
6. **Reset the control board** by restoring power and holding any reset button for the duration specified in your manual, then monitor whether E116 reappears.
7. **Replace the control board** if the code persists after verifying all sensors and wiring, using the exact State replacement part number listed in your service manual.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you cannot locate your model's manual, if diagnostic steps require opening sealed refrigerant circuits (common on heat-pump models), or if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt circuits. Heat-pump water heaters often need refrigerant-handling certification for component replacement. A pro can also access State's dealer-only service bulletins and warranty-replacement procedures that are not published for homeowners.
