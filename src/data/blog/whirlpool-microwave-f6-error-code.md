## Whirlpool Microwave F6 Error Code — What It Means

The F6 error code on a Whirlpool over-the-range microwave means the control system attempted to run a function and failed. Whirlpool identifies the relay control board or associated wiring as the likely fault path. This is a control-output fault, not just a button or display issue. The microwave cannot complete an operation it was commanded to perform.

The code points to a hardware failure in the relay circuit or the wiring that carries power to the component being controlled. In some field reports, door-switch or interlock problems can trigger similar symptoms, so the actual fault may vary by model. A power glitch can occasionally cause a false F6 that clears after a reset, which is why Whirlpool recommends a power cycle before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board or keypad when the actual fault is a failed relay control board or a loose wiring connector. Always inspect the wiring harness and connectors for damage or loose pins before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Defective relay control board (~50%)** The relay that switches power to the magnetron or other load has failed and the microwave cannot execute the cook function.
- **Loose, damaged, or open wiring (~25%)** A connector has backed out, a wire has broken, or heat has damaged the harness between the control board and the component it drives.
- **Door-switch or interlock fault (~15%)** A stuck or failed door switch prevents the relay from closing and the microwave reports an execution failure instead of a door error.
- **Stuck or shorted keypad (~5%)** A membrane panel contact has failed and sends a garbled signal that the control system cannot execute.
- **Power glitch or temporary control fault (~5%)** A voltage sag or brownout left the microcomputer in a fault state that clears after a full power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait one full minute to allow the microcomputer to reset, then restore power and check whether the F6 code returns.
2. **Verify the symptom** by attempting to run a cook cycle and noting whether the microwave starts, whether the display responds, and whether the error appears immediately or mid-cycle.
3. **Access the relay control board** by removing the outer cabinet or the vent grille and top cover (consult your model's service manual for the exact panel-removal sequence).
4. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** on the relay control board and the main harness for loose pins, heat damage, broken wires, or corrosion, and reseat or repair any faults you find.
5. **Test the relay control board path** by checking for voltage at the relay output when the microwave is commanded to cook (if you are comfortable with live voltage measurements), or replace the relay control board if wiring is intact and the code persists.
6. **Reassemble the cabinet**, restore power, and run a full cook cycle to confirm the F6 error is cleared and all functions operate normally.
7. **Document the repair** by noting the part number of the replaced board and the date, and keep the old board for warranty or core-return purposes if required.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 120 V AC live circuits, if you cannot locate or access the relay control board in your model, or if the F6 code persists after you have replaced the relay board and inspected all wiring. A technician has the service manual, voltage tables, and exact wiring diagrams for your model and can perform live relay-output tests that are not safe for most homeowners. Whirlpool recommends professional service assistance if the code does not clear after a power reset. If your microwave is still under warranty or a service contract, contact Whirlpool or your retailer before opening the cabinet to preserve your coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–180 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350.
