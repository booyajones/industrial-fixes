## Frigidaire Washer E24 Error Code — What It Means

E24 on a Frigidaire washer is a control-side drain fault. Unlike a simple clog or slow-drain error, E24 specifically indicates the control board has detected a problem in the circuit that powers or controls the drain pump. On many Frigidaire models, this code points to a failure in the drain pump relay or triac on the main control board, or in the related drain pump control circuit.

Because the code is control-oriented, you may see it even when the tub is empty or when the drain hose is clear. The control is reporting that it cannot properly energize the pump, not that the pump is mechanically blocked. On front-load models with LED-only displays, E24 appears as two flashes followed by four flashes.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump first when the actual fault is the relay or triac on the main control board. Before ordering a pump, test the pump winding for continuity and resistance, and confirm the pump connector is receiving voltage during the drain cycle.

## Common Causes

- **Failed main control board relay or triac (~55%)** The drain pump relay or triac on the control board has failed and cannot switch power to the pump.
- **Wiring harness or connector problem (~20%)** A loose, corroded, or heat-damaged connector or wire between the control board and drain pump prevents proper control signals or current flow.
- **Drain pump electrical fault (~15%)** The pump winding is open, shorted, or draws abnormal current, triggering a fault in the control circuit.
- **Moisture or corrosion at connectors (~7%)** Water intrusion or corrosion at the control board or pump terminals creates intermittent or high-resistance connections.
- **Temporary stored fault code (~3%)** A transient electrical glitch or power surge has logged the code without a permanent component failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** for at least five minutes to clear any temporary stored fault codes, then plug back in and test a drain or spin cycle to see if E24 returns.
2. **Verify the fault code pattern.** On LED-only models, confirm you see two flashes followed by four flashes. Write down the pattern to make sure you are diagnosing the correct code.
3. **Check the drain hose and external drain path.** Look for kinks, crushing, or blockage in the standpipe or laundry drain. Even though E24 is control-side, a mechanical restriction can sometimes trigger pump-circuit faults.
4. **Access the drain pump.** Unplug the washer, remove the front lower access panel or tip the machine back (depending on model), and locate the drain pump and its wiring connector.
5. **Inspect the pump connector and harness.** Look for loose pins, corrosion, heat damage, or moisture. Wiggle the connector and check for intermittent contact. Repair or replace damaged terminals or connectors as needed.
6. **Test the drain pump electrically.** Disconnect the pump connector and measure the resistance across the pump terminals. A typical drain pump winding reads 5 to 20 ohms, but consult your model's service data. An open or very low resistance indicates a failed pump.
7. **Test for voltage at the pump connector during drain.** Reconnect the pump, restore power, and start a drain or spin cycle. Use a multimeter to check for AC voltage at the pump connector. If the pump is good but no voltage appears, replace the main control board. If voltage is present but the pump does not run, replace the pump.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage AC circuits, if you cannot access the pump or control board on your model, or if your electrical tests point to the main control board but you prefer not to replace it yourself. A technician can verify the fault with OEM diagnostic modes, perform precise voltage and current measurements, and replace the control board with confidence that wiring and grounding are correct. If the washer is still under warranty or a service contract, always contact the authorized service network before opening the cabinet.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150–300 in parts, 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200–450.
