## Electrolux Dryer E31 Error Code — What It Means

The E31 error on an Electrolux dryer indicates the control board is receiving an abnormal signal from the moisture sensing system. Specifically, the moisture sensor feedback frequency is too high. This code points to a problem with the moisture sensing circuit rather than a heating or airflow fault. The dryer uses metal sensor bars inside the drum to detect moisture levels in clothes. When wet fabric bridges the bars, a small current flows and the control reads the signal frequency. If that frequency is outside the expected range, the control throws E31 and may stop the cycle or refuse to start.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the electronic control board when E31 appears, but contamination or a loose connector at the sensor is far more common. Clean the sensor bars and inspect the wiring harness for damage before ordering a control.

## Common Causes

- **Contaminated moisture sensor bars (~55%)** Lint, detergent, fabric softener, or mineral deposits coat the metal sensor strips inside the drum and distort the electrical signal the control reads.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring connector (~20%)** The harness plug at the sensor or at the control can work loose, corrode, or develop a poor pin contact that creates an intermittent or out-of-range signal.
- **Damaged sensor wiring harness (~10%)** Abrasion, pinching, or a broken conductor in the sensor wiring produces an open or high-resistance path that the control interprets as a bad frequency.
- **Faulty moisture sensor bars (~10%)** Physical corrosion, cracks, or loss of continuity in the sensor strips themselves can send an abnormal signal even when the wiring and connectors are clean.
- **Failed electronic control board (~5%)** A defect in the control's moisture-sensor input circuit or signal-processing logic can generate a false E31 even when the sensor circuit is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet or switch off the circuit breaker to cut all power before you touch any internal parts.
2. **Open the dryer door and locate the moisture sensor bars** on the inside of the drum, usually two parallel metal strips mounted on the rear drum wall or lint-filter housing area.
3. **Clean the sensor bars thoroughly** with a cloth dampened in rubbing alcohol or white vinegar to remove lint, detergent film, fabric softener residue, and any mineral deposits, then dry the bars completely.
4. **Inspect the sensor wiring** by tracing the wires from the sensor bars to the connector plug, looking for abrasion, cuts, pinch points, or broken insulation along the harness.
5. **Check both ends of the sensor connector** for corrosion, bent pins, or a loose fit at the sensor itself and at the control board, and reseat or clean any connector that looks suspect.
6. **Restore power and run a short cycle** to see if the E31 clears. If the code returns, unplug again and use a multimeter to check continuity across the sensor bars (consult your model's wiring diagram for expected resistance).
7. **Replace the moisture sensor assembly** if continuity is open or far out of range, or replace the electronic control board if the sensor circuit tests good but the fault persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you lack a multimeter to verify sensor continuity, or if the E31 code persists after you have cleaned the sensors and inspected the wiring. A pro can quickly test the sensor circuit end-to-end and confirm whether the control board itself has failed. Also call if the dryer shares a dedicated 240V circuit and you are not confident isolating power safely, or if the sensor replacement requires disassembling the drum or front panel on your particular model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts if a sensor or harness needs replacement, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 depending on part and labor.
