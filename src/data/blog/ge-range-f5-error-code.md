## GE Range F5 Error Code — What It Means

On GE ranges and ovens, the F5 error code points to a problem in the oven door latch or lock system and the control sensing circuit that monitors it. The fault typically originates in the door latch assembly itself, the latch position switches that tell the control board where the latch sits, or the wiring harness that connects them. In some cases the Electronic Oven Control (EOC) or Electronic Range Control (ERC) board may fail to correctly supervise the latch feedback circuit and trigger the code even when the mechanical parts are sound.

The code does not indicate a generic temperature problem. Instead it reflects a communication or mechanical failure in the self-cleaning door interlock system. When the control cannot confirm that the door latch has reached or left a commanded position, it aborts the cycle and displays F5 to prevent unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the expensive EOC control board first. Always inspect the door latch assembly, test the latch switches, and check every connector and wire in the latch harness before condemning the board.

## Common Causes

- **Door latch assembly stuck or broken (~45%)** The motor-driven latch mechanism binds, strips a gear, or fails to travel the full stroke so the switches never confirm lock or unlock.
- **Defective door latch switch (~25%)** One or both micro-switches that report latch position to the control board fail to change state, sending incorrect or missing feedback.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Connectors at the latch or control board work loose, or wires become pinched, chafed, or heat-damaged and lose continuity or short intermittently.
- **Failed EOC or ERC control board (~15%)** The supervisory circuit on the Electronic Oven Control or Electronic Range Control board that reads the latch switches develops a fault and cannot process the feedback correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or breakers feeding the range and wait one full minute, then restore power to see if the code clears on its own.
2. **Pull the range forward** and remove the rear access panel or the control-panel shroud to expose the door latch assembly and wiring harness (consult your model's service manual for exact fastener locations).
3. **Inspect the door latch mechanism** by gently moving the latch hook by hand through lock and unlock positions, checking for binding, broken plastic gears, or a stripped drive coupling.
4. **Check each latch switch** with a multimeter set to continuity, confirming that the switch changes state cleanly when the latch moves between positions, and verify that connectors are fully seated with no corrosion.
5. **Trace the wiring harness** from the latch assembly to the EOC or ERC control board, looking for pinched insulation, heat discoloration, loose spade terminals, or any sign of arcing or short circuits.
6. **Replace the door latch assembly** if the mechanism binds or a switch tests open or does not toggle, then reconnect all wiring and restore power to verify the code is gone.
7. **Replace the EOC or ERC control board** only if the latch assembly, switches, and all wiring test normal but the F5 code persists or returns immediately on power-up.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range with power disconnected, if you cannot locate or access the door latch assembly on your particular model, or if the fault returns after you have replaced both the latch assembly and verified every wire and connector. A technician has the factory service manual, wiring diagrams, and test procedures to isolate EOC board faults without replacing good parts. Also call a pro if your range is still under warranty or if you need the repair documented for a service contract or homeowner's insurance claim.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25–$80 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–$350.
