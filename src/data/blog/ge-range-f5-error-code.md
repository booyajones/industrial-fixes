## GE Range F5 Error Code — What It Means

On GE ranges and ovens, F5 typically indicates a problem with the oven door latch assembly, door latch switches, or the wiring between them and the control. The electronic oven control detects that the latch system is not changing state correctly during a self-clean cycle or other latch operation. Some GE models use F5 to flag a broader supervisory-circuit failure in the electronic range control itself. The exact meaning depends on your model family, so check your wiring diagram or service sheet if the common latch-related causes do not apply.

This code is unrelated to the F5 fault on GE microwaves, which points to a humidity-sensor short. Always confirm you are working on a range or wall oven before following the diagnostic steps below.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control first. Inspect the latch assembly, test each latch switch for proper actuation, and check the wiring harness for damage or loose connectors before buying a new control.

## Common Causes

- **Door latch assembly worn or mispositioned (~40%)** The latch motor, rod, or striker is damaged or out of alignment so the latch does not fully extend or retract.
- **Door latch switch stuck or out of adjustment (~30%)** One or more micro-switches fail to change state when the latch moves, leaving the control unable to verify latch position.
- **Wiring harness or connector fault (~15%)** A loose connector, broken wire, or heat-damaged harness between the latch assembly and the control creates an intermittent open or short.
- **Electronic oven control (EOC) supervisory-circuit failure (~15%)** When all external latch hardware and wiring test good, the control board itself may have a failed relay or circuit monitoring the latch.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait one full minute, then restore power and check whether F5 clears.
2. **Remove the oven door** (if required for access) and pull the bottom or rear service panel to expose the door latch assembly and wiring.
3. **Inspect the latch assembly** for physical damage, binding, or misalignment, and verify the latch rod moves freely in and out.
4. **Test each latch switch** with a multimeter set to continuity mode, actuating the switch plunger by hand to confirm it opens and closes cleanly.
5. **Inspect the wiring harness** between the latch assembly and the control board for loose connectors, broken wires, or heat damage, and reseat all connectors firmly.
6. **Replace the door latch assembly** if the mechanism binds or the switches are integrated and one has failed.
7. **Replace the electronic oven control** only if the latch assembly, switches, and wiring all test good and the code persists after reassembly and a second power reset.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet, if the wiring harness shows heat damage that suggests a deeper short, or if you have replaced both the latch assembly and switches yet F5 still appears. A pro can perform end-to-end circuit testing with a schematic and determine whether the control board truly needs replacement. Also call for help if your model uses a complex latching system integrated with convection fans or steam generators, since those configurations require service-level diagnostics to isolate the fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
