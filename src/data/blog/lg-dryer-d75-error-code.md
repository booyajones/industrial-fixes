## LG Dryer D75 Error Code — What It Means

The D75 code on your LG dryer indicates approximately 75% blockage in the exhaust vent system. LG groups this code with D80, D90, and D95 as part of the Flow Sense airflow restriction warning system. The code means exhaust air is not moving properly through the vent run, and the dryer has detected severe resistance that will cause long dry times, overheating, and potential damage if not corrected.

The Flow Sense system monitors back-pressure in the exhaust path and triggers the code when it detects that roughly three-quarters of normal airflow is blocked. The code can appear during a cycle or at the end, and it tells you to inspect and clean the entire venting path from the dryer rear all the way to the exterior termination before running another load.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the lint filter housing or even the control board when the real cause is simply a crushed transition duct behind the dryer or a lint-clogged exterior vent hood. Always inspect the entire vent path and verify air blows freely outside before ordering any dryer parts.

## Common Causes

- **Lint accumulation in the exhaust duct (~50%)** Lint builds up inside the metal or flexible duct over time, creating the 75% restriction LG detects.
- **Crushed or kinked transition duct (~20%)** The short connector hose behind the dryer gets pinched when the unit is pushed back, blocking airflow.
- **Blocked exterior vent hood (~15%)** Lint, birds' nests, or a stuck damper at the outside termination prevent exhaust air from escaping.
- **Long or poorly routed vent run (~10%)** Excessive duct length or multiple elbows create resistance that the Flow Sense system reads as severe blockage.
- **Lint filter not fully seated (~5%)** A filter that is not pushed all the way in or damaged housing allows lint to bypass into the exhaust path.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off and unplug the dryer** (or trip the circuit breaker if hardwired) and pull the unit away from the wall so you can access the rear exhaust connection.
2. **Remove and inspect the lint filter** to confirm it is clean and fully seated in its housing, then check the filter cavity for lint buildup or damage.
3. **Disconnect the transition duct** from the dryer exhaust port and inspect it for crushing, kinks, or lint accumulation. Replace it if damaged or excessively clogged.
4. **Inspect the entire vent run** from the dryer to the exterior termination. Look for lint buildup, crushed sections, excessive elbows, or duct damage. Use a vent brush or vacuum to clean out all accessible sections.
5. **Go outside and check the vent hood** while someone runs the dryer. Verify that air blows strongly and that the damper opens freely. Clear any lint, debris, or obstructions.
6. **Reconnect all ducting** securely with metal clamps (avoid screws inside the duct that catch lint), push the dryer back gently to avoid kinking the transition duct, and restore power.
7. **Reset the dryer** by waiting about 5 seconds after power is restored, close the door firmly, then start a normal cycle and verify the D75 code does not return and dry times are normal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the D75 code returns after you have thoroughly cleaned the entire vent system, replaced any damaged ducting, verified strong airflow at the exterior hood, and reset the dryer. A persistent code with a clean vent usually means a Flow Sense pressure switch, wiring harness, or control-board issue that requires a technician with service documentation and test equipment. Also call a pro if your vent run is very long, routed through walls or the attic in a way you cannot safely access, or if you are uncomfortable disconnecting gas supply lines on a gas dryer model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in duct or vent parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $100-200 for vent cleaning or duct replacement.
