## Kenmore Range F01 Error Code — What It Means

The F01 error code on a Kenmore range can indicate different faults depending on the exact model. On many Kenmore electric and gas ranges, F01 often points to a problem with the main electronic control board (also called the ERC or clock/timer board) or a communication failure between the control board and other components. Some models use F01 to signal a stuck key on the control panel, a wiring harness issue, or a sensor fault. Because Kenmore ranges are built by multiple manufacturers under the Kenmore brand, the exact meaning of F01 varies by model series.

Always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the back of the range for the precise definition of F01 for your unit. In general, F01 codes point to electrical or control issues rather than mechanical failures. The range may refuse to heat, display the error continuously, or beep repeatedly when F01 appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board immediately without checking for loose or corroded wire harness connections at the board, which can cause identical symptoms and cost nothing to fix.

## Common Causes

- **Failed main control board (~50%)** The electronic control board develops internal faults from power surges, heat, or component aging, and sends F01 as a self-diagnostic flag.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness (~25%)** Wire connectors between the control board and other components work loose or develop corrosion, breaking communication and triggering F01.
- **Stuck keypad or control panel button (~15%)** A stuck button on the control panel sends a continuous signal that the control board interprets as a fault and logs as F01.
- **Faulty oven temperature sensor or RTD (~10%)** A shorted or open temperature sensor can cause the control board to register a communication fault and display F01 on some models.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely before beginning any work.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** by taking out the screws along the top and sides of the back panel.
3. **Locate the main control board**, usually mounted near the top rear of the range behind the control panel.
4. **Inspect all wire harness connectors** plugged into the control board for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks. Disconnect and reconnect each connector firmly.
5. **Check the control panel** for stuck or jammed buttons. Press each key to confirm it clicks and releases normally.
6. **If connections and buttons are good**, the control board itself is faulty. Note the model number on the old board and order a direct replacement.
7. **Disconnect the old control board** by unplugging all wire harnesses and removing the mounting screws. Transfer any mounting brackets if needed.
8. **Install the new control board** by screwing it into place and reconnecting all wire harnesses in the same positions as the original.
9. **Restore power** and test the range by selecting a burner or oven function. The F01 code should not reappear if the repair was successful.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt electric range wiring or if the F01 code persists after you have replaced the control board and checked all connections. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics with a multimeter to trace intermittent wiring faults or confirm whether a sensor or secondary board is causing the issue. Also call a pro if you see burn marks or melted insulation on the wiring harness, as this may indicate a deeper electrical problem that requires tracing and rewiring.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $100-250 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
