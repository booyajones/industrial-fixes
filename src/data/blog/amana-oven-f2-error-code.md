## Amana Oven F2 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 error code on an Amana oven typically indicates a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit or the electronic control board detecting an out-of-range temperature reading. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific interpretation. In most cases the control board has measured a signal from the temperature sensor that is either too high, too low, or intermittent, prompting it to shut down heating to protect the appliance. The fault may lie in the sensor probe itself, the wiring harness, connector contacts, or the control board's sensor input circuitry.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, which can cost over two hundred dollars, when the actual problem is a failed sensor probe or a loose connector. Always test the sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect the harness before ordering a control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or drifted oven temperature sensor probe. (~55%)** The sensor's internal resistance has changed outside the control board's expected range, causing the board to flag an error.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector. (~20%)** Intermittent contact at the harness plug or at the sensor terminals creates an open or high-resistance reading.
- **Damaged sensor wiring harness. (~10%)** Wires routed near the oven cavity may be melted, pinched, or broken, interrupting the signal path.
- **Failed electronic control board. (~10%)** The control's analog-to-digital input circuit or processor has failed and misreads a good sensor signal.
- **Incorrect sensor installation. (~5%)** The probe tip may not be fully seated in the mounting bracket or may be touching the oven wall, causing erratic readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker to prevent shock.
2. **Remove the rear access panel or bottom panel** from the back of the oven to expose the sensor probe and control board. You may need to pull the range away from the wall.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor probe**, usually mounted inside the rear wall of the oven cavity and connected by a two-wire harness to the control board.
4. **Disconnect the sensor harness** at the plug near the control board and inspect both the plug and the sensor terminals for corrosion or burn marks. Clean and reseat if necessary.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. Consult your model's service documentation for the expected resistance at room temperature. Out-of-range readings confirm a failed sensor.
6. **Replace the sensor probe** if the resistance is incorrect. Remove the mounting screw or bracket inside the oven, pull the old probe out, thread the new probe through the same path, and secure it firmly.
7. **Reconnect all wiring**, reassemble panels, restore power, and test the oven through a bake cycle to verify the F2 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the oven chassis or if you have tested the sensor and wiring and both are within specification but the F2 code continues to appear. A technician can verify whether the electronic control board input circuit has failed and can also check for less common issues such as a short in the cavity wiring or a ground fault. If your oven is still under warranty or if you need the repair completed quickly, a service call will save time and reduce the risk of ordering the wrong part.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
