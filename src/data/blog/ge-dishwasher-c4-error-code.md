## GE Dishwasher C4 Error Code — What It Means

On GE dishwashers, the C4 code indicates an overfill condition or that the machine has detected too much water. The control may display this if the float assembly signals high water level or if the unit filled twice after a power interruption. The code does not point to a drain or cooling fault.

The dishwasher uses a float switch assembly, typically an upside-down cup-like part in the tub floor, to monitor water level. When the float rises too high or the switch fails to reset, the control throws C4 and stops the cycle to prevent flooding.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board when C4 appears, but a stuck or soap-clogged float in the tub floor is the usual culprit. Always inspect and free the float assembly before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck float assembly (~50%)** Soap scum, mineral scale, or food debris jams the float so it cannot drop, triggering a false overfill signal even when water level is normal.
- **Failed float switch (~25%)** The switch inside the float chamber sticks closed or its contacts corrode, sending a continuous high-water signal to the control board.
- **Debris in the sump (~15%)** A clogged sump or partial drain blockage prevents proper water evacuation, leaving enough standing water to lift the float at the start of the next cycle.
- **Power interruption anomaly (~10%)** A brief outage or hard reset during the fill phase can confuse the control logic into thinking the machine filled twice, tripping C4 on restart.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cancel the cycle and reset power** by pressing Cancel/Drain or turning off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds, then restore power and check if C4 clears.
2. **Locate the float assembly** in the front corner of the tub floor, an inverted cup-like plastic part that rides up and down to sense water level.
3. **Lift the float gently and release it** to verify it drops freely under its own weight. If it sticks, remove any visible soap film, food particles, or mineral deposits around the float and its housing.
4. **Test the float switch electrically** if the float moves freely but the code persists. With power off, access the switch terminals under the tub and check for continuity with a multimeter. The switch should be open when the float is down and closed when lifted.
5. **Inspect the sump area** by removing the lower spray arm and filter assembly. Clear any debris, check the drain impeller for obstructions, and reassemble.
6. **Run a rinse cycle** to confirm the float drops after fill and the code does not return. If C4 reappears immediately, replace the float switch or float assembly.
7. **Replace the defective float switch or assembly** if cleaning and electrical tests show a failed component. Order the part by your model number and swap it following the service sheet or online guide for your series.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the float, verified free movement, tested the switch with a meter, and the C4 code still returns every cycle. Persistent overfill signals after replacing the float assembly may point to a control board fault or a wiring short in the float circuit, both of which require schematic-level diagnosis. Also call if you are uncomfortable working around 120-volt connections under the tub or if your model requires disassembly beyond the kick panel to reach the float switch.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
