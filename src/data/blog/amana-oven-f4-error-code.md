## Amana Oven F4 Error Code — What It Means

The F4 error code on Amana ovens typically indicates a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit. This can mean the sensor itself has failed (shorted or open), the wiring between the sensor and control board is damaged, or the control board is not reading the sensor correctly. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for your specific unit. The oven will not heat or function normally until the fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board first, thinking it is at fault. Always test the temperature sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect the wiring harness before replacing the more expensive control board.

## Common Causes

- **Defective oven temperature sensor (~60%)** The sensor probe may have an open or shorted element, sending an out-of-range signal to the control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring or connector (~25%)** Wires can fray, burn, or corrode where they pass through the oven wall or at the connector plug.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** The control board's sensor input circuit may have failed, causing it to misread a good sensor.
- **Poor connector contact (~5%)** Oxidation or looseness at the connector between sensor and board can create intermittent resistance.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** to the oven at the circuit breaker or unplug the unit to make sure safe working conditions.
2. **Remove the oven racks** and locate the temperature sensor probe, usually mounted at the upper rear wall of the oven cavity.
3. **Disconnect the sensor connector** at the control board (you may need to remove the back panel or control panel cover to access it).
4. **Measure the sensor resistance** using a multimeter set to ohms; consult your model's table for the expected value at room temperature (typically 1,000 to 1,200 ohms).
5. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the sensor to the control board for any visible damage, burns, or loose pins at the connector.
6. **Replace the sensor** if resistance is out of range or wiring is damaged; thread the new sensor through the oven wall and secure it with the mounting bracket.
7. **Reconnect the sensor** to the control board, make sure the connector is fully seated, and restore power to test the oven.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working near live electrical connections, if the control board replacement is required, or if repeated sensor and wiring replacements do not clear the F4 error. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board and verify the circuit path. Also call a pro if your oven is still under warranty, as self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-50 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
