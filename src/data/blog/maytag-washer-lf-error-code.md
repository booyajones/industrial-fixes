## What this code means
The LF code (and related displays F8 E1 or Lo FL) stands for Long Fill. Maytag defines it as the control not detecting the correct amount of incoming water within the fill window. In practical terms, your washer is filling too slowly or not at all.

The machine expects water to reach a certain level within a set time. When flow is restricted or absent, the timer runs out and the control throws the LF fault to protect the washer from attempting to wash without enough water.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the water inlet valve first, assuming it has failed. Before buying a valve, disconnect the hoses and check household flow from both wall taps into a bucket. If flow is strong there but the screens at the valve connections are packed with sediment, a five-minute cleaning solves the problem.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens** Sediment and mineral deposits build up in the fine mesh screens where hoses attach to the washer, choking off flow even when household pressure is good.
- **Water supply valves not fully open or weak household pressure** One or both wall shutoffs may be partially closed, or upstream plumbing issues can reduce available flow.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hoses** Hoses pinched behind the washer or internally collapsed from age restrict flow before water ever reaches the inlet valve.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** If screens are clean and supply flow is strong but fill remains slow, the valve solenoids or internal passages may be blocked or electrically failed.
- **Drain hose siphoning** Incorrect drain installation without an air gap can create a siphon that drains water as fast as the machine fills, confusing the control into throwing LF.
- **Pressure sensor or control fault** A stuck pressure switch or faulty control board can misread the actual water level and report a long-fill condition when the tub is actually filling normally.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves at the wall.
2. **Run a bucket test**: disconnect both inlet hoses at the wall connections and turn each supply valve back on briefly over a bucket to verify strong, unrestricted flow from the household plumbing.
3. **Inspect the inlet hoses** for kinks, crushing, or internal blockage, and replace any hose that is cracked, aged, or restricted.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet screens** at the washer's valve connections by gently prying them out with needle-nose pliers, rinsing under water, and scrubbing away sediment with a soft brush and mild soap.
5. **Check drain hose installation**: confirm the drain hose has an air gap at the standpipe and is not taped or sealed, which can cause siphoning.
6. **Reconnect hoses and restore power**, then run a test fill cycle to see if the LF code clears.
7. **If the code persists** after supply, hoses, screens, and drain checks are good, test the water inlet valve for electrical continuity and mechanical operation, and replace the valve assembly if it fails to open or shows no resistance change across its solenoid coils.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified strong household flow, cleaned the inlet screens, confirmed hoses and drain installation are correct, and the LF code still appears. At that point the issue is likely the inlet valve assembly or a pressure-sensing fault that requires multimeter testing and part-specific diagnostics. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with water connections, lack the tools to remove and clean screens safely, or if the washer continues to fault after a valve replacement and you suspect a control board or wiring problem.
