## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E28 Error — What It Means

The E28 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater generally indicates a flame-sensing problem. The unit has attempted to ignite and establish a flame, but the control board is not receiving the expected signal from the flame rod that confirms stable combustion. This safety interlock prevents the heater from operating when it cannot verify that gas is burning properly.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary across Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, always consult your specific unit's installation and service manual or the wiring diagram inside the cover. In many cases the flame rod circuit is at fault, but gas-supply issues, grounding problems, or control-board faults can also trigger the same code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a corroded flame rod or a loose ground wire. Inspect and clean the flame rod and verify electrical grounding before buying any circuit board.

## Common Causes

- **Corroded or dirty flame rod (~50%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame-sensing rod insulates it from the flame and prevents the board from detecting ionization current.
- **Poor electrical grounding (~20%)** A loose ground wire or corroded ground connection prevents the flame-rod circuit from completing properly.
- **Intermittent gas supply (~15%)** Low gas pressure or air in the line can cause the flame to be weak or unstable, so the sensor fails to detect it reliably.
- **Failed flame rod (~10%)** The flame rod itself can crack or corrode internally, breaking the sensing circuit even when the rod looks clean.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The flame-detection circuitry on the main board can fail, misreading a good signal or generating false errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the front cover** to access the burner compartment and locate the flame rod, a small metal probe near the burner.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon deposits, soot, or white corrosion on the tip and along the ceramic insulator.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently using fine-grit emery cloth or steel wool, wiping away all residue without bending the rod.
5. **Check the ground wire** connection at the unit's chassis and at the electrical junction box, and tighten any loose screws.
6. **Reassemble the cover**, restore gas and power, and run a hot-water tap to initiate a call for heat and observe the ignition cycle.
7. **Monitor the display** to confirm the E28 code has cleared and that the burner stays lit for at least two minutes without cycling off.

## When to Call a Pro

Because tankless water heaters involve both natural gas (or propane) combustion and line-voltage electrical circuits, any work beyond simple cleaning and visual inspection should be handled by a licensed plumber or HVAC technician. If cleaning the flame rod and verifying grounding do not clear the E28 error, a professional can safely test gas pressure, measure flame-rod micro-amp current, and replace the control board or other ignition components. Additionally, many jurisdictions require permitted work and a pressure test whenever gas fittings are disturbed, so call a pro if you need to replace the flame rod, gas valve, or burner assembly.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
