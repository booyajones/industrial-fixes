## State Water Heater E175 Error Code — What It Means

E175 on a State tankless water heater indicates an ignition failure fault. The unit attempted to light the burner but could not establish or prove flame during the ignition sequence. State shares the A.O. Smith tankless platform, and this type of code means the control board did not detect combustion after calling for heat. The fault protects the system by locking out operation until the underlying issue is resolved.

[Jump to Fix](#fix)

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screen** Lint, debris, or blockage on the intake screens restricts combustion air and is the most common cause of ignition failure on State tankless units.
- **Low gas supply or pressure** Undersized gas piping, a partially closed valve, or insufficient supply pressure prevents the burner from igniting reliably.
- **Restricted water flow** A dirty inlet filter or low flow rate can inhibit ignition because the unit requires minimum flow to start the heating cycle.
- **Blocked venting or exhaust** Improper vent termination, excessive elbows, or blockage in the exhaust path starves the combustion process of fresh air.
- **Dirty flame rod or sensor** Carbon buildup or contamination on the flame rod prevents the control board from detecting the presence of flame.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve before starting any diagnostic work.
2. Inspect the air intake screens first for lint or debris and clean them thoroughly with a soft brush or vacuum.
3. Check the inlet water filter by shutting off the cold supply, removing the strainer from the inlet connection, and rinsing it under running water.
4. Verify gas supply by confirming the manual gas valve is fully open and that the installation meets the minimum pipe sizing and pressure requirements in your model's manual.
5. Examine the venting and intake piping for blockage, improper termination, or excessive length and correct any restrictions or code violations.
6. Remove and clean the flame rod or flame sensor with fine emery cloth or a soft abrasive pad to remove carbon deposits.
7. Restore power and gas, run a hot-water tap to call for heat, and observe whether the unit ignites and the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the error persists after cleaning the intake screens, inlet filter, and flame rod. Low gas pressure, incorrect vent installation, a faulty gas valve, or control-board issues require test equipment and experience with combustion appliances. Any work involving gas piping, pressure testing, or burner adjustment should be performed by a qualified professional to avoid safety hazards and code violations.
