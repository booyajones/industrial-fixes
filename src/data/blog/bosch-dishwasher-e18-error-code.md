## Bosch Dishwasher E18 Error Code — What It Means

The E18 error code on a Bosch dishwasher indicates a water supply or water inlet flow problem. The machine has detected that too little water is flowing in, or the water level is too low during the fill cycle. This prevents the dishwasher from starting or completing a wash cycle properly.

Bosch's support documentation describes E18 as a water flow issue where the incoming supply is restricted or insufficient. The dishwasher expects approximately 10 liters per minute of flow at the tap when fully open. When flow falls below this threshold due to supply-side restrictions, the control board registers the fault and displays E18.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve inside the dishwasher first. Before spending money on a valve, disconnect the inlet hose at the dishwasher and open the tap into a bucket to verify you get strong flow (about 10 liters per minute). Most E18 faults are upstream of the valve.

## Common Causes

- **Inlet hose kinked or trapped (~35%)** The supply hose between the wall tap and the dishwasher is bent, crushed, or pinched behind the appliance, restricting flow.
- **Blocked inlet filter or screen (~30%)** Sediment, scale, or debris has accumulated in the filter at the hose connection or inside the AquaStop hose assembly, choking off water flow.
- **Low house water pressure or supply flow (~20%)** The incoming plumbing supply delivers less than the required 10 liters per minute due to low pressure, a partly closed shutoff valve, or limescale buildup in the tap.
- **Tap or shutoff valve not fully open (~10%)** The wall shutoff valve or angle stop is only partially open, jammed, or restricted by mineral deposits.
- **Failed water inlet valve (~5%)** The solenoid valve inside the dishwasher that controls water entry is clogged, stuck, or electrically faulty (only after verifying good supply flow).

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and isolate water.** Unplug the dishwasher from the outlet or switch off the circuit breaker. Turn the shutoff valve under the sink or at the wall clockwise to close the water supply completely.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** enough to inspect the inlet hose routing. Look for any kinks, sharp bends, or points where the hose is trapped or crushed between the appliance and the wall. Straighten and reposition the hose if needed.
3. **Disconnect the inlet hose** at the dishwasher connection (usually a threaded nut). Place a towel underneath to catch residual water. Remove the small mesh filter or screen from the threaded fitting at the dishwasher end and inspect it for sediment or scale.
4. **Clean the inlet filter** under running tap water using a small brush or toothbrush to dislodge debris. If the filter is heavily scaled, soak it briefly in white vinegar and rinse. Reinstall the filter securely in the hose fitting.
5. **Test the tap flow.** With the inlet hose still disconnected from the dishwasher, open the shutoff valve fully and let water flow into a bucket for one minute. Measure the volume. You should collect approximately 10 liters. If flow is weak, check the tap itself for blockage or limescale.
6. **Reconnect the inlet hose** to the dishwasher, hand-tighten the fitting, and turn the water supply back on. Check for leaks around the connection.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle.** Plug the dishwasher back in or switch the breaker on. Start a short rinse cycle and observe whether the machine fills normally and the E18 error clears. If the error persists and supply flow was confirmed good, the internal inlet valve or flow sensor likely requires replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified strong water flow at the tap (10 liters per minute), cleaned all accessible filters, straightened the inlet hose, and the E18 error still appears. At that point the fault is inside the machine, typically the inlet valve solenoid or an internal flow sensor. A technician will need to remove panels, test electrical continuity, and replace the valve assembly or sensor. Also call a pro if you discover a leak at the inlet valve inside the cabinet, if the AquaStop system has activated and will not reset, or if you are uncomfortable working with water connections and electrical components together.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-25 in parts (if only cleaning filters), 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call.
