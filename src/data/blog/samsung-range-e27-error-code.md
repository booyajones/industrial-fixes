## Samsung Range E27 Error Code — What It Means

The E27 error code on Samsung ranges typically indicates a communication or electrical fault between the main control board and other components, or a sensor issue. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the range for your specific appliance. The code often appears when the control system detects an unexpected signal or loss of continuity in a circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board when a loose harness connector or corroded wire terminal is actually at fault. Inspect all connectors and measure continuity on sensor circuits before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded harness connector (~40%)** Vibration and heat cycling loosen the multi-pin plugs between the main board and the touch-pad or sensor assemblies, causing intermittent signals.
- **Failed main control board (~30%)** Internal relays or logic chips on the control board fail and misread sensor inputs or lose communication with the user interface.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or RTD probe (~15%)** The oven sensor or cooktop sensor develops an open circuit or drifts out of specification, sending invalid data to the control.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Wires pinched by the oven door hinge or chafed against sheet metal break internally or short together.
- **Touch-pad or user-interface board fault (~5%)** Moisture or a failed membrane switch on the control panel sends spurious commands that the main board interprets as an error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the back panel** or control-access cover using a nut driver or screwdriver to expose the main control board and wiring.
3. **Inspect and reseat every connector** on the main control board, the touch-pad cable, and any plug near the oven or cooktop sensors, looking for corrosion or loose pins.
4. **Measure the oven temperature sensor resistance** using a multimeter set to ohms. Consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance at room temperature. Replace the sensor if the reading is open or far out of range.
5. **Check the wiring harness** along the door hinge and behind the control panel for pinched, chafed, or burned insulation. Repair or replace damaged sections.
6. **Swap the main control board** if all connectors and sensors test good. Transfer each harness plug one at a time to avoid confusion.
7. **Restore power and test** by running a bake cycle or cooktop burner to confirm the error does not return and all functions operate normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet, if the error persists after reseating connectors and replacing the sensor, or if you lack a multimeter to test resistance and continuity. A pro can access factory service mode to read detailed fault logs and verify that the new control board communicates correctly with all subsystems. If the range uses 240-volt direct wiring and you are unsure how to safely disconnect power at the breaker, professional service is the safer choice.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
