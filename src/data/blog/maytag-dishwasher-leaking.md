## Maytag Dishwasher Leaking — What's Happening

A Maytag dishwasher leaking is a symptom, not a single fault. Water can show up at the front door, underneath the machine, or around the sides. Maytag's own guidance points to the door gasket, water supply line, drain hose, filter, detergent type, leveling, and door latch as the common culprits.

The leak location helps narrow the cause. Front-door drips usually mean a gasket or latch problem. Water pooling under the left front corner often traces to the inlet connection. Leaks from the back or right side point to the drain hose or clamp issues.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Door gasket dirty, loose, or damaged** Debris on the seal or a worn, cracked, or improperly seated gasket lets water escape around the door during the cycle.
- **Water supply line loose or cracked** The inlet hose connection at the left front can crack, loosen, or split at the fitting, causing a steady drip or puddle underneath.
- **Drain hose clogged, kinked, or installed wrong** A blocked, damaged, or improperly routed drain line, missing high loop, or loose clamp lets water back up or leak out during the drain cycle.
- **Clogged filter backing up water** A plugged filter at the tub bottom forces water to overflow or leak around seals instead of draining properly.
- **Excess suds from wrong detergent** Using regular dish soap or too much detergent creates foam that pushes out past the door seal or vent.
- **Dishwasher not level or door latch weak** A tilted cabinet or loose latch prevents the door from closing square, leaving a gap for water to escape.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Shut off power at the breaker and close the water supply valve under the sink before any inspection or repair.
2. Identify where water appears: front door, left-front corner underneath, or rear and right side to narrow the failure zone.
3. Inspect the door gasket for food debris, tears, looseness, or gaps, wipe it clean, and press it firmly into the channel all the way around.
4. Remove the toe kick panel and check the water inlet connection at the left front for dampness, tighten the coupling, and replace the supply line if cracked.
5. Pull out the dishwasher enough to trace the drain hose from the pump to the sink or disposal, verify the high loop or air gap is correct, check all clamps, and clear any kinks or blockages.
6. Remove and rinse the filter at the tub floor, reinstall it fully seated, and confirm the spray arm spins freely.
7. Place a level across the front of the dishwasher and adjust the feet until it sits flat side to side.
8. Run a short rinse cycle with the door closed and watch for leaks at each checkpoint to confirm the repair before returning panels.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the leak continues after you have cleaned the gasket, tightened all hose connections, cleared the filter, and verified leveling. Internal pump seals, cracked tub welds, and failed water inlet valves require cabinet removal, multimeter testing, and sometimes soldering or epoxy work that goes beyond typical owner access. If water appears inside the door panel or around electronic components, stop using the machine and get professional help to avoid shock or control-board damage.
