## LG Washer E21 Error Code — What It Means

E21 is not a standard LG error code in LG's published washer error list. LG documents codes like dE and OE for drainage faults, but not E21. When E21 appears on an LG washer, it is most likely a model-specific, regional, or misread code. In practice, when technicians or repair sites use E21, they usually mean the washer is not draining within the expected time or has a drain pump or drain path problem.

LG's official drain troubleshooting pathway focuses on checking the drain hose, drain filter, and whether the drain pump is frozen or otherwise unable to empty the tub. Third-party sources describing E21 on washers consistently describe it as a drainage or pump-out problem caused by a clogged filter, blocked hose, or faulty drain pump.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump immediately when the real problem is lint, coins, or debris blocking the filter or a kinked hose. Clean the filter and inspect the hose routing before buying a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain filter or pump trap (~40%)** Lint, coins, bobby pins, and other debris accumulate in the filter housing and block water flow, preventing the tub from emptying.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~25%)** The hose may be pinched behind the washer, routed too high, or clogged internally, limiting discharge and causing a timeout fault.
- **Debris in the drain pump impeller (~15%)** Small objects or fabric fragments lodge in the pump inlet, outlet, or impeller area and prevent the pump from moving water even when it runs.
- **Failed drain pump motor (~12%)** The pump is electrically energized but the motor is seized, the impeller is broken, or internal windings have failed, so no water is discharged.
- **House drain restriction (~5%)** A clog in the standpipe or sink drain causes water to back up into the washer, making it appear that the washer cannot drain when the appliance is functioning normally.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~3%)** Less commonly, electrical connections to the pump are loose or corroded, or the control board fails to command the pump on, leading to a drain timeout.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves.
2. **Locate the drain filter access panel** at the front lower corner of the washer and open it.
3. **Place a shallow pan or towels** on the floor to catch residual water, then slowly unscrew the drain filter cap counterclockwise.
4. **Remove the filter** and clean out all lint, coins, hairpins, and debris from the filter and the housing cavity, then rinse the filter under running water.
5. **Inspect the drain hose** at the rear of the washer for kinks, pinching, or incorrect routing, and straighten or reposition it so water can flow freely to the standpipe or sink.
6. **Check the household drain** by pouring a bucket of water into the standpipe or sink to confirm it drains quickly without backup.
7. **If the filter and hose are clear but the error persists**, access the drain pump (usually by tipping the washer back or removing a lower access panel), remove the pump inlet and outlet hoses, and inspect the impeller for debris or damage.
8. **Test the pump electrically** if your model allows, or replace the pump if it is seized, broken, or does not run when powered, then reassemble all connections.
9. **Reinstall the filter**, close the access panel, plug in the washer, and run a drain/spin or rinse/spin cycle to verify the tub empties completely and the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the filter, cleared the hose, and inspected the pump but the washer still does not drain, or if you are uncomfortable accessing the pump assembly or working with electrical connections. A technician can verify pump voltage and resistance with a meter, diagnose control board faults, and replace the pump or wiring harness safely. Also call a pro if the error returns repeatedly after initial repairs, suggesting an intermittent electrical fault or a problem with the pressure sensor or water-level switch that requires service-manual diagnostics.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
