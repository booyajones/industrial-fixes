## Navien Tankless Water Heater E331 Error — What It Means

The E331 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates the unit has detected a flame failure or ignition issue during operation. This means the burner either failed to ignite when called for hot water or the flame was lost unexpectedly after ignition. The control board monitors flame presence through a flame rod sensor and will shut down the unit and display this code when it cannot confirm stable combustion.

Because this involves gas combustion and safety interlocks, the error protects against incomplete combustion or gas leaks. The fault may be intermittent or persistent depending on the underlying cause. Check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for model-specific diagnostic details, as the exact threshold and detection logic can vary between Navien models.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the gas valve or control board when the real problem is a flame rod coated with carbon buildup. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper or steel wool first before ordering expensive components.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply interruption or low pressure (~35%)** Insufficient gas flow from a partially closed valve, regulator issue, or empty propane tank prevents reliable ignition or sustains flame loss.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~30%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame sensing rod block the signal to the control board, causing false flame-out detection.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Obstructed exhaust or intake pipes create negative pressure or oxygen starvation that extinguishes the flame during operation.
- **Faulty igniter or weak spark (~10%)** A worn igniter or poor grounding connection fails to generate a strong enough spark to light the gas reliably.
- **Gas valve malfunction (~7%)** A failing gas valve may open inconsistently or close prematurely, cutting fuel to the burner and triggering the flame-loss fault.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~3%)** Loose flame-rod connections or a defective control board may misread flame signals or fail to command proper ignition sequences.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners to access the burner assembly and flame rod.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon buildup, corrosion, or physical damage; gently clean the rod with fine sandpaper or steel wool if deposits are present.
4. **Check all wiring connections** to the flame rod, igniter, and gas valve for looseness or corrosion; reseat any suspect connectors.
5. **Verify that the exhaust and intake pipes** are clear of obstructions, nests, or condensation ice, and that all joints are sealed and properly sloped.
6. **Restore gas and power** and initiate a hot-water call; observe the ignition sequence and listen for the burner to light and remain stable.
7. **If the error persists** after cleaning and checks, measure gas inlet pressure with a manometer (consult your model's table for the correct range) or call a licensed technician to test the igniter, gas valve, and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas-appliance technician if you are not comfortable working around gas connections, if gas odors are present, or if the error returns after cleaning the flame rod and verifying open gas supply. Professionals have manometers to measure inlet and manifold gas pressure, combustion analyzers to check air-fuel ratios, and the training to safely test the gas valve and control board. Because flame-failure codes involve combustion safety, any uncertainty about venting, gas flow, or electrical diagnostics warrants expert help to prevent carbon monoxide hazards or equipment damage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
