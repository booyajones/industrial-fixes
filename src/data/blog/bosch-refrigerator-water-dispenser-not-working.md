## Bosch Refrigerator Water Dispenser Not Working — What's Happening

A Bosch refrigerator water dispenser that stops working is not tied to a single fault code. Instead, it is a symptom that points to a blockage or failure somewhere in the water delivery chain. The path runs from your house supply through a shutoff valve, a supply line, an inlet valve, a filter, and finally to the dispenser reservoir and tube. Any restriction or electrical failure in that sequence will stop water flow at the dispenser paddle.

Bosch service data shows the most common field failures are insufficient supply pressure, a clogged or incorrectly seated water filter, a frozen reservoir tube, a kinked supply line, or a failed inlet valve. In some cases, a faulty door switch or dispenser switch prevents the control board from energizing the water circuit. Diagnosis starts at the house supply and works forward through each stage.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged or overdue water filter** Bosch recommends replacing the filter every six months, and a clogged or improperly seated filter will restrict flow and stop dispensing.
- **Low house water pressure** The inlet valve requires at least 20 psi to open correctly, and low pressure is a common cause of no-dispense complaints.
- **Frozen water line or reservoir tube** Ice buildup in the supply tube or reservoir will block flow, often caused by low pressure or a weak inlet valve letting water drip and freeze.
- **Kinked or damaged supply line** A pinched or kinked supply line behind the refrigerator reduces or stops water delivery to the inlet valve.
- **Closed or partially closed house shutoff valve** If the shutoff valve is not fully open, the refrigerator will not receive adequate flow to fill the dispenser system.
- **Defective water inlet valve** A failed solenoid coil or stuck inlet valve will not open to let water through even when supply pressure is good.
- **Faulty dispenser switch or door switch** A broken dispenser paddle switch or door-ajar switch can prevent the control board from signaling the inlet valve to open.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Shut off power at the breaker and close the house water shutoff valve before starting any work.
2. Verify the house shutoff valve is fully open and inspect the supply line behind the refrigerator for kinks, pinches, or damage.
3. Disconnect the supply line at the inlet valve and direct it into a bucket, then open the shutoff valve to test flow. A weak stream points to low pressure or a restricted supply line upstream.
4. Check house water pressure with a gauge. The inlet valve needs at least 20 psi to function correctly.
5. Replace the water filter if it is older than six months or suspect, and confirm the new filter is seated correctly in its housing.
6. Hold the dispenser paddle down for up to three minutes to purge air from the reservoir and refill the system after filter replacement.
7. Inspect the water tube and reservoir in the freezer compartment for ice blockage. If frozen, thaw carefully with warm air and investigate the underlying cause, usually low pressure or a leaking inlet valve.
8. Test the inlet valve solenoid coils for continuity with a multimeter. Replace the valve assembly if any coil is open or shows no continuity.
9. Check the dispenser paddle switch and door switch for continuity and proper operation. Replace any switch that fails the test.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are not comfortable working with water connections under pressure, if testing the inlet valve and switches does not reveal a clear fault, or if the control board may be involved. A technician can also troubleshoot intermittent electrical faults in the dispenser circuit and verify correct pressure at the valve inlet. If the freezer compartment must be disassembled to reach the reservoir tube or if the problem recurs after basic repairs, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent repeat service calls.
