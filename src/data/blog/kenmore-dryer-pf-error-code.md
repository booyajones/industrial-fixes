## What this code means
A PF code on a Kenmore dryer stands for Power Failure. The dryer detected that electrical power was interrupted during the cycle or at power-up, and the control stored that event in the display. On Whirlpool-built Kenmore dryers, the usual immediate action is to touch and hold START to resume the cycle or press POWER to clear the display.

PF is not, by itself, a specific sensor fault. It is usually a control response to lost power, line noise, or an interrupted start-up sequence rather than a single failed laundry component. The code can appear after a recent outage, breaker trip, loose plug, damaged power cord, or even if keys were pressed too early after power-up on some models. If the code recurs after stable power is verified, a failing electronic control or control-board power-supply section becomes a stronger suspect.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when PF appears, but most cases are resolved by checking the house breaker, outlet, and dryer power cord for loose connections or heat damage first.

## Common Causes

- **Recent power outage or breaker trip (~40%)** A brief house power interruption or tripped circuit breaker causes the dryer to store a PF code in the display.
- **Loose or damaged power cord or plug (~30%)** Intermittent contact at the dryer plug, wall receptacle, or cord terminals creates momentary power loss that triggers PF.
- **Poor wall receptacle contact (~15%)** A worn, burned, or loose outlet can cause voltage drops or dropouts that the control interprets as power failure.
- **Failing electronic control board (~10%)** If PF recurs after supply-side checks and power is stable, the main control board power-supply section may be failing.
- **Keys pressed too early after power-up (~5%)** On some models, pressing buttons before the control finishes its startup delay can trigger a PF code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the code.** Press and hold START to resume the cycle, or press POWER (or CANCEL) to clear the display. Run a short test cycle to see if the dryer operates normally.
2. **Inspect the house breaker and wall outlet.** Look for a tripped breaker, loose outlet, discoloration, or burn marks. Reset any tripped breaker and tighten outlet screws if loose.
3. **Check the dryer power cord and plug.** Unplug the dryer and examine the plug terminals, cord jacket, and connection at the dryer terminal block for looseness, heat damage, or corrosion. Reconnect or replace as needed.
4. **Perform a power reset.** Turn the laundry circuit breaker off for one minute, then restore power. This clears stored control power and allows the control to reinitialize.
5. **Run a short test cycle and monitor.** Start a timed dry cycle and watch for the PF code to return. If the dryer runs without interruption, the issue was transient supply-side.
6. **Inspect internal wiring (if PF recurs and power is stable).** Disconnect power at the breaker. Remove the dryer cabinet or back panel and check wiring harnesses, connectors, and solder joints on the control board for intermittent connection.
7. **Replace the failed component.** If the cord, outlet, or breaker shows damage, replace it. If all supply-side checks pass and PF is intermittent, replace the main electronic control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside a live electrical panel, if you see sparking or heavy burn marks at the outlet or breaker, or if voltage testing is required under load. Also call a pro if the PF code recurs after you have verified stable house power and inspected the cord and outlet, since diagnosis may require control-board voltage measurement and internal harness inspection with the cabinet removed. If the dryer shares a circuit with other heavy loads and you suspect undersized wiring or breaker nuisance tripping, an electrician should evaluate the branch circuit and upgrade it if necessary.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in parts (cord or outlet), 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 (depending on whether cord, outlet, or control board is replaced).
