## GE Dishwasher F9 Error Code — What It Means

GE's published fault code tables do not list an F9 code for dishwashers. The standard GE drain-related codes are C3 (will not drain) and C1 (pump-out exceeded 2 minutes). If your GE dishwasher is showing what looks like F9, you are likely dealing with a misread display, a model-specific code not in the general literature, or confusion with another brand's fault system. The safest approach is to treat the complaint as a drain system problem. Most drain faults involve a clogged hose, blocked air gap, or obstructed garbage disposer connection. A small number trace to a failed drain pump or control board issue.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged or kinked drain hose** Food debris, grease buildup, or a bent hose prevents water from leaving the tub within the control's timeout window.
- **Blocked air gap** If your sink has an air gap fitting, mineral deposits or food particles can seal the vent and stop drainage completely.
- **Garbage disposer knockout plug still installed** A new disposer ships with a plastic knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet that must be removed before connecting the drain line.
- **Obstructed or failed drain pump** Broken glass, plastic labels, or pump-motor failure will stop the machine from moving water out of the sump.
- **Transient control glitch** Voltage sag or a stuck relay can log a false fault that clears after a full power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the exact code and model number** by checking the display again and noting the full model tag inside the door. GE's published code list does not show a generic F9 dishwasher fault, so verifying what the machine actually reports is the first task.
2. **Power-reset the dishwasher** by switching off the circuit breaker or unplugging the unit for 30 seconds, then restore power and retry a cycle. Many transient faults clear after a hard reset.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** from the dishwasher sump to the sink or disposer connection. Straighten any kinks, look inside the hose for blockages, and confirm the hose is routed with a high loop or air gap per installation instructions.
4. **Check the air gap** (if installed) by removing the chrome cap at the sink and clearing any debris from the internal float and ports.
5. **Verify the garbage disposer connection** by disconnecting the drain hose at the disposer inlet and confirming the knockout plug has been removed. Run the disposer for 15 seconds to clear any blockage on the sink side.
6. **Inspect the drain pump and sump area** by removing the lower dish rack and filter assembly. Look for broken glass, plastic, or other debris around the pump impeller and clear anything you find.
7. **Replace the drain pump or control board** if the entire drain path is clear, the machine still will not pump out, and you measure no continuity or correct resistance across the pump terminals. Consult your model's service sheet for component test values, since GE does not publish a universal drain-pump resistance spec in general literature.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you cannot locate the exact fault code in your model's service literature, if the drain pump tests within specification but the machine still logs a drain fault, or if you are uncomfortable working with 120-volt wiring and water at the same time. A qualified service provider can run the built-in diagnostic mode, measure pump current draw, and confirm whether the issue is mechanical or electronic. Because GE does not publish F9 as a standard dishwasher code, professional help is especially useful for verifying what the control is actually reporting and whether a board reflash or replacement is required.
