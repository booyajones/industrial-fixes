## Trane Furnace E05 Error Code — What It Means

The E05 error code on many Trane furnaces indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition. The control board is reporting that it attempted to light the burner but either did not detect a flame after ignition, or sensed an unstable or intermittent flame signal. This safety feature shuts down the furnace to prevent unburned gas from accumulating.

Because exact meanings can vary by model and year, always consult your furnace's owner's manual or wiring diagram to confirm the definition of E05 for your specific unit. In general, flame-sensing codes point to dirty flame sensors, faulty ignition components, or gas-supply issues.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor. Clean the sensor with fine steel wool or sandpaper before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty flame sensor (~55%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame sensor rod prevents it from detecting the flame current, causing the board to shut down the burner.
- **Weak or cracked igniter (~20%)** A failing hot-surface igniter may glow but not reach the temperature needed to reliably light the gas, or it may crack and fail intermittently.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~10%)** A partly closed manual valve, a clogged gas line, or low pressure from the utility can starve the burner and prevent ignition.
- **Faulty gas valve (~8%)** A stuck or weak gas valve may not open fully or may open inconsistently, resulting in no flame or a weak flame that the sensor cannot detect.
- **Corroded or loose flame-sensor wiring (~5%)** A poor electrical connection at the flame sensor terminal or control board reduces the flame signal and triggers a false fault.
- **Failed control board (~2%)** The integrated furnace control board may have a defective flame-sensing circuit or relay that incorrectly reports a flame fault.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the igniter glow bright orange for several seconds before the code appears?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The igniter is working. The problem is likely a dirty flame sensor or gas-supply issue. Clean the sensor rod and verify the gas valve is open.<br><strong>No:</strong> The igniter is weak or failed. Inspect it for cracks and test it with a multimeter, or replace it if it does not glow at all.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After cleaning the flame sensor, does the furnace run for a few cycles before the E05 returns?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The sensor is marginal or the burner is running too rich, depositing soot quickly. Check the air-to-fuel mixture and consider replacing the sensor.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor is likely still dirty, wired poorly, or the flame is not present. Recheck sensor mounting, wiring, and gas flow.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you hear the gas valve click open when the igniter glows?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Gas is reaching the burner. If no flame appears, check for blockages in the burner orifices or a very low gas pressure.<br><strong>No:</strong> The gas valve may be faulty, the wiring may be loose, or the control board is not sending the valve signal. Test valve coil resistance and connections.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the furnace at the service disconnect or breaker, then switch the thermostat to Off and close the manual gas shutoff valve if you will be removing any gas components.
2. **Remove the blower-compartment door** and locate the flame sensor, a thin metal rod extending into the burner assembly, usually held by a single screw or bracket.
3. **Unplug or unscrew the flame sensor** from its mounting bracket and gently pull the sensor wire connector free from the rod or terminal.
4. **Clean the sensor rod** with fine steel wool, emery cloth, or a soft abrasive pad until the metal is shiny, taking care not to bend or damage the rod.
5. **Reinstall the sensor** in its bracket, reconnect the wire, and make sure the rod sits in the flame path without touching any metal except the mounting bracket.
6. **Restore gas and power**, then set the thermostat to call for heat and observe the ignition sequence through the sight glass or by opening the burner door briefly.
7. **Monitor the furnace** through several cycles to confirm the E05 does not return, and check that the flame is steady and blue with no yellow tips or excessive soot.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if cleaning the flame sensor and checking the igniter do not resolve the E05 code, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a failed gas valve. Professionals have combustion analyzers to measure air-fuel ratios, manometers to check gas pressure, and the training to safely diagnose control-board and valve faults. Also call a pro if you smell gas at any time, or if the furnace cycles on the error code repeatedly after you have confirmed the sensor is clean and the igniter glows properly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
