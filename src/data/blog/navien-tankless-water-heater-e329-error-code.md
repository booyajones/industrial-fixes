## Navien E329 Error Code — What It Means

The E329 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a communication failure between the main control board and one of the internal components, typically related to the flame rod, ignition system, or sensor circuits. The exact definition can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front cover for the precise meaning on your unit.

This code usually appears when the control board cannot send or receive signals properly due to loose connections, corroded terminals, or a failed component. In many cases the problem is a poor electrical connection rather than a failed part, so checking all wiring harnesses and connectors is the first step.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real issue is a loose or corroded connector on a sensor or igniter harness. Inspect and reseat all visible wiring connectors before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~45%)** Vibration, heat, and humidity can loosen or corrode the push-on connectors between the control board and sensors or igniters, interrupting communication.
- **Failed flame rod or sensor (~25%)** A flame rod, flow sensor, or temperature sensor that has failed internally or developed a short will prevent the control board from reading valid signals.
- **Control board fault (~15%)** The main PCB can develop a failed communication port or internal circuit due to power surges, moisture, or component age.
- **Damaged wire harness (~10%)** Wires that are pinched, chafed, or have broken strands inside the insulation can cause intermittent or total loss of signal.
- **Grounding issue (~5%)** Inadequate grounding or a loose ground wire can create electrical noise that disrupts digital communication between components.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** at the circuit breaker and the manual gas shutoff valve to make sure safe access to internal components.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners (typically on the sides and bottom) and lifting the panel away to expose the control board and wiring.
3. **Inspect all wiring connectors** on the control board, sensors, and igniters. Look for loose plugs, corrosion (green or white deposits), burn marks, or pushed-out pins.
4. **Reseat each connector** by pulling it straight off and then pushing it firmly back on until you feel or hear a click. Pay special attention to the flame rod, flow sensor, and thermistor connectors.
5. **Check for damaged wires** by gently flexing the harness near connectors and looking for cracks in the insulation, pinch points, or strands that have separated.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then press the reset button or power cycle the unit by turning the breaker off for 60 seconds and back on.
7. **Monitor the display** as the heater runs through its startup sequence and attempts to ignite. If the E329 code reappears, consult the wiring diagram in the manual to identify which specific component is on the communication circuit for that code, then test or replace that part.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot identify which component corresponds to the E329 code on your specific model, or if reseating connectors and inspecting wiring does not resolve the error. A pro has model-specific service manuals, a multimeter to test sensor resistance values, and the training to safely diagnose control board faults. Also call a pro if you see evidence of water intrusion inside the unit, repeated error codes after parts replacement, or if the unit is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
