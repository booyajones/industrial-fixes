## Whirlpool Refrigerator Freezing Food — What's Happening

A Whirlpool refrigerator freezing food in the fresh-food compartment means cold air from the freezer is being delivered too aggressively or the control system is calling for cooling longer than it should. This is not a fault code, but a symptom of an overcooling condition. On side-by-side models especially, a freezer set too cold can make the refrigerator section too cold because air flows between the compartments. The control board, sensors, and airflow path all work together to maintain the right temperature, and a fault in any part of that system can push the fresh-food section below safe storage range.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Temperature set too cold** The refrigerator or freezer dial is set lower than factory recommendation, causing excessive cooling in the fresh-food section.
- **Blocked air vents** Food or containers placed directly against the cold-air vents or duct openings obstruct proper airflow and direct freezing air onto items.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermistor** The sensor reads temperature incorrectly and tells the control board to keep cooling even when the compartment is already too cold.
- **Leaky or worn door gasket** A torn, warped, or loose door seal lets warm air in, causing the compressor to run longer and overcool the compartment.
- **Damper stuck open** The air damper that regulates cold-air flow from freezer to refrigerator fails in the open position, allowing continuous cold air into the fresh-food section.
- **Defective control board or user interface** The main control sends continuous cooling demand to the compressor and fans, ignoring actual compartment temperature.
- **Ice maker misconfigured or left on** On some models an active ice maker or incorrect ice-maker settings can contribute to overcooling in the refrigerator compartment.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Place a refrigerator thermometer in a glass of water in the fresh-food section and let it sit for 24 hours to measure actual temperature instead of relying on the display.
2. Check the temperature settings and adjust the refrigerator to 37°F and the freezer to 0°F, then wait another 24 hours and re-measure.
3. Inspect all air vents inside the refrigerator compartment and remove any food, shelves, or containers blocking the cold-air path or sensor area.
4. Perform a door-seal test by closing the door on a strip of paper at multiple points around the perimeter and tugging gently. If the paper pulls out easily or you see light gaps, the gasket is not sealing.
5. Locate the temperature sensor or thermistor (usually near the air vent or on the rear wall) and disconnect it. Use a multimeter to check its resistance against the manufacturer's chart for your model, or compare measured compartment temperature to the setpoint after 24 hours. Replace the sensor if it reads more than about 2°F off target.
6. Check the air damper assembly if your model uses one. Open the refrigerator and listen or look for the damper opening and closing during a cooling cycle. If it stays open or does not move, replace the damper.
7. Power-cycle the refrigerator by unplugging for two minutes, then plug back in and monitor for 24 hours. If the problem persists, inspect the main control board for signs of burn marks or test compressor and fan relay outputs with a multimeter.
8. If all settings, seals, sensors, and damper check out but the refrigerator still freezes food, inspect the evaporator for abnormal frost patterns or airflow restrictions that may indicate a sealed-system or defrost fault requiring advanced diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable testing electrical components with a multimeter, if the control board or sealed system requires diagnosis, or if the problem continues after you have corrected settings, cleared vents, replaced the door seal, and verified the sensor reads correctly. Refrigerant and sealed-system work requires EPA certification and specialized equipment. A qualified appliance technician can perform pressure tests, evaporator inspections, and board-level diagnostics safely and in line with warranty requirements.
