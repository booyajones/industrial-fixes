## Electrolux Washer EF2 Error Code — What It Means

EF2 is an excess foam or suds warning on Electrolux washers. The control board detects too much detergent foam, usually while the machine is trying to drain. Electrolux support lists EF2 as a warning code most often caused by a blocked drain pipe or a blocked and dirty filter. Independent repair guidance describes EF2 as excess detergent dosing or excessive foam during draining.

The code appears when suds cannot clear normally from the drum. This happens either because you have used too much detergent (creating more foam than the drain system can handle) or because a restriction in the drain path (clogged filter, kinked hose) prevents suds and water from leaving the tub. The machine stops the cycle to protect the pump and prevent overflow.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the drain pump when the real problem is simply a clogged drain filter or too much detergent. Before ordering any parts, clean the filter at the lower front access panel and verify you are using HE detergent in the correct amount.

## Common Causes

- **Too much detergent or non-HE detergent (~50%)** Overdosing detergent or using regular detergent instead of high-efficiency (HE) detergent creates excessive foam that the washer cannot drain quickly enough, triggering the EF2 warning.
- **Clogged or dirty drain filter (~30%)** Lint, coins, and debris collect in the drain filter at the lower front of the machine and restrict water flow, allowing suds to build up in the drum during the drain cycle.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~15%)** A drain hose that is kinked, crushed behind the washer, or clogged with lint slows drainage and prevents foam from clearing, causing the control to register excess suds.
- **Improper drain hose installation (~5%)** If the drain hose is inserted too far into the standpipe or routed incorrectly, it can create a siphon or air-lock that traps suds in the tub.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Stop the cycle and allow foam to settle.** Press the Pause or Cancel button and wait 15-20 minutes for suds to break down naturally. Do not open the door until the drum unlocks.
2. **Verify detergent type and amount.** Check your detergent bottle to confirm it is labeled HE (high efficiency). Consult your washer's manual for the correct dosing and reduce the amount by half if you have been using more than recommended.
3. **Clean the drain filter.** Locate the small access door at the lower front left or right of the washer. Place a shallow pan or towels underneath, twist the filter cap counterclockwise, and pull it out. Remove lint, coins, and debris. Rinse the filter under running water and reinstall it by turning clockwise until snug.
4. **Inspect the drain hose.** Pull the washer away from the wall and examine the drain hose for kinks, crushing, or blockage. Straighten any kinks. If the hose feels clogged, disconnect it from the back of the washer and the standpipe, then flush it with water or replace it.
5. **Check drain hose installation.** Confirm the drain hose is inserted no more than 6-8 inches into the standpipe and that the end of the hose is above the water level in the tub to prevent siphoning. Secure it with a tie or bracket if it has shifted.
6. **Run a rinse-and-spin cycle.** With the filter and hose clean and no detergent in the dispenser, start a rinse cycle to verify the machine drains completely and the code does not return.
7. **Monitor future washes.** Use only HE detergent in the amount recommended for your load size and water hardness. Clean the drain filter every 2-3 months to prevent future blockages.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the EF2 code returns after you have cleaned the drain filter, cleared the drain hose, and verified you are using the correct type and amount of detergent. Persistent codes may indicate a failing drain pump, a faulty pressure sensor, or a control board issue that requires diagnostic tools and component-level testing. Also call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if the washer shows additional error codes or unusual behavior during the drain cycle.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts if drain filter or hose needs replacement, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 for a service call if component replacement is needed.
