## What this code means
F23 on a Whirlpool dryer signals an exhaust thermistor fault in the temperature-sensing circuit. The thermistor monitors exhaust air temperature at the blower housing. When the control board loses signal or sees an out-of-range reading from that sensor, it halts the cycle and displays F23. Whirlpool's official troubleshooting starts with a full power reset and a timed dry cycle to confirm whether the fault is persistent or a one-time glitch.

This code appears most often on Duet and similar platforms. It points to a problem in the outlet or exhaust thermistor, its wiring harness, or the control board connections. The dryer will not run normally until the temperature sensor circuit is restored. Restricted venting and lint buildup can contribute to repeat failures by stressing the thermistor, but the F23 code itself indicates an electrical or sensor fault rather than a mechanical blockage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Measure the exhaust thermistor resistance (around 10–11 kΩ at room temperature) and inspect the connector and harness for damage before spending money on electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed exhaust/outlet thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor at the blower housing reads open, shorted, or outside the normal range and the control board interprets this as a fault.
- **Loose or corroded thermistor connector (~25%)** Vibration or heat cycles cause the plug at the thermistor or along the harness to lose contact, sending intermittent or no signal to the board.
- **Damaged thermistor wiring harness (~15%)** Worn insulation, pinched wires, or unintended continuity to ground in the red thermistor leads interrupt the signal path.
- **Control board or board connector fault (~8%)** If the thermistor and harness test good, a failing control board or poor solder joint at the board connector can still generate F23.
- **Restricted venting or lint buildup (~2%)** Heavy lint at the blower or a kinked exhaust hose creates high-temperature conditions that stress or damage the thermistor over time, leading to failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** for a full five minutes to reset the control board, then restore power and start a timed dry cycle to see if F23 returns.
2. **Unplug the dryer** and remove the lower or front access panel to reach the blower housing and exhaust area where the outlet thermistor is mounted.
3. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** and use a multimeter set to resistance (Ω) to measure across the thermistor terminals; a healthy sensor reads around 10–11 kΩ at room temperature.
4. **Inspect the thermistor wiring harness** for cuts, frayed insulation, or continuity to ground; check both the red leads and the connector terminals for corrosion or bent pins.
5. **Replace the outlet/exhaust thermistor** if it reads open, shorted, or outside the normal range, and make sure the new sensor snaps or screws securely into the blower housing.
6. **Reassemble the panels**, restore power, and run a full timed dry cycle to confirm the F23 code does not return.
7. **If the code persists** after thermistor and harness replacement, inspect the control board connectors for loose or corroded pins and consider control board replacement or professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if the thermistor and harness both test good but the F23 code remains, or if you suspect the control board itself is faulty. A technician has the meters and experience to trace intermittent faults in the control-board circuit and to verify that all high-temperature safety components are functioning correctly. Also call if the dryer shares a 240 V circuit with other appliances and you are unsure how to safely power it down, or if you discover scorched wiring or burning odors that suggest a more serious electrical problem.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
