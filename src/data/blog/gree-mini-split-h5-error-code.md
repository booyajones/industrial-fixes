## Gree Mini Split H5 Error Code — What It Means

H5 on a Gree mini split signals that the intelligent power module (IPM) on the outdoor unit has entered protection mode. The IPM is the power electronics section that drives the inverter compressor, and it shuts down when it detects over-current, under- or over-voltage, or other conditions that could damage the compressor or the board itself. In practice, this means the outdoor inverter board has stopped commanding the compressor because something pushed the system outside safe operating limits.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted airflow** Dirty indoor filters, evaporator coil, outdoor coil, or blower wheel force the system into abnormal load and temperature conditions that trip IPM protection.
- **Outdoor fan or blower fault** A jammed, slow-running, or failed outdoor fan motor or capacitor reduces heat rejection and can push current or temperature beyond safe limits.
- **Wiring or connection error** Loose terminals, incorrect harness placement, or board-to-compressor wiring mistakes after service work can trigger the IPM protection.
- **Compressor electrical fault** Unequal winding resistance or winding-to-ground leakage in the compressor windings will cause the IPM to detect abnormal current and shut down.
- **Failed IPM or poor thermal contact** The IPM itself may have failed, or loose mounting and insufficient thermal transfer to the heatsink can cause the module to overheat and trip.
- **Refrigerant or system load issue** Charge problems, restrictions, or pressure abnormalities can create operating conditions that the inverter interprets as a protection event.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the unit** and confirm the H5 code returns or clears to determine whether the fault is latched or intermittent.
2. **Clean the indoor filter, evaporator coil, and outdoor coil** thoroughly, and inspect the blower wheel for dust buildup. Verify the outdoor unit has adequate clearance and the fan spins freely and runs at normal speed.
3. **Check the outdoor unit wiring and board connections.** Verify all terminals are tight, all harness plugs are seated in the correct position, and no wires were moved during prior cleaning or service.
4. **Test the compressor electrically** by disconnecting the compressor leads and measuring phase-to-phase resistance across U-V, U-W, and V-W. Resistance should be equal across all three pairs. Then measure each winding to ground. You should see infinite resistance with no leakage.
5. **Verify incoming voltage and system pressures.** Confirm the supply voltage is within specification and compare standing pressure to outdoor temperature to rule out charge or refrigerant problems.
6. **Inspect the IPM and heatsink interface.** Remove the cover and verify the module is mounted securely with no air gap and that thermal transfer is intact. Look for signs of overheating or loose fasteners.
7. **Replace the failed component** once testing isolates the fault. Common end-points are the IPM, outdoor PCB, compressor, or outdoor fan motor and capacitor, depending on which test revealed the problem.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line voltage, performing compressor winding tests, or interpreting inverter board diagnostics. If cleaning the coils and verifying wiring does not clear the H5 code, the fault is likely in the compressor, IPM, or outdoor board, and those repairs require refrigerant handling, specialized tools, and electrical diagnostics that are outside typical homeowner scope. A pro can also verify refrigerant charge and system pressures to rule out load-related causes.
