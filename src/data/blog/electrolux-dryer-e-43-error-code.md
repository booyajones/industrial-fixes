## Electrolux Dryer E43 Error Code — What It Means

The E43 error code on Electrolux dryers does not have a single universal meaning across all models. Different Electrolux dryer platforms assign different fault definitions to this code. Some models use E43 to indicate a sensor circuit problem, while others may assign it to a control communication issue or a component mismatch. Without documentation specific to your exact model number, it is not safe to assume a particular fault.

The best first step is to consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer cabinet to confirm what E43 means for your unit. General troubleshooting includes checking for loose connectors, damaged wiring harnesses, and verifying that sensors and control boards are seated properly. Power cycling the dryer by unplugging it for a few minutes can sometimes clear transient faults.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board without first checking wiring connectors and sensor continuity. Inspect all harness plugs and test sensor resistance with a multimeter before ordering expensive boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** Vibration and heat can work connectors loose over time, breaking signal paths between sensors and the control board.
- **Faulty thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** A sensor with an open circuit or out-of-range resistance will return invalid readings that trigger many error codes.
- **Main control board glitch or component failure (~20%)** Control boards can develop solder cracks or failed relay contacts that generate spurious fault codes.
- **Damaged wire harness (~15%)** Wires chafed by the drum or pinched during service can short or break, interrupting communication between components.
- **Incompatible or mismatched control component (~5%)** If a part was recently replaced, using a component not designed for your exact model can produce code mismatches.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker, then wait five minutes to allow capacitors to discharge and the control to reset.
2. **Remove the top or rear access panel** using a screwdriver or nut driver to expose the control board, wiring harnesses, and sensor locations.
3. **Inspect all connectors** on the main control board, user interface board, and sensors for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks, then unplug and firmly reseat each one.
4. **Locate the thermistor or temperature sensor** (typically mounted near the blower housing or on the heater duct) and disconnect it, then use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance and compare to the range in your service manual.
5. **Check the wiring harness** for visible damage, pinch points, or chafing where wires pass through metal edges or around the drum, and repair or replace any damaged sections with high-temperature wire and heat-shrink tubing.
6. **Test the main control board** by inspecting solder joints under magnification for cracks and checking relay contacts for carbon buildup; if the board shows physical damage or if all other components test good, replace the control board with the exact part number for your model.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to verify the error has cleared, monitoring the display and listening for normal operation through the dry cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if systematic testing of sensors and wiring does not reveal an obvious fault. A technician has access to factory service manuals with exact resistance specifications and fault-code tables for your model, and can use specialized diagnostic software to read additional fault history stored in the control board. Also call a pro if the control board shows signs of electrical arcing or if the error persists after you have replaced a sensor and verified all wiring integrity, as the issue may involve multiple interacting faults that require experience to diagnose efficiently.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-150 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-280.
