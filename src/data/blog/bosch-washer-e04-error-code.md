## Bosch Washer E04 Error Code — What It Means

The E04 error code on Bosch washing machines typically relates to a drain or water evacuation issue. The machine has detected that water is not leaving the drum as expected within the allotted time, which triggers the error and halts the cycle. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the machine's wiring diagram if available.

This error is designed to protect the washer from overflow or water damage. Most often it points to a blockage in the drain path rather than a failed component. The machine's pressure sensor or drain pump may also be involved, but clogs and kinks are the usual culprits.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump when the real problem is a clog in the filter, coin trap, or hose. Always clear the filter and inspect the hose for obstructions before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain filter or coin trap (~40%)** Lint, coins, hair, and small debris collect in the filter housing and block water flow, preventing the machine from draining.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~20%)** The flexible drain hose can kink behind the machine or become clogged with detergent residue and debris, stopping water from exiting.
- **Faulty drain pump (~15%)** The pump motor or impeller may fail or jam, preventing it from expelling water even when the hose and filter are clear.
- **Pressure switch or sensor malfunction (~15%)** The water-level sensor or its air-trap hose may be clogged or damaged, sending false signals that the drum has not drained.
- **Drain standpipe too low or blocked (~7%)** If the household standpipe is lower than the machine's outlet or is clogged, water may siphon back or fail to drain properly.
- **Control board error (~3%)** Rarely, the main control board may misread drain signals or fail, triggering E04 even when the drain system is functioning.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and unplug** the washer, then move it forward enough to access the rear panel and drain hose.
2. **Open the small access door** at the lower front of the machine and place a shallow pan or towels underneath to catch residual water.
3. **Unscrew and remove the drain filter** (turn counterclockwise), pull out any debris, and rinse the filter and housing under running water.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** at the back of the machine for kinks, sags, or clogs. Detach the hose from the standpipe and flush it with water if needed.
5. **Check the drain pump** by removing the lower front panel or rear access panel (consult your model's service manual for screw locations). Look for objects jamming the impeller and spin it by hand to confirm it turns freely.
6. **Test the pressure switch** air hose (a small rubber tube running from the tub to the switch). Remove it and blow through to confirm it is clear, then reconnect.
7. **Reinstall all parts** in reverse order, plug in the machine, and run a drain or spin cycle to verify the error has cleared. If E04 persists and all hoses are clear, replace the drain pump or call a technician to test the control board and pressure switch.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared the filter and hose, tested the pump impeller, and the error still appears. A technician can test the pressure switch, inspect the control board for faults, and verify that the wiring harness to the pump is intact. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if the machine is still under warranty, since some repairs may void coverage if performed by a non-authorized person.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
