## What this code means
The C8 (or C80) error on a Samsung dryer indicates that approximately 80% of the exhaust vent is clogged or blocked. This is an airflow restriction alert, not a component failure. The dryer has detected that airflow is severely limited, which causes heat to build up inside the machine. Samsung also uses the related codes C90 and C9 to indicate even worse blockage (around 90% clogged). The error is triggered by restricted airflow through the lint filter, vent hose, wall duct, or exterior termination. In some cases, an improper electrical supply (such as using an extension cord or sharing a circuit) can also trigger the code by affecting the dryer's ability to regulate temperature and airflow correctly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the heating element or thermostats first, when the real problem is external venting. Always clean the entire vent system and test the dryer before ordering internal parts.

## Common Causes

- **Lint filter clogged or housing packed with lint** A heavily loaded or damaged lint screen blocks airflow and is the first item Samsung tells technicians to check for this code.
- **Exhaust vent hose crushed, kinked, or disconnected** The flexible duct behind the dryer can be pinched when the machine is pushed against the wall, or it may have come loose from the wall outlet.
- **Wall duct or exterior vent hood blocked** Lint buildup in the rigid ductwork, a bird nest in the exterior hood, or a stuck damper can reduce airflow to near zero.
- **Vent duct too long or with too many turns** An excessively long run or multiple 90-degree elbows can exceed the dryer's airflow capacity and trigger a blockage alert.
- **Dryer sharing a circuit or powered through an extension cord** Samsung explicitly requires the dryer to be on its own circuit with no extension cord, as inadequate electrical supply can interfere with heat and airflow control.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dryer** by unplugging it or turning off the circuit breaker for at least 60 seconds, then restart to clear any temporary fault.
2. **Remove and clean the lint filter** thoroughly under running water if needed, and use a vacuum or vent brush to clear any lint from the filter housing inside the dryer door opening.
3. **Pull the dryer away from the wall** and disconnect the vent hose from both the dryer outlet and the wall duct to inspect for kinks, crushing, tears, or heavy lint accumulation.
4. **Inspect the wall duct and exterior vent hood** by running a vent cleaning brush or shop vacuum through the entire length, and make sure the exterior damper opens freely and is not blocked by lint, debris, or nests.
5. **Verify the electrical supply** by confirming the dryer is plugged directly into a dedicated outlet (not an extension cord) and that no other major appliances share the same circuit breaker.
6. **Reconnect the vent hose** using the shortest, straightest path possible with rigid or semi-rigid metal duct, and secure all connections with metal foil tape or clamps.
7. **Run a test cycle** and monitor airflow at the exterior vent. If strong airflow is present and the error does not return, the repair is complete. If the code persists, request service for internal airflow diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the error persists after you have cleaned the lint filter, inspected and cleared the entire vent system, and verified the dryer is on its own circuit. A technician will perform a full airflow diagnosis, check internal sensors and thermostats, and inspect the blower housing and duct transition inside the cabinet. Also call for service if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt electric dryer connections, if the vent duct runs through walls or ceilings you cannot access, or if the exterior termination is on the roof or high on an exterior wall.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 for vent cleaning tools or replacement vent hose, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for vent cleaning and inspection.
