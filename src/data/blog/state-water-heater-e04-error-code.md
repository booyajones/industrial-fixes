## State Water Heater E04 Error Code — What It Means

E04 is not a universal error code across all State water heater models. Its exact meaning depends on your specific unit and control platform. On some State models and similar platforms, E04 indicates a thermistor or temperature-sensor fault in an electric storage water heater. On certain tankless models from other manufacturers, the same code can mean a water-supply or low-flow problem. Because State does not publish a single E04 definition that covers every product, you must identify your exact model number and consult that unit's service documentation before diagnosing or replacing parts.

In practice, if your State unit is an electric storage tank, E04 usually points to a problem in the upper-element sensor circuit: a failed thermistor, loose wiring, or corroded terminal. If your unit is a tankless gas or electric model, the code may instead indicate insufficient incoming water flow or pressure. Without model-specific guidance, any repair is guesswork and risks replacing the wrong component.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor** An open, shorted, or drift-out-of-range sensor in the upper tank or element circuit will trigger a fault on electric storage models.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections** Vibration, moisture, or age can open terminals in the sensor harness or element circuit and mimic a sensor failure.
- **Low incoming water pressure or flow** On tankless-style units, a closed isolation valve, clogged inlet screen, or supply pressure below minimum will prevent ignition and log a flow fault.
- **Faulty upper-element circuit** A burned-out upper heating element or its limit switch can open the control loop and be read by the board as a sensor error.
- **Control board misreading sensor data** Voltage spikes, moisture intrusion, or component drift on the board itself can cause false E04 codes even when sensors test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the model and serial number** on the water heater's rating plate and write them down, then search for that model's service manual or control-board legend to confirm what E04 means for your exact unit.
2. **Turn off power** at the breaker (electric) or shut the gas valve to OFF (gas), wait thirty seconds, then restore power and observe whether E04 returns immediately or after a heating cycle.
3. **Check water supply** if you have a tankless model by opening a hot tap fully, verifying that both isolation valves are open, and inspecting the inlet screen or filter for debris or scale.
4. **Inspect sensor and element wiring** on electric storage tanks by removing the upper access panel, photographing the connections, then checking every terminal and connector for tightness, corrosion, or burn marks.
5. **Test the thermistor or sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) and compare the reading at room temperature to the resistance table in your service manual. Do not guess the correct value.
6. **Isolate the fault** by disconnecting the suspect sensor from the control board and measuring its resistance independently. If it reads open (infinite) or far outside spec, replace the sensor. If it tests good, move to the element or wiring harness.
7. **Replace only the failed component** once confirmed by electrical test. After installation, restore power, reset the code if a manual reset button exists, and run a full heating cycle to verify the fault is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you cannot locate a service manual for your exact State model, if electrical testing is outside your skill set, or if the E04 code persists after you have verified wiring integrity and tested the sensor to specification. Technicians carry manufacturer diagnostic software, model-specific resistance tables, and the correct OEM sensors. Also call for help if you smell gas, see water pooling under the tank, or if the unit is still under warranty, since DIY work may void coverage.
