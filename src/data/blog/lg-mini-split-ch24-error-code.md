## LG Mini-Split CH24 Error — What It Means

CH24 on LG mini-split systems is a pressure-switch error. On most residential split and multi-split units it indicates a high-pressure or low-pressure trip, while on commercial Multi V platforms it specifically signals a high-pressure trip. The code appears when refrigerant discharge pressure exceeds the safety threshold (typically around 35 bar on splits) or when the pressure switch circuit itself opens due to a wiring or component fault. The control board logs CH24 and shuts down compressor operation to prevent damage to the sealed system.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted outdoor airflow** A dirty condenser coil, blocked discharge grille, poor clearances, or debris around the outdoor unit drive discharge pressure high enough to trip the switch.
- **Failed or slow outdoor fan** If the condenser fan motor is not running at full speed or has stopped entirely, the coil cannot reject heat and pressure climbs until the switch opens.
- **Refrigerant overcharge or non-condensables** Excess refrigerant or air and moisture in the system raise head pressure even when airflow and the fan are normal.
- **Faulty pressure switch or wiring** The high-pressure switch itself can fail open, or corroded terminals and broken wires in the pressure-switch circuit can generate a false trip.
- **Poor outdoor-unit ventilation** Placing the condenser in a tight enclosure, corner, or area with no cross-ventilation causes hot discharge air to recirculate and repeatedly trip CH24.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm your model and platform** by checking the service manual or LG's fault-code reference, because CH24 can map to high-pressure only or to a broader pressure-switch trip depending on the product family.
2. **Inspect the outdoor unit for airflow restrictions.** Remove leaves, grass clippings, and debris from the coil fins and discharge grille, then verify that the unit has the manufacturer's minimum clearances on all sides.
3. **Verify outdoor fan operation.** Power the system and watch the condenser fan spin up to full speed within a few seconds, then listen for unusual noise or wobble that suggests a failing motor or bent blade.
4. **Test the pressure switch resistance** with power off and the switch connector unplugged. A normal high-pressure switch will measure approximately 0.1 to 0.6 ohms across its terminals when closed.
5. **Measure refrigerant pressures** with manifold gauges on the service ports, then compare high-side and low-side readings to the pressure table in your model's service literature to identify overcharge or non-condensables.
6. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** in the pressure-switch circuit, including the harness between the outdoor PCB and the switch, for corrosion, loose pins, or breaks that can open the safety circuit.
7. **Replace the defective component.** If airflow and pressures are correct but the switch reads open or out of specification, install a new pressure switch. If the switch and wiring test good but CH24 persists, replace the outdoor control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not EPA-certified to handle refrigerant, if manifold-gauge readings show abnormal pressures that require recovery and recharge, or if you have cleaned the coil and verified the fan but CH24 returns immediately. Pressure-switch replacement and board-level diagnosis also require specialized tools and familiarity with live voltage on the outdoor PCB. A professional can perform a full refrigerant analysis, check for non-condensables, and confirm that the system is within specification before releasing it back to service.
