## State Water Heater E119 Error Code — What It Means

E119 is not widely documented as a standard State water heater code in official manufacturer resources. On some boiler platforms (notably Baxi and Potterton models), E119 signals low system water pressure that has dropped below the safe operating range, typically under 0.5 bar. The unit shuts down to protect the heat exchanger from damage. On a subset of Baxi models, E119 can also appear when a frozen condensate pipe triggers a safety lockout.

Because State water heaters use a range of control boards across different model families, the exact meaning of E119 on your specific unit may vary. If your State appliance is a sealed-system model (such as a combination or system unit with a pressure gauge), low pressure is the most common trigger. If you see a pressure gauge reading in the red zone or below 0.5 bar, repressurizing the system will usually clear the fault. Always verify the code definition in your model's installation and service manual or on the unit's data plate before proceeding.

## Common Causes

- **System pressure drop below minimum threshold** Normal slow pressure loss over time in a sealed heating circuit, or recent venting of radiators, allows pressure to fall below 0.5 bar and trips the fault.
- **Leak in the heating circuit or pressure relief valve weeping** A hidden leak in pipework, a radiator valve, or a pressure relief valve that drips will cause repeated pressure loss and recurring E119 faults.
- **Filling loop left partially open or valve not seating** An improperly closed filling-loop valve or worn valve seat can allow water to escape or prevent the system from holding pressure after refilling.
- **Faulty pressure sensor or control-board misread** A pressure transducer that drifts out of calibration or a control board with corrupt firmware may display E119 even when system pressure is normal.
- **Frozen condensate pipe (on models with condensate drainage)** If your State unit is a condensing model, a frozen condensate pipe can block drainage and trigger a safety shutdown that shows as E119 on some control platforms.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check the system pressure gauge** on the front of the unit and note the reading in bar or psi. Look for a red or green zone marker. If the needle sits below 0.5 bar or in the red zone, the system needs repressurizing.
2. **Locate the filling loop or Easy-Fill lever** underneath or on the side of the unit. A filling loop is typically a braided hose with a valve at each end, or a single lever marked 'Fill' on integrated systems.
3. **Open the filling valves slowly** (turn both valves counterclockwise on a two-valve loop, or lift the Easy-Fill lever) and watch the pressure gauge rise. Stop when the needle reaches approximately 1 to 1.5 bar or enters the green zone on the gauge.
4. **Close the filling valves completely** by turning them clockwise or pushing the lever back down. Check that both valves or the lever are fully seated so no water continues to enter the system.
5. **Restart the unit** by pressing the reset button or cycling power at the isolation switch. Observe whether E119 clears and the unit fires normally.
6. **Monitor pressure over the next 24 hours**. If the gauge drops back into the red zone or E119 returns, inspect all visible pipework, radiator valves, and the pressure relief discharge pipe for signs of water or corrosion that indicate a leak.
7. **If the unit is a condensing model and outdoor temperature is below freezing**, inspect the white PVC condensate pipe where it exits the building. Pour warm (not boiling) water over any frozen section to thaw the blockage, then restart the unit and verify the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if repressurizing the system does not clear E119, if pressure falls again within hours or days, or if you cannot locate a filling loop or pressure gauge on your unit. A recurring fault usually means a hidden leak, a faulty pressure sensor, or a control-board issue that requires diagnostic tools and replacement parts matched to your exact State model. Also call for help if you see active water dripping from the pressure relief discharge pipe, if the condensate pipe remains frozen after careful thawing attempts, or if your model's manual lists E119 as a code that requires factory service. Do not bypass pressure safeties or operate the unit with the gauge in the red zone, because low water volume can damage the heat exchanger and void your warranty.
