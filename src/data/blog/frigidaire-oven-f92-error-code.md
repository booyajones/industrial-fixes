## What this code means
Frigidaire groups F92 within the F90–F94 family of door lock-related faults. The control board has detected a problem with the door lock or door-latch system. One repair-field interpretation of F92 is that the oven door is detected as staying open longer than allowed while the unit is running, pointing to a door-position or door-switch detection problem. Frigidaire's own support pages do not break out a unique public definition for F92 beyond the door-lock family.

In practice, the code can appear if the lock mechanism is binding, a door-switch is faulty, wiring between the lock and control is loose, or the electronic oven control is not reading the lock state correctly. Many false lock codes clear with a simple reset, so always try that first before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the electronic oven control board first. Test the door-lock switch and inspect all wiring to the lock assembly before replacing the control.

## Common Causes

- **Door lock assembly failure or binding (~45%)** The motor-driven lock mechanism sticks, breaks, or does not complete its travel, so the control never sees a fully latched state.
- **Faulty door-switch or lock micro-switch (~25%)** The switch that tells the control the door is closed or latched fails to change state, triggering the lock-family fault.
- **Loose or damaged wiring to the lock assembly (~15%)** Connectors at the lock or control board become oxidized or vibrated loose, breaking the signal circuit.
- **Obstructed or damaged door hinges (~8%)** Bent or sagging hinges prevent the door from closing to the exact position where the lock and switch engage properly.
- **Electronic oven control board failure (~7%)** The lock-detection circuit on the control board itself is faulty, even though the lock hardware and wiring are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the oven or switching off the breaker for at least 30 seconds to reset the control board, then restore power and test.
2. **Open and close the oven door** firmly and listen for the lock motor during a self-clean start to verify the lock mechanism attempts to engage.
3. **Inspect the door-lock assembly** behind the front panel for binding, obstruction, or visible damage to the latch rod or motor.
4. **Check all wiring and connectors** between the lock assembly and the electronic oven control for loose pins, corrosion, or broken wires, and reseat every connector.
5. **Test the door-switch or lock micro-switch** with a multimeter for continuity change when the mechanism moves; replace the switch or lock assembly if it does not change state.
6. **Verify door-hinge alignment** by checking that the door sits square in the frame when closed; adjust or replace hinges if the door sags or binds.
7. **Replace the electronic oven control** only if the lock assembly, switch, and all wiring test good and the F92 code persists after every other component has been ruled out.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working behind the control panel or if multimeter testing and wiring inspection are outside your skill set. Also call for service if you have replaced the door-lock assembly and verified all wiring but the F92 code returns, since the fault may lie in the control board's lock-detection circuit. Frigidaire's official support workflow directs unresolved lock-family codes to service after a reset attempt. A technician has the wiring diagrams and test procedures to pinpoint control-board faults without unnecessary part swaps.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–120 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
