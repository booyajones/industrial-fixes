## Whirlpool F5 Error Code — What It Means

The F5 error code on Whirlpool ranges and ovens (not microwaves) indicates a problem with the door-latch or door-lock circuit. According to Whirlpool Product Help, this code signals a fault in the oven door latch assembly, door latch switches, or the wiring connecting them. The control board is not receiving the correct signal that the door is properly latched.

This code typically appears on self-cleaning ovens that use a motorized latch mechanism. The control needs confirmation that the door is secured before allowing certain operations. If you see F5 on a microwave display, verify your model number because Whirlpool's indexed F5 documentation applies to ranges and ovens, not microwave ovens.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door latch assembly** The motorized latch mechanism binds, fails to move fully, or the motor itself burns out and cannot signal a closed position.
- **Faulty door latch switch** One or more micro-switches that detect latch position fail open, fail closed, or develop intermittent contact due to heat or wear.
- **Loose or damaged wiring** Connectors in the latch circuit work loose from vibration, or wires develop breaks or heat damage near the hinge area.
- **Misaligned latch mechanism** The door sags or the latch striker plate shifts so the latch cannot fully engage, preventing the switches from closing.
- **Control board memory glitch** A transient fault or power surge leaves the control in an error state even after the latch circuit is functioning normally.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** for at least one full minute to reset the control board and clear any transient faults.
2. **Restore power and wait one minute.** If the F5 code reappears within that time, the fault is active and not a memory glitch.
3. **Inspect the door latch assembly** for binding, visible damage, or failure to move smoothly through its full travel when you manually open and close the door.
4. **Check all door-latch switches and connectors.** Look for loose pins, heat discoloration on the switch body, or wires that pull free easily from the connector.
5. **Test the latch switches with a multimeter** if accessible. With the door unlatched the switch should read open, and fully latched it should read continuity (closed).
6. **Replace the door latch assembly** if the mechanism is jammed, the motor does not run, or the latch will not fully extend and retract.
7. **Replace faulty latch switches or repair damaged wiring** if you confirmed a switch that does not change state or found broken strands or burned insulation in the harness.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the power reset does not clear the code and you are not comfortable working inside the oven door or around line-voltage wiring. Latch replacement often requires partial door disassembly and routing new connectors through hinge channels. If you replace the latch assembly and switches but the F5 persists, the control board itself may have failed and will require diagnostics with a schematic and meter. Professional service is also the safer choice if your oven is still under warranty or if you cannot safely lock out power at the breaker.
