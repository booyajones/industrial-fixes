## KitchenAid Refrigerator Freezing Food — What's Happening

A KitchenAid refrigerator that freezes food in the fresh food compartment is experiencing a temperature-control or airflow problem, not a single fault code. The condition means the refrigerator section is getting too much cold air, too little warm-air mixing, or the control system is misreading temperature and overcooling the fresh food compartment.

KitchenAid specifically notes that blocked vents can disrupt airflow and that items placed directly in the airflow path from the freezer to the refrigerator may freeze. On some models, if the ice maker is turned on but not connected to water, the refrigerator may run too cold and freeze food in the crisper drawers.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Temperature controls set too cold** The refrigerator or freezer control dials are adjusted too far toward the coldest settings, causing the compressor to overcool the fresh food section.
- **Blocked air vents inside the compartments** Food containers or packages are blocking the internal vents that regulate cold air flow from the freezer to the refrigerator, disrupting temperature balance.
- **Food placed directly in the airflow path** Temperature-sensitive items are positioned right in front of the cold air discharge point where freezer air enters the refrigerator compartment.
- **Ice maker on without water supply connected** The ice maker is turned on but no water line is hooked up, which can cause the refrigerator to run too cold and freeze food in the crisper drawers on certain KitchenAid models.
- **Faulty door gasket or door alignment** A torn or loose door seal allows warm air infiltration, causing the compressor to run continuously and overcool the compartment.
- **Defective thermistor or control board** The temperature sensor or main control board is misreading compartment temperature and calling for too much cooling, even when the actual temperature is already low.
- **Evaporator icing or defrost system failure** Frost buildup on the evaporator coil or a malfunctioning defrost cycle alters airflow and temperature regulation, leading to cold spots and frozen food.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Place an accurate refrigerator thermometer in the fresh food section and check the actual temperature after 8 hours, not just the control display reading.
2. Adjust the refrigerator control one setting warmer and the freezer control one setting warmer if needed, then wait 8 hours for temperatures to stabilize before making further changes.
3. Inspect all interior air vents in both the refrigerator and freezer compartments and remove any food or containers blocking the vent openings.
4. Move temperature-sensitive items away from the direct cold air discharge area, typically at the back or top of the refrigerator section.
5. Check the ice maker status and verify the water supply connection is present and functioning, or turn the ice maker off if no water line is connected.
6. Test the door gasket seal by closing the door on a dollar bill or sheet of paper around the entire perimeter and tugging gently. Replace the gasket if the paper pulls out easily at any point.
7. Remove the evaporator cover panel and inspect the evaporator coil for excessive frost buildup. If icing is present, check the defrost heater, defrost thermostat, and defrost timer or control board for proper operation.
8. Test the refrigerator and freezer thermistors with a multimeter for correct resistance values per the service manual, and check continuity from each thermistor to the main control board connector. Replace the thermistor if out of specification, or replace the control board if sensor readings are correct but overcooling persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if adjusting controls and clearing vents does not resolve freezing after 24 hours, if you find heavy frost on the evaporator coil and are not comfortable testing the defrost system, or if thermistor or control board diagnostics require a wiring diagram and multimeter work. A technician can accurately measure refrigerant charge, test sealed-system performance, and replace control boards or sensors with proper calibration.
