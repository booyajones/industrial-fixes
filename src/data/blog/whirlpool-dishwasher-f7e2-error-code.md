## Whirlpool Dishwasher F7E2 Error — What It Means

F7E2 on a Whirlpool dishwasher is a wash motor or circulation pump fault code. Whirlpool's product-help page instructs users to cycle power to reset the control before further diagnosis, which indicates the code is handled as a dishwasher motor-related control fault. The code can be triggered by motor failure, motor speed sensing issues, motor overheat conditions, or problems in the motor circuit. Some sources incorrectly describe F7E2 as a water-inlet or thermistor problem, but those explanations conflict with Whirlpool's official reset-first dishwasher help page and consistent technician reports.

The code does not always mean the motor itself is bad. It can appear when debris blocks the impeller and stalls the pump, when connectors corrode or loosen between the control and motor, when a speed sensor or Hall sensor fails on models that use feedback sensing, or when the main control board's motor drive output develops a fault. A power reset clears many transient faults, so the first step is always to cycle power and retest before replacing any parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but the real cause is often a seized or dirty circulation pump or a bad connector at the motor. Always inspect the sump, test the motor electrically, and check the harness before ordering a control.

## Common Causes

- **Debris blocking the impeller (~35%)** Glass, food particles, or foreign objects in the sump or pump area stall the wash motor and trigger an overheat or stall fault.
- **Circulation or wash motor failure (~30%)** The motor may be seized, open, shorted, or running intermittently due to internal wear or overheating.
- **Corroded or loose wiring and connectors (~15%)** Harness connectors between the control board and the motor assembly can corrode, come loose, or suffer pin or heat damage.
- **Speed sensor or Hall sensor issue (~10%)** On models that use motor speed feedback, a failed sensor or sensor wiring problem prevents the control from reading motor speed.
- **Main control board motor drive fault (~10%)** When the motor, sensor, and harness all check good but the code returns, the control board's motor drive output may be defective.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cycle power first.** Unplug the dishwasher or turn off the circuit breaker for at least 1 minute, then restore power and run a test cycle to see if the code returns.
2. **Run a service diagnostic or fault retrieval** if your model supports it, because the displayed code alone does not confirm whether the fault is in the motor, sensor, wiring, or control board.
3. **Inspect the sump, filter, and impeller area.** Pull the lower rack and spray arm, remove the filter assembly, and look for glass, food debris, or foreign objects that could stall or jam the wash motor.
4. **Check all wiring and connectors** at the circulation pump motor and at the main control board for corrosion, looseness, pin damage, or heat damage, and clean or repair any issues found.
5. **Test the circulation motor electrically and mechanically.** Disconnect power, then check for continuity and proper resistance per your model's tech sheet. Spin the impeller by hand to confirm it is not seized. If the motor is noisy, open, shorted, or will not run in diagnostics, replace it.
6. **Verify the speed sensor circuit** on models that use a Hall sensor or motor feedback. Check sensor wiring and resistance values against the tech sheet, and replace the sensor or pump assembly if defective.
7. **Replace the main control board** only after confirming that the motor, sensor, and harness are all good and that incoming line voltage is correct, because a control fault is less common than a mechanical or wiring issue.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with 120 V electrical connections, if you cannot access the sump and motor area due to cabinetry or installation constraints, or if the fault returns after you have replaced the motor and verified all wiring. A technician can run factory diagnostics to isolate control board faults, obtain the correct model-specific resistance and voltage specifications from the tech sheet, and safely test live circuits. Also call a pro if the dishwasher is still under warranty, because self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-$250 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-$350.
