## State Water Heater E70 Error Code — What It Means

The E70 error code on State water heaters is not consistently defined across all models in available manufacturer documentation. State is part of the A.O. Smith product family, and tankless error codes in this family typically relate to ignition failure, air intake blockage, vent restriction, low gas flow, low water flow, or scale buildup. Without your exact model number and service manual, the specific meaning of E70 cannot be confirmed.

Based on common State and A.O. Smith tankless diagnostics, most ignition-related faults trace back to blocked air intake screens, restricted venting, insufficient gas supply, or low water flow. The fault may appear at startup, during firing, or after a period of operation, and each timing pattern points to different root causes. Always consult your model's owner's manual or data-plate wiring diagram to confirm the exact definition of E70 before replacing any parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the igniter or gas valve first. Clean the air intake screen and verify proper venting before ordering parts, since blocked intake is the leading cause of ignition faults on State tankless units.

## Common Causes

- **Air intake blockage (~40%)** Lint, dust, or debris on the intake screen restricts combustion air and prevents ignition or stable flame proving.
- **Vent restriction or improper termination (~25%)** Blocked exhaust, too many elbows, or incorrect vent pipe diameter causes pressure-switch faults or flame instability.
- **Low gas supply or pressure (~15%)** Partially closed shutoff valve, undersized gas line, or low meter pressure prevents igniter from lighting the burner.
- **Low water flow or dirty inlet filter (~10%)** Clogged inlet screen or insufficient flow rate stops the unit from firing or causes intermittent shutdown.
- **Scale or carbon buildup (~7%)** Hard-water deposits or soot on the heat exchanger or flame sensor reduce heat transfer and flame detection reliability.
- **Faulty flame sensor or igniter (~3%)** Failed flame rod, dirty sensor tip, or worn igniter electrode prevents ignition or flame proving even when air and gas are correct.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the exact model number** from the data plate and download the service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the E70 definition for your unit.
2. **Power off** the water heater at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and close the gas shutoff valve.
3. **Remove and clean the air intake screen or filter** by rinsing under warm water and gently brushing away lint, dust, or pet hair.
4. **Inspect the vent system** for blockages, incorrect termination, excessive elbows, or crushed pipe, and clear or correct any issues.
5. **Check the gas shutoff valve** is fully open and verify the water inlet filter is clean and water flow meets the minimum GPM rating in your manual.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then reset the unit and observe whether the fault clears. If it does not, proceed to sensor and igniter inspection.
7. **If the fault persists**, use a multimeter to test flame-sensor continuity and visually inspect the igniter electrode for cracks or carbon buildup, replacing only confirmed failed parts.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the fault reappears after cleaning the intake and venting, or if you lack the tools to test flame sensors and igniters safely. A pro should also be called if the unit has a history of hard-water service and requires heat-exchanger descaling, or if the control board or gas valve is suspect. Any work involving gas-line modifications, pressure testing, or electrical diagnostics on the control board is best left to a trained service technician with the correct manuals and parts for your specific State model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 in parts if filter or screen, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 service call and cleaning.
