## Frigidaire Refrigerator Door Bin Replacement — What This Part Does

The door shelf bin is the clear or white plastic bucket that clips into the inside of your refrigerator door to hold jars, bottles, and condiments. It mounts on rails or stabs molded into the door liner and relies on side tabs or locking hooks to stay in place. These bins fail from overloading with heavy items, impact from closing the door too hard, or repeated removal that cracks the mounting tabs. Because Frigidaire designs bins specific to each model and door position, a cracked or loose bin cannot be shimmed or glued and must be replaced with the exact OEM part for your refrigerator model.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **Visible cracks in the bin walls or corners** Hairline or wide cracks appear along the sides, bottom, or mounting tabs of the bin, allowing liquids to leak or the bin to flex under load.
- **Bin sags or pulls away from the door** The bin no longer sits flush against the door and tilts forward or downward when loaded, indicating broken retaining tabs or worn mounting hooks.
- **Bottles or jars fall out when the door opens** Items slide forward or topple because the bin has lost its grip on the door rails and cannot hold its position.
- **Broken or missing side clips** The plastic tabs or locking ears that snap the bin onto the door rails are chipped, sheared off, or missing entirely.
- **Bin rattles or slides up and down** The bin moves freely in its mounting slots instead of locking into place, producing noise when the door opens or closes.
- **Door won't close completely or gasket won't seal** A deformed or out-of-position bin protrudes inward and blocks the door from shutting, preventing the gasket from sealing and causing warm air infiltration.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Unplug the refrigerator or switch off the circuit breaker to cut power to the unit.
2. Open the refrigerator door and remove all items from the damaged door bin.
3. Locate the model and serial number plate inside the refrigerator (usually on the side wall or ceiling) and write down the full model number to look up the correct replacement bin part number for your specific model and door position.
4. Inspect the door liner mounting rails or stabs where the bin attaches to confirm they are not cracked or broken (if the door rails are damaged, the new bin will not lock in place and you will need professional door liner repair).
5. Grasp the old bin at both sides and lift straight up or pull the bottom edge forward to disengage the side tabs from the door rails (some bins require you to press or pry the locking tabs inward with a flat-blade screwdriver while lifting).
6. Compare the old bin to the new OEM replacement to verify that the width, height, and tab geometry match before proceeding.
7. Align the side tabs or hooks of the new bin with the slots or stabs on the door liner and press down firmly until you hear or feel the bin click into the locked position.
8. Load a few bottles or jars into the new bin and gently pull on it to confirm it holds securely and does not tilt or slide.
9. Close the refrigerator door fully to verify the bin does not interfere with the door gasket or prevent the door from sealing, then restore power and return the unit to normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

If the door liner rails or mounting stabs are cracked or broken, the new bin will not lock in place and you will need a technician to evaluate whether the door liner can be replaced or the unit requires a full door assembly. If multiple bins are loose or the door itself is warped or damaged, a professional can inspect for structural issues or hinge misalignment that may cause repeated bin failures. For any repair that requires removing the door or disassembling the door liner, professional service is recommended to avoid damaging gaskets or internal wiring.
