## Gree Mini Split C5 Error Code — What It Means

On Gree mini splits, a C5 error code indicates a PCB jumper wire cap or configuration jumper fault. The indoor control board is not detecting the correct jumper, or the jumper is missing, damaged, or installed in the wrong position. This is not a refrigerant or sensor issue. Gree's own documentation states that C5 is related to jumper problems and specifically notes that if the control board was replaced, the jumper must be transferred from the old board to the new one.

The jumper cap configures the board to match your specific model and capacity. Without the correct jumper in place, the board cannot operate properly and will throw the C5 code. This fault most often appears immediately after a board replacement when the technician forgets to move the jumper from the original board.

## Common Causes

- **Board replaced without transferring jumper** The most common cause is a control board replacement where the technician did not move the jumper cap from the old board to the new one.
- **Jumper cap installed in wrong position** The jumper is present but seated on the wrong pins or not matching the model's required configuration.
- **Jumper cap not fully seated** The jumper is partially loose or not making solid contact with both pins on the board.
- **Jumper cap damaged or open** The jumper itself has a broken internal connection or is physically cracked or missing.
- **Defective control board** The board cannot read the jumper correctly even when the jumper is confirmed good and correctly installed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Power off the unit completely at the breaker or disconnect before opening the indoor unit cover or control box.
2. Remove the front panel and locate the indoor control board inside the air handler.
3. Inspect the board for the jumper cap, which is a small plastic connector that bridges two pins on the board.
4. If a board was recently replaced, verify that the jumper was transferred from the original board to the replacement board.
5. Reseat the jumper firmly onto the correct pins, consulting your model's service manual or the label on the board for the proper position.
6. Check the jumper cap itself for physical damage or bent pins, and test for continuity across the cap if you have a multimeter.
7. Restore power and observe whether the C5 code clears. If the code remains after confirming the jumper is good and correctly seated, replace the indoor control board.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable working inside the air handler or do not have experience handling control boards, call a licensed HVAC technician. This repair requires opening the unit while de-energized, identifying the correct jumper position for your specific model, and potentially diagnosing a faulty board. If you have transferred or reseated the jumper and the C5 code does not clear, a professional can confirm the board is defective and source the correct replacement with the proper jumper configuration.
