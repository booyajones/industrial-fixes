## Samsung C-71 Error Code — What It Means

The C-71 error code appears on Samsung microwave ovens and signals a problem with the steam sensor or humidity-sensing circuit. The oven has detected either excessive moisture in the sensing area or a fault in the components that monitor steam during cooking. Unlike simple oven malfunctions, this code is tied specifically to the steam-generation and moisture-monitoring system rather than general heating or control-board issues.

Because Samsung does not publish a detailed public service bulletin for C-71, the most reliable interpretation comes from field technician experience. The code typically points to moisture intrusion into electronics, a disconnected or corroded wire on the steam generator, or a failure in the steam sensor itself. In some cases the pump or main control board may also be involved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first, but a simple visual inspection of the steam-generator wire connector and drying out moisture-exposed areas clears the code in a large share of cases.

## Common Causes

- **Moisture intrusion (~35%)** Steam or condensation has entered the electronics bay and is affecting the humidity sensor or its circuit traces.
- **Loose or disconnected steam-generator wire (~30%)** The connector on the steam generator has vibrated loose or corroded, breaking the sensor signal path.
- **Defective steam sensor (~20%)** The humidity sensor itself has drifted out of range or failed, sending invalid readings to the control board.
- **Faulty pump (~10%)** The steam-generation pump is stuck, leaking, or drawing excessive current and triggering a fault.
- **Defective main PCB (~5%)** The control board's moisture-sensing circuit has failed, misreading normal humidity as a fault condition.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and wait at least 60 seconds to reset the control board and allow residual charge to dissipate.
2. **Remove the outer cabinet** by taking out the screws along the top and sides, then lifting or sliding the cover off to access the steam-generator and sensor area.
3. **Inspect for visible moisture** around the control board, sensor harness, and steam-generator compartment, and wipe dry any damp surfaces with a lint-free cloth.
4. **Check the steam-generator wire connection** for looseness, corrosion, or broken pins, and reseat the connector firmly if it is loose.
5. **Test or replace the steam sensor** by disconnecting its plug and measuring resistance (consult your model's service manual for the correct range) or install a new sensor if readings are out of spec.
6. **Reassemble the cabinet**, restore power, and run a short test cycle to confirm the code has cleared.
7. **If the code returns**, replace the main PCB or contact a technician to diagnose the control-board moisture-sensing circuit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if drying and reseating connectors does not clear the code, if you lack a multimeter to test the steam sensor, or if you are uncomfortable working around high-voltage circuits inside the microwave cabinet. Professional diagnosis is also warranted when the pump or control board requires replacement, because pump access often involves removing multiple subassemblies and verifying water or steam-line seals. A qualified appliance repair tech can perform voltage and continuity tests across the steam-sensing circuit and identify board-level faults that are difficult to isolate without a schematic.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts (sensor or wiring harness), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 service call and diagnosis.
