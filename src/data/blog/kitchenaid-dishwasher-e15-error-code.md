## KitchenAid Dishwasher E15 Error Code — What It Means

The E15 error code on a KitchenAid dishwasher typically indicates that the appliance has detected water in the base pan beneath the tub. Many models use a float switch or sensor in this pan to protect against leaks. When water accumulates, the switch activates and triggers the code to prevent further flooding and damage.

The code may appear after a genuine leak or after water from cleaning or installation settles in the pan. Once triggered, the dishwasher often will not run until the pan is dry and the float returns to its normal position. In some cases the code persists even after the pan is emptied, pointing to a stuck float, damaged sensor, or a continuing leak that needs repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board assuming an electronic fault, when the real issue is simply water sitting in the base pan or a loose fill-line connection that can be tightened by hand.

## Common Causes

- **Water in the base pan (~50%)** A loose fill-line connection, overfilled rinse-aid dispenser, or door-seal drip lets water collect in the pan and trip the float switch.
- **Stuck or damaged float switch (~20%)** Detergent residue or a cracked pivot can prevent the float from dropping, keeping the error active even when the pan is dry.
- **Cracked or split hose (~15%)** Age and detergent exposure can crack the fill or drain hose, allowing slow leaks into the base.
- **Failed door seal (~10%)** A torn or misaligned gasket lets wash water escape during the cycle and pool underneath.
- **Faulty leak sensor wiring (~5%)** Corrosion or a pinched connector to the float switch can send a false signal to the control.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or turning off the breaker to avoid shock during inspection.
2. **Pull the machine forward** far enough to access the base pan, or tilt it back gently against a wall to expose the underside.
3. **Remove any standing water** from the base pan using towels, a sponge, or a wet-dry vacuum until the pan is completely dry.
4. **Locate the float switch** in the center or corner of the pan and lift or wiggle it to confirm it moves freely and drops back down under its own weight.
5. **Inspect all hose connections** at the fill valve, sump, and drain pump for loose clamps, cracks, or mineral buildup, and tighten or replace any suspect fittings.
6. **Check the door gasket** around the entire perimeter for tears, stiff spots, or gaps, and press it firmly into its channel if it has shifted out of place.
7. **Restore power and run a short rinse cycle** while watching the base pan and all connections for new drips, then clear any remaining error by holding the cancel or start button as directed in your model's manual.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the source of the leak after inspecting all hoses and seals, if water reappears in the base pan within one or two cycles despite tightening connections, or if the float switch and wiring appear intact but the code will not clear. A technician can pressure-test the tub and pump assembly, check for hairline cracks in the sump housing, and safely replace internal components that require disassembly of the lower spray-arm mount and pump cover. Also call if you are uncomfortable working under a tilted appliance or if the machine is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if hose or clamp needs replacing, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250 for diagnosis and hose or float-switch replacement.
