## What this code means
The F9E1 or E1F9 code on a Maytag dishwasher signals that the control has detected a long drain condition. The dishwasher is unable to drain water fast enough or cannot drain at all. This is a drain-system problem, not a fill issue.

The code usually appears when standing water remains in the tub after a cycle or when the control times out waiting for the water to leave. The fault can be triggered by blockages anywhere in the drain path, a failed pump motor, or restrictions in the external drain connection (disposal or air gap).

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump motor first without checking for clogs. Always remove and clean the filter, inspect the sump and impeller area for debris, and check the drain hose and disposal connection before buying a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain filter or sump (~40%)** Food particles, broken glass, or debris in the filter or sump area block water flow and prevent the pump from clearing the tub.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~20%)** A drain hose that is kinked, pinched under the dishwasher, or clogged with debris stops water from leaving the pump.
- **Garbage disposal or air-gap blockage (~15%)** A clogged disposal inlet port or air-gap cap restricts flow and causes the dishwasher to back up.
- **Jammed or clogged pump impeller/grinder (~15%)** Debris lodged in the pump housing, impeller, or grinder assembly prevents the pump from moving water even if the motor runs.
- **Failed drain pump motor (~10%)** The pump motor has burned out or is electrically open and will not spin to drain the tub.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and confirm the dishwasher is safe to open and work on.
2. **Open the door and check for standing water** in the bottom of the tub to confirm a drainage problem rather than a false code.
3. **Remove the lower dish rack** and locate the circular filter assembly at the bottom center of the tub.
4. **Twist and lift out the filter** and cylindrical screen, then scoop out any debris from the sump cavity below.
5. **Inspect the drain hose** under the sink or behind the dishwasher for kinks, clogs, or improper routing (it should loop high to prevent backflow).
6. **Check the garbage disposal and air gap** (if present) by removing the drain hose from the disposal inlet and clearing any blockage, or by removing the air-gap cap and flushing it.
7. **If water still does not drain**, access the pump by removing the lower spray arm and tower, unscrew the pump cover, and inspect the impeller and grinder area for debris or a jammed wheel.
8. **Test the drain pump motor** with a multimeter set to ohms (normal range is 40–80 ohms). If the reading is infinite or zero, replace the pump.
9. **Reassemble the dishwasher** and restore power, then run a rinse or drain cycle to verify the F9E1 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared every blockage in the filter, sump, hose, disposal, and air gap but the code returns and you are not comfortable testing electrical components with a multimeter or removing the pump assembly. Also call if you find water leaking from the pump housing or tub during your inspection, because that may indicate a cracked sump or failed gasket that requires disassembly of the lower tub. If the pump tests good electrically but still will not drain, the control board or wiring harness may be at fault and will need professional diagnosis.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–90 in parts if pump replacement is needed, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300 service call and parts.
