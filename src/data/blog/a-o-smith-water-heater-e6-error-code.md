## A.O. Smith E6 Error Code — What It Means

The E6 error code on an A.O. Smith water heater does not have a single universal meaning across all models. A.O. Smith uses different control platforms depending on the product line, so E6 may indicate a water flow problem, a combustion fault, a temperature sensor issue, or another system-specific condition. To determine the exact definition for your unit, locate the model and serial number on the rating plate or scan the QR code if present, then consult the model-specific manual or the manufacturer's online support resources.

On many tankless and on-demand water heater platforms from various manufacturers, E6 typically relates to flow sensing, combustion air pressure, or temperature control faults. Common triggers include low inlet water flow, a dirty or failed flow sensor, gas supply interruptions, or thermostat malfunctions. Because A.O. Smith directs service providers to verify code meanings by exact model, always confirm the fault definition before beginning repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Low or interrupted water flow** Insufficient inlet flow from a closed valve, clogged inlet filter, or leak prevents the heater from detecting proper flow and triggers a fault.
- **Faulty or dirty flow sensor** Scale, debris, or a failed flow meter stops accurate flow measurement and causes the control board to register an error.
- **Temperature sensor or thermostat failure** A malfunctioning temperature sensor or control prevents the unit from maintaining setpoint and may generate an E6 code on some models.
- **Combustion air or gas supply problem** On gas-fired tankless models, a blocked vent, failed pressure switch, or low gas pressure can interrupt ignition or flame stability.
- **Control board glitch or wiring issue** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a transient board fault can produce false error codes that clear with a power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact model number** from the rating plate on the unit and look up the E6 code definition in your model-specific manual or on the A.O. Smith support website.
2. **Power-cycle the heater** by turning off the circuit breaker or gas control for 60 seconds, then restoring power to see if the code clears as a transient fault.
3. **Check inlet water flow** by confirming that the main shutoff valve is fully open, the inlet screen or filter is clean, and no leaks are present on supply lines.
4. **Inspect and clean the flow sensor** by shutting off water and power, removing the sensor from the inlet manifold, flushing debris, and checking for scale buildup or damage.
5. **Test gas supply and combustion components** (gas models only) by verifying inlet pressure at the gas valve, checking vent piping for blockages, and listening for fan operation during ignition.
6. **Examine the temperature sensor and thermostat** by measuring resistance or voltage per your model's service manual and replacing any sensor that reads out of specification.
7. **Call A.O. Smith technical support or a licensed technician** if the code persists after basic checks, since model-specific diagnostics and control board access may be required.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate a model-specific code definition, if the error returns immediately after a reset, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, electrical controls, or water heater disassembly. Licensed plumbers and HVAC technicians have access to A.O. Smith's service bulletins, wiring diagrams, and calibrated test equipment needed for control board diagnostics and warranty-compliant repairs. If your unit is still under warranty, using a factory-authorized service provider will protect your coverage.
