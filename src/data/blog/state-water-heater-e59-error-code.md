## State Water Heater E59 Error Code — What It Means

The E59 error code on State water heaters indicates the unit has detected a fault condition and shut down to protect itself. While the exact definition of E59 is not documented in widely available State service literature, State and A. O. Smith family tankless and hybrid units use error codes to flag problems with combustion air supply, venting, ignition, gas flow, or condensate drainage. The control board logs the code and stops normal operation until the underlying issue is corrected.

Because State models vary widely in design (tankless gas, hybrid electric heat pump, and conventional tank styles), the specific component or system flagged by E59 depends on your model number. Always consult your unit's service manual or label to confirm the code definition and the correct troubleshooting sequence for your appliance.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted air intake** Lint, debris, or ice on the intake screen starves the combustion chamber of fresh air and triggers a fault shutdown.
- **Vent obstruction or improper termination** A crushed, sagging, or incorrectly installed vent pipe prevents exhaust gases from leaving and causes the unit to lock out.
- **Insufficient gas supply or pressure** A partially closed gas valve, undersized piping, or low manifold pressure stops ignition and generates a fault code.
- **Condensate drain line clog** On condensing models, a plugged neutralizer or drain trap floods the heat exchanger and trips a drainage safety switch.
- **Faulty flame sensor or ignition component** Corrosion or carbon buildup on the flame rod prevents the board from confirming ignition and the unit shuts down.
- **Heat exchanger or internal airflow restriction** Scale, soot, or debris inside the exchanger reduces airflow or combustion efficiency and the control flags an abnormal condition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the model number** on your State water heater's data plate and record the full error code display to cross-check against the unit-specific service guide.
2. **Inspect the air intake screen** for lint, leaves, snow, or debris and clean or replace the filter if airflow is restricted.
3. **Check the entire vent path** from the unit to the termination cap outside, looking for sags, disconnections, blockages, or improper slope that trap condensate or exhaust.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas shutoff valve is fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
5. **Clear the condensate drain line** (if your model is a condensing type) by flushing the neutralizer cartridge and trap with water and verifying free flow to the drain.
6. **Power-cycle the unit** by switching off the circuit breaker or disconnect for 30 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the heater attempts a normal start sequence.
7. **Call a licensed technician** if the E59 code returns after these checks, especially if you cannot locate a service bulletin that defines the code or if you lack tools to measure gas pressure and combustion parameters safely.

## When to Call a Pro

Contact a qualified water heater technician if the E59 code reappears after you have cleared the intake and vent, confirmed gas supply, and flushed the condensate system. Persistent codes often point to a failing control board, fan motor, gas valve, or heat exchanger that requires diagnostic tools (manometer, multimeter, combustion analyzer) and factory training to repair safely. Because State does not publish a universal E59 definition in widely available service guides, professional access to model-specific service bulletins and warranty parts lookup is usually necessary to resolve the fault correctly the first time.
