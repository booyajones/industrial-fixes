## LG Dryer tE3 Error Code — What It Means

LG's support states that a tE3 code (or tE1) indicates the control board has detected a problem with the dryer's thermistor. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that helps the dryer maintain the correct internal temperature during a cycle.

When the sensor reads out of range, loses connection, or the control board cannot interpret its signal, the dryer halts and displays tE3. LG's official troubleshooting recommends a power reset as the first step before replacing any parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when tE3 persists, but the thermistor itself or a loose connector is usually the fault. Test the thermistor's resistance with a multimeter before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~55%)** The temperature sensor itself has drifted out of range or stopped responding, so the control board cannot regulate heat properly.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors (~20%)** The harness plug at the thermistor or control board has vibrated loose, corroded, or broken, interrupting the sensor circuit.
- **Airflow restriction or lint buildup (~15%)** Clogged lint filter, exhaust duct, or internal blower housing causes overheating that the thermistor detects and reports as a fault.
- **Control board misreading the sensor (~8%)** The main control board's analog-to-digital circuit is faulty and cannot interpret a good thermistor signal correctly.
- **Temporary glitch requiring reset (~2%)** A power surge or static event locks the control in error mode even though all hardware is functional.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** or turn off both circuit breakers (electric models use two breakers). Wait two minutes, then press and hold the START/PAUSE button for about five seconds to discharge the control board.
2. **Restore power and run a short test cycle** to see whether tE3 returns. If the code does not reappear, the issue was a temporary glitch. If it does return, continue diagnostics.
3. **Clean the lint filter, exhaust duct, and blower housing.** Remove the front or rear access panel (consult your model's service manual), vacuum lint from around the blower wheel, and verify the exhaust vent run is short, rigid metal, and free of kinks.
4. **Locate the thermistor.** It is typically mounted on the blower housing or heater duct and has a two-wire plug. Unplug the dryer, disconnect the thermistor connector, and pull the sensor from its clip or mounting bracket.
5. **Test the thermistor with a multimeter** set to resistance (Ω). At room temperature it should read roughly 10 to 50 kΩ (exact values vary by model). Warm it gently with your hand or a heat gun and watch for a smooth change in resistance. An open circuit, shorted reading, or no change indicates a failed sensor.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the thermistor to the control board. Look for frayed insulation, pushed-out connector pins, or corrosion. Repair or replace any damaged wiring.
7. **Replace the thermistor** if testing shows it is faulty. Snap the new sensor into the mounting bracket, reconnect the plug, reassemble the dryer, and run a full heated cycle to confirm the code is gone. If tE3 persists with a known-good thermistor and intact wiring, the control board is suspect and should be replaced by a technician or as a last resort.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you have tested the thermistor and wiring and both check good but the code persists (pointing to a control board fault), or if the dryer uses gas and you smell gas at any point. A qualified technician has the meter, wiring diagrams, and board-level diagnostic tools to isolate whether the problem is truly the sensor circuit or a fault in the control board's input stage. Professionals can also verify that airflow, gas valve operation (on gas models), and high-limit thermostats are all functioning correctly so you do not replace parts unnecessarily.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 for thermistor, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call with thermistor replacement.
