## Bosch Washer F17 Error Code — What It Means

The F17 error code (also shown as E17 on some Bosch models) means the washer's water supply time has been exceeded. The machine did not fill with water fast enough during the allowed fill window. Bosch uses the same wording for both E17 and F17 codes.

This fault is almost always caused by a restriction somewhere in the water supply path, not a control-board glitch. The machine is reporting that it opened the inlet valve but water did not arrive quickly enough to satisfy the fill timer.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the inlet valve or even the control board when the real culprit is a clogged inlet screen filter or a supply tap that was not turned fully open. Always verify full water flow from the household tap and clean the screen before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or partly open water tap (~30%)** The household shutoff valve behind the washer is not turned fully counterclockwise, restricting flow.
- **Blocked inlet screen or filter (~25%)** Sediment, mineral scale, or debris accumulates in the mesh filter where the supply hose connects to the machine.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hose (~20%)** The hose is bent, twisted, or pinched between the wall and the washer cabinet, reducing flow.
- **Low household water pressure (~15%)** Weak municipal supply, a partially closed main valve, or demand from other fixtures drops pressure below the washer's minimum.
- **Faulty inlet valve (~8%)** The solenoid coil or valve body fails to open fully or stays mechanically restricted even when energized.
- **Wiring or control-board fault (~2%)** A broken wire, corroded connector, or control-board relay prevents the inlet valve from receiving power or being timed correctly.

## Quick Diagnosis

<details class="dtree"><summary>After cleaning the inlet screen, does the washer fill normally when you restart the cycle?</summary>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the washer** and unplug the power cord from the wall outlet.
2. **Close the water supply tap** (turn clockwise) at the wall behind the washer.
3. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the back panel and the inlet hose connection.
4. **Disconnect the inlet hose** from the back of the washer by unscrewing the threaded coupling, and have a towel ready to catch residual water.
5. **Remove and inspect the inlet screen** (a small mesh filter inside the hose port on the machine). Rinse it under running water and use a soft brush or toothpick to clear any sediment or scale. Do not use metal tools that can tear the mesh.
6. **Check the hose** for kinks, twists, or internal blockage. Straighten any bends and verify water flows freely through the hose by holding one end over a bucket and opening the tap briefly.
7. **Reconnect the hose** to the washer, turn the supply tap fully open (counterclockwise), restore power, and start a rinse or test cycle. The F17 code should clear if water now fills at the correct rate.
8. **Test the inlet valve** if the screen and hose are clean but the error persists. Use a multimeter to measure the coil resistance across the valve terminals (should read approximately 2,000 to 4,000 ohms). Replace the valve if the coil is open (infinite resistance) or shorted (near-zero resistance).
9. **Inspect wiring and connectors** from the control board to the inlet valve if the valve coil tests good. Look for loose pins, corrosion, or damaged insulation, and repair or replace the harness as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified that the supply tap is fully open, the inlet hose is not kinked, the inlet screen is clean, and household water pressure is adequate (test by filling a bucket at another faucet), yet the F17 code returns every cycle. At that point the inlet valve, pressure-switch circuit, or control board may need diagnosis with specialized test equipment. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with plumbing connections, testing electrical components with a multimeter, or accessing the back of the washer in a tight laundry space.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
