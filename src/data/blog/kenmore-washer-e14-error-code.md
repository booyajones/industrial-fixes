## Kenmore Washer E14 Error Code — What It Means

E14 is not a universal Kenmore error code, but when it appears it typically indicates a long fill or water fill timeout fault. This means the washer is taking too long to reach the expected water level during a cycle. Many Kenmore washers use Whirlpool-family diagnostics, so the exact meaning depends on your specific model number. In most cases this fault is labeled LF (Long Fill) in Kenmore documentation, and E14 is a platform-specific variant of the same problem.

The washer expects to fill to a set level within a fixed time window. When that does not happen, the control board times out and halts the cycle. The fault can be caused by restricted water delivery (closed valves, kinked hoses, clogged screens, low house pressure, or a defective inlet valve) or by a faulty level-sensing system (pressure switch, pressure hose, or sensor circuit). Identifying which system is at fault requires a step-by-step check of the water supply path first, then the pressure-sensing components if the supply is normal.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or partially closed water supply valves** If either the hot or cold supply faucet at the wall is not fully open, the washer will fill slowly and trigger the timeout.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hoses** A hose that is bent sharply behind the machine or pinched against the wall restricts flow and causes a long fill.
- **Clogged inlet screens** Sediment and debris from the water supply collect in the fine mesh screens at the inlet valve connection and block flow.
- **Low house water pressure** Weak municipal supply or a partially closed main shutoff can prevent the washer from filling quickly enough to satisfy the timer.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** A defective valve may open partially, fail to open at all, or have internal debris that limits flow even when commanded correctly by the control.
- **Pressure switch or pressure hose fault** If the washer is filling but not recognizing the water level, a blocked, leaking, or disconnected pressure hose or a failed pressure switch will prevent the control from seeing that the tub is full.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number** on the washer's lid or frame tag, because Kenmore error codes and diagnostics are platform-dependent and the exact definition of E14 varies by model.
2. **Check both supply valves** at the wall and confirm that hot and cold water faucets are fully open, then turn off the valves and disconnect the inlet hoses at the back of the machine.
3. **Inspect the inlet hoses** for kinks, crushing, or internal blockage, and straighten or replace any damaged hose before reconnecting.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet screens** at the water inlet valve connection on the back of the washer, rinsing away sediment and debris under running water, then reinstall the screens and hoses.
5. **Test water pressure** by turning on the supply valves and checking flow from the disconnected hoses into a bucket. If flow is weak or slow from either line, investigate the house supply or main shutoff.
6. **Test the water inlet valve** by running a fill cycle and listening for the valve to energize. If the valve hums but does not deliver water, or if it does not energize at all, test for voltage at the valve terminals and replace the valve if defective.
7. **Inspect the pressure hose and pressure switch** if the washer fills normally but still faults. Disconnect the small rubber or vinyl hose from the tub to the pressure switch, blow through it to confirm it is clear, check for leaks or cracks, and test or replace the pressure switch if the hose is intact.
8. **Run the washer's diagnostic mode** to read stored fault codes and execute the automatic fill test, which will help separate a true supply fault from a control or sensor issue, then clear the code and retest the cycle after repairs.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified that both supply valves are open, hoses are clear, screens are clean, and house water pressure is adequate, but the washer still times out on fill. A technician can use service mode diagnostics to test the inlet valve electrically, measure pressure-switch actuation points, and trace control-board signals to isolate intermittent faults or board-level failures. Also call for help if you are not comfortable working with water connections under pressure, if the washer requires disassembly to access the pressure switch or valve wiring, or if the fault returns after you have replaced the inlet valve and pressure components.
