## State Water Heater E125 Error Code — What It Means

The E125 error code is not documented in available State Water Heater manufacturer sources. However, E125 is a recognized fault code on Baxi and Potterton boiler systems, where it indicates a circulation problem or poor water flow in the heating circuit. If your State unit displays E125, it likely points to a similar hydraulic issue: low system pressure, pump failure, airlocks, or blockages preventing proper water movement through the system.

Because this code is not officially confirmed for State equipment, always cross-reference your model's service manual or contact State customer support to verify the fault definition. Treat E125 as a circulation-related alert and follow standard water-flow diagnostics until you have model-specific guidance.

## Common Causes

- **Low system pressure** Pressure below the operating range (often around 1.0 to 1.5 bar on similar systems) starves the pump and prevents adequate circulation.
- **Pump failure or weak performance** A seized, worn, or electrically faulty circulation pump cannot move water through the heat exchanger and piping.
- **Airlocks in the system** Trapped air pockets block water flow and create zones of stagnant fluid that trigger circulation faults.
- **Blockages or restricted flow** Sediment buildup, scale in the heat exchanger, or debris in pipes and valves choke the hydraulic circuit.
- **System leaks causing pressure loss** Slow leaks at joints, valves, or the pressure relief valve drop system pressure over time and compromise circulation.
- **Faulty wiring or PCB issues** Loose connections or control-board faults may prevent the pump from receiving power or running at the correct speed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check system pressure** on the gauge and compare it to your model's recommended operating range (typically 1.0 to 2.0 bar on similar units). If pressure is low, locate the fill valve and add water until the gauge reaches the normal zone.
2. **Inspect for visible leaks** around pipe joints, valves, the heat exchanger, and the pressure relief valve. Tighten fittings or replace failed seals as needed to stop pressure loss.
3. **Test the circulation pump** by listening for hum or vibration when the unit calls for heat. If the pump is silent or making grinding noises, turn off power and check electrical connections. Tap the pump body lightly to free a stuck rotor. Replace the pump if it does not spin.
4. **Bleed air from the system** by opening bleed valves on radiators or high points in the piping until water flows steadily without sputtering. Close each valve tightly and recheck system pressure afterward.
5. **Inspect for blockages** by feeling inlet and outlet pipes for temperature differences. If the heat exchanger or pipework is cold downstream of the pump, flush the system or disassemble and clean components to remove sediment and scale.
6. **Examine electrical components** including pump wiring, terminal connections, and the printed circuit board for burn marks, corrosion, or loose plugs. Repair or replace damaged parts as required.
7. **Reset the unit** only after correcting the underlying hydraulic or electrical fault. Follow your model's reset procedure (usually a button hold or power cycle). Monitor the system through several heating cycles to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot locate an E125 definition in your State water heater's documentation, if system pressure will not hold after refilling and leak checks, or if the circulation pump shows no electrical faults but still will not run. A professional can perform a power flush, swap the pump or heat exchanger safely, and verify that control-board signals are correct. Because E125 is not a widely published State code, expert diagnosis will prevent misdiagnosis and unnecessary part swaps. Always involve a pro for any work on gas connections, high-voltage wiring, or pressurized hydronic systems if you are unsure.
