## Gree F4 Error Code — What It Means

On most Gree mini-split models, the F4 error code indicates that the outdoor heat-exchanger pipe temperature sensor (also called the outdoor tube thermistor) is either open-circuit or short-circuit. This sensor monitors the temperature of the refrigerant coil in the outdoor unit and sends that reading to the control board. When the board detects a resistance value that is too high (open), too low (short), or completely absent, it throws the F4 fault and shuts down operation to protect the compressor.

Gree's official error-code documentation lists F4 under the sensor fault category and identifies it specifically as an outdoor tube sensor malfunction. The system cannot safely operate without an accurate outdoor coil temperature reading, so the fault will prevent normal heating or cooling until the sensor circuit is repaired.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor element** The sensor itself has drifted out of range, opened internally, or shorted due to age, heat cycling, or moisture exposure.
- **Loose or corroded wiring** The connector at the sensor or outdoor PCB has become loose, corroded, or contaminated with moisture or debris, breaking the electrical path.
- **Damaged sensor harness** The wiring between the sensor and the control board is cut, pinched, abraded, or has water intrusion that causes an intermittent or permanent fault.
- **Outdoor PCB input failure** The outdoor control board's sensor input circuit has failed, so even a good sensor reads as faulty or does not register at all.
- **Incorrect replacement sensor** A prior repair used a thermistor with the wrong resistance curve or specification, so the board interprets its readings as out-of-range.
- **Poor sensor mounting** The thermistor probe has come loose from the condenser pipe or is not making proper thermal contact, causing erratic or invalid readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the outdoor unit's breaker or disconnect switch and wait at least five minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the outdoor unit cover** and locate the outdoor coil temperature sensor. It is typically a small thermistor probe clipped or strapped to a refrigerant pipe on the heat exchanger.
3. **Inspect the sensor harness and connector** for looseness, corrosion, pinched or cut insulation, and moisture. Unplug and reseat the connector at both the sensor and the outdoor PCB, cleaning any corrosion with contact cleaner.
4. **Measure the thermistor resistance** using a multimeter. Compare the reading at ambient temperature to the temperature-to-resistance table in your model's service manual. An open circuit (infinite resistance) or very low resistance (near zero) confirms sensor failure.
5. **Replace the sensor** if the reading is out of specification. Use an exact replacement for your model and make sure the probe is firmly seated on the pipe with good thermal contact. Do not substitute a sensor with a different resistance curve.
6. **Restore power and test** by turning the system back on. If the F4 code clears and the unit operates normally, the repair is complete. If the fault remains after sensor replacement and wiring checks, the outdoor control board's sensor input may be faulty and require board replacement.
7. **Clear any stored fault codes** by cycling power or using the service menu if your model supports it, then monitor the system through a full heating or cooling cycle to confirm stable operation.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable working with line-voltage electrical components or do not have a multimeter and the skills to test thermistor resistance, call a licensed HVAC technician. Also call a pro if you have replaced the sensor and verified all wiring but the F4 code returns, since that points to a control board issue that requires careful diagnosis and possible refrigerant-side work. Technicians have access to Gree's full service documentation, temperature-to-resistance tables, and board-level diagnostic tools that are not published in owner materials.
