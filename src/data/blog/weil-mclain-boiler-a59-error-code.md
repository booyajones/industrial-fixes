## Weil-McLain Boiler A59 Error — What It Means

Weil-McLain does not use an error code labeled A59 in official documentation. The boiler most likely displays E03 (ignition failure or lockout) or A5 (flame loss after ignition). E03 means the burner failed to ignite within the allotted time or the main controller has detected an internal fault, often due to a gas valve issue or faulty ignitor. A5 means the burner lit successfully but could not maintain the flame, triggering a shutdown to prevent unsafe operation. In both cases the system shuts down and requires a reset or repair before it will run again.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or ignitor when a dirty flame sensor or blocked vent is the real problem. Clean the sensor and check vents before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor (~40%)** Carbon deposits or condensation coat the sensor rod so it cannot detect the flame, or the sensor is bent away from the burner path.
- **Weak or interrupted gas supply (~20%)** The shut-off valve is partially closed, the propane tank is empty, or gas line pressure is below specification.
- **Blocked intake or exhaust vent (~15%)** Bird nests, ice, or condensation in the flue or vent pipe restrict airflow and prevent proper combustion.
- **Faulty or warped ignitor (~15%)** The spark electrode is cracked, corroded, or positioned too far from the burner to produce a reliable arc.
- **Gas valve not opening (~10%)** The valve solenoid has failed electrically or mechanically, or the wiring harness connection is loose.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Access the Contractor Menu** by holding the up and down arrow buttons simultaneously until the menu appears, then navigate to Diagnostics and view Fault History to confirm the exact error code.
2. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the shut-off valve is fully open and, if using propane, the tank gauge shows fuel remaining.
3. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside for bird nests, ice, or debris, and clear any obstructions by hand or with a shop vacuum.
4. **Remove and clean the flame sensor** by loosening the mounting screw, sliding the sensor rod out, and polishing the tip with fine steel wool until shiny, then reinstall and check alignment with the burner flame path.
5. **Test the ignitor** by observing whether it sparks during a call for heat, or use a multimeter to measure resistance (consult your model's specifications), and replace if the electrode is cracked, corroded, or out of range.
6. **Check boiler water pressure** on the gauge (should be at least 12 psi) and use the fill valve to top up if low, then bleed air from the system if necessary.
7. **Reset the boiler** by pressing the reset button on the controller, wait for the burner to attempt ignition, and monitor for flame establishment and continued operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you cannot locate the exact error code in your manual, if the ignitor does not spark and you are uncomfortable working with gas or electrical wiring, or if the boiler continues to lock out after you have cleaned the flame sensor and verified gas supply. A technician has the tools to measure gas line pressure, test the gas valve solenoid, inspect the main controller for internal faults, and safely diagnose wiring issues. Gas appliances require precise adjustments and leak testing, so professional service is the safest route when free checks do not resolve the fault.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
