## Samsung Range C-D1 Error — What It Means

C-D1 (or CD1) on a Samsung range or oven is a door lock or latch fault. The control board sees that the lock mechanism did not move to the commanded position, did not return correctly, or is not electrically responding as expected. This code appears when the oven cannot confirm the door lock assembly is working properly, either because the latch is stuck, the motor is weak or jammed, or the position switch is not changing state.

The exact definition can vary slightly by model family, so always verify your specific model number. On most Samsung ranges the code points to a problem with the physical lock motor, the position switch that tells the control the door is locked or unlocked, or debris and misalignment that prevents the mechanism from moving freely.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real culprit is the door lock motor or a stuck latch. Always clean the latch area, inspect the wiring, and test the lock motor and switch with a meter before replacing the control.

## Common Causes

- **Debris or mechanical binding (~35%)** Grease, food particles, or a misaligned door prevent the lock mechanism from moving freely and the control sees a fault.
- **Faulty lock motor or actuator (~30%)** The motor is weak, stuck, noisy, or only hums instead of moving the cam and latch fully.
- **Failed lock position switch (~20%)** The switch that tells the control the door is locked or unlocked does not change state when the mechanism moves.
- **Wiring or connector issues (~10%)** Damaged harnesses, loose plugs, or heat damage between the control and the lock assembly interrupt communication.
- **Main control board fault (~5%)** The control logic or relay on the board itself is failing, especially if all field components test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cycle power at the breaker.** Turn off the breaker, wait 60 seconds, restore power, and test. If the error clears, monitor for recurrence.
2. **Verify your exact model number** and confirm the code definition in your owner's manual or wiring diagram, since Samsung code meanings can vary by platform.
3. **Inspect and clean the door lock and latch area.** Open the door, remove any visible grease, food debris, or obstructions around the lock mechanism, and confirm the door closes squarely without binding.
4. **Access the lock assembly from the rear or control side.** Remove the rear panel or control panel as required and locate the door lock motor and wiring harness.
5. **Inspect wiring and connectors** between the main control and the lock assembly for damage, loose terminals, heat damage, or rodent damage. Reseat all connectors firmly.
6. **Test the lock position switch with a continuity meter.** The switch should change state (open to closed or closed to open) when manually actuated. If it stays stuck in one state, replace it.
7. **Test the lock motor.** Apply power or use a meter to verify the motor moves strongly and does not just hum or grind. If the motor is noisy, weak, or jammed, replace the lock assembly or motor.
8. **Reassemble and test.** If you corrected mechanical binding or replaced a failed component, restore power, run a door lock cycle, and verify the error does not return. If the error persists and all field components test good, suspect the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the oven's rear panel or control assembly, if testing the lock motor and switch with a meter is outside your skill level, or if the error persists after you have cleaned the latch area, inspected wiring, and tested the lock components. A technician has the tools to verify continuity and voltage at the lock assembly, confirm the control board is sending correct signals, and replace the lock motor or control board safely. If the range is still under warranty, contact Samsung or an authorized service center before opening panels or replacing parts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $35-85 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
