## Samsung Range C-D1 Error — What It Means

The C-D1 code on a Samsung range signals a door lock or latch fault. The control board is not seeing the door lock system in the expected state, so the oven may refuse to heat or start a cleaning cycle. This code points to a problem with the lock actuator, the position switch inside the latch assembly, or a mechanical obstruction preventing the door from closing or locking fully.

The code does not indicate a temperature sensor or heating element issue. Instead, it reflects either a failure of the lock motor to move the latch cam, a switch that does not change continuity when operated, or loose wiring between the lock assembly and the control board. Less commonly, a control board communication fault after a power surge can trigger the code, but mechanical and electrical faults in the lock hardware are far more frequent.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board when the real fault is a jammed or failed door lock assembly. Always test the lock switch for continuity and check the motor for smooth rotation before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed lock actuator or motor (~40%)** The motor inside the latch assembly does not spin fully or only vibrates, so the control never sees the locked position and throws C-D1.
- **Stuck or faulty lock position switch (~25%)** The microswitch that signals lock state does not change continuity when the cam moves, leaving the control unable to confirm the door is secure.
- **Debris or misalignment in the latch path (~20%)** Food residue, grease buildup, or a bent strike plate blocks the latch from closing flush, preventing the lock from engaging.
- **Loose or damaged wiring at the lock assembly (~10%)** A connector has backed out or a wire has broken, interrupting the signal between the lock and the control board.
- **Control board lock-circuit fault (~5%)** After a power surge or transient event the control board fails to drive or read the lock properly, though this is less common than mechanical failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power at the circuit breaker** and confirm the range is fully de-energized before opening any panels or touching wiring.
2. **Inspect the door, hinges, and latch area** for food debris, grease, or bent hardware that could prevent the door from closing flush against the seal.
3. **Clean the latch strike and door gasket** with a degreaser and a non-abrasive pad, then close the door firmly to verify it seats without gaps.
4. **Restore power and perform a hard reset** by leaving the breaker off for at least five minutes, then turn it back on and check if the code clears.
5. **Access the lock assembly** by removing the oven door (if needed for clearance) and the rear or side service panel to expose the latch mechanism and harness connectors.
6. **Test the lock switch for continuity** using a multimeter while manually moving the latch cam back and forth; continuity should change from open to closed as the cam actuates the switch.
7. **Inspect and test the lock motor** by applying the correct voltage (consult your model's service manual) or by observing whether it spins smoothly rather than just vibrating or grinding; replace the entire lock assembly if the motor or switch fails the test, or repair any loose or broken connectors if wiring is at fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt wiring or if the lock assembly and wiring both test good but the code persists. In that case the main control board may have a failed lock-circuit driver, which requires model-specific diagnosis and typically a board replacement. Also call for service if the oven is still under warranty, since attempting your own repair can void coverage. A technician will have the service manual, correct voltage specs, and replacement boards on the truck to resolve control-level faults quickly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–120 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call and lock assembly.
