## MRCOOL Mini Split EH 11 Error — What It Means

The EH 11 error code on a MRCOOL mini split indicates a problem with the electric auxiliary heater circuit. The indoor unit has detected an abnormal condition in the electric heat strip or its control wiring, which may include open circuits, faulty connections, or a malfunctioning heater relay. Because the exact meaning can vary slightly by model series, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit cover for confirmation.

This code typically appears during heating mode when the system tries to energize the auxiliary electric heat but cannot verify proper operation. The mini split will continue to run the heat pump but will disable the electric heater until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire heater element assembly when only the thermal cutoff or relay has failed. Test continuity on the heater coil and check the relay control signal with a multimeter before buying the full heater.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded heater wiring (~40%)** Wire connections at the heater terminal block can loosen over time from thermal expansion and contraction, or corrode in humid environments, breaking the circuit.
- **Failed heater relay on the control board (~25%)** The relay that switches power to the auxiliary heater can stick open or fail, preventing the heater from receiving voltage even when the board commands it.
- **Tripped thermal cutoff on the heater element (~20%)** A one-time thermal cutoff embedded in the heater assembly can trip if airflow was blocked or the element overheated, permanently opening the circuit until replaced.
- **Open heater element coil (~10%)** The electric resistance coil itself can burn out or develop a break, though this is less common than wiring or relay issues.
- **Faulty heater thermostat or temperature sensor (~5%)** A defective thermostat or limit switch in the heater circuit can fail to close, preventing current flow and triggering the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and confirm the mini split display is dark before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the indoor unit's front cover and filter**, then locate the auxiliary heater assembly, usually mounted in the air path behind the evaporator coil.
3. **Inspect all wire connections** at the heater terminal block and the control board for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks, and tighten or clean them as needed.
4. **Test the heater element for continuity** using a multimeter set to resistance, disconnect one wire from the element and measure across its terminals; a good coil reads a low resistance, while an open coil reads infinite.
5. **Check the thermal cutoff and limit switches** embedded in the heater for continuity, and replace any that read open even when cool.
6. **Examine the heater relay on the control board** for visible damage, and test it by applying power and listening for a click when the system enters heat mode; replace the relay or board if it does not energize.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore power, and run a heating cycle to verify the error code clears and the auxiliary heater energizes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring or if you cannot safely access the heater assembly inside the indoor unit. A pro should also handle the repair if testing shows the control board itself has failed, since board replacement often requires reconfiguring DIP switches and verifying refrigerant pressures afterward. If you see evidence of arcing or repeated thermal damage, a technician can identify whether the root cause is an airflow restriction, a mis-wired heater, or a voltage problem at the breaker panel.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $180-400.
