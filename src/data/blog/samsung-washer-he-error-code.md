## Samsung Washer HE Error Code — What It Means

On Samsung washers, HE is generally a heating error. The washer is not detecting the expected water-heating behavior or there is a fault in the temperature-sensing circuit. The exact meaning varies by model, and Samsung notes that some codes are model-specific, so you should check your owner's manual or service guide for your exact model. Related codes like HC and CE can also point to temperature-related faults, and on some models the code can trigger when water temperature exceeds 50°C during draining.

The error can involve the heating element itself, the thermistor (temperature sensor), wiring and connectors, or in rare cases the control board. On models that rely on hot water supply rather than internal heating, incorrect water supply conditions (hoses reversed, closed valves, clogged screens) can also produce the code. A temporary control glitch may sometimes trigger HE and clear after a power reset.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board when the real fault is a failed thermistor or heating element. Always test the thermistor resistance and heater continuity with a multimeter before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Thermistor (temperature sensor) fault (~35%)** The thermistor circuit is open, shorted, or reporting an abnormal voltage, so the control board cannot read water temperature accurately.
- **Heating element failure (~30%)** The water heater or heating element has no continuity or does not produce heat, so the washer cannot reach the target temperature.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wiring (~15%)** Wiring harness connectors to the heater or thermistor are loose, heat-damaged, or corroded, interrupting the signal or power.
- **Wrong water supply condition (~10%)** Hot and cold hoses are reversed, inlet valve is closed, or hose mesh screens are clogged, preventing proper hot-water flow on models that use external hot supply.
- **Temporary control glitch (~7%)** A transient fault in the control board triggers the code and clears after a power reset.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The main control board has failed and cannot process heater or thermistor signals, though this is rare and should only be diagnosed after all other components test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify model and code.** Check the rating label and your owner's manual to confirm that HE is a heating error on your exact Samsung washer model, since code meanings can vary.
2. **Power-reset the washer.** Unplug the machine or switch off the circuit breaker for at least two minutes, then restore power. Clear the code and run a short test cycle to see if the error was transient.
3. **Check water supply conditions.** Confirm hot and cold hoses are on the correct inlets. Open both inlet valves fully. Remove the hoses and inspect the inlet mesh screens for debris. Clean or replace screens if clogged.
4. **Disconnect power and access the heater circuit.** Unplug the washer. Remove the rear or lower access panel (consult your service manual for your model). Locate the heating element and thermistor, usually mounted in the sump or tub base.
5. **Inspect wiring and connectors.** Check all harness plugs to the heater and thermistor for looseness, corrosion, or heat damage. Disconnect and reconnect each plug to make sure a clean contact. Replace any damaged connectors or wiring.
6. **Test the heating element.** Using a multimeter set to continuity or resistance, measure across the heater terminals. A good element shows continuity (typically a few tens of ohms, but consult your model's service data). No continuity means the element is open and must be replaced.
7. **Test the thermistor.** Measure the resistance across the thermistor terminals at room temperature. A good NTC thermistor will show a specific resistance that changes with temperature (consult your model's resistance table). An open circuit, short, or out-of-range reading indicates a failed sensor. Replace if faulty.
8. **Replace failed components.** Install a new heating element, thermistor, or wiring harness as needed. Reconnect all plugs, reassemble the panels, restore power, and run a test cycle. If all electrical components test good but the code persists, the control board may need replacement, but this is rare.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical circuits or if you do not have a multimeter and the skills to test heater and sensor components safely. A pro can quickly diagnose whether the fault is in the heating element, thermistor, wiring, or control board and will have model-specific service data and the correct OEM parts on hand. If you have already tested the heater and thermistor and both are good, a technician can evaluate the control board and perform any board-level diagnostics or replacements. Professional service typically runs $150 to $300 depending on the part and labor in your area.
