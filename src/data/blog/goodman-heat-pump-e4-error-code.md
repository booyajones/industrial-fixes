## Goodman E4 Error Code — What It Means

The E4 error code on most Goodman heat pump models indicates an indoor coil temperature sensor fault. The control board has detected a problem with the sensor that monitors your evaporator coil temperature, its wiring, or the signal it sends. This sensor (a thermistor) changes resistance with temperature, and the board uses that reading to manage defrost cycles, compressor operation, and safety limits. When the board sees an open circuit, short circuit, or out-of-range reading, it flags E4 and may disable heating or cooling to protect the system.

Because Goodman uses different control platforms across model families, the exact meaning of E4 can vary. Always confirm the definition in your model's service literature before diagnosing. The most consistent interpretation across documented sources is an indoor coil sensor circuit problem, not a refrigerant or compressor fault.

## Common Causes

- **Failed indoor coil thermistor** The sensor itself has drifted out of range, opened internally, or shorted, sending an invalid resistance reading to the control board.
- **Loose or disconnected sensor wiring** The plug at the sensor or control board has worked loose, corroded, or a wire has broken, creating an open circuit the board reads as a fault.
- **Sensor displaced from coil** The thermistor has slipped out of its mounting clip or lost contact with the coil tubing, causing erratic or incorrect temperature readings.
- **Damaged or rubbed-through wiring** Insulation has worn away where the sensor harness contacts a metal edge, causing intermittent shorts or grounds that trigger the error.
- **Control board input fault** The sensor and wiring test correctly but the board's sensor input circuit is defective, reading E4 even with a good signal.
- **Iced coil creating indirect symptoms** Severe icing from airflow or refrigerant problems can stress the sensor or freeze moisture into connectors, though this is not the direct cause of the code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your exact model number and pull the service manual** to confirm that E4 means indoor coil sensor fault on your platform, as Goodman code definitions are not universal across all heat pump models.
2. **Turn off power at the breaker and wait two minutes**, then restore power and check if the code clears. A transient glitch may reset, but if E4 returns immediately or after startup, proceed with component testing.
3. **Locate the indoor coil sensor** inside the air handler, usually a small thermistor clipped to a refrigerant line or inserted into a well on the evaporator coil. Inspect the sensor body, mounting, and connector for damage, corrosion, or loose fit.
4. **Disconnect the sensor plug and measure resistance** across the sensor leads with a multimeter set to the kilohm range. Compare the reading to the temperature-resistance table in your service manual. An open (infinite resistance), short (zero resistance), or wildly incorrect reading means the sensor has failed.
5. **Check sensor resistance while gently heating it** with your hand or a heat gun on low. Resistance should change smoothly. A stuck, jumping, or non-responsive reading confirms a bad sensor.
6. **Inspect the wiring from sensor to control board** for breaks, rub-through, pinched insulation, or poor pin contact at the board connector. Repair or replace damaged wire and make sure the sensor is seated firmly in its clip with good thermal contact to the coil.
7. **Replace the sensor if out of spec** or the wiring if damaged, then reassemble and restore power. Run the unit through a heating cycle and verify the code does not return and the board displays normal coil temperature readings.
8. **If the sensor and wiring test good but E4 persists**, the control board input is likely faulty and the board will need replacement by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside the air handler with power off, if you do not have a service manual with the resistance table for your model, or if the sensor and wiring both test correctly but the code will not clear. Also call a pro if the system is icing heavily or you suspect a refrigerant leak or airflow problem contributing to the fault. A technician has the tools to measure live sensor signals at the board, verify refrigerant charge, and replace the control board safely if needed. If your unit is under warranty, professional diagnosis and repair by an authorized Goodman servicer will protect your coverage.
