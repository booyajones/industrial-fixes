## GE Microwave F10 Error — What It Means

F10 on a GE microwave indicates a shorted touch screen according to GE's official fault-code table. This means the control system has detected an electrical short somewhere in the touch screen or keypad assembly circuit. GE notes that fault codes are intended for technician diagnosis and that some codes may clear after a power reset, but persistent faults require service.

This code is specific to GE microwaves. Some sources confuse it with range or oven F10 codes (which refer to runaway temperature), but that is not the GE microwave definition. The fault points directly to the user interface hardware, not the cooking system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first when F10 appears. Check the touch screen assembly and its wiring harness connections first, since GE defines F10 as a shorted touch screen, not a board fault.

## Common Causes

- **Failed touch screen or keypad assembly (~60%)** An internal short inside the touch panel or membrane keypad triggers the F10 fault.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~25%)** A pinched, corroded, or disconnected wire between the touch screen and control board can cause a short condition.
- **Faulty connector at the control board (~10%)** Loose or contaminated connector pins at the board may mimic a shorted touch screen.
- **Electronic control board misreading the keypad circuit (~5%)** The board itself can fail in a way that falsely detects a short, though this is less common for F10.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power** at the circuit breaker or fuse box for at least 30 seconds to attempt a reset, as GE advises some microwave fault codes can clear this way.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether F10 reappears immediately or after touching the keypad.
3. **Verify the complaint** by checking if the touch screen or keypad responds to button presses and whether the code is persistent.
4. **Inspect the touch screen assembly** for visible cracks, moisture, or debris around the keypad edges that could cause a short.
5. **Check wiring connections** by removing the outer case (with power off) and inspecting the harness connectors between the touch screen and the control board for looseness, corrosion, or damage.
6. **Replace the touch screen or keypad assembly** if the code persists and no wiring issues are found, since GE defines F10 as a shorted touch screen.
7. **Reapply power, clear the code, and test** all keypad functions and cooking modes to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the F10 code persists after a power reset. Diagnosing and replacing the touch screen or keypad assembly requires opening the microwave cabinet, disconnecting high-voltage components, and tracing low-voltage control wiring. Microwaves contain a high-voltage capacitor that can hold a dangerous charge even when unplugged. A technician has the tools to safely discharge the capacitor, isolate the faulty part (touch screen assembly, harness, or control board), and verify the repair with proper testing. If you are uncomfortable working inside the microwave or the unit is still under warranty, professional service is the safest choice.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350 for touch screen/keypad assembly replacement.
