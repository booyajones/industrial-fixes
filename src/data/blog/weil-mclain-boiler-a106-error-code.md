## Weil-McLain A106 Error — What It Means

The A106 code is not clearly documented in available Weil-McLain manufacturer materials, so the exact meaning depends on your specific boiler model and control board. Weil-McLain uses different fault codes across product families, and a code that appears on one platform may not exist or may mean something different on another. The closest documented fault in the provided materials is A01, which means no burner ignition after the boiler has called for heat and attempted to light but failed to prove flame.

Before attempting any repair, locate your model number and control board version, then consult the service manual or wiring diagram supplied with your unit to confirm what A106 means for your specific installation. If your documentation shows the code relates to ignition failure, the boiler has tried to start the burner but could not establish or verify flame within the allowed time window.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve when the real problem is a dirty or misaligned flame sensor or closed manual gas shutoff. Always verify gas supply and check the electrode condition before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply off or inadequate pressure (~30%)** The manual shutoff is closed, the meter valve is off, or incoming pressure is below the minimum specification (Aqua Balance models require 3.5 to 11 in. w.c.).
- **Dirty or misaligned ignition or flame-sensing electrode (~25%)** Carbon deposits, corrosion, or physical damage on the ignitor or flame rod prevent spark generation or flame detection.
- **Faulty gas valve (~20%)** The valve is commanded to open but does not deliver gas to the burner, or internal seats are worn and leaking.
- **Blocked condensate trap or drain (condensing models) (~15%)** Standing water or debris in the trap creates a pressure lock that prevents combustion airflow or safe ignition.
- **Dirty heat exchanger (~10%)** Soot or scale buildup restricts airflow and prevents stable flame carryover across the burner.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact model and control board.** Locate the rating plate on the boiler jacket and the control board label inside the panel, then download or reference the matching service manual to confirm what A106 means for your unit.
2. **Verify the gas supply.** Confirm the manual shutoff valve at the boiler and any upstream valves are fully open, and check that other gas appliances in the building are operating normally.
3. **Measure incoming gas pressure.** Use a manometer at the appliance inlet to confirm pressure is within the range specified for your model (Aqua Balance models require 3.5 to 11 in. w.c.).
4. **Inspect and clean the ignition and flame-sensing electrodes.** Turn off power and gas, remove the electrode assembly, and gently clean the ceramic insulator and metal tip with fine emery cloth or a soft brush, then check for cracks or shorts.
5. **Check the gas valve.** With power restored and a call for heat active, use a multimeter to confirm the valve coil is being energized during the trial for ignition, and listen or feel for the valve click.
6. **Inspect the condensate trap and drain (condensing models).** Remove the trap, empty and rinse it, verify the drain line has no sags or blockages, and reinstall with a proper water seal.
7. **Press reset once** after correcting the fault, and observe a full ignition cycle. If the code returns, stop resetting and call a qualified technician for combustion analysis and deeper component testing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician immediately if you smell gas, if the boiler has locked out multiple times, or if you are unfamiliar with gas appliance safety. Gas-fired equipment requires specialized combustion testing, pressure measurement, and electrical diagnostics that go beyond simple part replacement. A technician will use a manometer to verify supply and manifold pressures, a combustion analyzer to check flame quality and exhaust gases, and a multimeter to test control signals and safety interlocks. Attempting repair without these tools or training can create carbon-monoxide hazards, explosion risk, or warranty violations. Professional service typically costs $180 to $400 depending on the failed component and local labor rates.
