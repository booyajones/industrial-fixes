## What this code means
Frigidaire washer E13 typically indicates the washer has detected a water leak condition. Some service sources describe it as a water leak in the tub or an air leak in the water-level pressure sensor hose. The exact interpretation can vary slightly by model family, but the practical meaning is the same: the machine believes water is leaking where it should not be.

The code may trigger from an actual water leak (drips on the floor or inside the cabinet) or from a false leak signal caused by a faulty or disconnected pressure-sensor air hose. Until you locate and fix the source, do not continue cycling the washer.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the pressure sensor or even the control board when the real cause is a loose hose clamp or a detached pressure-sensor air hose. Inspect all water connections and the small air tube to the pressure switch before ordering any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or leaking fill hose connections (~30%)** Supply hoses at the wall or rear of the washer can loosen over time or develop mineral trails and drips.
- **Oversudsing or too much detergent (~20%)** Excessive suds can overflow the dispenser or door boot, triggering the leak detection.
- **Door boot or bellow leak (~15%)** On front-load models the rubber door seal can tear, crack, or allow water to seep during the cycle.
- **Pressure-sensor air hose detached or damaged (~15%)** The small air tube from the tub to the pressure switch can fall off, crack, or become clogged, sending a false leak signal.
- **Drain hose leak or improper position (~10%)** A split drain hose or incorrect standpipe height can cause backflow or drips.
- **Pump, tub-to-pump hose, or internal hose leak (~10%)** Internal hoses, clamps, or the drain pump itself can crack or loosen, pooling water in the base.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Stop the washer** and do not continue cycling until you find the leak source.
2. **Shut off the water supply** valves at the wall to prevent further leaking during inspection.
3. **Disconnect power** at the wall or breaker before opening any panels or touching internal parts.
4. **Inspect external connections first.** Tighten or replace inlet fill hoses at both the wall and the back of the washer, and check the drain hose for splits or loose clamps.
5. **Open the cabinet or service panel** and look for water trails, wet spots, or mineral deposits around the pump, tub-to-pump hose, dispenser hoses, and door boot (front-load models).
6. **Check the pressure-sensor air hose** if no water leak is visible. Trace the small rubber or vinyl tube from the bottom of the tub to the pressure switch on the control panel and confirm it is firmly attached, clear, and undamaged.
7. **Dry the machine and base**, restore power, and run a short monitored test cycle (rinse or drain-and-spin) to confirm the repair. Wait about five minutes after plugging back in to let the control reset.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you find a cracked outer tub, if the door boot requires removal of the front panel and special clamps, or if you have replaced the pressure-sensor air hose and tightened all connections but the E13 error returns. A pro can pressure-test internal circuits, diagnose a faulty pressure sensor with proper tools, and safely handle tub replacement or complex cabinet disassembly. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water connections or opening the washer cabinet.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in parts (hose, clamp, or door boot), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for service call and part.
