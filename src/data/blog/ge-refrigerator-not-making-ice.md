## GE Refrigerator Not Making Ice — What's Happening

When a GE refrigerator stops making ice, it means the icemaker cannot complete its harvest and refill cycle. GE states that the icemaker must reach 16°F before a cycle will begin, and the fresh food section should be at 37°F with the freezer at 0°F. If the freezer is too warm, ice forms slowly or not at all. GE also notes that a low food load in the freezer makes temperature less stable and reduces ice production.

Other common causes include restricted water flow from a clogged filter, a stuck rake or ejector arm preventing harvest, ice blocking the mold, or a frozen water line. GE recommends diagnosing the icemaker system as a whole rather than looking for a single fault code, because this symptom can come from temperature, water supply, or mechanical issues inside the icemaker assembly.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Freezer temperature above 16°F** GE states the icemaker will not start a cycle until the freezer reaches 16°F, and recommends a 0°F setting with a 37°F fresh food compartment.
- **Clogged or overdue water filter** A restricted filter blocks water flow to the icemaker and GE recommends replacement about every 6 months.
- **Low freezer food load** GE notes that a freezer less than 3/4 full causes unstable temperatures and reduces ice production.
- **Stuck rake or ejector arm** GE identifies a stuck rake or ejector as a mechanical fault that stops the icemaker from harvesting ice.
- **Frozen or blocked water line** Ice in the water line or a kink prevents water from reaching the icemaker fill valve.
- **Ice obstruction in the mold** Ice jammed in the mold or tray blocks the harvest cycle and prevents new ice from forming.
- **Failed icemaker assembly or water inlet valve** When basic checks do not restore operation, GE recommends service for the icemaker system or related water supply components.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm the icemaker is turned on and reset it if your model has a reset button or test function.
2. Check the temperature display and verify freezer is set to 0°F and fresh food to 37°F.
3. Inspect the freezer food load and add items if less than 3/4 full to stabilize temperature.
4. Replace the water filter if it is restricted, discolored, or more than 6 months old.
5. Inspect the water line behind the refrigerator and inside the freezer for kinks, ice blockage, or damage.
6. Open the icemaker and check the mold for ice jams and the rake or ejector arm for binding or stuck position.
7. Run the icemaker test or reset cycle and watch for harvest movement and water refill into the mold.
8. If the unit still will not cycle or refill after those checks, prepare to replace the icemaker assembly or call for service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the icemaker still will not cycle after you have verified temperature settings, replaced the water filter, cleared any ice blockage, and run the test cycle. GE recommends service when the icemaker assembly or water inlet valve requires replacement, or when freezer cooling issues persist and may involve the defrost system or sealed refrigeration components.
