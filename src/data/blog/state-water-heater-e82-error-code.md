## State Water Heater E82 Error Code — What It Means

The E82 error code on a State water heater indicates an ignition failure lockout. The control board attempted to light the burner during startup but did not detect a successful flame. As a safety measure, the unit has shut down and locked out to prevent unburned gas from accumulating. This code typically appears when the burner cannot ignite, when the flame sensor cannot prove combustion, or when gas supply or combustion air problems prevent proper lightoff.

Because error code definitions can vary by model and control platform, always verify that your specific unit displays E82 and consult your model's documentation if available. The code is most often resolved by cleaning the burner assembly and flame sensor, restoring proper gas flow, or addressing wiring issues in the ignition circuit.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or fouled flame sensor** Dust, soot, or corrosion on the flame-sensing probe prevents the control from detecting ignition even when the burner lights.
- **Contaminated burner or combustion chamber** Debris, rust, or spider webs in the burner assembly block gas flow or disrupt the flame pattern during startup.
- **Gas supply issue** The manual shutoff valve is closed, gas pressure is too low at the appliance, or the gas valve itself is not opening during the ignition sequence.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition assembly** A weak or failed igniter cannot produce a spark strong enough to light the gas, or wiring to the igniter is damaged.
- **Loose or corroded electrical connections** Connectors to the flame sensor, igniter, or gas valve are loose, corroded, or damaged and interrupt the ignition signal.
- **Combustion air or venting restriction** Blocked intake or exhaust pipes starve the burner of air or prevent proper draft, causing the flame to fail at startup.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off gas and power** at the manual shutoff valve and circuit breaker, then allow the unit to cool for at least 15 minutes before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the burner access cover** and visually inspect the burner chamber for dust, rust, spider webs, or other debris that could block gas flow or foul the flame sensor.
3. **Clean the flame sensor probe** with a fine abrasive pad or clean cloth (do not use harsh chemicals) and wipe the igniter electrode gently to remove any coating or corrosion.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming that the manual gas shutoff valve upstream of the heater is fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
5. **Inspect all wire connectors** to the igniter, flame sensor, and gas valve for tight fit, corrosion, or damage, and reseat any loose plugs.
6. **Reassemble the burner cover**, restore gas and power, and initiate a call for heat by raising the temperature setpoint or pressing the unit's reset button if available.
7. **Observe the ignition sequence** through the viewing window or burner door to confirm that the igniter sparks, the burner lights, and the flame stabilizes without the error returning.
8. **Perform a soap-bubble leak test** on all gas fittings you disturbed during service to confirm there are no gas leaks before leaving the unit in operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E82 code returns after you have cleaned the sensor and burner, verified gas supply, and checked all visible wiring. Persistent ignition failures can indicate a failing gas valve, a control board fault, or combustion air and venting problems that require pressure testing and flue analysis. Any work involving gas-valve replacement, control board diagnosis, or modification of venting must be performed by a qualified professional to meet local codes and maintain safe operation. If you smell gas at any point, leave the area immediately, shut off the gas at the meter if safe to do so, and call your utility or a licensed technician from outside the home.
