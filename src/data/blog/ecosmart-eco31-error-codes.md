## EcoSmart ECO31 Error Codes — What It Means

EcoSmart tankless electric water heaters display alpha-numeric codes when sensors or operating conditions fall outside normal range. The most common codes are E1 (inlet thermistor failure) and E2 (outlet thermistor failure). If you are seeing what looks like 'ECO31' on the display, double-check the screen under good lighting. It may actually be E1, E2, or another standard fault code. Some installers also confuse the model name (ECO 27, ECO 36, etc.) with an error message.

If the display truly shows an unfamiliar code or the unit simply will not heat, the problem often traces to low voltage, incorrect wiring, excessive flow rate, or a failed thermistor. EcoSmart electric units require a minimum of 208 volts and correct breaker sizing to function. Low heat output or intermittent operation can result from crossed wires, downstream mixing valves adding cold water, or flow rates that exceed the heater's capacity.

## Common Causes

- **Inlet thermistor fault (E1)** The temperature sensor at the cold-water inlet has failed or lost connection.
- **Outlet thermistor fault (E2)** The temperature sensor at the hot-water outlet has failed or reports out-of-range values.
- **Insufficient line voltage** Supply voltage below 208 V prevents the heating elements from reaching set temperature.
- **Incorrect or crossed wiring** Miswired heating elements or reversed polarity can trigger faults or prevent heating.
- **Flow rate too high** Water moving through the heater faster than rated capacity results in low outlet temperature.
- **Downstream mixing or tempering valve** A thermostatic valve or fixture mixing cold water masks the true heater outlet temperature.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breakers and confirm zero voltage at the unit terminals with a multimeter.
2. **Read the exact error code** from the display under good lighting and write it down. Distinguish between E1, E2, and the model number printed on the rating plate.
3. **Check supply voltage** with breakers on but heater off. Verify line voltage meets the minimum 208 V requirement and matches the unit's rating plate.
4. **Inspect wiring and connections** inside the junction box. Look for loose terminals, crossed wires between elements, and signs of overheating or corrosion.
5. **Test flow rate** by measuring gallons-per-minute at the outlet fixture. Compare the result to your model's flow-rate table and reduce flow if it exceeds capacity.
6. **Locate and test thermistors** if E1 or E2 is displayed. Remove sensor leads, measure resistance (consult your model's service manual for spec), and replace any out-of-range sensor.
7. **Check for mixing valves** downstream of the heater. Close or bypass any tempering valve temporarily to verify the heater's true outlet temperature before calling the fault permanent.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician if you measure low line voltage, find evidence of overheating at breaker lugs, or lack the tools to verify wiring polarity and element resistance. If thermistors test within specification but the fault persists, the main control board may have failed and requires factory training to diagnose safely. Any work inside the junction box on a 240 V appliance carries shock and arc-flash risk. If you are uncomfortable working with high-voltage AC or your local code requires permitted electrical work, stop and hire a professional before re-energizing the unit.
