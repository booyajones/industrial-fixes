## Navien E133 Error Code — What It Means

The E133 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with the flow sensor or a related temperature sensing issue. The control board is not receiving the expected signal from one or more sensors, which can prevent the unit from heating water properly or cause it to shut down. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for model-specific details.

This code often appears when the flow sensor is dirty, when sensor wiring has come loose, or when scale buildup in the heat exchanger interferes with accurate temperature readings. In some cases the sensor itself has failed. Because the code can point to more than one sensor or a communication issue, systematic troubleshooting is the best approach.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flow sensor when the real problem is simply a loose connector or scale buildup. Clean the sensor and check all wiring connections before ordering a new part.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen the flow sensor plug or create corrosion on the pins, interrupting the signal to the control board.
- **Scale or debris on the flow sensor paddle (~25%)** Hard water deposits or sediment can coat the flow sensor's paddle wheel, preventing it from spinning freely and generating an accurate signal.
- **Failed flow sensor (~20%)** The flow sensor's internal Hall-effect sensor or paddle mechanism can wear out or fail, sending no signal or an erratic signal to the board.
- **Wiring damage or short (~10%)** Rodents, pinched insulation, or age can damage the sensor wiring harness, creating an open circuit or short that triggers the error.
- **Control board communication fault (~10%)** A software glitch or component failure on the control board can misinterpret valid sensor signals and throw the code incorrectly.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the cold-water isolation valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and lifting it away from the unit.
3. **Locate the flow sensor** on the cold-water inlet pipe (it will have a small turbine or paddle wheel and a wiring connector).
4. **Disconnect the sensor connector** and inspect both the plug and socket for corrosion, moisture, or bent pins; clean with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Unscrew the flow sensor** from its housing (it usually has a hex body or mounting bolts) and remove it.
6. **Rinse the sensor under running water** to remove any scale or debris from the paddle wheel; gently spin the wheel by hand to confirm it moves freely.
7. **Reinstall the sensor**, tighten securely, and reconnect the wiring harness.
8. **Open the cold-water valve** and check for leaks around the sensor body.
9. **Restore power** at the breaker, open a hot-water tap, and observe whether the error code clears and the unit fires normally.
10. **If the code persists**, test the sensor with a multimeter (consult your model's service manual for resistance or voltage specs) or replace the flow sensor.
11. **Record the repair** in your maintenance log and schedule annual descaling to prevent future scale-related codes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with plumbing connections, if the error persists after cleaning and reseating the sensor, or if you suspect the control board or wiring harness is damaged. A professional can perform a full system flush, test sensor voltages with diagnostic software, and replace internal components that require specialized tools. If your unit is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
