## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E20 Error — What It Means

The E20 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically signals a problem with flame detection or ignition. The unit is attempting to fire but either cannot establish a flame or cannot confirm that ignition has occurred. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent gas accumulation or unsafe operation.

The exact meaning can vary slightly by model and firmware version, so consult your owner's manual or the diagnostic table on the unit's wiring diagram for your specific model. Common triggers include gas supply issues, dirty or corroded flame sensing components, improper combustion air flow, or ignition system faults.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the entire igniter assembly when the real problem is a corroded flame rod or interrupted gas supply. Check gas pressure and clean the flame rod before ordering expensive ignition parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Mineral deposits, soot, or corrosion on the flame sensing rod prevent the control board from confirming ignition.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low inlet gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an undersized gas line can prevent the burner from lighting reliably.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Obstructed exhaust or intake vents reduce airflow and disrupt the flame, triggering a detection fault.
- **Failed igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked ceramic insulator or failed spark module will prevent the burner from igniting at all.
- **Faulty control board or wiring (~10%)** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a defective control board can cause intermittent flame-detection signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve upstream of the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** or access panel according to your model's service instructions to expose the burner chamber and ignition components.
3. **Inspect the flame rod**, a small metal probe near the burner. Clean it gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft abrasive pad to remove any oxidation, soot, or mineral deposits.
4. **Check the igniter or spark electrode** for cracks in the ceramic insulator or excessive carbon buildup. Clean carefully with a dry brush and replace if damaged.
5. **Verify gas pressure** using a manometer at the gas valve test port, following the pressure specifications in your service manual. Adjust the regulator or contact a licensed gas technician if pressure is outside the acceptable range.
6. **Inspect the venting system** for blockages, kinks, or improper termination. Clear any obstructions and confirm that intake and exhaust pipes are correctly installed and sealed.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and initiate a hot-water call. Monitor the display for error codes and listen for normal ignition and flame operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas connections, if you lack the tools to measure gas pressure accurately, or if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and verifying gas supply. Tankless water heaters require precise combustion tuning and venting, and improper repairs can create carbon monoxide hazards or void warranties. A professional can test gas pressure, check electrical continuity, inspect the heat exchanger for scale or damage, and update firmware if needed.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
