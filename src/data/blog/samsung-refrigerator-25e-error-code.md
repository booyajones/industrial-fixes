## What this code means
The 25E code is a defrost-failure fault. Samsung support documentation confirms it appears when the refrigerator has run the defrost cycle for 80 minutes but failed to complete defrosting successfully within that window. The control board expected the evaporator to reach a target temperature during defrost, but the sensor feedback or actual temperature did not confirm success.

This is not a cooling failure code. It means the automatic defrost system, which melts accumulated frost off the evaporator coils, did not work. When defrost fails repeatedly, ice builds up on the evaporator and blocks airflow, which then causes poor cooling or no cooling in the fresh-food and freezer compartments. Samsung groups this code with defrost-sensor faults and notes that related problems can be caused by a frozen evaporator fan or door-seal air leaks that let warm, moist air into the cabinet and accelerate frost buildup.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first when they see an error code. Before doing that, visually inspect the evaporator area for heavy ice buildup and test the defrost heater for continuity. Most 25E faults trace to a failed heater, bad sensor, or wiring issue rather than the board itself.

## Common Causes

- **Defrost heater failure** The heater element develops an open circuit or reduced output and cannot melt the frost within 80 minutes, so the control board logs a defrost-failure code.
- **Iced-over evaporator from prior defrost failures** Repeated incomplete defrost cycles leave a thick ice blanket on the coils that blocks airflow and prevents the next defrost attempt from finishing on time.
- **Defrost sensor or thermistor fault** The sensor gives incorrect temperature feedback to the control, so the board thinks defrost is not complete even when the heater is working.
- **Wiring harness or connector problem** Loose pins, corrosion, or breaks in the wires between the control board and the defrost heater or sensor cause intermittent open circuits that abort the defrost cycle.
- **Control board or defrost-timer logic fault** The main board or its defrost-control circuitry fails to energize the heater correctly or to recognize valid sensor readings, logging a failure code even when all physical components test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or by unplugging the refrigerator to make sure safe access to internal components.
2. **Remove the evaporator cover panel** inside the freezer compartment (typically held by screws) and inspect the evaporator coils and fan for heavy frost or ice buildup.
3. **Check the evaporator fan** for ice seizure or physical blockage, since a frozen fan can be caused by open doors or worn door gaskets that let warm air in.
4. **Test the defrost heater** by disconnecting its harness and measuring resistance across the terminals with a multimeter (consult your model's service manual for the correct value, though one source suggests around 120 ohms for some models). A reading of infinite resistance means the heater is open and must be replaced.
5. **Test the defrost sensor or thermistor** and any thermal-fuse or safety-thermostat device in the defrost circuit for correct resistance or continuity, again using your model's specification table.
6. **Inspect all wiring harnesses and connectors** between the main control board and the defrost heater, sensor, and fan for looseness, corrosion, or heat damage.
7. **If ice is present, perform a full manual defrost** by leaving the unit unplugged with doors open until all ice melts (this can take 6–12 hours), then reassemble, restore power, and monitor for code recurrence. If the code returns immediately or after the next auto-defrost, replace the failed component identified in your tests rather than simply clearing the code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with electrical components or accessing the sealed evaporator compartment behind the freezer liner. Diagnosing a 25E fault requires multimeter testing of the defrost heater and sensor, inspecting internal wiring, and sometimes forcing a defrost cycle through service mode or by jumpering control-board terminals. A technician has the correct resistance values for your specific model, the tools to safely thaw and test the system, and the experience to distinguish between a simple heater replacement and a more complex control-board or wiring fault. Incorrect repairs or incomplete thawing can lead to recurring codes and compressor damage from restricted airflow.
