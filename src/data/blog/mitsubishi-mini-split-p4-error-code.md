## Mitsubishi Mini Split P4 Error — What It Means

The P4 code on a Mitsubishi Electric mini split is an inverter compressor drive fault in the outdoor unit. It means the power electronics that drive the compressor have detected a problem, either in the inverter module itself, the compressor windings, or the electrical supply feeding the outdoor unit. This is not a simple sensor error. It points to the outdoor inverter circuitry, wiring, or the compressor motor, and requires methodical electrical diagnosis before replacing any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low or unstable incoming voltage** Insufficient or fluctuating line voltage at the outdoor unit can cause the inverter to fault and trigger P4.
- **Loose, damaged, or mis-seated wiring and connectors** Bad connections between the main PCB, inverter module, compressor, or fan can interrupt the drive circuit and set the code.
- **Failed IPM or inverter module** The inverter power module that controls the compressor can fail internally and no longer drive the motor correctly.
- **Compressor winding problems** Imbalanced, shorted, open, or grounded compressor windings prevent the inverter from operating normally.
- **Outdoor fan failure or blocked condenser coil** Airflow restrictions or a dead fan overload the inverter section by trapping heat in the outdoor unit.
- **Outdoor main PCB failure** The control board that manages the inverter and compressor can develop burned traces, failed components, or connector corrosion.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill all power** to the indoor and outdoor units at the breaker, wait two minutes, then restore power and see if the P4 code clears on its own.
2. **Measure the supply voltage** at the outdoor unit terminals with a multimeter and compare the reading to the voltage listed on the unit nameplate to confirm it is within range and stable.
3. **Inspect all wiring and harnesses** inside the outdoor unit for loose terminals, burned connectors, corrosion, damaged insulation, or overheated PCB traces.
4. **Check the outdoor fan** for free rotation and proper operation, then clean the condenser coil if it is blocked with dirt or debris.
5. **Test the IPM and inverter module** per the service manual by measuring resistance between the specified terminals (you should see several megaohms and consistent readings between test points).
6. **Check compressor winding resistance** with an ohmmeter to confirm all three phases are balanced and that none are open or grounded to the case.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (IPM/inverter board, outdoor main PCB, compressor, or fan motor) only after your tests have isolated the failure, then cycle power and verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with high-voltage electrical components or if you do not own a multimeter and the tools to safely test inverter modules and compressor windings. Diagnosis of P4 requires measuring live voltage, testing power electronics, and interpreting resistance readings that vary by model. Replacing the wrong part (IPM, PCB, or compressor) is expensive and will not fix the problem if the root cause is not isolated first. A trained technician has the manufacturer service manuals, proper test equipment, and refrigerant recovery tools to diagnose and repair the fault correctly the first time.
