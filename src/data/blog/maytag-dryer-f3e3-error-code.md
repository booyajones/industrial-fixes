## Maytag Dryer F3E3 Error Code — What It Means

F3E3 on a Maytag dryer signals an inlet thermistor fault. The control board is detecting an abnormal signal from the temperature sensor located at the air inlet or blower-side air path. This is not simply a no-heat code. It tells you the dryer cannot correctly measure inlet air temperature, which prevents proper heat regulation.

The code will prevent the dryer from running a normal cycle until the sensor circuit is restored. The fault can be triggered by a failed sensor, damaged wiring, or poor connections in the thermistor circuit. In some cases, restricted airflow or heavy lint buildup can create temperature conditions that cause repeated faulting, even if the sensor itself is still functional.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the electronic control board first. Before ordering a board, measure the inlet thermistor resistance (should read around 50,000 ohms at room temperature) and inspect the wiring harness for loose or corroded terminals.

## Common Causes

- **Failed inlet thermistor (~50%)** The temperature sensor at the inlet has drifted out of range, reads open, or has failed internally.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wiring (~25%)** Connectors at the thermistor or along the harness have poor contact, corrosion, or damaged terminals that interrupt the signal.
- **Harness damage between sensor and control board (~10%)** The wire bundle has been pinched, chafed, or broken, creating an open or intermittent circuit.
- **Restricted airflow or heavy lint buildup (~10%)** Clogged lint screen, vent duct, or blower area causes abnormal inlet temperatures that trigger repeated sensor faults.
- **Faulty electronic control board (~5%)** If the thermistor and all wiring check good, the control board's sensor input circuit may have failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the circuit breaker** and leave power off for at least five minutes, then restore power and run a timed dry cycle to see if the code returns.
2. **Unplug the dryer** (or turn off the breaker again) and pull the unit away from the wall to access the rear or lower panel.
3. **Locate the inlet thermistor** near the blower housing or air-inlet duct and unplug its two-wire connector.
4. **Measure resistance** across the thermistor terminals with a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature, expect a reading around 50,000 ohms.
5. **Inspect the thermistor harness** from the sensor all the way to the control board, looking for loose pins, corrosion, burnt terminals, or chafed insulation.
6. **Replace the inlet thermistor** if it reads open, far from 50,000 ohms, or fluctuates when you gently move the wires.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle. If the code persists and wiring is verified good, replace the electronic control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with a multimeter, accessing internal dryer components, or interpreting resistance readings. A pro can quickly measure the thermistor, trace the wiring harness, and determine whether the control board is at fault. Also call for help if the dryer shares a 240-volt circuit with other appliances and you are unsure how to safely disconnect power, or if repeated repairs have not resolved the code and deeper diagnosis of the control-board sensor circuit is required.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–$50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–$250.
