## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E21 Error — What It Means

The E21 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates an issue related to water flow or temperature detection. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit for your specific heater. Common causes include insufficient flow through the unit, clogged inlet filter screens, or a faulty temperature sensor that cannot accurately read the water temperature. The heater shuts down to protect itself when it cannot maintain safe operating conditions.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor or thermistor before checking the inlet filter screens. Clean both hot and cold inlet screens first, as debris buildup can mimic sensor failures at a fraction of the cost.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screens (~45%)** Sediment and mineral buildup on the fine mesh screens restrict water flow below the minimum threshold required for the heater to operate.
- **Insufficient flow rate (~25%)** Flow below your model's minimum activation threshold (often caused by low supply pressure or too many fixtures open at once) prevents the unit from firing.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~15%)** The flow sensor paddle or turbine may be stuck or damaged, preventing the unit from detecting adequate water flow even when present.
- **Temperature sensor failure (~10%)** A thermistor or temperature sensor that reads incorrectly or fails open can trigger the error as the unit cannot confirm safe outlet temperatures.
- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger (~5%)** Heavy mineral deposits inside the heat exchanger restrict flow and cause back pressure, reducing effective flow through the unit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close both the hot and cold water isolation valves on the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the mounting screws and setting it aside.
3. **Locate the inlet filter screens** on the cold water inlet pipe (and hot outlet if equipped) and unscrew the service caps or union fittings by hand or with a wrench.
4. **Pull out the mesh screens** and rinse them under running water, using a soft brush to remove sediment and mineral buildup. Soak in white vinegar for 10 minutes if heavily scaled.
5. **Reinstall the clean screens** and tighten the service caps or unions snugly, then open the isolation valves slowly to check for leaks.
6. **Restore power** and open a hot water tap fully to test. If the error clears, the job is done. If it persists, check the flow rate at the tap against your model's minimum requirement in the manual.
7. **Test the flow sensor** by disconnecting its wiring harness and checking continuity or resistance per your model's service manual. Replace if readings are out of range or if the sensor paddle is stuck.
8. **Inspect temperature sensors** (thermistors) for damage or corrosion. Measure resistance while cold and compare to the specification chart in your manual. Replace any sensor that reads open or far out of range.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or tankless water heater technician if cleaning the filters and verifying flow rate does not clear the E21 code, if you are uncomfortable working with gas or electrical connections, or if you suspect scale buildup inside the heat exchanger that requires a chemical flush. Professionals have the tools and descaling solutions to clean the internal passages safely. Also call a pro if you need to replace the flow sensor or thermistor and are not confident reading wiring diagrams or testing electronic components with a multimeter. Any work involving gas lines or venting must be performed by a qualified technician to maintain warranty coverage and meet local codes.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
