## GE Dishwasher Not Cleaning Dishes — What's Happening

A GE dishwasher not cleaning dishes is a wash performance symptom, not a fault code. The machine completes or attempts its cycle but does not remove food soil effectively. This happens when the unit cannot generate enough hot water, spray pressure, or spray coverage to wash properly.

GE does not assign a specific error code to this complaint. Instead, it points to mechanical blockages, water supply issues, and user settings as the usual culprits. If your control panel shows an actual fault code, check your model's tech sheet for that specific code.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged filter assembly** Excess food debris in the fine filter or sump area reduces wash performance by blocking water flow and recirculation.
- **Blocked spray arm jets** Food particles or mineral buildup inside the spray arm holes reduce water distribution and prevent effective spray coverage on dishes.
- **Water temperature too low** GE dishwashers cannot heat cold water hot enough to wash effectively and require incoming water at least 120°F from the home supply line.
- **Low fill from restricted inlet valve or screen** Sediment in the inlet screen or a failing water inlet valve prevents the tub from filling to the correct level, reducing wash action.
- **Low water pressure at supply** GE specifies 20 to 120 PSI at the inlet, and pressure below this range can prevent proper fill and spray force.
- **Improper cycle or loading** Using a light or short cycle on heavily soiled loads, or overloading racks so spray arms cannot reach dishes, causes poor cleaning results.
- **Detergent or rinse aid issues** Too little detergent, old detergent, or missing rinse aid can leave dishes dirty even when water and spray function are normal.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the complaint by checking whether the problem affects the top rack, bottom rack, or all dishes to help narrow the cause.
2. Remove and clean the filter assembly and inspect the sump area at the tub bottom for soil buildup or debris.
3. Inspect all spray arms and clear any blocked jets or holes with a small pick or wire, then confirm the arms spin freely by hand.
4. Measure incoming water temperature at the nearest sink with a thermometer and confirm it reaches at least 120°F.
5. Verify the dishwasher is connected to the home's hot water line, not the cold line, and check that supply pressure is between 20 and 120 PSI.
6. Check the water inlet valve screen at the supply connection for sediment or restriction and clean or replace the screen if clogged.
7. Run a test cycle with a heavier wash setting and a known-good detergent load to confirm whether the issue was cycle selection or detergent related.
8. If fill is still low after clearing the screen, test or replace the water inlet valve to restore proper tub fill and wash action.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a tech if you have confirmed hot water supply, cleaned filters and spray arms, and verified cycle settings but dishes still come out dirty. A pro can measure actual fill volume, test the circulation pump and motor, check the diverter or wash impeller for blockage or wear, and run a calibration or service test mode to pinpoint internal faults that are not visible during a normal cycle. If the inlet valve or circulation pump needs replacement and you are not comfortable with water line work or disassembling the sump and pump housing, a service call is the safer option.
