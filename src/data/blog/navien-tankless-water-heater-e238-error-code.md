## Navien E238 Error Code — What It Means

The E238 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a communication or signal problem related to the water flow sensing system. This code typically appears when the control board is not receiving correct data from the flow sensor, which measures how much water is moving through the heater. Because Navien does not publish complete error code meanings in all consumer manuals, the exact trigger for E238 can vary slightly by model series.

The flow sensor tells the unit when to fire and how much heat to produce. When the sensor fails to communicate properly, the heater shuts down to prevent unsafe operation. This can stem from wiring issues, a failed sensor, debris blocking the sensor rotor, or a control board fault. Always consult your specific model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the exact meaning of this code for your unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first, but a simple multimeter continuity check of the flow sensor and its wiring usually identifies the real fault at a fraction of the cost.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or dirty flow sensor (~45%)** The Hall-effect flow sensor can fail electrically or its rotor can become jammed by sediment, preventing accurate flow readings.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~25%)** The multi-pin connector at the flow sensor or the control board can work loose or develop corrosion, interrupting the signal.
- **Control board communication fault (~15%)** The main PCB may have a failed input circuit that cannot read the sensor signal even when the sensor is good.
- **Water pressure or flow rate too low (~10%)** If incoming water pressure drops below the minimum threshold, the sensor may send erratic signals that trigger this code.
- **Damaged sensor wiring harness (~5%)** Rodents, pinching during installation, or heat exposure can break wires inside the insulation, causing intermittent faults.

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you hear the flow sensor rotor spinning when you open a hot tap (a faint whirring near the sensor)?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The rotor is moving, so suspect wiring or the sensor's electronic signal output. </details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the code clear after a power cycle and return only when you use hot water?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault is likely triggered by actual water flow, pointing to the sensor or low flow rate. Inspect the board for burn marks or moisture.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the cold water isolation valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws (typically on the bottom edge) and lifting it away from the unit.
3. **Locate the flow sensor** on the inlet water line (usually a cylindrical component with a wiring harness attached).
4. **Inspect the wiring connector** at the sensor and trace the harness back to the control board, checking for loose pins, corrosion, or damaged insulation.
5. **Disconnect the sensor's electrical connector** and use a multimeter to measure resistance across the sensor terminals (consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance range).
6. **Remove the flow sensor** by unscrewing the retaining bolts or clips, then inspect the rotor chamber for sediment, scale, or a stuck rotor.
7. **Install the replacement flow sensor** (or reinstall the cleaned sensor), reconnect the wiring harness, restore water and power, and test by running hot water to verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with 120-volt wiring, if the error persists after replacing the flow sensor and checking all connections, or if you suspect the control board itself has failed. Gas-fired tankless heaters also require proper venting and combustion checks after any repair, and a pro can perform a full system diagnostic with specialized tools to pinpoint intermittent faults. If your unit is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
