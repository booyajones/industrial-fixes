## LG Oven F6 Error Code — What It Means

F6 on LG ovens and ranges is identified by LG as an Upper Oven Heating Error. The control board has detected a problem with the upper oven's heating circuit, typically related to the temperature sensor (thermistor), its wiring, or the control board's ability to read or manage the heating feedback. This code appears when the sensor reading is out of range, the wiring is damaged, or the control cannot verify proper heating.

LG's published reset procedure is to press CLEAR/OFF or Power ON/OFF and, if the code returns, power-cycle the unit at the breaker for about 30 seconds. If the fault persists after a reset, the issue is hardware-related and requires inspection of the sensor circuit and possibly component replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first when the actual fault is a loose or corroded sensor connector or a failed sensor. Always test the upper oven thermistor and inspect the wiring before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed upper oven temperature sensor or thermistor (~50%)** The sensor reads out of range (open, shorted, or unstable) and the control board cannot verify heating.
- **Loose, damaged, or burned wiring or connectors (~25%)** Corrosion, heat damage, pinched wires, or poor connector fit between the sensor and control board interrupt the signal.
- **Control board or PCB fault (~20%)** The main control misreads the heating feedback or fails to manage the heating circuit correctly even when the sensor and wiring are good.
- **Temporary glitch or power surge (~5%)** A transient event causes a false fault that clears after a power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the exact model number** by checking the rating plate inside the oven door or on the back, because LG error codes can be model-family specific.
2. **Clear the fault** by pressing CLEAR/OFF or Power ON/OFF on the control panel.
3. **Power-cycle the unit** at the breaker by switching it off for about 30 seconds, then restoring power to see if the code returns.
4. **Unplug the range** or lock out the breaker before opening any panels or touching internal wiring.
5. **Access the upper oven sensor** by removing the upper oven interior panels or the rear service panel, depending on your model configuration.
6. **Inspect the sensor connector and wiring harness** for looseness, corrosion, heat damage, melted insulation, or pinched conductors between the sensor and the control board.
7. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter (consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance at room temperature) or replace the sensor if the reading is open, shorted, or unstable.
8. **Replace the sensor** if it tests bad or the wiring if damage is found, then reassemble and restore power.
9. **Run a bake or preheat cycle** in the upper oven to confirm the F6 code does not recur and the oven heats normally.
10. **Replace the main control board** if the sensor and wiring test good but the code persists, or call a qualified technician for board-level diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the F6 code returns after you have replaced the upper oven sensor and verified the wiring is intact, if you are uncomfortable working with 240 V electrical connections, or if your model requires disassembly beyond removing interior oven panels. A technician can perform precise resistance and voltage tests using the model-specific service manual, diagnose control board faults accurately, and replace the board safely. Also call if you suspect the wiring harness is damaged inside the chassis where routing is complex or if the oven is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
