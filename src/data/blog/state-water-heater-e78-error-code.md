## State Water Heater E78 Error Code — What It Means

The E78 code on a State water heater (part of the A. O. Smith family) indicates an ignition or combustion fault. While State does not publish a single universal definition for E78 across all models, this code type typically appears when the unit cannot light or sustain a flame. Common triggers include blocked air intake screens, low gas pressure, insufficient water flow, venting restrictions, or a dirty flame sensor. Because State produces many tankless and tank-style models with different control boards, always verify the exact code definition by scanning the QR code on your unit's rating plate to pull the correct manual.

On tankless State units, ignition-related codes like E78 are most often traced to blocked air intake paths. Lint, dust, or debris on the intake screens starves the burner of combustion air and prevents safe ignition. Other frequent causes include low gas supply pressure, clogged inlet water filters (which reduce flow below the minimum firing threshold), restricted exhaust vents, and contaminated flame rods that fail to prove ignition.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens or intake path** Lint, dust, or debris on the combustion air intake prevents adequate airflow and stops the burner from igniting safely.
- **Low gas pressure or closed gas valve** Insufficient gas supply pressure or a partially closed manual shutoff valve starves the burner and triggers ignition failure.
- **Restricted water flow or dirty inlet filter** A clogged inlet screen or low flow rate falls below the minimum threshold needed for the tankless unit to fire.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (flame rod)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame rod prevent the control board from detecting the flame, causing a shutdown.
- **Vent or exhaust blockage** Obstructed, improperly terminated, or restricted exhaust venting prevents safe combustion and halts ignition.
- **Scale or soot buildup in the combustion chamber** Accumulated debris in the heat exchanger or burner area disrupts flame stability and triggers recurring fault codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact model** by locating the rating plate on the water heater and scanning the QR code or noting the model and serial number to pull the correct State manual and troubleshooting chart for your unit.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the front or side of the unit, removing any lint, dust, pet hair, or debris that blocks the combustion air supply.
3. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve is fully open and verifying that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally to rule out a supply interruption.
4. **Inspect and clean the inlet water filter** by shutting off the cold water inlet valve, removing the filter screen, flushing debris, and reinstalling it to restore proper flow.
5. **Examine the exhaust vent path** for blockages, disconnected joints, debris, or improper termination, and clear or correct any restrictions you find.
6. **Clean the flame sensor (flame rod)** by shutting off power and gas, carefully removing the sensor from the burner assembly, wiping it with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits, and reinstalling it.
7. **Power-cycle the unit** by turning off the heater at the disconnect or breaker for 30 seconds, then restoring power and checking whether the code clears and the unit fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E78 code returns after you have cleaned the intake screens, confirmed gas supply, flushed the inlet filter, and cleared the vent path. Persistent ignition faults often require gas-pressure testing with a manometer, detailed flame-sensor or ignition-module diagnostics, or inspection of the heat exchanger for scale and soot that only a qualified technician can safely perform. Any work involving gas lines, pressure testing, or control-board replacement should be handled by a professional to protect your warranty and make sure code compliance.
