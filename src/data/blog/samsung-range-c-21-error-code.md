## What this code means
The C-21 code is an overheating protection fault. The control board has detected that the oven cavity temperature has exceeded its safe operating limit and has shut down to protect both the appliance and the user. Field observations suggest this may trip when internal temperatures climb above roughly 500–600°F, though Samsung has not published an exact threshold in available documentation.

This is not a door-lock or touch-panel error. The oven believes it is too hot and refuses to continue heating until the condition clears. The fault can appear during a bake cycle or self-clean if the temperature rises faster or higher than the control expects.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when the real culprit is the oven temperature sensor. Test the sensor with a multimeter at room temperature before ordering any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor** A drifting or open NTC thermistor causes the control to read false high temperatures or lose regulation entirely.
- **Blocked vents or poor airflow** Grease, debris, or obstructions around the rear or upper vent paths trap heat in the cavity or electronics area.
- **Stuck relay or control-board fault** A welded bake relay or malfunctioning control board fails to cycle the heating element off, causing runaway heat.
- **Voltage spikes or power anomalies** Electrical surges can confuse the control or cause the relay to behave erratically.
- **Software or control glitch** Some smart models misread or misprocess sensor data, triggering a false overheat alarm.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cancel the bake cycle and allow the oven to cool completely.** If the error is active, stop all heating and wait for the cavity to return to room temperature before troubleshooting.
2. **Power-cycle the appliance.** Unplug the range or switch off the dedicated breaker for at least two minutes, then restore power and check whether the error clears.
3. **Inspect and clear all vent paths.** Remove any grease, debris, or obstructions from the rear and upper vents to make sure unrestricted airflow around the cavity and electronics.
4. **Test the oven temperature sensor with a multimeter.** Disconnect the sensor harness, measure resistance at room temperature, and compare to the expected value (commonly around 1,000–1,100 ohms, but consult your model's service data).
5. **Check the bake relay and control-board connections.** If the sensor tests good, inspect the bake relay for welded contacts, burned connectors, or signs of overheating on the control board.
6. **Replace the faulty component.** Install a new temperature sensor or control board as indicated by your tests, reconnect all wiring, and verify proper seating of connectors.
7. **Run a monitored test bake.** Set the oven to 350°F and observe the first 20 minutes of operation to confirm the heating element cycles normally and the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt appliance wiring, if the control board shows signs of arcing or burned traces, or if the error persists after you have replaced the temperature sensor and verified clear airflow. A professional can perform relay-continuity tests under load, verify correct voltage at the bake element, and rule out intermittent control-board faults that are difficult to catch without monitoring equipment. If the oven is still under warranty, contact Samsung service before opening the appliance to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–60 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
