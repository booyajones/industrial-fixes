## State Water Heater E162 Error Code — What It Means

The E162 code on a State water heater signals a high-limit or over-temperature lockout. The unit sensed an unsafe temperature condition and shut itself down to prevent damage or hazard. This is a safety feature built into State and A. O. Smith electronic controls, common across tankless and certain tank-style models. The heater will not resume normal operation until the fault is cleared and the system is reset.

Because State uses different code sets across product families, the exact definition and diagnostic procedure for E162 may vary by model. Always check the rating plate QR code or model number against the manufacturer's literature before beginning repairs. The code typically points to a flow, combustion, or sensor problem rather than a simple thermostat adjustment.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted water flow** Clogged inlet screens, partially closed isolation valves, or blocked fixtures prevent adequate flow through the heat exchanger and trigger over-temperature protection.
- **Scale buildup in heat exchanger** Hard water deposits reduce heat transfer efficiency, causing localized hot spots that trip the high-limit sensor.
- **Venting or combustion air blockage** Obstructed exhaust vents or insufficient combustion air supply on gas units can lead to overheating and fault codes.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermistor** A sensor that reads incorrectly or fails under heat stress will send false over-temperature signals to the control board.
- **Control board or wiring faults** Damaged connectors, corrosion, or intermittent opens in the high-limit circuit can cause erratic lockouts even when physical conditions are normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the model number** on the rating plate and scan the QR code or look up the manual online to confirm the E162 definition and diagnostic steps for your specific State model.
2. **Allow the unit to cool completely** before resetting the fault. If the code returns immediately after reset without any hot water demand, treat it as an active safety issue.
3. **Inspect and clean the inlet water filter** or screen at the cold water inlet. Check that isolation valves are fully open and that supply pressure is adequate.
4. **Check for scale deposits** by inspecting accessible heat exchanger surfaces or performing a vinegar or descaling flush if your model and manual allow it. Follow the manufacturer's flush procedure and verify flow afterward.
5. **Verify venting and combustion air** paths are clear. Look for obstructions in the exhaust vent, air intake, or around the unit. Correct any installation defects or blockages.
6. **Test the temperature sensor** using the resistance or voltage values given in your model's service manual. Replace the sensor if readings are out of range, erratic, or unstable under heat.
7. **Run a full demand cycle** after repairs to confirm steady inlet and outlet temperatures, normal ignition sequence, and no recurrence of the E162 code. If the fault persists, inspect the control board wiring harness and connectors for damage or corrosion.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water heater technician if you are not comfortable working with gas lines, electrical controls, or high-temperature water systems. If the code returns after you have cleaned filters, checked venting, and verified water flow, the issue likely requires diagnostic equipment and model-specific test procedures. Professionals have access to manufacturer service manuals, thermistor resistance tables, and voltage specs that are not published in general homeowner guides. Any work involving the control board, gas valve, or sealed combustion chamber should be left to a qualified technician.
