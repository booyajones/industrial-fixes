## Navien Tankless Water Heater E173 Error — What It Means

The E173 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a problem with the flow detection system. The control board is receiving an unusual or out-of-range signal from the flow sensor, or the sensor is not detecting flow correctly when the unit attempts to fire. This can happen when the sensor is fouled with sediment or scale, when the sensor itself has failed, or when there is an issue with the wiring harness connecting the sensor to the main control board.

Because tankless water heaters rely on flow sensing to determine when to ignite and modulate the burner, any disruption in this signal will prevent the unit from heating water reliably. The E173 code is the control board's way of reporting that it cannot verify proper water flow through the heat exchanger, which is a safety and operational concern.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first, assuming an electronics failure. In most cases the flow sensor or its connections are at fault. Inspect and clean the flow sensor before replacing any circuit boards.

## Common Causes

- **Flow sensor contaminated (~50%)** Sediment, scale, or mineral buildup on the flow sensor paddle or turbine prevents accurate flow readings and triggers the error.
- **Failed flow sensor (~25%)** Internal electrical failure or mechanical wear in the flow sensor causes it to send incorrect signals to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~15%)** The connector at the flow sensor or the wiring harness develops corrosion or works loose, interrupting the signal path.
- **Clogged inlet filter (~10%)** A severely blocked inlet filter restricts flow to the point where the sensor cannot detect it reliably, even if the sensor is clean.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the cold-water supply valve to the heater, then open a hot tap downstream to relieve pressure.
2. **Remove the front service panel** and locate the flow sensor on the inlet water line (consult your model's service manual for exact position).
3. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the flow sensor by pressing the locking tab and pulling the plug straight out.
4. **Unscrew the flow sensor** from the water line using an adjustable wrench, catching any residual water with a towel.
5. **Inspect and clean the sensor** paddle or turbine with white vinegar or a descaling solution, flushing away all sediment and scale buildup.
6. **Reinstall the sensor** with a new O-ring or gasket if supplied, torque to hand-tight plus one-quarter turn, and reconnect the wiring harness until it clicks.
7. **Open the water supply** slowly, check for leaks around the sensor body, then restore power and run hot water to verify the error has cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after cleaning the flow sensor and checking all wiring, or if you lack the tools to safely disassemble and reassemble water connections. Professionals can also test the sensor with a multimeter to confirm electrical continuity and resistance values, and can inspect the control board for related faults. If your water supply has high mineral content and scale buildup recurs frequently, a pro can recommend and install a whole-house water softener or inline descaler to protect the tankless heater long term.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
