## EcoSmart Eco 21 Error Codes — What It Means

EcoSmart does not publish a fault code labeled 'ECO21.' If your unit displays an error, it will show E1, E2, E3, E4, E5, or S103. E1 signals an inlet thermistor problem. E2 indicates an outlet thermistor fault. E3 means both thermistors or the broader temperature-sensing circuit has failed. E4 appears when incoming water temperature is too high or the unit is overheating. E5 flags excessive flow rate beyond the heater's capacity. S103 points to low voltage or a power supply issue.

These codes help you isolate whether the problem is electrical, flow-related, or sensor-based. Most faults trace back to water flow outside the design window, faulty thermistors, or supply power issues. The Eco 21 activates at approximately 0.25 GPM, so any flow below that threshold will prevent the unit from firing.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty inlet or outlet thermistor** A failed sensor triggers E1, E2, or E3 and prevents accurate temperature readings.
- **Flow rate too high or too low** E5 appears when flow exceeds heating capacity, and no activation occurs below 0.25 GPM.
- **High incoming water temperature** E4 fires when supply water is already hot or the unit overheats during operation.
- **Low voltage or breaker fault** S103 or no display at all indicates inadequate power, tripped breakers, or incorrect wiring.
- **Crossed or loose electrical connections** New installations often show errors due to miswired phases or poor terminal contact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check the breaker panel** and confirm all breakers supplying the Eco 21 are on and not tripped, then verify voltage at the unit terminals matches your model's requirements.
2. **Measure flow rate** at the faucet or shower and reduce it if E5 appears, or increase it if the unit will not activate below 0.25 GPM.
3. **Inspect the inlet filter screen** for debris and flush the inlet strainer to restore proper flow and pressure.
4. **Test or replace the inlet thermistor** if E1 is displayed, and the outlet thermistor if E2 is shown, by disconnecting power, removing the sensor, and swapping in a new part.
5. **Address E3 codes** by replacing both thermistors or checking the wiring harness and sensor connector for corrosion or loose pins.
6. **Lower incoming water temperature** if E4 persists by adjusting your water heater or tempering valve upstream, or reduce flow rate to allow the unit to modulate properly.
7. **Consult the wiring diagram** on the inside cover if S103 or intermittent faults occur, and tighten all terminal screws and wire nuts to eliminate voltage sag under load.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician or plumber if you see S103 and cannot locate the voltage fault, if E3 persists after replacing both thermistors, or if the unit shows no display despite confirmed breaker and wiring integrity. Recurring E4 or E5 codes after adjusting flow and temperature may indicate a failed flow sensor, heating-element fault, or control-board issue that requires factory parts and diagnostic equipment. Always shut off power at the breaker before opening the cover, and contact EcoSmart support if error codes return immediately after part replacement.
