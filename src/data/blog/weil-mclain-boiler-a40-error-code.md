## Weil-McLain A40 Error Code — What It Means

A40 on a Weil-McLain boiler signals an ignition-failure lockout. The control called for heat, attempted ignition, and did not detect a valid flame signal. As a safety measure, the system locks out to prevent unburned gas from accumulating. The exact fault code meaning can vary by model and control board, so always consult your specific boiler's manual. On some Weil-McLain families (such as AquaBalance), similar ignition faults may appear as A01 rather than A40.

This is not a water-pressure or overtemperature fault. Those are separate fault classes on Weil-McLain controls. When you see A40, the boiler is telling you it cannot establish or prove flame during the ignition sequence.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first, when the real problem is a dirty or misaligned ignition electrode or a closed gas valve. Check gas supply and clean the electrode before swapping electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Gas valve closed or no gas supply (~35%)** The manual gas cock is shut, the gas meter is off, or a new installation was not properly purged.
- **Dirty, damaged, or misaligned ignition or flame-sensing electrode (~30%)** Carbon buildup, corrosion, or incorrect gap prevents reliable spark or flame detection.
- **Insufficient gas supply pressure (~15%)** Inlet pressure below the boiler's required range (for example, AquaBalance requires 3.5 to 11 in. w.c.) starves the burner.
- **Faulty gas valve (~10%)** The valve receives the signal to open but does not deliver gas to the burner.
- **Dirty heat exchanger or burner assembly (~7%)** Soot, scale, or debris blocks airflow or flame spread, preventing the flame sensor from detecting a stable flame.
- **Control board or internal controller fault (~3%)** A failed ignition module or board misreads the flame signal even when everything else is working correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your exact boiler model and control type.** Pull the installation or service manual from Weil-McLain's website or the paperwork packet on the boiler. Confirm that A40 is listed in the fault table for your model.
2. **Check the manual gas shutoff valve** upstream of the boiler. Make sure it is fully open. On new installations, confirm that the gas line has been purged of air.
3. **Inspect the ignition and flame-sensing electrodes.** Remove the burner cover or access panel. Look for carbon buildup, corrosion, cracks, or bent rods. Clean both electrodes with fine emery cloth or a Scotch-Brite pad. Check that the gap and position match the dimensions in the service manual.
4. **Test for spark during a call for heat.** With power on and the burner cover removed (if safe to do so), initiate a heat call and watch the ignitor. You should see a visible spark. If you do not, suspect the ignitor assembly, wiring, or control board.
5. **Measure inlet gas pressure** if you have a manometer and the boiler manual specifies a range (for example, 3.5 to 11 in. w.c. on AquaBalance models). Low pressure requires adjustments at the gas meter or regulator by a licensed technician or gas utility.
6. **Test the gas valve.** If spark is present but no flame lights, check that the gas valve receives the correct voltage signal from the control during ignition. If voltage is present but the valve does not click open, replace the gas valve.
7. **Clean the heat exchanger and burner.** If the boiler is overdue for annual service, remove and vacuum the burner assembly, clean the heat exchanger passages, and inspect for soot or scale that can block airflow or interfere with flame detection.
8. **Reset the lockout once** after completing repairs. If the A40 fault returns immediately, do not keep resetting. Treat it as an unresolved fault and call for professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician any time you are uncomfortable working around natural gas or propane, cannot safely access the burner chamber, or lack a multimeter and manometer to test voltage and gas pressure. Gas appliances require precise combustion adjustments and leak-free connections. If you have confirmed the gas valve is open and the electrodes are clean but the fault persists, a technician will test gas pressure, valve operation, flame current, and control-board signals in sequence to pinpoint the failure. Do not attempt repeated resets or bypass any safety lockouts. Ignition-failure codes exist to protect you from gas buildup and potential explosions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
