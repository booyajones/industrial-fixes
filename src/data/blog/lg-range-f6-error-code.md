## LG Range F6 Error Code — What It Means

F6 is an Upper Oven Heating Error according to LG USA Support. The control has detected an abnormal heating condition in the upper oven cavity and has stopped normal operation to protect the appliance. One third-party source notes that F6 (sometimes displayed as F6F16 or F16) can indicate temperature exceeding 650°F during cooking, though LG's official description is broader and simply refers to a heating error. The fault does not appear during self-cleaning mode.

LG instructs users to press CLEAR/OFF or Power ON/OFF to clear the code, then power-cycle the range at the breaker for 30 seconds if the fault returns. If the code persists after a full reset, the upper oven heating circuit or its sensing and control components need diagnosis and repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Test the upper oven temperature sensor for continuity and resistance at room temperature before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed upper oven temperature sensor (~45%)** The sensor (thermistor) can open, short, or drift out of range so the control cannot accurately verify oven temperature and throws F6.
- **Wiring or connector damage in the upper oven sensor circuit (~25%)** Loose terminals, heat-damaged harnesses, or corroded connectors between the sensor and the control board interrupt or distort the signal.
- **Stuck heating relay or control board failure (~20%)** A welded relay contact or failed switching circuit on the control board can energize the upper oven heater uncontrollably, causing rapid temperature rise.
- **Shorted upper oven heating element (~10%)** A short in the heater element itself can draw excessive current and generate uncontrolled heat that the control interprets as an error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the error and power-cycle the range.** Press CLEAR/OFF or Power ON/OFF on the control panel, then turn off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds and restore power as LG instructs.
2. **Reproduce the fault.** Run the upper oven in bake mode at a moderate temperature (for example 350°F) and watch whether F6 returns during preheat or steady-state operation.
3. **Locate and inspect the upper oven temperature sensor.** Unplug the range, remove the rear access panel or upper oven interior panel (consult your model's service manual), and find the sensor probe mounted inside the oven cavity.
4. **Test the sensor for continuity and resistance.** Disconnect the sensor harness plug and measure resistance across the sensor terminals at room temperature with a multimeter. Compare the reading to your model's specification table. Replace the sensor if it reads open, shorted, or far outside the expected range.
5. **Inspect all wiring and connectors.** Check the sensor harness, terminals at the control board, and any inline connectors for heat damage, corrosion, looseness, or broken wires. Repair or replace any damaged sections.
6. **Check for a stuck relay or shorted heater.** If the sensor and wiring test good, measure continuity across the upper oven heating element with power off. An unexpected short to ground or a relay that stays closed when the control is off points to a board or element fault.
7. **Reassemble and verify.** Install the new sensor or control board, secure all connectors, restore power, clear any stored codes, and run a full bake cycle in the upper oven to confirm F6 does not return and temperature cycles normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet, if the fault returns after you have replaced the sensor and inspected all wiring, or if you need to diagnose the control board relay circuits with specialized test equipment. Technicians have the service manual, wiring diagrams, and component-level test procedures to isolate stuck relays, shorted heating elements, or control board failures that are not obvious from basic continuity checks. If your range is still under warranty or you lack a multimeter and the confidence to safely disconnect and test electrical components, professional service is the best path.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-70 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
