## State Water Heater E151 Error Code — What It Means

The E151 code on State tankless water heaters indicates an ignition failure or combustion failure to start. The unit is attempting to fire but cannot establish or verify a flame. State tankless platforms share design and fault logic with A. O. Smith units, and this code means the ignition sequence has been interrupted or aborted. The heater will not produce hot water until the underlying cause is corrected.

Because State does not publish a universal E151 definition across all models, you should retrieve your exact model number from the rating plate or scan the QR code on the unit to access the model-specific manual and diagnostic chart. The most common real-world causes are blocked air intake screens, insufficient gas supply, or low water flow from a clogged inlet filter.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or vent screen** Lint, dust, or debris on the intake screen restricts combustion air and prevents ignition.
- **Dirty inlet water filter** A clogged filter reduces flow below the minimum gallons-per-minute threshold needed to trigger ignition.
- **Insufficient gas supply or low gas pressure** Closed valve, regulator issue, or undersized line starves the burner and blocks flame establishment.
- **Contaminated flame rod or sensor** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame-proving electrode prevents the board from confirming ignition.
- **Failed igniter or spark electrode** A cracked or fouled electrode cannot generate the spark needed to light the gas.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your exact model number** on the rating plate and scan the QR code or visit the State support site to download the correct service manual and verify the E151 definition for your unit.
2. **Turn off power and gas** at the shutoff valves before inspecting or cleaning any components.
3. **Remove and clean the air intake screen** by pulling the cover or grille and rinsing debris under running water, then reinstall it completely dry.
4. **Remove the inlet water filter** from the cold-water inlet, rinse it under high pressure to clear sediment, and replace it if the mesh is torn or heavily scaled.
5. **Verify the gas shutoff valve is fully open** and check that other gas appliances in the home are firing normally to rule out supply or pressure problems.
6. **Inspect the flame rod and igniter** inside the burner chamber for carbon deposits or cracks, gently clean the rod with fine sandpaper if accessible, and replace if damaged.
7. **Restore power and gas, then test** by opening a hot tap to call for heat and watch for ignition through the viewing window or listen for the burner to light and remain stable.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the error persists after you have cleaned the intake and filter and confirmed gas supply. Measuring inlet and manifold gas pressures, testing ignition timing and flame current, and adjusting or replacing the gas valve require calibrated gauges and combustion-analysis tools. If you cannot retrieve the correct manual for your model or if multiple error codes appear in sequence, a technician with State or A. O. Smith training will diagnose the board, wiring, and flame-proving circuit safely.
