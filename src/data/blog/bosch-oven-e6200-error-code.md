## What this code means
E6200 is a fan-related fault code on Bosch ovens. Independent Bosch and Siemens troubleshooting guidance identifies E6200 as a problem with the fan in the oven. The fault typically appears when you start a convection or CircoTherm mode and the oven stops heating. The error points to a failure in the convection fan circuit, which can include the fan motor, the fan blade, the wiring and connectors that supply power to the fan, or the control board output that drives the fan.

The code does not indicate a heating element or temperature sensor problem. Field reports from Bosch and Neff ovens confirm the fault is isolated to fan-based cooking modes and involves either a mechanical obstruction of the fan, a failed fan motor, damaged wiring, or a control board that cannot properly drive the fan circuit. Diagnosis requires inspecting the fan area for obstructions, checking the fan wiring and connectors, and testing the fan motor and control outputs.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first, assuming an error code always means electronics. Instead, disconnect power and inspect the fan area for debris and check the fan harness connectors for looseness or damage before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Fan motor failure (~40%)** The convection fan motor windings burn out, bearings seize, or the motor does not respond to control signals, preventing the fan from spinning during convection modes.
- **Fan obstruction or blade damage (~25%)** Debris, food particles, or a bent fan blade prevent the fan from turning freely or cause the motor to stall and trip the fault.
- **Loose or damaged fan wiring or connector (~20%)** The harness plug at the fan motor becomes unseated, the pins corrode, or the wires break due to heat or vibration, interrupting the circuit.
- **Crooked or misaligned fan mounting (~10%)** The fan motor or shroud is installed off-center or the mounting hardware loosens, causing the blade to rub and the motor to draw excess current or stall.
- **Control board fan-drive circuit failure (~5%)** The electronic control board loses the ability to supply power or control signals to the fan motor, even though the motor and wiring are intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect all power** to the oven at the breaker and wait at least five minutes for stored charge in capacitors to dissipate before touching any internal components.
2. **Remove the oven's rear panel or interior back wall** to access the convection fan assembly, following your model's service manual for screw locations and panel clips.
3. **Inspect the fan area for debris** and try turning the fan blade by hand to confirm it spins freely without rubbing or binding against the shroud or housing.
4. **Check the fan motor wiring harness and connector** for secure seating, bent or corroded pins, heat damage, or broken wires, and reseat or repair any loose connections.
5. **Test the fan motor** by checking for continuity across its terminals (consult your model's wiring diagram for pin identification) and replace the motor if it shows an open circuit or the bearings are seized.
6. **Examine the fan mounting hardware** to confirm the motor and blade are aligned straight and the shroud is not warped or pressing against the blade.
7. **Reassemble the oven, restore power, and run a convection cycle** to verify the fan spins and the E6200 code does not return, confirming the repair resolved the fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt oven wiring or if you do not have a multimeter and wiring diagram to safely test the fan circuit. A professional can isolate whether the fault is in the fan motor, the harness, or the control board without guessing, and many Bosch ovens require partial disassembly of the cabinet and insulation to reach the fan. If the fan motor and wiring check good but the code returns, the control board may need specialized diagnostics or programming that only a trained tech or authorized Bosch service center can perform.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on whether the fan motor, wiring harness, or control board needs replacement.
