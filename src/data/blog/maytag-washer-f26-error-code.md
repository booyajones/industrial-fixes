## What this code means
On Maytag top-load washers, F26 is a door switch error or lid switch fault. The control board is not receiving the expected signal from the lid-switch circuit, so it cannot verify that the lid is properly closed and latched. The machine will not run a cycle until this fault is resolved.

This code is specific to Maytag top-load platforms. It tells you the switch circuit is reporting an unexpected state, either because the lid is not mechanically latched, the switch itself has failed, or there is a wiring problem in that circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some people replace the control board thinking it is reading the switch incorrectly. Test the lid switch for proper actuation and inspect the wiring harness first before assuming a board fault.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty lid switch assembly (~50%)** The switch contacts fail or the actuator does not report the closed state correctly, so the control never sees a valid latch signal.
- **Lid not fully closed or latched (~25%)** The lid appears shut but the latch mechanism has not clicked into place, leaving the switch open.
- **Loose or damaged wiring at the lid-switch connector (~15%)** The harness plug is partially unseated or wires have broken at the switch terminals, interrupting the circuit.
- **Repeated cycles without opening the lid (~10%)** Running back-to-back cycles without ever opening the door can leave the control in a state mismatch that triggers F26.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker, wait 5 minutes, then restore power to attempt a control reset.
2. **Open and close the lid** firmly, listening for a distinct click that confirms the latch has engaged.
3. **Unplug the washer** and pull it forward to access the rear or top cabinet screws, then remove the cabinet or top panel according to your model's service manual.
4. **Locate the lid-switch assembly** near the lid hinge or striker, disconnect the wiring harness plug, and inspect both the connector pins and the switch body for damage or corrosion.
5. **Test the switch** by using a multimeter set to continuity (if you have one) and manually actuating the switch lever or plunger to confirm it opens and closes the circuit, or simply replace the switch if it shows any physical damage or does not click.
6. **Install the new lid switch** by plugging in the harness, securing any mounting clips or screws, and reassembling the cabinet.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to verify the F26 code does not reappear and the washer completes a normal wash.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the cabinet, if the wiring harness shows widespread damage that suggests a larger electrical fault, or if replacing the lid switch does not clear the F26 code. A pro can trace the switch circuit back to the control board and check for board-level faults or a defective striker mechanism that may not be obvious during a visual inspection. Also call for help if your washer is still under warranty, since opening the cabinet yourself may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
