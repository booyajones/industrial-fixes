## Maytag Washer F26 Error Code — What It Means

The F26 code on a Maytag washer indicates a door-switch or door-closure fault. The control believes the door is not securely closed, the door switch is damaged, or the switch signal does not match normal operation. This code appears when the washer detects that the door has not been opened after multiple cycles, the door is not fully latched, or the door switch circuit is sending an invalid signal.

The washer will not start or continue the cycle until the door state is resolved. The fault can be intermittent if the switch contact is worn or the wiring connection is loose. In many cases a simple door re-latch or power reset clears the code, but a failed door switch assembly will require replacement to restore normal operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when F26 appears, but the fault almost always lives at the door switch itself or its wiring. Inspect the door switch, latch area, and harness connector first before ordering any control electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Door not fully latched (~40%)** The door is not pushed shut completely or the strike and latch are not aligned, so the switch never closes the circuit.
- **Faulty door switch (~35%)** The door switch assembly has failed mechanically or electrically and no longer changes state when the door closes.
- **Loose or corroded wiring at the door switch (~15%)** The harness connector at the door switch is loose, corroded, or has a broken terminal that interrupts the signal to the control.
- **Worn door strike or latch hardware (~8%)** The plastic strike on the door frame or the latch tongue on the door is worn or broken, preventing the switch plunger from being fully actuated.
- **Obstruction in the door seal or latch area (~2%)** A piece of clothing, detergent buildup, or foreign object is blocking the door from closing far enough to trip the switch.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the washer to make sure safe work.
2. **Inspect the door latch area** for clothing, detergent residue, or damage to the strike and latch tongue. Clean or remove any obstructions.
3. **Close the door firmly** and listen for a distinct click. If the door does not latch securely, adjust the strike alignment or replace worn latch hardware.
4. **Reset the washer** by leaving power off for at least one minute, then restore power and attempt a test cycle to see if F26 clears.
5. **Access the door switch** by removing the top panel (on top-load models) or the front panel and door boot (on front-load models, consult your service manual for the exact procedure).
6. **Inspect the door switch harness connector** for loose terminals, corrosion, or broken wires. Clean contacts with electrical contact cleaner and reseat the connector firmly.
7. **Test the door switch** for continuity with a multimeter across its terminals while manually actuating the plunger. Replace the switch assembly if it does not change state or shows no continuity when closed.
8. **Reassemble the washer**, restore power, and run a short test cycle to confirm F26 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable removing panels to access the door switch, if the wiring harness appears damaged beyond the connector, or if replacing the door switch does not resolve F26. A pro can trace the circuit back to the main control, check for intermittent faults with specialized meters, and confirm that no deeper control-board issue is masking a simpler door-switch fault. Professional diagnosis is also wise if your washer is still under warranty or if you see any signs of water damage or arcing at the switch area.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
