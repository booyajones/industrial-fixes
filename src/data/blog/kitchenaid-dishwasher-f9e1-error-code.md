## KitchenAid Dishwasher F9E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F9E1 code on a KitchenAid dishwasher signals a drain fault. The control board has detected that water is not leaving the tub within the expected time window. This is a drain-path logic error, not a fill or heating problem.

The dishwasher may stop mid-cycle, leave standing water in the bottom, or flash the code at the end of a wash. The fault points to a blockage, restriction, or weak pump somewhere in the drain system rather than a failed pump in most cases.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump or control board when the real culprit is a disposer knockout plug left in place or a kinked hose. Always verify the drain hose and disposer inlet are completely clear before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Garbage disposer knockout plug not removed (~35%)** On new installations the plastic knockout inside the disposer inlet must be punched out before connecting the dishwasher drain hose.
- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~30%)** A pinch, twist, or food debris inside the hose prevents water from exiting quickly enough.
- **Blocked drain pump or impeller (~20%)** Food particles, broken glass, or hard water buildup can jam the impeller or restrict flow through the pump housing.
- **Clogged filter or sump area (~10%)** Debris in the bottom filter basket or sump can slow water flow to the pump and trigger a timeout.
- **Control board false fault (~5%)** Occasionally the board logs F9E1 even when the drain system works correctly in manual tests, pointing to a control-logic or sensor issue.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off** the dishwasher at the breaker or unplug it for 60 seconds to reset the control board, then restore power and run a short cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Check the garbage disposer** if your dishwasher drains into one. Remove the hose from the disposer inlet and look inside the nipple for the knockout plug. If present, knock it out from inside the disposer with a screwdriver and hammer, then fish out the plug.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** from the dishwasher to the sink or air gap. Look for kinks, pinches, or loops that sag below the cabinet floor. Disconnect both ends and flush with water if you suspect a clog.
4. **Remove and clean the filter** in the bottom of the tub. Twist or lift it out according to your model, rinse under running water, and check the sump opening below for debris or buildup.
5. **Access the drain pump** by removing the lower spray arm, filter, and any cover plates. Spin the impeller by hand to confirm it turns freely. Remove any glass, plastic, or food caught in the blades.
6. **Run a manual drain test** by pouring a gallon of water into the empty tub and starting a drain cycle. If water exits quickly, the drain path is clear and the fault may be in the control board or wiring.
7. **Replace the drain pump** if the impeller is damaged, the motor hums but does not spin, or water does not exit during the manual test. If all mechanical checks pass and the code persists, consult a technician to test the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have confirmed the drain hose is clear, the filter is clean, the impeller spins freely, and the dishwasher still throws F9E1. At that point the fault is likely in the control board, the pressure switch, or wiring that requires meter diagnostics. Also call if you are uncomfortable disconnecting plumbing or working inside the tub. A pro visit typically costs between $150 and $300 and includes diagnosis and the first hour of labor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (hose or pump), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
