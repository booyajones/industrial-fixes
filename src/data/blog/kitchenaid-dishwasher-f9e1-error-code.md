## KitchenAid Dishwasher F9E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F9E1 error code on a KitchenAid dishwasher signals "Can't Drain Cycle Power." The control board has detected that the unit failed to evacuate water properly during the cycle. This is a drain-system fault, not a fill or temperature issue.

The code appears when the dishwasher times out waiting for water to leave the tub. Common triggers include blocked drain paths, kinked hoses, a plugged garbage disposer connection, or excess suds interfering with water management. In field practice, plumbing and obstruction issues account for the majority of F9E1 calls. When those check out and the code persists, a failing drain pump or control-board fault is the next suspect.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump first, but most F9E1 codes are caused by kinked hoses or a forgotten disposal knockout plug. Inspect the entire drain path and test the disposer connection before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~35%)** A pinched, looped, or debris-filled drain hose prevents water from leaving the tub and triggers the F9E1 timeout.
- **Blocked garbage disposer or missing knockout plug (~30%)** When the dishwasher drains through a disposer, a forgotten knockout plug or food debris in the disposer inlet blocks flow and the control flags F9E1.
- **Food debris obstruction in the drain system (~15%)** Glass shards, labels, or food particles can lodge in the sump, drain valve, or hose and prevent proper evacuation.
- **Excess suds or incorrect detergent (~10%)** Using regular dish soap or too much detergent creates foam that interferes with the drain sensor and water-level management, causing a false or real drain fault.
- **Failing drain pump (~8%)** When plumbing checks are clear and the unit still times out, the drain pump may have a jammed impeller, failed motor, or open winding.
- **Main control board fault (~2%)** In rare cases the control board misreads water level or drain status even when the pump operates correctly, requiring board replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds, then restore power and check if the code clears.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** from the dishwasher pump outlet to the disposal or drain line for kinks, loops below the connection point, or visible clogs.
3. **Check the garbage disposer connection** and confirm the knockout plug was removed during installation. Run the disposer to clear any debris in the inlet.
4. **Remove the lower dish rack and spray arm**, lift out the filter assembly, and inspect the sump and drain area for food particles, glass, or foreign objects.
5. **Run a drain cycle** and listen for the drain pump. If it hums but does not pump, turn off power and check the impeller for obstructions or a seized bearing.
6. **Test the drain pump electrically** by disconnecting power, removing the pump, and measuring resistance across the motor terminals (typical pumps show 30-150 ohms, but consult your model's service sheet for the exact spec).
7. **Replace the drain pump** if it is jammed, open, or does not run. If the pump works and water drains normally but F9E1 persists, the main control board is the remaining suspect and should be replaced or diagnosed by a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have confirmed the drain hose is clear, the disposer connection is open, the sump is clean, and the drain pump runs but F9E1 still appears after a reset. In that scenario the main control board is likely misreading water level or drain status, and board-level diagnostics require a technician with a service manual and multimeter. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with the dishwasher's internal plumbing, pump wiring, or if the unit is under warranty. A service call typically costs between $150 and $300 and includes diagnosis, the trip charge, and labor for pump or board replacement if needed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts if a hose or pump is needed, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
