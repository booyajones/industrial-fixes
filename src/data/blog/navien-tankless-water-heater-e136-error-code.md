## Navien E136 Error Code — What It Means

The E136 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a communication failure between the main control board and another component within the system. This can involve wiring, connectors, or the control board itself. The unit will typically shut down or fail to fire until the communication path is restored. Because Navien models vary and documentation for this exact code is limited, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit cover to confirm the specific meaning for your model.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board assuming it has failed, when the real problem is a loose or corroded connector or damaged wire. Inspect all visible connectors and harnesses first before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~45%)** Vibration, moisture, or age can cause connectors between the control board and sensors or other modules to work loose or develop corrosion, breaking the communication signal.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~25%)** Rodent damage, pinching during installation, or heat exposure can break conductors inside the wire jacket, interrupting signals even when the connector looks intact.
- **Failed control board (~15%)** The main PCB can develop solder cracks, blown traces, or component failures that prevent it from communicating with other modules.
- **Failed sensor or peripheral module (~10%)** A temperature sensor, flow sensor, or other component may have an internal fault that stops it from responding to the control board.
- **Ground or grounding issue (~5%)** A poor or missing ground connection can introduce electrical noise that disrupts low-voltage communication signals between components.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to expose the control board and wiring. Consult your model's service manual for cover removal steps.
3. **Inspect all connectors** on the control board and trace each harness to sensors, ignition modules, and other components. Look for loose plugs, corrosion, or moisture.
4. **Disconnect and reconnect** each connector firmly, checking for bent pins or damaged sockets. Clean any corroded contacts with electrical contact cleaner.
5. **Examine the wiring harness** along its entire length for pinched insulation, burn marks, or rodent damage. Flex each section gently to reveal hidden breaks.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then turn on the unit and observe whether the E136 error returns. If it does, measure continuity on suspect wires with a multimeter.
7. **Replace the control board** if all wiring and connectors are intact and the error persists. Match the part number on the existing board and transfer connectors one at a time to avoid mixing them up.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with low-voltage wiring or if you have restored all connections and the error persists. A pro can use diagnostic software to interrogate the control board, test component communication in real time, and determine whether the board or a peripheral module has failed. Also call a pro if you smell gas, see active water leaks, or if the unit repeatedly faults after you clear the error.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-400.
