## State Water Heater E130 Error Code — What It Means

The E130 error code is not clearly documented in available State Water Heaters manufacturer materials, but in similar appliances it signals a flue thermostat sensor tripped on lockout. This means the appliance detected unsafe exhaust temperatures or a sensor fault and shut down to protect the system. The exact meaning depends on your specific State model and control platform, so verify your model number against the manual before proceeding. The code typically indicates a problem with the flue temperature sensor itself, its wiring, or the venting and combustion-air safety chain.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or misreading flue temperature sensor** The sensor that monitors exhaust gas temperature has failed internally or is sending false readings to the control board.
- **Loose, corroded, or moisture-damaged sensor wiring** Connector pins at the sensor or control board are corroded, loose, or shorted by water intrusion.
- **Sensor not properly clipped or mounted** The flue thermostat is not seated correctly against the flue pipe or sensing surface, causing it to read ambient air instead of exhaust gas.
- **Blocked venting or termination** The exhaust vent is obstructed by debris, ice, or a bird nest, causing high flue temperatures or pressure switch faults.
- **Air pressure switch tubing kinked or blocked** Silicone tubing that connects the air pressure switch is melted, kinked, has holes, or is blocked by condensate or dust.
- **Condensate trap clogged or dry** On condensing models, a blocked or empty trap disrupts the pressure-switch circuit and triggers safety lockouts.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your exact State model number** from the data plate and look up E130 in the model-specific manual, because code definitions vary by control platform and the search results do not confirm a State-specific meaning.
2. **Power off the unit** at the breaker or service disconnect and wait two minutes for the control board to reset before opening any panels.
3. **Inspect the flue thermostat sensor** (usually a clip-on or bolt-on device on the flue pipe near the top of the tank) and confirm it is firmly seated and the mounting clip is not loose or damaged.
4. **Check all sensor wiring and connectors** for corrosion, pushed-out pins, moisture, or melted insulation, and clean or reseat any corroded terminals with electronic contact cleaner.
5. **Verify the venting system** is clear by looking at the termination outside for obstructions and checking inside for loose joints, sagging pipe, or condensate pooling in horizontal runs.
6. **Inspect air pressure switch tubing** (if your model has one) for kinks, holes, melting, or blockage, and verify the switch makes and breaks cleanly when you blow gently into the hose.
7. **Check and clean the condensate trap** on condensing models, refill it with water to the proper level, and confirm the drain line is not clogged.
8. **Restore power and monitor** the display for three heating cycles, and if the E130 returns immediately after sensor and venting checks, plan to replace the sensor, pressure switch circuit, or control board in that order.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate a State manual that lists E130 for your model, if gas inlet pressure needs verification (this requires a manometer and should not be adjusted by homeowners), if the fault persists after you have confirmed sensor mounting and wiring are correct, or if you see evidence of exhaust gas spillage or soot around the flue. Control board and gas-valve work requires proper combustion analysis and safety checks that are outside DIY scope.
