## Frigidaire Oven F4 Error Code — What It Means

The F4 error code on a Frigidaire oven usually indicates a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit, often a short circuit or sensor failure. The control board continuously monitors the resistance reading from the sensor probe. When it detects an abnormal signal such as a reading that is out of range or a shorted wire the board throws the F4 code and may shut down heating to protect the appliance.

Because error code definitions can vary by model and year, always cross-reference your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel. On most Frigidaire ranges the F4 points to the RTD (resistance temperature detector) sensor or its wiring harness, though some older models may use slightly different fault assignments.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the electronic control board first, but in most F4 cases the sensor probe or its connector is at fault. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Shorted or failed RTD sensor probe (~60%)** The metal-sheathed probe at the rear oven wall develops internal shorts or drift over time, sending invalid resistance values to the control board.
- **Damaged or pinched sensor wiring (~20%)** The thin wires between the sensor and the control can be pinched behind the oven cavity or burned by heat, creating a short to ground.
- **Corroded or loose sensor connector (~10%)** Moisture or heat can corrode the two-pin plug where the sensor harness meets the main wiring, causing intermittent or high-resistance contact.
- **Failed electronic control board (~10%)** The board's sensor input circuit can fail and misread a healthy sensor, though this is much less common than probe failure.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the sensor probe measure between 900 and 1200 ohms at room temperature with a multimeter?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The sensor itself is likely good. Check for damaged wiring, a loose connector, or a faulty control board input circuit.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor is shorted (zero ohms) or open (infinite ohms). Replace the RTD probe.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Do you see any visible char marks, melted insulation, or pinched wires along the sensor harness?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Repair or replace the damaged section of the harness. If the damage is at the probe, replace the entire sensor assembly.<br><strong>No:</strong> Focus on the sensor probe and connector. Clean the connector pins and retest resistance before replacing the probe.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error clear for a while after reseating the sensor connector, then return during a bake cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The connector or harness has an intermittent fault. Replace the sensor assembly with its pigtail to eliminate the weak connection.<br><strong>No:</strong> The fault is steady. Measure sensor resistance and replace the probe if the reading is out of range.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the range or slide it away from the wall to access the rear panel.
2. **Remove the oven back panel** by taking out the screws along its perimeter so you can see the temperature sensor probe and its wiring.
3. **Locate the sensor probe**, a metal tube about four inches long protruding into the oven cavity from the rear wall, with two wires leading to a small connector.
4. **Disconnect the two-pin connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance across the sensor's terminals. At room temperature a typical RTD reads between 900 and 1200 ohms. Zero ohms means a short and infinite resistance means an open sensor.
5. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the sensor to the control board for pinched, burned, or bare copper. Wiggle the harness gently and watch the meter for jumps in resistance.
6. **Replace the sensor probe** if it reads out of range or if the wiring shows damage near the probe. Unscrew the mounting bracket, pull the old probe out through the rear wall, and push the new sensor into the same hole. Secure the bracket and reconnect the harness.
7. **Reassemble the back panel**, restore power, and run a test bake cycle to confirm the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have tested the sensor and found it in range, inspected all visible wiring without finding damage, and the F4 code persists. At that point the electronic control board's sensor input circuit may have failed, which requires board-level diagnostics and sometimes a new control. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working behind the oven with line voltage present or if your range is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-60 in parts, 20-40 minutes. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
