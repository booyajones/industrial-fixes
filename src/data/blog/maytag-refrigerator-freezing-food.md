## Maytag Refrigerator Freezing Food — What's Happening

A Maytag refrigerator freezing food means the fresh-food compartment is getting colder than the setpoint. The refrigerator section is receiving too much cold air from the freezer side, or the controls and sensing components are not regulating properly.

In side-by-side Maytag models, if the ice maker is not connected to a water supply line but remains turned on, the unit can run too cold, crisper drawers may freeze, and the freezer may get too cold. Maytag also notes that a faulty temperature sensor or control panel can cause continued freezing after basic settings are corrected.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Temperature set too cold** Refrigerator or freezer setpoint below recommended range causes overcooling in the fresh-food section.
- **Blocked air vents** Food packages placed directly in the airflow path from freezer to refrigerator allow excess cold air into the compartment.
- **Ice maker on without water connection** Maytag states an ice maker turned on but not connected to water can make the refrigerator run too cold and freeze food in the crisper.
- **Door gasket leaking** A torn, warped, or worn door seal lets warm air in and causes the control to compensate by overcooling.
- **Faulty temperature sensor** A failed thermistor sends incorrect readings to the control board, resulting in improper cooling regulation.
- **Control panel or board failure** Maytag notes that a defective control panel can cause continued freezing after other common causes are corrected.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify where food is freezing and check current setpoints against Maytag's recommended 37°F refrigerator and 0°F freezer (or 38°F to 40°F fridge, 0°F to 2°F freezer for ideal food storage).
2. Inspect all air vents between freezer and fresh-food sections and move any food or shelving blocking the airflow path.
3. Check ice maker status and verify it is either properly connected to a water supply or turned off if unused.
4. Inspect the door gasket for tears, warping, or poor closure using the paper test (if paper pulls out with little resistance, the seal may be bad).
5. Power-cycle the refrigerator by unplugging for about one minute, then restore power and allow the unit to stabilize.
6. Place a glass of water in the refrigerator for 24 hours, then measure the water temperature with a thermometer to compare against the setpoint.
7. If measured temperature does not match the setpoint and all other checks pass, suspect the temperature sensor or control panel.
8. Replace only confirmed failed components after ruling out settings, airflow, door seals, and sensor behavior.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if freezing continues after you have adjusted the temperature settings, cleared all air vents, verified ice maker connection status, and replaced a damaged door gasket. A technician can test the temperature sensor with a multimeter, verify control-board signal behavior, and replace electronics safely. Control-board diagnosis requires specific voltage and resistance checks that are best handled by a qualified appliance repair tech.
