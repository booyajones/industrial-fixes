## State Water Heater E56 Error — What It Means

There is no official E56 error code in State Water Heaters' diagnostic manual. State is a sub-brand of A.O. Smith, and their units use codes like E03, E10, E20, or numeric codes such as 11, 12, and 24 for ignition and flow faults. The E56 code actually belongs to RADIANT (SFK-56) tankless water heaters, where it indicates a low water flow error. On RADIANT units, the control board cannot detect sufficient flow to allow the heater to ignite or operate safely.

The most common cause of E56 on RADIANT SFK-56 units is a displaced magnet in the water inlet sensor connector. This magnet can slide out of position, breaking the flow sensor's signal to the control board. Other causes include a clogged inlet filter, low supply pressure, or a faulty flow sensor. If you are working on a State-branded water heater and see an error code, double-check the display. It is likely E03, E10, E20, or a two-digit numeric code, not E56.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor or control board when the magnet in the inlet connector has simply slid out of place. Use a magnetic screwdriver to realign the magnet and listen for a click before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Displaced magnet in inlet connector (~60%)** The magnet inside the black connector at the water inlet slides out of alignment, breaking the flow sensor signal and triggering E56 on RADIANT units.
- **Clogged inlet filter (~20%)** Sediment or debris in the inlet screen reduces water flow below the minimum threshold, preventing ignition.
- **Low supply pressure or blockage (~10%)** Insufficient incoming water pressure or a closed valve upstream of the heater starves the unit of flow.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~7%)** The flow sensor itself can fail or lose calibration, sending incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Control board communication error (~3%)** Loose or corroded wiring between the flow sensor and the control board can interrupt the signal and mimic a flow fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the brand and model.** Confirm the unit is a RADIANT SFK-56 tankless water heater. State water heaters do not use E56. If you have a State unit, consult the owner's manual for the correct code interpretation.
2. **Locate the water inlet connector.** Find the black connector at the cold water inlet, marked "water inlet." This houses the flow sensor magnet.
3. **Realign the magnet.** Use a magnetic screwdriver or magnetic tool to slide the magnet back into position inside the connector until you hear or feel a click. This is the most common fix for E56 on RADIANT units.
4. **Clean the inlet filter.** Remove the inlet screen and rinse it under running water to clear sediment. Reinstall the filter and check if the code clears.
5. **Check water supply pressure.** Confirm the inlet pressure is at least 40 psi. Open any closed valves upstream and verify no kinked or blocked supply lines are restricting flow.
6. **Inspect wiring and connectors.** Examine the wires between the flow sensor and the control board for corrosion, loose terminals, or damage. Reseat any loose connectors.
7. **Replace the flow sensor or control board.** If the error persists after magnet realignment, filter cleaning, and pressure checks, replace the flow sensor first. If the new sensor does not resolve the fault, the control board may need replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are unsure whether your unit is a RADIANT or State model, if realigning the magnet and cleaning the filter do not resolve E56, or if you suspect a faulty flow sensor or control board. A technician can measure flow rates, test sensor voltage, and replace electronic components safely. Gas connections, venting, and electrical hookups on tankless heaters also require licensed service in many jurisdictions. If the unit is still under warranty, professional installation of replacement parts may be required to maintain coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts if magnet realignment or filter cleaning fails and sensor replacement is needed, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
