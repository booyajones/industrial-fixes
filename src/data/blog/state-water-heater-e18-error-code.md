## State Water Heater E18 Error Code — What It Means

The E18 error code on some State hydronic equipment indicates that the supply water temperature has exceeded 212°F, the boiling point of water at sea level. This is an overheat condition that triggers a safety shutdown to prevent damage or dangerous pressure buildup. The fault is typically tied to restricted flow, pump failure, air pockets preventing circulation, or a malfunctioning temperature sensor feeding false readings to the control board.

This code is not universally defined across all State water heater models. If you have a residential tankless or tank-style unit and see E18, consult your specific model's service manual to confirm the exact fault definition. The troubleshooting steps below address the verified overheat scenario found in hydronic and boiler applications.

## Common Causes

- **Air trapped in the system** Air pockets block hot water circulation, causing the unit to overheat locally even though flow appears normal.
- **Circulation pump failure** A stuck, weak, or dead pump stops water movement, letting the heat exchanger climb past safe temperature limits.
- **Faulty or waterlogged expansion tank** A failed expansion tank allows pressure spikes and flow stagnation, contributing to overheating during firing cycles.
- **Sludge or restriction in piping** Scale, sediment, or debris narrows passages and chokes flow, preventing adequate heat removal from the unit.
- **Temperature sensor fault** A drifting or shorted sensor reports incorrect high readings, triggering the code even when actual water temperature is safe.
- **Low system flow or closed valves** Partially closed isolation valves, undersized piping, or clogged filters starve the heater of the flow needed to carry heat away.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas valve if equipped, then let the unit cool for at least 30 minutes before opening any fittings.
2. **Check circulation pump operation** by listening and feeling for vibration, and confirm that the pump's rotor spins freely if it has a manual release screw on the front.
3. **Bleed air from the system** at high-point air vents and the pump's bleed screw, watching for solid water flow with no bubbles or sputtering.
4. **Inspect the expansion tank** by pressing the Schrader valve on top: if water sprays out, the bladder is ruptured and the tank must be replaced.
5. **Flush and clean the heat exchanger** and any inline strainers or filters, removing sediment that restricts flow through the unit.
6. **Test the supply temperature sensor** with a multimeter and compare resistance to your model's specification chart, replacing the sensor if readings are out of range or erratic.
7. **Restore power and water flow**, monitor the display for normal operation, and verify that the supply temperature stays well below 212°F during a full heating cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or heating technician if the error returns after bleeding air and checking the pump, if you see leaks or pressure-relief discharge, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas piping and electrical controls. Persistent overheating can damage the heat exchanger and create unsafe operating conditions. A pro can perform a complete flow analysis, verify sensor calibration with calibrated instruments, and inspect internal passages for hidden blockages that are not accessible during routine maintenance.
