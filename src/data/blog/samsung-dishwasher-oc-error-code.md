## What this code means
The OC or oC error code on a Samsung dishwasher means the control board has detected an over-level or overflow water condition. In practice, the machine believes there is too much water in the tub, either because it is not draining correctly, is being overfilled, or the water-level sensing path is giving a false reading.

Samsung support ties this code to overflow or slow drain behavior. The dishwasher may show standing water at the end of a cycle, or the code may appear mid-cycle when the control detects water above the normal level. The fault can result from a mechanical blockage, excess suds from detergent, a water supply valve that does not close fully, a defective flow meter, or a faulty main control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, thinking it is an electronics fault. Always clean the filter, inspect the drain hose for kinks, and check for excess suds before replacing any electronic components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or clogged filter assembly** Debris in the fine filter or filter insert restricts drainage and causes water to back up above the normal level.
- **Kinked, crushed, or improperly routed drain hose** A restricted drain hose prevents water from leaving the tub, or wastewater flows back into the machine if the hose is not looped correctly.
- **Excess detergent or suds** Samsung support notes the code can appear at the end of the cycle from too much detergent or pods used on small loads, creating excessive foam that triggers the overflow sensor.
- **Water supply valve not closing fully** If the inlet valve does not shut off completely, water continues to enter the tub even when the machine is off or not filling.
- **Defective flow meter or water-level sensing component** A faulty flow meter or float switch can send incorrect signals to the control board, making it believe the tub is overfilled when it is not.
- **Main PCB or control board fault** A defective control board may incorrectly interpret sensor signals or fail to command the drain pump and inlet valve properly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cancel the cycle and power off the dishwasher.** Turn off the circuit breaker and inspect the tub for standing water or visible overflow before starting diagnostic work.
2. **Remove and clean the filter assembly.** Pull out the lower rack, twist and lift the filter insert, remove the fine filter, rinse and scrub away all food debris and grease, then reinstall both filters correctly.
3. **Inspect the drain hose end-to-end.** Follow the hose from the dishwasher to the sink drain or garbage disposal, looking for kinks, crushing, or poor routing, and confirm the hose is looped high under the counter so wastewater cannot siphon back into the machine.
4. **Check the sink drain and garbage disposal.** Run water in the sink to verify it drains freely, and clear any blockage in the disposal before reconnecting the dishwasher drain hose.
5. **Look for excess suds or detergent buildup.** Open the door and check for foam in the tub, reduce the detergent amount if you are using pods or more than one tablespoon of powder, and run a rinse cycle to clear residual suds.
6. **Turn off the circuit breaker for one hour.** Samsung support recommends this reset step to clear the error code, then restore power and run a short cycle to see if the fault returns.
7. **Run diagnostic mode and isolate the component.** If the code persists after cleaning and drain checks, enter service mode (consult your model's service manual) to test the drain pump, inlet valve, flow meter, and control board, then replace the defective part and retest the cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the OC or oC code returns after you have cleaned the filter, cleared the drain path, and reduced detergent use. Persistent faults usually require diagnostic mode testing to isolate the water inlet valve, flow meter, drain pump impeller, or main control board. A technician has the tools and service manual to measure valve operation, verify sensor signals, and safely replace electronic components. Also call a pro if you see water pooling under the dishwasher or if the unit trips the breaker, since those symptoms point to a more serious leak or electrical fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in parts (if a hose or filter needs replacing), 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 depending on the component replaced.
