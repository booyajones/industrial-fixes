## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E58 Error — What It Means

The E58 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with the flame detection system. The unit either cannot detect a flame after ignition or is seeing an unstable flame signal. This safety feature prevents the heater from operating when combustion cannot be verified. The error may appear during startup or during operation if the flame signal drops out. Because different Ruud models use similar codes for different conditions, consult your specific model's manual or the diagnostic chart inside the front panel to confirm the exact meaning for your unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the entire ignition control board when the actual problem is a flame rod coated with carbon or corrosion. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper or steel wool and check the wire connection before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame sensing electrode prevent it from reading the flame signal correctly.
- **Inadequate gas pressure or flow (~25%)** Low gas supply pressure or a partly closed valve starves the burner and creates a weak or unstable flame.
- **Flame rod wiring fault (~15%)** A loose connection, corroded terminal, or broken wire at the flame rod or control board interrupts the flame signal.
- **Failed flame rod (~10%)** The flame sensing electrode itself can crack or short, making it unable to generate a proper signal.
- **Ignition control board failure (~5%)** The circuit that processes the flame signal may fail, though this is less common than sensor or supply issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws and lifting the panel away to access the burner compartment.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner assembly, and disconnect the wire terminal.
4. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or steel wool until the metal is shiny, removing all carbon deposits and corrosion.
5. **Inspect the wire and terminal** for cracks, corrosion, or looseness, and clean or tighten the connection as needed.
6. **Reinstall the flame rod** and reconnect the wire, making sure the connection is snug and the rod is properly positioned near the burner.
7. **Turn on the gas** and restore power, then run hot water to test whether the error clears and the unit fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working around gas appliances or if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and checking connections. Professionals have manometers to measure gas pressure, multimeters to test the flame signal in microamps, and the training to diagnose control board faults safely. Because tankless heaters involve both gas and high-voltage ignition systems, mistakes can create fire or carbon monoxide hazards. A pro visit typically includes a full combustion analysis and system check to prevent future errors.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
