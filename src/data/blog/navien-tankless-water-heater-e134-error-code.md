## Navien E134 Error Code — What It Means

The E134 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically signals a communication fault or sensor reading problem within the unit's control system. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the inside of the unit cover for model-specific details.

This code often appears when the main PCB (printed circuit board) cannot read data from one of the temperature or flow sensors, or when wiring connections become loose or corroded. The heater will usually shut down or fail to ignite until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main PCB when the real issue is a loose connector or a single failed sensor. Always inspect and reseat all wiring harness connections and test individual sensors with a multimeter before ordering a new control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~40%)** Vibration or moisture can cause connectors at the PCB or sensors to lose contact, preventing the board from reading sensor data.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~30%)** Inlet, outlet, or heat-exchanger temperature sensors can drift out of range or fail open, triggering a communication error.
- **Damaged sensor wiring (~15%)** Pinched, chafed, or rodent-damaged wiring between sensors and the PCB interrupts the signal path.
- **Faulty main PCB (~10%)** The control board itself may have a failed sensor input circuit or corrupted firmware, though this is less common.
- **Grounding or electrical noise issue (~5%)** Poor grounding or nearby electrical interference can create transient faults that the PCB interprets as a sensor error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws at the bottom and lifting the panel away.
3. **Locate the main PCB** (usually mounted inside the lower or upper compartment) and identify all sensor wire connectors.
4. **Reseat each connector** by unplugging and firmly reconnecting it, checking for corrosion or bent pins.
5. **Inspect all sensor wiring** along its length for pinching, chafing, or damage; look for burn marks near the heat exchanger.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then run hot water and observe whether the E134 code reappears.
7. **If the code persists**, use a multimeter to measure resistance at each temperature sensor and compare to the chart in your manual; replace any sensor that reads open or far out of range.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections, if multimeter testing is beyond your skill level, or if the error persists after reseating connectors and replacing an obviously faulty sensor. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics with manufacturer software, verify proper grounding, and safely handle gas and high-voltage components. Professional service is also recommended if you find burn marks or melted insulation, which may indicate a deeper electrical fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
