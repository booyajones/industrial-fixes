## A.O. Smith Water Heater E07 Error Code — What It Means

The E07 error code on A.O. Smith water heaters signals a fault detected by the control system. The exact meaning of this code varies by model and control board version, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the heater for the precise definition. In many cases, error codes in this range point to sensor issues, wiring problems, or control board faults.

Because A.O. Smith produces many models with different control systems, E07 may refer to different sensor circuits or safety limits depending on your unit. Do not assume the code means the same thing across all models. Check the documentation that came with your water heater or contact A.O. Smith support with your model and serial number to confirm what E07 indicates on your specific unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first when a loose wire or dirty connector is actually at fault. Inspect all sensor connections and test sensor resistance before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen connections or corrode terminals, breaking the signal path between sensors and the control board.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~30%)** Sensors that monitor inlet, outlet, or tank temperature can drift out of range or fail open, triggering a fault code.
- **Faulty control board (~20%)** The electronic control can develop solder cracks, component failures, or firmware glitches that generate false error codes.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Chafed or pinched wires between the control and sensors create intermittent short circuits or open circuits.
- **Incorrect sensor installation (~5%)** A sensor that is not fully seated in its well or uses the wrong gasket may read incorrectly and trigger an error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply if applicable.
2. **Locate the control board** behind the access panel on the front or side of the heater and take a photo of all wiring before disconnecting anything.
3. **Inspect every wire connection** at the control board and at each sensor, looking for loose terminals, corrosion, or burnt insulation.
4. **Consult your owner's manual** or the wiring diagram on the heater to identify which sensor or circuit E07 references on your model.
5. **Test sensor resistance** using a multimeter set to ohms, comparing your readings to the specifications in the manual or on the diagram.
6. **Replace the faulty sensor or control board** as indicated by your tests, making sure to match part numbers exactly and route wires the same way as original.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then monitor the display to confirm the E07 code does not return and the heater operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical wiring or multimeter testing. Gas-fired models require safe handling of the gas supply and venting, so a professional should handle any work beyond simple sensor replacement. If you have tested all sensors and wiring and the code persists, a technician can perform advanced diagnostics and access factory service bulletins that may explain intermittent faults. Always call a pro if you smell gas, see water pooling around the heater, or lack the tools to safely disconnect power and test components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
