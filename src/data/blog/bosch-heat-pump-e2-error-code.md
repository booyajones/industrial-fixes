## Bosch Heat Pump E2 Error Code — What It Means

The E2 error code on Bosch equipment is not universal across all product lines and its exact meaning depends on your specific model. In Bosch water heaters, E2 indicates a problem with the inlet cold water temperature sensor reading outside normal parameters or failing entirely. For Bosch heat pumps, the code definition is model-dependent and must be verified against your unit's service manual or installation documentation. Temperature sensor faults are common across both platforms.

Because the provided documentation covers Bosch water heaters specifically, the guidance below reflects that application. If you own a Bosch heat pump, confirm the E2 definition from your model's nameplate and manual before proceeding, as sensor locations and circuits differ between product families.

## Common Causes

- **Sensor positioned incorrectly on pipe** The cold water sensor sits on an elbow or wrong section instead of the top of the diagonal copper run.
- **Corrosion or contamination on sensor body** Mineral deposits or rust interfere with thermal contact between the sensor and the pipe surface.
- **Loose or dirty spade connector terminals** The blue sensor wires have corroded or poorly seated spade connections that break circuit continuity.
- **Sensor itself has failed** Internal thermistor or circuit damage prevents accurate temperature readings even after cleaning and reseating.
- **Preheated water entering inlet** If the unit is connected to a recirculation loop or solar preheat, the inlet reads abnormally high and triggers the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press the reset button** on the control board to clear the fault code and see if the error returns immediately or after a heating cycle.
2. **Verify the unit is used for domestic hot water only** and that no preheated water from a recirculation system or solar collector is feeding the cold inlet.
3. **Locate the cold water temperature sensor** on the half-inch copper pipe in the lower right area of the unit and confirm it is seated on the top of the diagonal pipe section, not on an elbow or horizontal run.
4. **Inspect the sensor body** for corrosion, mineral buildup, or poor thermal contact and clean both the sensor and the pipe surface with a Scotch-Brite pad or fine sandpaper.
5. **Check the blue-wire spade connectors** on both the sensor and the control board for corrosion, dirt, or looseness and clean or reseat as needed.
6. **Swap the inlet cold sensor with the outlet hot sensor** as a diagnostic test since both sensors are identical on these units; if the fault moves to a different code, the sensor is bad.
7. **Replace the inlet cold water temperature sensor** if cleaning, repositioning, and swapping do not clear the E2 code, then reset and monitor through several heating cycles.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if the E2 code persists after you have cleaned and repositioned the sensor, verified wiring integrity, and ruled out preheated inlet water. A pro can measure sensor resistance against the manufacturer's table, test control-board inputs with a multimeter, and replace the board if the sensor circuit has failed internally. For Bosch heat pumps specifically, if your model documentation defines E2 differently or the fault involves refrigerant-side sensors or controls, professional diagnosis is the safest path since those systems require EPA certification and specialized gauges.
