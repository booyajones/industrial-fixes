## Weil-McLain Boiler A15 Error Code — What It Means

The A15 error code on Weil-McLain boilers indicates a fault condition detected by the control system. Because Weil-McLain produces many boiler models with different control boards and firmware versions, the exact meaning of A15 can vary by model and year. Common interpretations include a safety lockout, a communication issue between components, or a sensor reading outside expected limits. Without model-specific documentation, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the boiler cabinet to confirm what A15 means for your unit.

Most A15 codes point to problems with electrical connections, control board communication, or a sensor that has drifted out of range. The code typically forces the boiler into a safe shutdown until the fault is cleared and the system is reset. Addressing the root cause before simply resetting the boiler will prevent repeated lockouts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board immediately, but many A15 errors stem from loose or corroded wire connections or a failed sensor. Check all harness connectors and sensor terminals before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen connectors or corrode terminals at the control board, sensors, or junction boxes, interrupting communication and triggering a fault.
- **Failed temperature or pressure sensor (~30%)** A sensor reading outside normal range or sending no signal at all will cause the control to halt operation and log an error.
- **Control board firmware or hardware fault (~20%)** The main control board may have a memory error, corrupted firmware, or failed component that reports a fault condition.
- **Grounding or electrical noise issue (~10%)** Poor grounding or electrical interference from nearby equipment can disrupt low-voltage control signals and generate spurious error codes.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~5%)** Rodent damage, pinching, or insulation breakdown in the low-voltage wiring can create intermittent shorts or open circuits.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the boiler switch and the circuit breaker to safely inspect the unit.
2. **Locate your owner's manual** or the wiring diagram inside the boiler cabinet and look up the A15 code definition for your model.
3. **Inspect all low-voltage wire connectors** at the control board, sensor terminals, and junction boxes for loose pins, corrosion, or burn marks.
4. **Clean and reseat** any suspect connectors using electronics contact cleaner and firm pressure to lock each plug.
5. **Restore power** and observe the display. If A15 persists, note which sensor or zone the manual associates with the code.
6. **Test the suspect sensor** with a multimeter, checking resistance or voltage against the values in your service manual.
7. **Replace the failed sensor or control board** if testing confirms it is out of specification, using the exact part number for your boiler model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician if you are not comfortable working with low-voltage wiring, if the manual does not clearly define A15 for your model, or if you have tested sensors and connections without resolving the fault. Boiler controls interact with gas valves, high-limit switches, and other safety devices, so misdiagnosis can create hazardous conditions. A technician has model-specific diagnostic software, wiring schematics, and the training to safely trace communication faults and replace sealed components. Professional service is also advisable if the boiler is still under warranty, since DIY repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
