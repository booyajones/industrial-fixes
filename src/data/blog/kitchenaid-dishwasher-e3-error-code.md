## KitchenAid Dishwasher E3 Error — What It Means

The E3 error code on KitchenAid dishwashers typically indicates a problem with water filling or pressure sensing, though the exact definition can vary by model. The control board expects water to reach a certain level within a set time, and when that does not happen or the pressure sensor reads incorrectly, it throws the E3 code and stops the cycle.

Because KitchenAid uses different control systems across its dishwasher lineup, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the door to confirm what E3 means for your specific model. In most cases the fault points to the water inlet valve, the pressure switch or sensor, a kinked fill hose, or a clogged filter interfering with water flow.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board when they see an E3 code. Test the inlet valve for continuity and check the pressure sensor hose for clogs or kinks before spending money on electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty water inlet valve (~35%)** The solenoid coil or valve diaphragm fails and water does not enter the tub fast enough to satisfy the timer.
- **Clogged or disconnected pressure sensor hose (~25%)** The small air tube running from the sump to the pressure switch becomes blocked with grease or detergent buildup, preventing accurate level readings.
- **Defective pressure switch or sensor (~20%)** The switch itself fails to close its contacts even when water is present, so the board never sees confirmation of a fill.
- **Low household water pressure or closed shutoff valve (~10%)** The supply line is kinked, the shutoff valve is only partially open, or municipal pressure is too low for the dishwasher to fill in time.
- **Clogged inlet screen or filter (~7%)** The mesh screen inside the inlet valve or the main tub filter traps sediment and restricts flow enough to trigger the error.
- **Control board misreading signals (~3%)** Loose connector pins or a failing board misinterpret sensor data and throw a false E3 code even when fill is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the circuit breaker, then turn off the water supply valve under the sink.
2. **Remove the lower access panel** at the front base and set it aside so you can reach the inlet valve and pressure components.
3. **Inspect the inlet hose and valve screen** for kinks, debris, or a closed shutoff valve. Disconnect the hose and flush the screen with water if sediment is present.
4. **Test the inlet valve solenoid** with a multimeter set to resistance. You should read 500 to 1500 ohms across the coil terminals; open or shorted readings mean the valve has failed.
5. **Check the pressure sensor hose** running from the sump to the pressure switch. Disconnect it at both ends and blow through it to confirm it is clear, then reconnect firmly.
6. **Test the pressure switch** by applying gentle suction to its inlet port while checking continuity across its terminals. The switch should click and show continuity when you apply vacuum.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (inlet valve, pressure switch, or sensor) and reassemble the access panel. Restore power and water, then run a short test cycle to verify the E3 code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with household electrical connections, if the control board itself shows signs of burn marks or corrosion, or if you have replaced both the inlet valve and pressure sensor but the E3 code persists. A technician can perform voltage tests at the board and check for wiring faults that are harder to trace. Also call for help if the dishwasher is still under warranty, since DIY repairs can void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
