## Bosch Washer Not Dispensing Detergent — What's Happening

When a Bosch washer does not dispense detergent, it means detergent remains in the drawer after the cycle because the drawer was not flushed properly or the dispenser path is blocked. On Bosch dishwashers, the symptom means the detergent cup door did not open correctly or the detergent was not released into the wash water.

This is not an error code. It is a functional failure of the dispensing system. Bosch support identifies low water pressure, clogged dispenser components, and defective water inlet valves as the most common culprits.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Low water pressure or poor water supply** Bosch states the dispenser system requires at least 20 psi water pressure and adequate flow (about 10 litres per minute at the tap) to flush detergent from the drawer.
- **Clogged dispenser drawer or housing** Residue buildup from detergent, fabric softener, or hard water frequently blocks the siphon paths and prevents proper flushing.
- **Obstruction or damage to the dispenser mechanism** On dishwashers, the detergent cup door can be blocked by dishes or utensils, or the hinge and spring mechanism can be broken or warped.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** If water pressure is adequate but the dispenser still does not flush, the inlet valve may not be opening correctly or is defective.
- **Clogged inlet screen or filter** Sediment or debris in the inlet hose screens can restrict water flow to the dispenser, mimicking low pressure.
- **Control board or wiring issue** If voltage does not reach the inlet valve or dispenser-related component, the control board or wiring harness may be at fault.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Run a test cycle and confirm detergent remains in the drawer or cup after the wash completes.
2. Open the water supply tap fully and verify strong flow. Bosch specifies a target of about 10 litres per minute at the tap for washers.
3. Remove the dispenser drawer (or open the dishwasher detergent cup) and clean all residue, caked detergent, and buildup from the drawer, housing, and siphon paths.
4. Inspect the dispenser for broken parts, warped cups, stuck lids, weak springs, damaged hinges, or misalignment. On dishwashers, check that racks and utensils do not block the detergent door.
5. Check the inlet hose screens and filters for sediment or debris and clean or replace as needed.
6. Test the water inlet valve for proper operation. If water pressure is acceptable but the dispenser is not flushed, replace the inlet valve.
7. Use a multimeter to verify voltage reaches the inlet valve or dispenser component during the fill or wash cycle. If not, inspect wiring and the control board.
8. Retest with an empty load after repair to confirm detergent is flushed or dispensed correctly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified adequate water pressure and cleaned all dispenser components but detergent still does not flush, or if you are not comfortable testing electrical components with a multimeter. A technician can test the inlet valve under operating conditions, trace wiring faults, and diagnose control board issues that require specialized tools or board replacement.
