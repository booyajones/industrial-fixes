## Lennox Furnace E207 Error Code — What It Means

The E207 error code on Lennox furnaces typically indicates a communication problem between control boards or a sensor fault. Because error code definitions can vary across Lennox furnace models and vintages, always refer to your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the blower compartment for the exact meaning on your unit. In general, E207-series codes point to issues with data transmission, sensor readings, or control module faults that prevent the furnace from operating normally.

The exact component involved depends on your furnace model. Some units use this code for pressure switch issues, while others may flag a flame sensor or thermostat communication problem. Without model-specific documentation, any repair attempt should start with basic checks of wiring, connections, and sensor condition before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace control boards when the real problem is a loose or corroded wire harness connector. Inspect and reseat all low-voltage connections before ordering any circuit board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** Vibration and temperature cycles can loosen plug connections between the control board and sensors or between control modules, interrupting communication.
- **Failed sensor (~25%)** Flame sensors, pressure switches, or temperature sensors may fail or drift out of range, sending invalid data that triggers a communication fault.
- **Control board fault (~20%)** The main control board or a secondary communication module may develop a hardware failure that prevents normal data exchange.
- **Thermostat wiring or compatibility issue (~15%)** Incorrect wiring at the thermostat or a mismatch between a smart thermostat and the furnace control can cause communication errors.
- **Interference or grounding problem (~5%)** Electrical noise from nearby equipment or a poor ground connection can corrupt low-voltage signals and trigger error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the furnace at the electrical disconnect or circuit breaker and wait at least 60 seconds to allow the control board to reset fully.
2. **Restore power** and observe the display. If E207 clears and the furnace operates normally, monitor it over the next few heating cycles for recurrence.
3. **Remove the blower-compartment door** and locate the control board (usually mounted inside the cabinet on a bracket). Check all wire harness plugs for a secure fit and push each one firmly into place.
4. **Inspect sensor connectors** for the flame sensor, pressure switch, and any temperature sensors. Look for corrosion, bent pins, or moisture on the terminals.
5. **Consult your furnace's wiring diagram** (typically affixed to the inside of a panel) or your owner's manual to identify which component or communication path is associated with code E207 on your specific model.
6. **Test the furnace** after reseating connectors. If the error persists, record the exact model and serial number and call a licensed HVAC technician for diagnostic testing of sensors and control boards.
7. **Do not attempt** to replace control boards or sensors without confirming the diagnosis, as error codes can be caused by wiring faults rather than component failure.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the error persists after reseating all connectors and performing a power reset, if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components, or if you cannot locate model-specific documentation for the E207 code. Diagnosing communication faults often requires a multimeter and familiarity with low-voltage control circuits. Gas furnaces also pose safety risks if sensors or controls are mishandled. A qualified technician can test each sensor, measure signal voltages, and confirm whether the control board or a field device has failed.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
