## Whirlpool Dryer F2 E4 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 E4 error code on many Whirlpool dryer models indicates a fault in the temperature sensing circuit. This typically means the dryer's control board is not receiving the expected signal from the thermistor, a temperature sensor that monitors heat levels inside the drum. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer cabinet for confirmation.

The dryer may stop mid-cycle or refuse to start when this code appears. The control is protecting the machine from running without accurate temperature feedback, which could lead to overheating or poor drying performance.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the thermistor or a loose connector is actually at fault. Test the thermistor's resistance with a multimeter and inspect all wiring connections before buying a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor sensor has failed or is reading out of range, sending incorrect resistance values to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~25%)** Connections to the thermistor or control board are loose, corroded, or damaged, breaking the signal path.
- **Failed main control board (~15%)** The electronic control board itself has a circuit fault and cannot process the thermistor signal correctly.
- **Pinched or damaged harness (~10%)** The wiring harness between the thermistor and the board has been pinched or cut, creating an open or short circuit.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the dryer run at all when you start a cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The control board is powered and can start the dryer, pointing toward a thermistor or wiring issue rather than a total board failure.<br><strong>No:</strong> You may have a broader electrical fault or the control board may be completely dead, which suggests checking the main power supply and thermal fuse first.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>When you measure the thermistor resistance with a multimeter at room temperature, does it read within normal range?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The thermistor is likely good, so inspect the wiring and connectors for damage or poor contact, or suspect the control board.<br><strong>No:</strong> The thermistor is faulty and should be replaced, as an out-of-range reading will trigger the F2 E4 code.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are all wire connectors on the thermistor and control board firmly seated with no visible corrosion?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Wiring connections are sound, so the issue is likely the thermistor itself or the control board circuitry.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean the connectors, reseat them firmly, and test the dryer again before replacing any parts.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet or turn off the circuit breaker to cut all power before working inside the cabinet.
2. **Remove the lower front panel or top cover** (depending on your model) to access the drum area and locate the thermistor, usually mounted on the blower housing or duct.
3. **Disconnect the thermistor wire connector** and use a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) to measure across the two thermistor terminals at room temperature.
4. **Compare the reading** to the specification in your model's service manual or wiring diagram (typical range is 10,000 to 50,000 ohms at room temperature, but consult your model's table).
5. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** from the thermistor to the main control board for damage, corrosion, or loose fit, and clean or repair any issues you find.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the resistance is out of range or if the sensor shows visible damage or cracking.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify the F2 E4 code is cleared and the dryer operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if you have tested the thermistor and wiring and found them all within specification but the error persists. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board and check for intermittent faults that are hard to catch with a simple multimeter. Also call a pro if you lack the tools to safely access the dryer's internal components or if your warranty is still in effect and you want to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
