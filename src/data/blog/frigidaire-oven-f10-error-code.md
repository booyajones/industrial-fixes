## Frigidaire Oven F10 Error Code — What It Means

F10 on a Frigidaire electric range or oven indicates a thermal runaway condition. The control board has detected that the oven temperature is rising abnormally or the oven continues heating when it should have stopped. This is a safety shutdown fault designed to prevent damage or fire. The control measures actual oven temperature through a sensor and compares it to expected values. When the temperature climbs too high or the heating element stays energized when it should be off, the board throws F10 and halts operation.

This fault is not a user setting mistake. It signals a real hardware problem in the temperature control loop. The oven may feel excessively hot to the touch, or the code may appear even during preheat if the sensor or control circuitry has failed. Frigidaire's own support documentation labels F10 as thermal runaway and directs users to reset power and call for service if the fault returns.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the oven temperature sensor first because it is easy to access. Before buying a new sensor, measure its resistance with a multimeter at room temperature. If you read roughly 1,080 to 1,100 ohms the sensor is usually fine and the control board relay is the real problem.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck relay on the electronic control board (~50%)** The relay that switches power to the oven heating element can stick in the closed position, causing the element to heat continuously even after the control signals it to stop.
- **Failed or out-of-spec oven temperature sensor (~30%)** If the RTD sensor drifts out of its normal resistance range or reports erratic values, the control misreads oven temperature and may trigger a thermal runaway fault.
- **Damaged wiring or connector between sensor and control (~15%)** Corroded terminals, pinched wires, or loose connections in the sensor harness cause intermittent or incorrect temperature readings that confuse the control board.
- **Shorted or grounded heating element (~5%)** A heating element with internal damage or a ground fault can draw current in unexpected ways and contribute to runaway heating behavior.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power immediately** if the oven is actively overheating or will not stop heating when you cancel the cycle. Turn off the circuit breaker to make the unit safe.
2. **Reset the control board** by switching off power at the breaker for at least 30 seconds, then restore power. Frigidaire recommends this as the first troubleshooting step for F10.
3. **Test whether the fault returns** by running a short bake cycle at a moderate temperature. If F10 reappears, proceed with component testing rather than repeated resets.
4. **Measure the oven temperature sensor resistance**. Disconnect power, access the sensor inside the oven cavity, unplug it, and use a multimeter to check resistance at room temperature. You should read approximately 1,080 to 1,100 ohms. A reading far outside that range means the sensor has failed.
5. **Inspect the sensor wiring and connector** for corrosion, loose pins, or damaged insulation. Measure continuity from the sensor leads through to the control board connector to confirm the harness is intact.
6. **Examine the electronic control board** for visible damage such as burned traces, discolored relays, or corrosion. If the sensor and wiring test good but F10 persists, the control board relay is likely stuck or the board logic has failed.
7. **Replace the failed component**. Install a new oven temperature sensor if resistance was out of spec, repair or replace damaged wiring, or swap the electronic control board if the relay or control logic is at fault. Reassemble, restore power, and run a full bake test to confirm stable operation and no return of F10.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the oven continues to heat after you cancel a cycle and disconnect power at the breaker is the only way to stop it. That behavior indicates a stuck relay or serious control board fault that poses a fire risk. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet, measuring resistance with a multimeter, or handling 240-volt connections. A qualified appliance technician has the tools to safely diagnose relay operation, verify sensor calibration, and replace the control board with the correct part for your model. If F10 returns after you have already replaced the sensor and inspected wiring, professional testing of the control board and heating element circuits will save you from replacing parts by trial and error.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
