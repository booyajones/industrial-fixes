## Manitowoc Ice Machine E26 Error — What It Means

The E26 error code on a Manitowoc ice machine indicates a problem with the refrigeration system or related sensors. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific definition. Generally, this code points to temperature-related issues that prevent the machine from completing a freeze cycle.

Because Manitowoc uses model-specific diagnostics, the E26 code may relate to evaporator temperature, ambient temperature sensors, or refrigerant flow problems. The machine's control board monitors several temperature points and will halt operation when readings fall outside acceptable ranges.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace the control board before checking sensors and coils. Test the thermistor resistance with a multimeter and clean the condenser first to rule out simple blockages.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or blocked condenser coil (~40%)** Dust, grease, and debris restrict airflow through the condenser, causing high head pressure and triggering temperature faults.
- **Failed evaporator or ambient thermistor (~25%)** A thermistor that has drifted out of range or failed open sends incorrect temperature data to the control board.
- **Low refrigerant charge (~15%)** A slow leak or undercharged system prevents the evaporator from reaching target temperature, flagging a sensor error.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** The board may misinterpret valid sensor signals or have a failed input circuit that reads temperatures incorrectly.
- **Poor ventilation or high ambient temperature (~7%)** Placing the machine in a hot room or blocking air intake causes the refrigeration system to overheat and fault out.
- **Damaged wiring or loose connector (~3%)** A loose pin, corroded terminal, or pinched wire between the sensor and board can create intermittent open-circuit readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off and unplug** the ice machine at the wall or breaker to safely work on electrical and mechanical components.
2. **Remove the front and side panels** according to your model's service manual to access the condenser coil and internal wiring.
3. **Clean the condenser coil** with a soft brush and shop vacuum, working carefully to avoid bending fins or damaging refrigerant lines.
4. **Locate the evaporator and ambient thermistors** by tracing the wiring harness from the control board, noting each sensor's position and connector.
5. **Disconnect each thermistor** and measure its resistance with a digital multimeter, comparing readings to the service manual table for your model.
6. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** for corrosion, loose pins, or damage, and reseat any suspect connections firmly.
7. **Reassemble the machine** and restore power, then run a test cycle and observe whether the E26 code returns or the unit completes a freeze successfully.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed refrigeration technician if cleaning the condenser and power-cycling do not clear the error, if you lack a multimeter to test thermistors, or if diagnostics point to low refrigerant or a sealed-system fault. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification and specialized gauges. A pro can also retrieve detailed fault logs from the control board and perform leak checks that are beyond typical DIY capability.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-500.
