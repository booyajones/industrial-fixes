## Whirlpool Dryer F3 E3 Error — What It Means

The F3 E3 error code on many Whirlpool dryers signals a problem with the temperature sensing or heating system. The dryer's control board detects either an open circuit in the thermistor (a temperature sensor) or an unusual temperature condition that prevents safe operation. The code may appear when the dryer cannot regulate heat properly.

Because the exact meaning can vary slightly by model, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer cabinet. In most cases the fault points to a failed thermistor, broken wiring, or poor airflow that causes the dryer to overheat and trip a thermal limit. The dryer will not run until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the heating element or control board when the actual problem is a broken thermistor wire or clogged exhaust vent. Test the thermistor resistance with a multimeter and check the vent for blockages before buying expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor (temperature sensor) develops an open or short circuit and sends incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Broken or corroded thermistor wiring (~25%)** Wires between the thermistor and control board become damaged, loose, or corroded and interrupt the signal.
- **Clogged exhaust vent (~15%)** Lint buildup in the vent restricts airflow and causes the dryer to overheat, triggering a thermal safety fault.
- **Faulty control board (~7%)** The electronic control board fails to read the thermistor signal correctly or develops a bad relay.
- **Thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat open (~3%)** A thermal safety device has blown due to previous overheating and now prevents the circuit from completing.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the exhaust vent pipe feel warm or hot during operation, or is it completely cold?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The heating element is working. Focus on the thermistor and its wiring as the likely fault.<br><strong>No:</strong> The dryer may not be heating at all. Check the thermal fuse and high-limit thermostat in addition to the thermistor.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>With power off, does the thermistor measure between 10,000 and 12,000 ohms at room temperature with a multimeter?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The thermistor is likely good. Inspect the wiring harness and connectors for breaks or corrosion.<br><strong>No:</strong> The thermistor is faulty. Replace it and retest the dryer.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the exhaust vent duct clear and straight, with no kinks or heavy lint buildup?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Airflow is adequate. The fault is in the electrical components.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean the vent thoroughly and straighten any kinks before replacing parts, as restricted airflow can cause false codes.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off the gas supply if you have a gas model.
2. **Pull the dryer away** from the wall and disconnect the exhaust vent duct, then inspect the duct and vent hood outside for lint clogs or damage.
3. **Remove the dryer's top or front panel** according to your model's service instructions to access the drum and blower housing.
4. **Locate the thermistor**, usually a small black or white sensor with two wires clipped or screwed to the blower housing or exhaust duct.
5. **Disconnect the thermistor wiring** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance across the sensor terminals (consult your model's table for the correct reading at room temperature).
6. **Inspect the wire harness** from the thermistor back to the control board for breaks, chafing, or loose connectors, and repair or replace as needed.
7. **Replace the thermistor** if it reads open (infinite resistance) or far outside the expected range, securing the new sensor in the same location and reconnecting the wires.
8. **Reassemble the dryer**, plug it in, and run a short test cycle to verify the F3 E3 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or removing dryer panels. A technician should also diagnose the fault if you have tested the thermistor and wiring and found them intact, since the control board may need replacement or there may be a less common thermal safety device issue. If the dryer has recurring overheating problems even after cleaning the vent and replacing the thermistor, a professional can check for blocked internal ducts or a failing blower motor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
