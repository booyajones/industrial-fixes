## Navien Tankless Water Heater E139 Error — What It Means

The E139 error on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a flame-sensing or ignition-verification fault. The control board is not receiving a proper signal from the flame rod (flame rectification sensor) during or after ignition. This can happen when the rod fails to detect combustion, even if a flame is present, or when the signal is too weak or erratic.

Because the meaning of error codes can vary slightly across Navien model families, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for the precise definition. In general, E139 points to an issue in the flame-detection circuit rather than a complete failure to ignite.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the gas valve or igniter when the real culprit is simply a flame rod coated in carbon or soot. Inspect and clean the flame rod first; it takes five minutes and costs nothing.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or carbon-fouled flame rod (~55%)** Soot and combustion residue insulate the rod and block the ionization signal needed to confirm flame presence.
- **Misaligned or bent flame rod (~20%)** If the rod tip has shifted or been knocked out of position, it may not sit in the flame envelope and cannot sense combustion.
- **Worn or corroded flame rod (~10%)** Over time the ceramic insulator can crack or the rod surface can corrode, weakening the rectification signal.
- **Weak or unstable flame (~10%)** Low gas pressure, dirty burner ports, or insufficient combustion air can produce a flame too small or erratic to reliably ionize the rod.
- **Faulty flame-rod wiring or connection (~5%)** A loose connector or damaged wire between the rod and the control board interrupts the signal path.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve upstream of the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the retaining screws, then locate the flame rod assembly inside the combustion chamber.
3. **Disconnect the flame-rod wire** from the ceramic insulator by gently pulling the spade terminal or unplugging the connector.
4. **Unscrew or unclip the flame rod** from its bracket and withdraw it carefully to avoid bending the probe.
5. **Clean the rod** with 400-grit sandpaper or a non-abrasive pad, removing all soot and oxidation from the metal surface and ceramic insulator.
6. **Inspect the rod tip and insulator** for cracks or heavy corrosion; replace the assembly if damaged.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod**, making sure the tip is centered in the burner flame path and the mounting bracket is secure, then reconnect the wire.
8. **Restore gas and power**, initiate a hot-water call, and observe the ignition sequence to confirm the error clears and the flame stabilizes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning and realigning the flame rod does not resolve the error, or if you find a cracked ceramic insulator or corroded wiring that requires part replacement. Tankless water heaters involve both natural gas (or propane) and high-voltage components, so any work beyond basic inspection and cleaning is best left to a qualified service professional. A technician can also measure gas pressure, inspect the burner and heat exchanger for blockages, and verify proper combustion air supply to rule out upstream issues that mimic a flame-rod fault.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $120-250.
