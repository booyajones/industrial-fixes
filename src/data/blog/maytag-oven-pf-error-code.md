## Maytag Oven PF Error Code — What It Means

The PF code on a Maytag oven stands for power failure. The control board detected that electrical power to the oven was interrupted. This is not a fault inside the oven itself but a stored record that the unit lost power at some point. Maytag documentation lists PF, PF id=27, and PF id=30 all as indicators of a recent power interruption.

The code can appear after a brief outage, a tripped breaker, or line voltage disturbances such as brownouts or power-supply noise during restoration. The oven's control stores the event and displays PF until you acknowledge it. Clearing the code requires pressing the Cancel button and, on some models, resetting the clock.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board thinking the oven has failed internally. Before ordering any parts, perform a full power reset by turning off the breaker for one minute and verify that household voltage is stable and the breaker is sized correctly.

## Common Causes

- **Tripped circuit breaker or blown fuse (~40%)** A breaker trip or fuse failure cuts power completely and the oven logs PF when power is restored.
- **Brief household power outage (~30%)** A utility interruption or neighborhood brownout interrupts supply voltage long enough for the control to detect and store the event.
- **Loose or corroded supply wiring (~15%)** Poor connections at the terminal block or wall receptacle can cause intermittent power loss that triggers PF.
- **Low or fluctuating line voltage (~10%)** Line noise, voltage sags during heavy appliance startup, or undersized wiring can cause the control to see a power-quality fault.
- **Failing control board or interface (~5%)** If the display cannot be cleared or PF returns immediately after a proper reset with verified stable power, the control electronics may be at fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press the Cancel button** on the oven control panel to clear the PF code from the display.
2. **Check the clock display.** If the time is flashing or incorrect, reset the time of day according to your owner's manual.
3. **Inspect the main circuit breaker** or fuse that supplies the oven. If it is tripped, reset it. If it trips again immediately, stop and call an electrician to check for a short or overload.
4. **Perform a full power reset** by switching off the oven breaker or removing the fuse for one full minute, then restore power and watch the display for one minute to confirm PF does not return.
5. **Examine the supply wiring** at the terminal block on the back of the oven. Look for loose, corroded, or burned connections. Tighten any loose screws and clean corrosion with a wire brush (power off first).
6. **Test household voltage** with a multimeter at the wall outlet or terminal block. Verify that you have the correct voltage for your oven model (typically 240 V for electric ranges). Consult your model's wiring diagram for exact specifications.
7. **Monitor for recurrence.** Use the oven through several cook cycles. If PF reappears without any known power event, the control board or control interface is the likely fault and should be replaced by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the circuit breaker trips repeatedly, if you measure incorrect or fluctuating voltage at the terminal block, or if the PF code returns immediately after a proper reset with confirmed stable power. A technician can perform load testing, verify that the control board's internal power supply is functioning correctly, and safely replace the control or interface board. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working inside the oven's electrical junction box or if local code requires a licensed electrician for any appliance wiring work.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0 (reset only), or $150-250 for control board if proven faulty, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for service call and diagnostics, $250-400 if control board replacement is needed.
