## Bosch Washer E23 Error Code — What It Means

The E23 error code on a Bosch front-load washing machine signals that the AquaStop safety system has detected water in the base pan underneath the drum. The AquaStop sensor sits in this pan and triggers when any leak from internal hoses, seals, the pump, or the drum allows water to collect where it should not be. When the code appears, the machine immediately stops the wash cycle, drains the tub, and locks the door to prevent further operation until the leak is found and repaired.

This code is a symptom of water escape, not a single component failure. The machine is protecting your floor by refusing to run while water is present in the chassis. The fault will not clear until you remove the source of the leak and dry out the base pan so the sensor resets.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the door seal or inlet valve first, assuming visible leaks are the culprit. Before buying parts, tip the machine back to inspect the base pan and trace the actual source of water, which is often hidden at the pump or internal hose connection.

## Common Causes

- **Drain pump seal or housing failure (~40%)** Cracks in the pump housing or worn seals allow water to leak from the pump into the base pan during the drain cycle.
- **Loose or damaged hose connections (~25%)** Inlet hoses or internal drain hoses that are kinked, cracked, or not fully seated at the clamp allow water to drip into the chassis.
- **Door seal (tub seal) deterioration (~15%)** The rubber gasket around the door becomes brittle, torn, or misaligned and lets water escape during the wash cycle.
- **Detergent drawer overflow (~10%)** Using too much detergent or a clogged siphon in the drawer causes suds or water to spill over and run down into the base pan.
- **Internal drum or bearing seal leak (~10%)** A crack in the plastic tub or a failed rear bearing seal allows water to bypass the tub and pool in the base.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power and water supply.** Unplug the machine from the wall outlet and turn off both hot and cold water taps. Wait five minutes to allow internal electronics and the AquaStop sensor to reset.
2. **Inspect external hoses and door seal.** Check the inlet hoses at both the machine and wall connection for kinks, cracks, or loose fittings. Examine the rubber door seal for visible tears, debris, or misalignment. Tighten or replace any damaged hose.
3. **Verify the machine is level.** Place a spirit level on top of the machine from side to side and front to back. Adjust the front leveling feet by screwing them in or out until the machine sits perfectly level.
4. **Access the base pan.** Remove the lower front panel or rear panel (depending on your model) using a T20 Torx bit. Tilt the machine back gently and inspect the base pan for standing water. Note the wettest area to trace the leak source.
5. **Inspect the drain pump.** Locate the drain pump at the bottom front or rear. Check the pump housing for cracks and the seals for wear. Disconnect the hoses (have a towel ready) and remove the pump. If the housing is cracked or seals are damaged, replace the pump assembly.
6. **Check internal hoses and clamps.** Follow the drain hose from the pump to the rear outlet and inspect for loose clamps or splits. Check the air pressure hose and any internal recirculation hoses for proper seating.
7. **Dry the base pan and reassemble.** Use towels or a shop vacuum to remove all water from the base pan. Allow the pan to air-dry completely or use a fan to speed drying. Reinstall the panels, reconnect hoses, restore water and power, and run a short rinse cycle to verify the leak is resolved.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you find water in the base pan but cannot locate the source after checking hoses, pump, and door seal. A hidden crack in the inner tub or a failed rear bearing seal requires disassembly of the drum and cabinet, which is a multi-hour job with special tools. Also call if the machine will not drain even after clearing the pump filter and hoses, or if the door remains locked after power cycling and the error persists with no visible leak. A technician can perform a leak test under pressure and access wiring diagrams to rule out a faulty AquaStop sensor itself, which is rare but possible.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-120 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
