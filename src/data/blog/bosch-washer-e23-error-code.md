## Bosch Washer E23 Error Code — What It Means

The E23 or F23 error code on a Bosch washer means the AquaStop leak-protection system has been activated. Bosch designed this system to shut the machine down immediately when it detects water where it should not be, usually in the base pan beneath the drum. The machine will not run until the fault is corrected.

Bosch's official guidance states that when E23 appears, you must turn off the water tap and do not use the washer until the leak source is found and repaired. The code does not point to a single failed part but rather signals that water has escaped from the normal wash-tub path and triggered the leak sensor.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the drain pump or inlet valve without first inspecting the door seal and drain filter. Before buying any part, dry the base pan, run a short test cycle, and watch closely where water appears.

## Common Causes

- **Water leaked into base pan (~50%)** A cracked door seal, loose hose clamp, split drain hose, or weeping pump lets water drip into the base where the AquaStop float switch is triggered.
- **Door seal or bellow tear (~20%)** The rubber door gasket can crack or develop holes, especially near the fold at the bottom, allowing water to run down into the cabinet during the wash or spin.
- **Clogged or kinked drain hose (~15%)** A blocked drain filter or kinked drain hose causes water to back up and overflow into the cabinet rather than pumping out properly.
- **Inlet valve overfill or leak (~10%)** A stuck or failed inlet valve can continue filling even when the machine should stop, or it may leak internally into the base.
- **Detergent overuse or foaming (~5%)** Too much detergent or the wrong type creates excessive suds that overflow the tub and run down into the base pan.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the water tap** at the wall and unplug the washer immediately. Bosch instructs you not to use the machine until the leak is fixed.
2. **Inspect the exterior** for puddles or damp areas around the base, behind the machine, and under the door. Note where water appears.
3. **Open the drain filter access** (usually lower front) and remove the filter. Clean it of lint, coins, and debris, then check the pump impeller area for blockage or cracks.
4. **Examine the door seal** all the way around, paying special attention to the bottom fold where water pools. Look for splits, tears, or mold that indicate a leak path.
5. **Tilt the washer back carefully** against a wall (with help) to let any water in the base pan drain out. Dry the base thoroughly with towels or let it air for several hours.
6. **Check hose connections** at the inlet valve (back top), tub-to-pump hose (inside cabinet if accessible), and drain hose routing. Tighten clamps and replace any cracked hoses.
7. **Reconnect power and water**, then run a short rinse or drain cycle while watching the door seal, pump area, and hose connections closely. If E23 returns, the leak source is still active and you will need to replace the leaking component or call a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the E23 error returns immediately after you have dried the base and run a test cycle, or if you cannot locate the leak source after inspecting the door seal, hoses, and pump. Also call if you are uncomfortable tilting the washer or disassembling cabinet panels to reach internal hose clamps. A pro can pressure-test the tub, inspect the AquaStop float switch and wiring, and replace internal components that require cabinet disassembly. Bosch recommends contacting service if the AquaStop system itself has failed, because continuing to run the machine with an active leak risks water damage to your floor and cabinetry.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts (seal, hose, or pump), 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on the leak source and part.
