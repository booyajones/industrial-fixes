## Electrolux Dryer E23 Error Code — What It Means

The E23 error code on Electrolux dryers typically indicates a communication or sensor problem within the control system. Because specific meanings can vary across model families, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram for your exact model to confirm whether E23 relates to a temperature sensor, control board communication, or another circuit fault.

In general, when the control board cannot read a sensor correctly or detects a wiring issue, it will display an error code to prevent unsafe operation. The dryer may refuse to start, stop mid-cycle, or fail to heat properly when this code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but a loose or corroded connector at the thermistor or control board is far more common. Always inspect and reseat every connector before ordering expensive boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~40%)** Vibration and heat cycles can work connectors loose or introduce corrosion, breaking the signal path between sensors and the control board.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** The thermistor may drift out of range or fail open, sending an invalid reading that triggers the error.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires can be pinched by the drum, chafed by sharp metal edges, or burnt by contact with the heater assembly.
- **Faulty control board (~12%)** The main electronic control board may have a failed input circuit or corrupted firmware that misreads sensor data.
- **Grounding or short-circuit fault (~8%)** A wire touching the cabinet or another terminal can create a short that the control board interprets as a sensor fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker.
2. **Remove the top or rear panel** to access the control board and wiring harness, following your model's service manual.
3. **Inspect and reseat every connector** on the control board, thermistor, and any sensor plugs, checking for corrosion or loose pins.
4. **Trace the wiring harness** from the control board to each sensor, looking for pinched, chafed, or burnt insulation.
5. **Test the thermistor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode; compare the reading at room temperature to the specification in your wiring diagram.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the resistance is out of range or the sensor is visibly damaged.
7. **Reassemble the panels**, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the error code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connectors or disassembling the cabinet panels. Also call if you have verified wiring and sensors are intact but the error persists, as control board diagnosis often requires specialized tools and the board itself can be expensive. A technician can test input circuits with a multimeter and firmware diagnostics to pinpoint the fault without unnecessary part swaps.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
