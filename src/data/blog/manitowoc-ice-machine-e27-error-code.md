## Manitowoc Ice Machine E27 Error — What It Means

The E27 error on a Manitowoc ice machine indicates the freeze cycle has taken too long to complete. The machine expects ice to form within a certain time window, and when that does not happen the control board halts operation and displays this fault. This timeout protects the compressor and other components from running indefinitely without producing ice.

The code usually points to a problem preventing proper freezing: insufficient water entering the evaporator, weak refrigerant charge, dirty condenser coils, or a failing compressor. Because the freeze cycle depends on both adequate water supply and proper refrigeration, you will need to check both the water side and the refrigeration side to find the root cause.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many technicians replace the water inlet valve first, but a clogged inline water filter or scale buildup in the distribution tubes is usually to blame. Check and clean the filter and flush the water system before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged water filter or inlet screen (~40%)** Scale, sediment, or mineral deposits block water flow into the machine so the evaporator never fills completely and ice formation stalls.
- **Low refrigerant charge (~25%)** A slow leak or undercharge prevents the evaporator from reaching the low temperature needed to freeze water within the allotted time.
- **Dirty condenser coil (~20%)** Dust and lint on the condenser reduce heat rejection, raising head pressure and slowing the freeze cycle enough to trigger the timeout.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~10%)** A weak or partially open valve delivers insufficient water volume even when the filter is clean, starving the evaporator.
- **Failing compressor or expansion device (~5%)** Worn compressor valves or a clogged metering device reduce refrigeration capacity so the freeze cycle runs long without completing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the machine** at the breaker or unplug the unit to make sure safe access to all components.
2. **Locate and remove the water filter** (usually in the supply line upstream of the inlet valve) and inspect it for sediment, scale, or discoloration.
3. **Flush or replace the filter** if you see any buildup, then check the small mesh screen inside the inlet valve body and clean it with a soft brush.
4. **Remove the front or side panel** to expose the condenser coil and use a vacuum with a soft brush or a coil cleaning brush to remove dust and lint from the fins.
5. **Restore power and initiate a cleaning or manual freeze cycle** from the control panel to observe water flow into the evaporator and listen for smooth compressor operation.
6. **Monitor the freeze cycle** to confirm ice begins forming within the normal time window and the E27 code does not reappear.
7. **If the code persists** after cleaning the water and air sides, call a refrigeration technician to test refrigerant pressures and compressor performance.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed refrigeration technician if cleaning the water filter and condenser coil does not clear the E27 code. Low refrigerant charge, a failing compressor, or a blocked expansion valve all require gauges, recovery equipment, and EPA certification to diagnose and repair safely. Attempting refrigerant work without proper tools and training can damage the sealed system and void any remaining warranty. A technician will measure suction and discharge pressures, check superheat and subcooling, and perform a compressor amp draw test to pinpoint the fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
