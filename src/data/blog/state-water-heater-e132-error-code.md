## State Water Heater E132 Error Code — What It Means

The E132 error code on State tankless water heaters typically indicates an ignition or combustion failure. State tankless units are built on the A. O. Smith platform, and while manufacturer documentation does not always list every model-specific code by number, E132 falls into the ignition-failure class common to these platforms. The heater detects a problem preventing reliable flame ignition or flame sensing, so it shuts down to protect the appliance and your home.

Because State publishes dozens of tankless models with different control boards, the exact meaning of E132 can vary. Always locate your unit's rating plate or QR code, note the full model number, and download the matching manual or tech sheet from the manufacturer. That document will give you the precise definition and any model-specific diagnostics for E132.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screen** Lint, dust, or debris restricts combustion air flow and prevents reliable ignition.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** A partially closed gas valve, empty propane tank, or low line pressure stops the burner from lighting.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents the board from confirming ignition.
- **Clogged inlet water filter** Sediment restricts flow below the minimum threshold needed to trigger a heating cycle.
- **Blocked or restricted vent pipe** Ice, bird nests, or condensate blockage in the exhaust vent causes combustion-air problems.
- **Faulty igniter or gas valve** A failing igniter assembly or stuck gas valve solenoid cannot complete the ignition sequence.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power and gas.** Turn off the circuit breaker or unplug the unit, then close the manual gas shutoff valve at the heater to work safely.
2. **Verify your model number.** Locate the rating plate or scan the QR code on the side of the cabinet and download the exact manual for your State tankless model so you can confirm the E132 definition and wiring diagram.
3. **Check the gas supply.** Open the manual gas valve fully, confirm propane tanks have fuel, and look for any kinks or closed valves upstream of the heater.
4. **Inspect and clean the air intake screen.** Remove the intake screen or cover and wash away lint, dust, or debris with water, then reinstall it securely.
5. **Remove and rinse the inlet water filter.** Unscrew the cold-water inlet service valve, pull the mesh screen, rinse under a faucet, and reinstall to restore full flow.
6. **Inspect the flame sensor or igniter.** Remove the burner-chamber cover (consult your manual for fastener locations), locate the flame rod or igniter, and gently clean any carbon deposits with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth.
7. **Restore power and gas, then test.** Open the gas valve, turn the breaker back on, open a hot tap, and watch the display; if E132 returns immediately, call a licensed technician for control-board or gas-valve diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

If you have cleaned the air intake, confirmed full gas pressure, rinsed the inlet filter, and inspected the flame sensor but E132 still appears, call a licensed plumber or gas technician. Persistent ignition codes often point to a failing gas valve, faulty control board, or incorrect combustion-air vent installation that requires a combustion analyzer and manufacturer training to diagnose safely. Any work involving gas piping, pressure testing, or control-board replacement should be left to a professional to protect your warranty and meet local codes.
