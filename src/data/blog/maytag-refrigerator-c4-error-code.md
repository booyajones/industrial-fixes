## Maytag Refrigerator C4 Error Code — What It Means

The C4 code on your Maytag refrigerator indicates a problem with the freezer compartment defrost sensor circuit. This sensor, typically a thermistor, monitors temperature during defrost cycles and normal cooling. When the control board detects a reading that is out of range, open, shorted, or inconsistent, it throws the C4 fault. The code does not appear in all Maytag service literature and may be model-specific, so always verify against your unit's tech sheet. The underlying issue is usually a failed sensor, damaged wiring, or a corroded connector rather than a sealed-system or compressor fault.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or out-of-range thermistor** The freezer defrost sensor itself has drifted, shorted, or opened and no longer reads within the acceptable range.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector** The plug at the sensor or control board is not seated fully, has corrosion, or shows moisture damage that creates an intermittent or open circuit.
- **Damaged harness or pinched wire** The wire running from the sensor to the main control is pinched, cut, or has broken insulation, causing a short or open.
- **Ice or frost buildup on the sensor** Moisture intrusion or evaporator frost has reached the sensor or its connector, distorting the resistance reading or creating a short.
- **Control board misinterpretation** The main control itself has a fault in the sensor input circuit, reading an error even when the sensor and wiring test correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your model number and locate the tech sheet** inside the refrigerator or online, because C4 is not universally documented and diagnostic steps vary by platform.
2. **Unplug the refrigerator** and access the evaporator or freezer compartment to locate the defrost sensor or thermistor, usually clipped to the evaporator coil or its housing.
3. **Inspect the sensor connector and harness visually** for corrosion, frost, moisture, loose pins, or damaged insulation before removing any parts.
4. **Disconnect the sensor and measure its resistance** with a multimeter at room temperature or the current compartment temperature, then compare the reading to your model's service data or typical thermistor curves.
5. **Check for continuity and proper voltage** at the harness connector leading to the control board if the sensor itself reads within range, to rule out a wiring or board fault.
6. **Inspect the evaporator and airflow path** for excessive frost, blocked vents, or defrost-system problems that could cause real temperature swings and trigger false sensor faults.
7. **Replace the faulty sensor, repair damaged wiring, or replace the main control** depending on your test results, then restore power and monitor for 24 hours to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working inside the freezer compartment, measuring resistance with a multimeter, or interpreting your model's service diagnostics. Also call if you replace the sensor and repair all visible wiring but the C4 code returns, since that points to a control board fault or a more complex defrost-system issue that requires model-specific troubleshooting and part cross-reference. If your unit is still under warranty, contact Maytag or an authorized service provider before opening any panels.
