## Mitsubishi U6 Error Code — What It Means

On Mitsubishi Electric mini-splits, U6 indicates a compressor overcurrent interruption or abnormality of the power module in the outdoor unit. This is not a simple filter or indoor airflow issue. Mitsubishi error-code references list U6 as a fault in the inverter/power module circuit path or a protective trip due to excessive current draw from the compressor. Technicians treat this as an outdoor inverter or compressor electrical fault until proven otherwise.

The fault belongs to the same family as Mitsubishi UF and UP overcurrent codes. It points to a problem in the outdoor drive circuit, either in the inverter board that powers the compressor or in the compressor motor itself. In rare cases, incorrect supply voltage or closed service valves can also trigger the code, but the root cause is usually a failed power PCB or a compressor that is drawing too much current due to mechanical seizure or winding failure.

## Common Causes

- **Failed outdoor inverter board or power PCB** The power module on the outdoor circuit board has failed or developed a diode/transistor abnormality, preventing safe current delivery to the compressor.
- **Compressor electrical or mechanical failure** The compressor windings are shorted, grounded, or the bearings are seized, causing the drive circuit to see overcurrent and trip.
- **Damaged wiring or connections between inverter and compressor** Heat-damaged, broken, or rodent-chewed wiring between the outdoor power PCB and compressor terminals causes intermittent high current or fault signals.
- **Incorrect supply voltage at the outdoor unit** Line voltage that does not match the unit's rated input (single-phase or three-phase) can cause the inverter to misfire or the compressor to draw excess current.
- **Closed outdoor service valves** If the liquid or suction service valve is shut, the compressor runs against a blockage and the inverter detects overcurrent.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the outdoor disconnect and wait three to five minutes to allow the inverter capacitors to fully discharge and the controller to reset.
2. **Verify the model number and consult the specific fault table** for your unit, because Mitsubishi fault meanings can vary by platform and controller family.
3. **Check that outdoor supply voltage** matches the unit nameplate rating (single-phase or three-phase) at the outdoor disconnect, and confirm both service valves are fully open in the running position.
4. **Inspect all wiring harnesses and terminal connections** between the outdoor power PCB and the compressor, looking for loose terminals, heat damage, rodent chewing, or visible burn marks.
5. **Visually inspect the outdoor inverter/power PCB** for burnt components, discolored solder joints, or cracked traces on the board surface.
6. **Measure compressor electrical condition** by testing resistance across the compressor terminals and from each terminal to ground, then compare drive output signals from the power PCB to expected operation if you have the service manual.
7. **Replace the outdoor inverter board** if the wiring and compressor test correctly but the fault persists, or **replace the compressor** if windings are shorted, open, or bearings are seized, then restore power and retest operation to verify the fault does not recur.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician immediately if you see U6. This fault involves high-voltage inverter circuits and refrigerant-side components that require EPA certification, specialized multimeter skills, and model-specific service manuals. Diagnosing whether the inverter board or the compressor has failed requires measuring drive outputs under power and interpreting winding-resistance values against factory tables. Compressor replacement also means recovering refrigerant, brazing, evacuation, and recharge. Attempting this repair without proper tools and training risks electric shock, refrigerant release, and expensive secondary damage to the new parts.
