## MRCOOL Mini Split EH 13 Error — What It Means

The EH 13 error code on a MRCOOL mini split indicates a fault with the electric auxiliary heater circuit. This code typically appears when the control board detects a problem preventing the heater from operating safely, such as an open circuit, a tripped thermal cutout, or a wiring issue. The system will usually disable the heater function until the fault is cleared.

The code protects the unit from overheating or electrical damage. It does not always mean the heater element itself has failed. Loose connections, damaged wiring, or safety switches can all trigger the same code. Check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit for the specific fault table for your model, as exact definitions can vary.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the entire heater element when the real problem is a tripped thermal cutout switch or a corroded wire terminal. Test continuity across the cutout and inspect all connections before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Tripped thermal cutout (~35%)** The safety switch opens when the heater overheats, often due to restricted airflow or a dirty filter.
- **Loose or corroded heater wiring (~30%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen terminals or corrode connections, creating an open circuit.
- **Failed heater element (~20%)** The resistance coil can burn out or develop an open, preventing current flow.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The relay or driver circuit that powers the heater can fail or lose communication.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~5%)** Pinched or chafed wires between the board and heater can short or open the circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and the disconnect switch near the outdoor unit.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** by lifting the front panel and unscrewing the service cover to expose the heater assembly.
3. **Inspect all wire terminals** at the heater element and thermal cutout for burn marks, corrosion, or loose connections. Tighten or clean as needed.
4. **Test the thermal cutout** with a multimeter set to continuity. It should read near zero ohms when cool. If open, replace it or wait for it to cool and reset.
5. **Check the heater element** for continuity. Consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected resistance value. An open reading means the element has failed.
6. **Reassemble the unit**, restore power, and run a heat cycle to verify the error clears and the heater operates.
7. **If the code persists**, check the control board connections and consider calling a technician to test the board's relay output.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with line voltage or if you lack a multimeter and wiring diagram. Heater circuits carry 120V or 240V and require careful testing to isolate the fault. A technician can safely measure voltage at the heater terminals, test the control board relay, and replace components without risking electric shock. If the thermal cutout keeps tripping even after you clean the filter and restore airflow, the underlying cause may be a failed fan motor or a refrigerant issue that affects heating capacity, both of which need professional diagnosis.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
