## What this code means
E15 on a Bosch dishwasher means the appliance's leak-protection system has detected water in the base pan or bottom tray, so the safety switch has been activated. Bosch describes this as a water leakage condition and a water-in-the-base event, not a generic electronic fault.

The code triggers when water accumulates in the tray underneath the dishwasher tub, signaling that something inside has leaked or overflowed. The safety float or switch in that base compartment activates to prevent further flooding and shuts down normal operation until the leak is addressed and the water is removed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board thinking E15 is an electronic fault. The code is a leak-protection activation, so always remove water from the base and inspect door seals, hoses, and connections before replacing any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Water in the base pan from an internal leak (~100%)** Any leak source that allows water to reach the base tray will trip the safety float and generate E15.
- **Worn or damaged door seal (~30%)** A cracked, torn, or improperly seated door gasket lets water escape during the cycle and drip into the base.
- **Loose or leaking hoses (~25%)** Internal drain, circulation, or inlet hose connections can work loose or develop cracks, allowing water to seep into the base pan.
- **Drain hose kinks or blockages (~15%)** A kinked or clogged drain hose can cause back-pressure that leads to overflow or slow leakage at connections.
- **Water inlet valve leak (~15%)** A faulty inlet valve may drip continuously or fail to seal properly, depositing water in the base over time.
- **Cracks or damage in the tub or sump (~10%)** Physical impact, age, or manufacturing defects can crack the plastic tub or sump housing, letting water escape into the base.
- **Debris around the float or safety switch (~5%)** Detergent residue, mineral buildup, or foreign objects in the base tray can keep the polystyrene float stuck in the triggered position even after water is removed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power and water.** Switch off the dishwasher at the breaker or unplug it, then close the water supply valve under the sink before opening the machine.
2. **Access the base pan.** Remove the toe-kick panel or lower front cover and pull the dishwasher forward slightly if needed to reach the bottom tray area.
3. **Remove standing water.** Use towels, a sponge, or a wet-dry vacuum to soak up all water in the base pan, and tilt the unit gently if necessary to drain residual moisture.
4. **Inspect the door seal.** Run your hand around the entire door gasket looking for cracks, tears, hardening, or gaps that would allow water to escape during a cycle.
5. **Check all hose connections.** Examine the drain hose, inlet hose, and internal circulation hoses for loose clamps, cracks, kinks, or wet spots that indicate a slow leak.
6. **Inspect the inlet valve and sump.** Look for drips at the inlet valve body and check the sump housing and tub seams for cracks or seepage points.
7. **Clean and dry the float area.** Remove the polystyrene float disk from the base compartment, rinse off any detergent film or debris, dry it thoroughly, and reinstall so the safety switch can reset.
8. **Reassemble and test.** Replace panels, restore power and water, and run a short rinse cycle while watching the base area for any new leaks or recurrence of E15.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the leak source after inspecting the door seal, hoses, and inlet valve, or if the tub or sump shows cracks that require disassembly or replacement. Also contact Bosch service or a qualified technician if E15 returns immediately after drying the base and repairing obvious leaks, since persistent activation may indicate a hidden internal crack, a faulty leak-protection switch, or a problem that requires factory diagnostic tools and service documentation for your specific model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in parts (seal, clamp, or hose), 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for service call, diagnosis, and leak repair.
