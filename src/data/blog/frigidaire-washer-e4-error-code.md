## What this code means
On Frigidaire top-loading washers, the E4 error code signals that the unit is out of balance during the spin cycle. The washer will attempt to correct the imbalance automatically, but if it cannot resolve the condition after three attempts, it will stop and prompt you to redistribute the load.

This code is tied to installation and load distribution rather than a component failure. Frigidaire support documentation identifies unbalanced loads, the washer not resting firmly on the floor, leveling-leg issues, and foam shipping support left in place as the primary causes of E4 errors.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace suspension rods or dampers thinking the washer is mechanically faulty. Before ordering parts, verify the washer is level, all four legs are firmly on the floor, and the load is balanced.

## Common Causes

- **Unbalanced load (~40%)** Heavy items piled to one side of the basket prevent the washer from achieving stable spin speed, triggering the out-of-balance detection after three automatic correction attempts.
- **Washer not sitting firmly or level (~30%)** If one or more leveling legs are not properly adjusted or the floor is uneven, the machine rocks during spin and registers an imbalance even with a well-distributed load.
- **Foam shipping support not removed (~15%)** Frigidaire specifically calls out foam shipping blocks or supports that were not removed during installation, which restrict tub movement and cause balance faults.
- **Leveling-leg issues (~10%)** Legs that are not locked in place or are unevenly extended allow the washer to shift during spin, preventing the automatic balancing system from working correctly.
- **Single heavy garment or blanket (~5%)** A large towel, comforter, or rug absorbs water unevenly and creates a one-sided weight that the washer cannot redistribute on its own.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Pause the cycle and redistribute the load.** Open the lid, rearrange heavy items so they are evenly spread around the basket, and close the lid to resume the spin.
2. **Check that the washer is level.** Place a bubble level on top of the washer front-to-back and side-to-side. Adjust the leveling legs by turning them clockwise to extend or counterclockwise to retract until the bubble is centered in both directions.
3. **Confirm all four legs are firmly on the floor.** Rock the washer gently by pushing on opposite corners. If it moves, adjust the legs and lock them by tightening the jam nuts against the base.
4. **Inspect for shipping materials.** If the unit was recently installed or moved, look beneath and inside the cabinet for foam blocks or supports and remove any that remain.
5. **Run a test spin with a balanced load.** Load a mix of small and medium items evenly around the basket, select a spin-only or rinse-and-spin cycle, and confirm the washer completes spin without stopping.
6. **Clear the code.** Once the load is balanced and the washer is level, press the cancel or pause button to clear E4, then restart the cycle.
7. **Verify the floor is solid and level.** If the washer sits on a wood subfloor or uneven surface, install a washer platform or shim the legs to create a stable base that does not flex during spin.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified the washer is level, all shipping materials are removed, and the load is balanced, yet E4 persists. At that point the issue may be a worn suspension rod, broken tub spring, or faulty balance-ring assembly that requires disassembly and diagnosis beyond typical homeowner tools. A technician can also assess whether the floor itself needs reinforcement or whether the machine requires relocation to a more stable surface.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0 in most cases, 10-20 min to level or redistribute. A pro service call runs about $80-150 service call if installation correction is needed.
