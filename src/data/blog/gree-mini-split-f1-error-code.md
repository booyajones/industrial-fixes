## Gree Mini-Split F1 Error Code — What It Means

The F1 error code on a Gree mini-split indicates an indoor ambient sensor malfunction. Gree groups all F-series faults as sensor malfunctions, and F1 specifically refers to the room temperature sensor in the indoor unit. This sensor is a thermistor that monitors air temperature and tells the control board when to cycle the compressor and fan. When the sensor fails, goes out of range, or loses its connection to the control board, the unit throws F1 and stops normal operation to prevent damage.

The indoor ambient sensor is a small thermistor located in the air stream or near the evaporator coil inside the head unit. It feeds a resistance signal to the indoor PCB. If that signal is missing, shorted, or nonsensical, the board cannot regulate temperature and flags the fault. Most F1 codes trace to a loose connector, damaged wire, or a dead thermistor rather than a board problem.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or unplugged sensor connector** The thermistor plug at the indoor PCB has worked loose, causing an open circuit that the board reads as a sensor fault.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** The sensor lead has been pinched during installation, rubbed through by vibration, corroded by moisture, or chewed by rodents.
- **Failed indoor ambient thermistor** The room temperature sensor itself has failed open, shorted, or drifted out of its normal resistance range.
- **Moisture or corrosion in connectors** Water intrusion or humidity has corroded the sensor plug or PCB terminals, breaking electrical contact.
- **Indoor PCB sensor-input circuit fault** If the sensor and wiring test good, the control board's sensor interface circuitry may have failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off at the disconnect or breaker.** Do not work on live sensor circuits or the indoor unit while energized.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** and locate the indoor ambient thermistor and its connector on the main PCB.
3. **Inspect the sensor connector and wiring** for looseness, corrosion, broken insulation, or pinched wires, and reseat the plug firmly.
4. **Disconnect the sensor from the PCB** and measure its resistance with a multimeter on a 20 kΩ range. A healthy room sensor should read around 10 kΩ at normal room temperature.
5. **Warm the sensor between your fingers** and watch the resistance change smoothly. A dead open, dead short, or erratic jump indicates a failed thermistor.
6. **Replace the indoor ambient sensor** if resistance is out of range or does not change smoothly with temperature.
7. **If the fault persists with a known-good sensor,** replace the indoor main control board or diagnose the sensor-input circuit on the PCB.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the indoor unit, if you do not own a multimeter or have no experience testing resistance, or if you have replaced the sensor and harness but the F1 code returns. Diagnosing a faulty PCB sensor-input circuit requires schematic-level troubleshooting and may involve replacing the entire indoor control board. A pro can also verify refrigerant charge and other system parameters after the repair to confirm normal operation.
