## Kenmore Dishwasher E2 Error Code — What It Means

The E2 error code on many Kenmore dishwasher models typically indicates a drainage issue. The control board is detecting that water is not leaving the tub within the expected time window. This can stem from a physical blockage in the drain path, a faulty drain pump, or a problem with the sensors and wiring that monitor water level. Because Kenmore models vary and some error codes differ by production year, always cross-check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the door to confirm the exact meaning for your specific model number.

The code will usually halt the wash cycle and may flash on the display along with a beep pattern. In most cases the root cause is not an electronic failure but rather something blocking the flow of water out of the machine.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump right away, but a clogged filter or kinked hose causes the same error. Clean the filter and inspect the entire drain path before buying a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filter or sump (~45%)** Food particles, broken glass, or grease build up in the fine mesh filter and prevent water from reaching the drain pump inlet.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~20%)** The flexible drain hose can sag, kink at a sharp bend under the sink, or accumulate a grease plug that slows drainage below the timeout threshold.
- **Failed drain pump (~20%)** The impeller inside the pump can crack, seize, or clog with debris so it hums but does not move water.
- **Faulty pressure switch or sensor (~10%)** A stuck pressure switch or air-chamber blockage tells the board water is still present even after the pump runs.
- **Wiring or connector issues (~5%)** Corroded terminals or a loose plug between the pump and control board can interrupt the signal and trigger the error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the water supply valve under the sink.
2. **Remove the lower dish rack** and unscrew or twist out the cylindrical filter assembly from the sump at the bottom of the tub.
3. **Rinse the filter** under running water and use a soft brush to clear any grease or trapped debris from the mesh.
4. **Inspect the sump cavity** below the filter for broken glass, toothpicks, or other foreign objects and remove them by hand.
5. **Pull the dishwasher forward** enough to access the drain hose connection at the back or side, then disconnect the hose and check for kinks or internal blockage.
6. **Test the drain pump** by reconnecting power and starting a drain or cancel cycle, listening for the motor hum and checking that water exits the hose.
7. **Replace the drain pump** if it does not run or if the impeller is cracked or jammed; remove the lower spray arm and pump cover, disconnect the wiring harness and hose clamps, lift out the old pump, and install the new unit in reverse order.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the filter and hose but the error persists and you are not comfortable testing electrical connections with a multimeter. A technician can quickly verify pump voltage, check the pressure switch and air chamber for blockage, and diagnose control-board faults. Also call if you find water leaking from the pump area or if the dishwasher has a history of tripping the breaker, since that may indicate a short circuit requiring safer handling and potential board replacement.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
