## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E91 Error — What It Means

The E91 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a fault in the ignition or flame detection system. The unit is unable to establish or maintain a flame during the heating cycle. Because exact code definitions can vary between Ruud models and firmware versions, always check your specific unit's owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for the precise meaning.

This code may appear when the gas valve fails to open, the igniter fails to spark, the flame sensor is dirty or corroded, or gas pressure is too low. The heater will shut down as a safety precaution when it cannot verify combustion is occurring.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve when the real issue is a dirty flame sensor or a kinked gas supply line. Clean the sensor and verify full gas pressure before ordering valves.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~40%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensor rod prevents the control board from detecting the flame, triggering a shutdown.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~25%)** Insufficient gas pressure, a closed manual valve, or an empty propane tank will prevent ignition and trigger the error.
- **Failed gas valve (~20%)** The gas valve solenoid may stick closed or fail electrically, blocking gas flow to the burner.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition circuit (~10%)** A cracked igniter electrode or a failed ignition control module will prevent spark generation and flame establishment.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The main control board may fail to send proper signals to the gas valve or igniter, or misread the flame sensor input.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the manual gas shutoff valve at the unit inlet.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners, then locate the flame sensor rod near the burner assembly.
3. **Clean the flame sensor** with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits and corrosion, then wipe with a dry cloth.
4. **Inspect the igniter electrode** for cracks or heavy deposits and clean gently if accessible, taking care not to break the ceramic.
5. **Check all wire connections** at the gas valve, igniter, and flame sensor for looseness or corrosion and reseat any that appear suspect.
6. **Restore gas and power**, then monitor the startup sequence to confirm spark, flame, and normal operation without the error returning.
7. **If the error persists**, measure gas supply pressure with a manometer (consult your model's table for required inlet pressure) or call a licensed technician to test the gas valve and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas connections, if you do not have a manometer to measure gas pressure, or if cleaning the flame sensor does not resolve the E91 code. Any work on the gas valve, ignition control module, or main control board requires specialized diagnostic tools and knowledge of gas appliance safety codes. Professional service also includes combustion analysis and venting inspection to confirm safe operation after repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
