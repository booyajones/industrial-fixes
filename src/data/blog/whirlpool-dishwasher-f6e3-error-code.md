## Whirlpool F6E3 Dishwasher Error — What It Means

The F6E3 code on a Whirlpool dishwasher indicates that the machine has detected excess foam or suds in the wash system. When suds are present, Whirlpool says the dishwasher may not operate properly or may not fill with water. The sensing system has recognized a suds condition and triggered the fault to prevent damage or improper cleaning.

This is not a component failure code. Instead, it signals that something in the wash chemistry has created too much foam, usually from the wrong detergent product or too much of the right product. The suds can remain in the sump and drain path even after dishes are removed, so the machine will need to be flushed before normal operation can resume.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes assume F6E3 points to a faulty sensor or control board. In reality, Whirlpool's published fix is to remove suds and correct detergent use, not to replace electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Wrong detergent used (~50%)** Laundry detergent, hand soap, or hand-wash dish soap introduced into the dishwasher creates excessive foam that the machine detects.
- **Too much dishwasher detergent (~30%)** Overdosing automatic dishwasher detergent beyond the dispenser's recommended fill line can trigger a suds condition.
- **Rinse aid cap left off or not replaced (~15%)** After filling the rinse aid dispenser, leaving the cap off or not seating it properly can allow the product to leak into the tub and contribute to foaming.
- **Residual suds in sump or drain path (~5%)** If foam was severe enough to interrupt draining or sensing, suds can remain in the sump and drain components until they are flushed out with clean cycles.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code and inspect for foam.** Open the dishwasher door and look in the tub and sump area for visible suds or foam buildup.
2. **Identify the detergent history.** Confirm with everyone in the household whether laundry detergent, hand soap, or hand-wash dish soap was used, or whether automatic dishwasher detergent was overdosed.
3. **Remove as many suds as possible.** Use a cup, sponge, or absorbent towels to scoop and soak up visible foam from the tub and sump, then remove all dishes.
4. **Add 2–3 tablespoons of vegetable cooking oil.** Pour the oil directly into the bottom of the empty tub to suppress the suds, following Whirlpool's published corrective procedure.
5. **Run a full cycle with no detergent and no dishes.** Select a normal or heavy wash cycle and let it complete to flush the suds from the sump and drain path.
6. **Run a second full cycle with detergent but no dishes.** Place the normal dose of automatic dishwasher detergent in the dispenser cups and run another complete wash to confirm the machine fills, washes, and drains without error.
7. **Check the rinse aid dispenser cap.** make sure the cap is on and seated properly so rinse aid does not leak into the tub and create foam in future cycles.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the F6E3 code persists after you have removed all suds, run two full empty cycles, and verified that only the correct automatic dishwasher detergent is being used at the recommended dose. A technician can inspect the sump, drain pump, and sensing components for residual foam or a rare fault in the wash system. However, because Whirlpool's published guidance for F6E3 does not point to a replaceable part and the corrective action is to flush suds, most cases resolve with the oil-and-cycle procedure and proper detergent use going forward.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0–5 in cooking oil and time, 2–3 hours total for two full cycles. A pro service call runs about $100–180 if a technician is called, though the fix itself requires no parts.
