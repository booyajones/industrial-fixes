## Fujitsu E:24 Error Code — What It Means

The E:24 code on a Fujitsu mini-split indicates an excessive high-pressure protection fault during cooling mode. This means the system has detected abnormally high refrigerant discharge or condensing pressure and has shut down to prevent damage. The fault is specifically tied to cooling operation and is not a simple sensor wiring issue.

Because Fujitsu code formatting can vary by controller generation and region, you should confirm the exact meaning for your specific model by checking the service manual or indoor controller display. The E:24 designation is found on certain Fujitsu fault tables as code 24 for high-pressure protection on cooling.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil** Debris, cottonwood, or dirt buildup on the condenser coil restricts heat rejection and drives head pressure beyond the safe limit.
- **Failed or slow outdoor fan motor** If the condenser fan does not run or spins slowly, airflow drops and the outdoor unit cannot expel heat properly.
- **Blocked indoor airflow** Clogged filters or obstructed return-air grilles reduce evaporator heat transfer and push condensing pressure higher.
- **Refrigerant overcharge or noncondensables** If the system was recently serviced and too much refrigerant or air was introduced, pressure rises abnormally during cooling.
- **Restriction in the refrigerant circuit** A kinked line set, partially blocked electronic expansion valve, or plugged filter drier chokes refrigerant flow and elevates discharge pressure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault code** at the indoor controller or outdoor board and note your exact Fujitsu model number, since code display formats vary by controller generation.
2. **Inspect and clean the indoor filter** and check that supply and return grilles are not blocked by furniture, drapes, or dust buildup.
3. **Clean the outdoor coil** by rinsing from the inside out with low-pressure water or using coil cleaner to remove dirt, grass, and debris.
4. **Check outdoor fan operation** by powering on cooling mode and confirming the fan starts, spins at full speed, and is not obstructed by leaves or damaged blades.
5. **Measure refrigerant pressures** using manifold gauges on the service ports (if you have gauges and recovery equipment) and compare to the model-specific pressure table in your service manual.
6. **Inspect the line set and connections** for kinks, oil stains, or frost patterns that suggest a restriction or leak, and check that all valves are fully open.
7. **Reset the system** by cycling power at the breaker after correcting the root cause, then monitor one full cooling cycle to confirm the fault does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if cleaning the coils and filters does not clear the fault, if you do not own refrigerant gauges and recovery equipment, or if you suspect overcharge or a refrigerant-side restriction. High-pressure faults can also point to compressor valve problems or control-board failures that require pressure testing, electrical diagnosis, and sometimes refrigerant recovery and recharge. Because Fujitsu systems use inverter-driven compressors and specific refrigerant blends, attempting refrigerant work without proper tools and EPA certification can damage the unit and void your warranty.
