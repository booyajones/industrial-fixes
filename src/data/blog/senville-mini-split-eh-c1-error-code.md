## Senville EH C1 Error Code — What It Means

The EH C1 error appears on a Senville mini-split indoor unit that has detected refrigerant leakage. In a multi-zone system, other indoor units may show EC C1 to relay the same alert, but the unit displaying EH C1 is the one that originally sensed the problem. The alarm does not always mean refrigerant is actively escaping. It can also trigger if the leak sensor itself is faulty or if wiring to the sensor has failed.

Because refrigerant systems are sealed and pressurized, even a small leak will eventually compromise cooling performance and may cause compressor damage if ignored. Senville specifies that if no leak or sensor fault is found, the technician should also investigate refrigerant-circuit blockages as a possible cause of the alarm.

## Common Causes

- **Refrigerant leak at flare joints or indoor coil** Oil residue or bubbles at connections indicate escaping refrigerant, the most common trigger for EH C1.
- **Faulty refrigerant leak sensor** The sensor inside the detecting indoor unit can fail and report a leak that does not exist.
- **Wiring or connector problem at the leak sensor** Loose or corroded connections between the sensor and the indoor control board cause false alarms.
- **Indoor PCB logic fault** If the sensor and wiring test normal but the code persists, the indoor control board may be misinterpreting signals.
- **Refrigerant-circuit blockage or restriction** Senville notes that a blockage in the system can sometimes trigger leak-detection logic even when no leak is present.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the entire system** by switching off all indoor units at the breaker, waiting two full minutes, then restoring power and checking whether the error clears.
2. **Identify which indoor unit originally detected the leak.** In a multi-zone installation, the unit showing EH C1 is the source; units displaying EC C1 are only relaying the alarm.
3. **Inspect refrigerant connections on the detecting unit** for oil stains, then apply soapy water or use an electronic leak detector at flare nuts, service ports, and coil joints.
4. **Repair any confirmed leak** by recovering refrigerant, tightening or replacing the leaking component, evacuating the system, and recharging to the nameplate specification.
5. **Test the leak sensor and its wiring** if no leak is found. Disconnect the sensor connector at the indoor board and measure continuity and resistance according to your service manual.
6. **Replace the indoor control board** if the sensor tests normal, wiring is intact, and the alarm continues after a power cycle.
7. **Check for refrigerant-side restrictions** using gauge readings and superheat/subcooling measurements if all electrical components are confirmed good but the error will not clear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the error does not clear after a full power cycle, if you see oil around refrigerant fittings, or if you are not certified to handle refrigerant. Leak detection, evacuation, and recharging require EPA Section 608 certification, a vacuum pump, manifold gauges, and refrigerant-recovery equipment. Attempting repairs without proper tools can release refrigerant into the atmosphere (a federal violation) and leave moisture in the system that will destroy the compressor. A technician will also have access to Senville's service wiring diagrams and sensor specifications that are not published in homeowner manuals.
