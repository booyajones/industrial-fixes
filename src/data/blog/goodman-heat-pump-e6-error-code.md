## Goodman Heat Pump E6 Error Code — What It Means

The E6 error code on Goodman heat pumps is commonly reported as a compressor overload or compressor protection fault, though the exact meaning can vary by model and control board. The code typically appears when the compressor is working too hard, overheating, or experiencing electrical stress that triggers the built-in overload protection. This can stem from refrigerant problems, airflow restrictions, electrical faults, or a failing compressor itself.

Because Goodman does not publish a universal E6 definition across all heat pump platforms, always verify the code against your specific model's service literature or control board fault chart. The compressor is the heart of your heat pump, and an overload fault is the system's way of preventing permanent damage when operating conditions fall outside safe limits.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil** Restricted airflow across the condenser forces the compressor to work harder and run hotter, triggering overload protection.
- **Low refrigerant charge** Insufficient refrigerant increases compressor load and can cause the unit to overheat or struggle to maintain pressure.
- **Electrical connection problems** Loose, corroded, or burned terminals at the compressor or control board can create intermittent faults or voltage drops that mimic or cause overload conditions.
- **Failing compressor** A compressor with worn bearings, a locked rotor, or internal short will draw excessive current and trip its overload protection repeatedly.
- **Outdoor fan motor failure** If the outdoor fan is not running or is running slowly, heat rejection suffers and head pressure climbs, overloading the compressor.
- **Refrigerant restriction** A clogged filter-drier or kinked line can starve the compressor or create abnormal pressure conditions that lead to overload.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code in your model's manual.** Check the service literature or control board label for your exact Goodman outdoor unit model number to confirm the manufacturer definition of E6 before proceeding.
2. **Power-cycle the system.** Turn off the unit at the thermostat and disconnect power at the outdoor disconnect for two minutes, then restore power and recheck for the fault to rule out a transient error.
3. **Inspect and clean the outdoor coil.** Remove any debris, grass clippings, or dirt blocking the condenser fins and rinse the coil with a garden hose from the inside out if accessible.
4. **Check outdoor fan operation.** With power restored, confirm the outdoor fan is spinning freely at full speed when the unit calls for cooling or heating, and listen for unusual noise or grinding.
5. **Examine all electrical connections.** Open the outdoor unit's electrical cover and inspect compressor terminals, contactor connections, and control board plugs for loose wires, burn marks, or corrosion.
6. **Test compressor operation.** If the fault persists and all other conditions are normal, measure compressor amperage with a clamp meter and compare to the nameplate rating to detect locked-rotor, high-current, or intermittent overload conditions.
7. **Call a licensed HVAC technician** if the outdoor coil is clean, fan runs properly, and wiring looks good, as refrigerant charge, compressor testing, and advanced electrical diagnostics require gauges, recovery equipment, and model-specific knowledge.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the E6 fault returns after you have cleaned the coil and verified fan and electrical connections, or if you are not comfortable working with live 240-volt circuits. Compressor overload diagnosis requires refrigerant pressure and temperature measurements, electrical load testing, and sometimes compressor terminal resistance checks that need specialized tools and EPA certification for refrigerant work. A technician will also cross-reference your exact Goodman model's fault code chart to confirm whether E6 has any board-specific or sensor-related meaning on your platform, and can safely recover refrigerant before replacing a compressor or other sealed-system component.
