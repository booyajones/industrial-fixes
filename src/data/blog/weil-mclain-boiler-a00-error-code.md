## Weil-McLain Boiler A00 Error Code — What It Means

The A00 error code does not appear in Weil-McLain boiler documentation. The code you are seeing is almost certainly A01, which means the boiler's control module tried to light the burner but failed to detect a flame after the ignition sequence. This triggers a lockout condition that stops the boiler from operating. The display may show diagnostic codes (like d3) briefly before locking out and showing A01.

If your display truly shows A00 and not A01, check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the boiler jacket, because the exact meaning varies by model. For Aqua Balance and Aqua Balance Series 2 boilers, A01 is the standard no-ignition fault and the steps below apply to that code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the ignitor or control module without checking gas supply pressure or purging air from the line. Measure gas pressure with a manometer (must be 3.5 to 11 inches water column) and verify the manual shutoff valve is fully open before replacing any parts.

## Common Causes

- **No gas supply or line not purged (~35%)** The manual shutoff valve is closed, the utility has interrupted service, or the gas line was never purged after installation (common in new installs).
- **Insufficient gas pressure (~25%)** Supply pressure is below the required 3.5 to 11 inches water column, starving the burner.
- **Faulty ignition or detection electrode (~20%)** The ignitor is not sparking, is cracked, or the flame sensor is dirty or damaged and cannot detect the flame.
- **Faulty gas valve (~10%)** The gas valve relay has failed or the modulating valve is stuck, preventing gas flow even when the control calls for heat.
- **Air trapped in heat exchanger (~6%)** Air pockets in the heat exchanger prevent proper flame detection or water circulation, triggering lockout.
- **Blocked condensate trap (~4%)** The condensate trap is clogged or double-trapped, creating back pressure that disrupts burner operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify gas supply.** Walk to the manual gas shutoff valve at the boiler and confirm the handle is parallel to the pipe (fully open). Check that the utility has not shut off service.
2. **Reset the lockout.** Press the reset button on the control module for 1.5 seconds. Initiate a call for heat (raise the thermostat) and watch for the ignition sequence.
3. **Listen for the ignitor.** During the ignition attempt, listen for a rapid clicking sound near the burner. If you hear no spark, the ignitor or control module may be faulty.
4. **Measure gas pressure.** Use a manometer to check supply pressure at the gas valve inlet. Pressure must be between 3.5 and 11 inches water column. Low pressure requires utility or regulator repair.
5. **Inspect the ignition and detection electrodes.** Remove the burner cover and visually check the ignitor for cracks or carbon buildup. Clean or replace the electrode if damaged.
6. **Purge air from the heat exchanger.** If you hear sloshing or gurgling, open the purge valves on the supply and return lines and run the circulator to push air out of the system.
7. **Clear the condensate trap.** Remove the condensate trap, flush it with water, and reinstall. Never loop the condensate drain hose or create a double trap.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician immediately if you smell gas, if the gas valve or regulator shows physical damage, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. A technician with a manometer can measure gas pressure accurately and diagnose valve or electrode faults without guesswork. Air purging and electrode testing require access to internal components and an understanding of the ignition sequence. If you have already confirmed gas supply and reset the boiler without success, the problem is likely a failed valve, electrode, or control module that needs professional diagnosis and replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
