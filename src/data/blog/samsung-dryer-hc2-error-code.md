## Samsung Dryer HC2 Error Code — What It Means

HC2 is a gas ignition error code on Samsung gas dryers. The dryer has detected a fault in the ignition system, meaning the burner is not lighting or the flame is not being sensed correctly. Samsung's official support documentation identifies HC2 specifically as a gas ignition fault, not a general heating or temperature problem. The code appears when the control board cannot confirm proper ignition after calling for heat.

Do not confuse HC2 with HC or HE codes, which can indicate different issues. Samsung's public troubleshooting guidance for HC2 is straightforward: power the dryer off for 2 to 3 minutes, then turn it back on and retry the cycle. If the code returns after the reset, Samsung directs you to contact service support. The fault lies somewhere in the gas burner, ignition, or flame-sensing circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the control board first, assuming the error is electronic. Test the igniter for continuity and inspect the gas valve coils before replacing any circuit board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed igniter (~40%)** The igniter does not glow hot enough to open the gas valve or has lost continuity, preventing the burner from lighting.
- **Defective gas valve coils (~25%)** One or both gas valve solenoid coils have failed open or shorted, blocking gas flow even when the igniter is good.
- **Flame sensor fault (~20%)** The flame sensor is dirty, corroded, or broken and cannot prove ignition to the control board, so the code trips even if the burner briefly lights.
- **No gas supply or closed valve (~10%)** The gas shutoff valve is closed, kinked, or the house gas pressure is too low for the appliance to operate.
- **Control board or wiring harness damage (~5%)** Loose connectors, corroded terminals, or a failed main control board prevent the ignition sequence from completing normally.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dryer** for 2 to 3 minutes by unplugging it or turning off the circuit breaker, then power it back on and restart the cycle as Samsung directs.
2. **Confirm the gas supply** by checking that the gas shutoff valve to the dryer is fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are working normally.
3. **Unplug the dryer and disconnect the gas line** (cap the supply side immediately), then pull the dryer out and remove the front or rear access panel to reach the burner assembly.
4. **Inspect the igniter visually** for cracks, breaks, or burn marks, then use a multimeter to test continuity across the igniter terminals (consult your model's service data for the correct resistance range).
5. **Test the gas valve coils** for continuity and compare readings to the manufacturer specification for your exact model number.
6. **Check the flame sensor** for corrosion, soot buildup, or physical damage, and verify continuity if the service manual provides a test procedure.
7. **Replace any failed component** (igniter, gas valve coil, or flame sensor), reassemble the dryer, reconnect the gas line with a leak test using soapy water, restore power, and run a full heat cycle to confirm ignition and stable operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional immediately if you smell gas, are not confident working with gas lines, or lack the tools to test ignition components safely. Gas dryer repairs require shutting off and disconnecting the gas supply, electrical testing of live circuits, and leak testing after reconnection. A qualified appliance technician or gas service technician has the gauges, multimeter, service manuals, and experience to diagnose HC2 quickly and replace the correct part without risk. If you have already performed the Samsung-recommended power reset and verified the gas valve is open but the code returns, professional diagnosis is the safest and most efficient next step.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–$300.
