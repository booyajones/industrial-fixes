## Trane Heat Pump E5 Error Code — What It Means

The E5 error code on Trane heat pumps indicates a voltage protection fault in the unit's inverter or control electronics. The control system has detected an abnormal supply voltage condition or internal DC bus voltage problem and has shut down the unit to prevent damage. This is not a sensor fault. The exact definition can vary slightly by model family, so always verify your specific unit's code table in the service manual.

## Common Causes

- **Low or high incoming line voltage** Utility supply voltage outside the acceptable range triggers the protection circuit and stops the unit.
- **Power interruption or brownout history** Recent voltage dips or repeated power cycles can leave the control board in a voltage-fault state.
- **Loose or damaged wiring connections** Corroded terminals, loose power connections, or damaged harnesses create voltage instability that the board reads as a fault.
- **Failed inverter or main control board** If incoming power is correct, the fault may be in the power electronics that monitor or regulate internal voltages.
- **Bad contactor or disconnect** A failing contactor or loose disconnect can cause intermittent voltage drop under load.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your model's code table** in the service manual or on the unit label because E5 meanings are not identical across all Trane platforms.
2. **Measure incoming line voltage** at the unit's power terminals with a multimeter and compare to the nameplate voltage range while the unit is off and during attempted startup.
3. **Inspect all power terminals, the disconnect, and the contactor** for looseness, heat damage, corrosion, or burn marks and tighten or clean as needed.
4. **Check all wiring harnesses** from the disconnect to the control board for damage, chafing, or intermittent connections and repair or replace damaged sections.
5. **Power-cycle the unit** only after correcting any voltage or wiring issues. Turn off the breaker for two minutes, restore power, and observe whether the fault returns.
6. **Test the inverter or main control board** if incoming voltage is within spec and wiring is intact. If the E5 code reappears immediately after a reset, the board is likely faulty.
7. **Replace the failed component** once you have confirmed the fault source through voltage and continuity tests rather than guessing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line voltage or if incoming voltage tests normal but the fault persists. Voltage protection faults can point to failed inverter boards or internal control issues that require diagnostic equipment and manufacturer training to isolate safely. A technician will measure supply voltage under load, test the control board's internal regulation circuits, and replace only the confirmed failed component. Do not repeatedly reset the unit or replace parts without testing, as repeated voltage faults can damage other components.
