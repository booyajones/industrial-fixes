## Rheem Heat Pump A108 Error Code — What It Means

The A108 error code on Rheem heat pumps indicates a fault detected by the system control board, though the exact meaning can vary by model and series. Without specific documentation for your unit, A108 often points to a communication error between components, a sensor reading outside expected limits, or a wiring issue. Your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the access panel will provide the precise definition for your model. Do not assume the code means the same thing across all Rheem heat pump platforms.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real problem is a loose wire harness or a failed temperature sensor. Always check connector seating and measure sensor resistance before ordering expensive boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wire connector (~30%)** Vibration and weather exposure can work connectors loose or let moisture corrode pins, interrupting communication between the board and sensors.
- **Failed outdoor temperature sensor (~25%)** Sensors exposed to weather can drift out of range or develop open circuits, causing the board to log a fault.
- **Communication error between indoor and outdoor units (~20%)** A break or short in the low-voltage control wire linking the air handler and condenser will trigger communication codes on many models.
- **Control board firmware glitch (~15%)** A transient power surge or brownout can leave the board in a fault state that clears with a power cycle.
- **Defective control board (~10%)** The main board itself can fail, though this is less common than wiring and sensor problems.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or outdoor disconnect and wait two minutes to allow capacitors to discharge and the board to reset.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether the A108 code reappears immediately or after a few minutes of operation.
3. **Locate the control board** inside the outdoor unit or air handler, depending on your model, and remove the access panel.
4. **Inspect every wire connector** on the board for loose pins, corrosion, or burn marks. Reseat each connector firmly.
5. **Check the outdoor temperature sensor** lead and connector. If your multimeter shows an open circuit or a resistance far outside the expected range (consult your model's service chart), replace the sensor.
6. **Examine the low-voltage control wiring** running between indoor and outdoor units for breaks, pinches, or shorts.
7. **If all wiring and sensors test good**, record the error code and contact a qualified HVAC technician to test board outputs and firmware, or to replace the control board if confirmed defective.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the code persists after you have power-cycled the unit, reseated all connectors, and verified that sensors and wiring show no obvious damage. Diagnosing communication faults often requires a multimeter, wiring diagrams, and familiarity with Rheem board pinouts. Refrigerant or electrical work on heat pumps also requires EPA certification and high-voltage safety training. A professional can retrieve detailed fault logs from the board, measure sensor curves against factory specifications, and safely replace the control board if needed.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
