## State Water Heater E109 Error — What It Means

The E109 code on a State-branded water heater typically indicates a fault in the air supply or combustion fan system. The control board has detected that airflow is insufficient or that the fan is not operating correctly, and the unit shuts down to protect itself from unsafe conditions. This code is most commonly documented in Navien tankless systems under State branding, where it points to problems with the air inlet, the fan motor itself, or obstructions in the exhaust path.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged or dirty air inlet filter** Dust, lint, and debris block airflow into the combustion chamber, causing the unit to detect low air pressure and trigger the fault.
- **Fan motor failure or seized blower** The combustion fan may have failed electrically or mechanically stopped rotating due to bearing wear, corrosion, or foreign objects.
- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent** Bird nests, ice, or vent pipe damage can obstruct exhaust gases and prevent proper air circulation.
- **Loose or corroded wiring to the fan** Poor connections at the fan motor harness or control board can interrupt the signal or power supply to the blower.
- **Water intrusion into the fan or gas assembly** Leaks or condensation can short circuits or damage the fan, triggering air-system faults.
- **Faulty PCB or fan control circuit** If the control board cannot drive or sense the fan correctly, it may report an E109 even when the fan hardware is intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** at the service switches and shut-off valve, then let the unit cool for at least ten minutes.
2. **Remove and inspect the air inlet filter** on the front or side of the unit, wash it under warm water, and let it dry completely before reinstalling.
3. **Check the combustion fan** by rotating the blower wheel by hand to confirm it spins freely without grinding or binding.
4. **Inspect all exhaust and intake vent pipes** for blockages, ice, or damage, and clear any obstructions you find outside.
5. **Examine wiring connections** at the fan motor and on the main PCB for corrosion, loose pins, or visible burn marks, and reseat any suspect connectors.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then attempt a reset by pressing the reset button on the control panel or cycling power off and on after thirty seconds.
7. **Monitor the display** for one full heating cycle to confirm the code does not return, and listen for normal fan operation during ignition.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the code persists after cleaning the filter and confirming free fan rotation, or if you see water inside the combustion chamber or gas assembly. Professionals have the gas-pressure gauges, multimeters, and manufacturer scan tools needed to test fan speed, verify PCB outputs, and safely diagnose intermittent electrical faults. Any work involving gas piping, combustion seals, or control-board replacement should be handled by a qualified service provider to maintain warranty coverage and code compliance.
