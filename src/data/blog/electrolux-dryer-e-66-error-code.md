## Electrolux Dryer E66 Error Code — What It Means

E66 on an Electrolux dryer indicates a heating safety fault involving the thermal cutoff, thermal limiter, or high-limit circuit. The code is triggered when the control detects an open thermal safety device or a sensor circuit failure related to overheating protection. In most cases, this means a thermal fuse or thermal limiter has tripped open because the dryer overheated, or the control can no longer verify the safety circuit is intact.

The fault is a protective shutdown. It stops the heating element from operating when airflow is restricted or when a temperature limit has been exceeded. Some Electrolux IQ-Touch models report E66 as a heating sensor circuit failure detected by the main control, but the underlying cause is typically the same: a thermal safety component has opened or the circuit path has been interrupted.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Always test the thermal fuse and thermal limiter for continuity with a meter and inspect the entire vent system for blockages before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted exhaust airflow (~50%)** Lint buildup in the screen, blower housing, internal ducting, flexible vent line, or exterior termination causes the dryer to overheat and trip the thermal safety device.
- **Failed thermal fuse or thermal limiter (~30%)** The thermal cutoff has tripped open due to a previous overheating event and now remains open, blocking the heating circuit and triggering E66.
- **Failed high-limit thermostat (~10%)** The high-limit thermostat on the heater housing has opened and will not reset, interrupting the safety circuit.
- **Damaged wiring or connectors (~5%)** Heat damage, corrosion, or a loose spade terminal at the thermal safety devices or heater assembly creates an intermittent or permanent open in the safety circuit.
- **Control board sensing issue (~5%)** The main control board reports E66 because of a relay or signal-path fault even when the thermal safety hardware is intact, though this is uncommon.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and pull it away from the wall to access the rear panel and exhaust vent connection.
2. **Remove and clean the lint screen** thoroughly, then disconnect the flexible vent duct and inspect it for lint buildup, crushing, or kinks.
3. **Check the exterior vent hood** to confirm the flap opens freely and no bird nests, lint clumps, or debris are blocking the termination.
4. **Remove the rear access panel** (or top and front panel on some models) to reach the blower housing and heater assembly.
5. **Locate the thermal fuse and high-limit thermostat** mounted on or near the heater housing or blower, then disconnect one lead from each and test for continuity with a multimeter.
6. **Replace any open thermal safety device** with an exact replacement part for your model, and inspect all wiring and spade terminals for heat damage or corrosion.
7. **Reassemble the dryer, reconnect the vent duct** with a straight, short run if possible, and restore power. Run a timed heat cycle and verify E66 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt dryer wiring, cannot locate the thermal safety components behind panels and shrouds, or if the fault returns after you have replaced the thermal fuse and confirmed the vent system is clean and unrestricted. A professional can perform voltage and continuity tests across the entire heating circuit, check for control board relay failures, and verify airflow with measurement tools to rule out blower motor or housing damage that a visual inspection might miss.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
