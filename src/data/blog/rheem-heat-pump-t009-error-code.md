## Rheem Heat Pump T009 Error — What It Means

The T009 error code on Rheem heat pump systems generally indicates a problem with a temperature sensor or thermistor circuit. The exact sensor location and function can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's service panel for specifics. The system relies on thermistors to monitor refrigerant line temperatures, outdoor ambient temperature, or indoor coil temperature, and when a sensor reads out of range or loses connection, the control board flags the fault and may disable heating or cooling to protect the compressor.

Because Rheem uses different sensor configurations across product lines, the T009 code may point to an outdoor coil sensor, suction line sensor, or discharge line sensor depending on your model. The control board compares sensor resistance to expected values at a given temperature. When resistance falls outside that window, the code appears. Common triggers include a failed thermistor, corroded or loose wiring, damaged connectors, or water intrusion at the sensor plug.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board when a simple thermistor swap or connector cleaning would fix the problem. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter at ambient temperature and compare to the chart in your service manual before buying any board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The sensor itself drifts out of spec or breaks internally, sending an invalid resistance reading to the control board.
- **Corroded or loose connector (~25%)** Moisture or dirt enters the plug where the sensor attaches to the wiring harness, creating high resistance or an open circuit.
- **Damaged wiring (~15%)** The wire from the sensor to the board is cut, pinched, or shorted by rodents, installation damage, or vibration wear.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The board's sensor input circuit fails, misreading a good thermistor as out of range.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the disconnect switch or breaker serving the heat pump and verify with a non-contact tester.
2. **Locate the thermistor** referenced in your model's wiring diagram, usually clipped to a refrigerant line or mounted on the outdoor coil.
3. **Unplug the sensor connector** and inspect both the plug and receptacle for corrosion, water, or bent pins. Clean with electrical contact cleaner and a small brush if needed.
4. **Measure resistance** across the sensor terminals with a digital multimeter set to ohms, then compare the reading to the resistance-temperature table in your service manual or on the unit's wiring diagram.
5. **Replace the thermistor** if resistance is out of spec. Match the part number exactly, slide the new sensor into the clip or mounting bracket, and plug in the connector until it clicks.
6. **Inspect the wiring** from the sensor back to the control board for cuts, abrasion, or pinch points, and repair or replace damaged sections with matching gauge wire and crimp connectors.
7. **Restore power** and monitor the display. If the T009 code does not return within a few minutes and the system runs normally, the repair is successful. If the code reappears, verify wiring continuity and consider control board testing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring, if the wiring diagram is unclear, or if you have replaced the thermistor and cleaned all connections but the T009 code persists. A pro can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board's sensor input circuit, check refrigerant pressures to rule out system faults that mimic sensor errors, and access factory service bulletins for model-specific quirks. Also call if the sensor is mounted inside a sealed compartment that requires recovering refrigerant or if you lack a multimeter and cannot verify sensor resistance yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
