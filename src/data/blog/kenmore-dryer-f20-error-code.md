## Kenmore F20 Error Code — What It Means

The Kenmore F20 error code signals a water fill problem. The washer's flow sensor detects low water volume entering the tub, or the tub pressure sensor (pressure switch) reports that no water is present despite the fill cycle running. In plain terms, the machine cannot reach the required water level and flags the fault to prevent operating dry or overfilling.

This code can trigger from both external and internal causes. External issues include shut or partially open supply valves, kinked hoses, or frozen lines in winter. Internal causes range from clogged inlet screens to a failed flow meter, a leaking pressure switch hose, or faulty inlet valves. The machine is designed to halt the cycle when it detects insufficient flow, protecting the motor and pump from damage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the inlet valve first, but clogged inlet screens or a tiny hole in the pressure switch hose are far more common. Clean the screens and inspect the 1/4-inch pressure hose before buying a new valve.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens (~40%)** Lint, sand, or hard water sediment blocks the fine mesh screens inside the hose connectors at the back of the washer, reducing flow below the sensor threshold.
- **External water supply restrictions (~25%)** Valves not fully open, kinked supply hoses, or frozen water lines (common in winter) prevent adequate flow into the machine.
- **Failed flow meter (flow sensor) (~15%)** The internal sensor that measures water volume can fail electrically, become loose, or break internally, sending false low-flow signals to the control board.
- **Leaking or blocked pressure switch hose (~10%)** A tiny hole in the 1/4-inch plastic hose or lint clogging its connection at the bottom of the tub prevents the pressure switch from sensing water level.
- **Faulty inlet valves (~7%)** The hot or cold inlet solenoid valve fails to open electronically when commanded by the control board, blocking water entry.
- **Drain obstruction (~3%)** Small items or lint blocking the drain pump or filter can confuse the fill and drain cycle logic, causing premature fault detection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off both water supply valves** and unplug the washer from the outlet.
2. **Disconnect the inlet hoses** from the back of the washer. Inspect the hoses for kinks or damage.
3. **Remove and clean the inlet screens** visible inside the threaded hose connectors on the washer. Use a small brush or rinse under running water. If a secondary fine mesh screen sits behind the first, clean that too.
4. **Reconnect the hoses** and turn the valves fully open. Run a short fill test cycle to see if the F20 clears.
5. **Access the rear panel** (remove screws) to locate the flow meter, typically mounted between the inlet valves and the tub inlet. Check for loose wires or rattling. Replace the flow meter if suspect.
6. **Locate the 1/4-inch pressure switch hose** running from the bottom of the tub to the pressure switch (often near the control board). Remove it and inspect for tiny holes or cracks. Replace with new 1/4-inch tubing if damaged.
7. **Clean the hose connection port** at the bottom of the tub to remove any lint buildup that could block pressure sensing.
8. **Test the pressure switch** by blowing into it. Listen for two distinct clicks. If absent, replace the switch.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have completed the free checks (opened valves, cleared hoses, cleaned screens) and replaced the flow meter or pressure switch hose without resolving the F20. A pro can test the inlet valves with a multimeter, inspect the pressure switch with precise continuity checks, and diagnose control board faults that may falsely trigger the error. Also call if you are uncomfortable accessing the rear panel, working with electrical connectors, or handling water supply lines under pressure. Most F20 cases are simple blockages, but persistent codes after DIY repairs often point to wiring faults or a failing control board that require professional tools and experience.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-60 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
