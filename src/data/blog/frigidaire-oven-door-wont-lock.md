## Frigidaire Oven Door Won't Lock — What's Happening

When your Frigidaire oven door won't lock, the control has detected a problem with the door lock system or the mechanical latch isn't moving as expected. Frigidaire classifies door lock faults under codes F90 through F94, and the oven may block normal cooking functions until the lock state is resolved for safety. The lock feature is designed to physically secure the door and control panel when activated, then release when canceled.

Frigidaire's instructions confirm the lock is normally activated or canceled by holding the OFF button for 6 seconds, and the door lock motor should begin moving and complete the cycle in about 15 seconds. If the door stays unlocked, won't release after self-clean, or triggers a fault code, the issue is typically mechanical wear in the lock assembly, a wiring problem between the lock and control board, or a temporary software glitch that a power reset can clear.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Stuck or worn door lock assembly** The lock motor, latch, or linkage can fail mechanically from repeated use or heat exposure, preventing the latch from moving into the locked position.
- **Faulty wiring or connectors** Loose, corroded, or damaged harness connections between the lock assembly and the control board can stop the lock signal or feedback from reaching the control.
- **Temporary software or logic glitch** The control may falsely report a lock fault that clears with a 30-second power reset, which Frigidaire recommends as the first step.
- **Residual heat lock after self-clean** The door may remain locked until the oven cools enough to release, so a door that won't unlock can be mistaken for one that won't lock at all.
- **Failed latch feedback switch** The switch that tells the control board the door is latched can fail, causing the control to think the lock never engaged.
- **Control board failure** The electronic control may fail to command the lock motor correctly or misread the lock state, triggering a fault code and blocking operation.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the symptom by confirming whether the door is failing to lock, failing to unlock, or showing an F90 through F94 code when you attempt to lock or unlock.
2. Reset power by unplugging the range for 30 seconds or switching the breaker off and back on to clear any transient faults in the control.
3. Test the lock command by holding the OFF button for 6 seconds and watching whether the lock motor begins moving and completes the cycle in about 15 seconds.
4. Inspect the lock assembly with power disconnected by checking the latch and linkage for broken parts, binding, debris, or heat-related distortion that would prevent movement.
5. Inspect the wiring harness at both the lock assembly and the control board for loose connectors, corrosion, damaged terminals, or burnt spots.
6. Test the lock motor circuit if accessible by verifying whether the motor runs when commanded and whether the latch feedback switch changes state correctly as the latch moves.
7. Replace the failed component once you have isolated the defective lock assembly, motor, switch, harness connector, or control board rather than bypassing the safety system.
8. Call for service if the reset does not clear the fault and you cannot isolate a mechanical or wiring failure, as Frigidaire directs unresolved door lock issues to professional repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the power reset and lock-command test do not restore normal operation, if you see an F90 through F94 code that won't clear, or if you are uncomfortable working with the wiring and control board behind the back panel. A qualified appliance technician has the service manual, electrical test equipment, and genuine Frigidaire parts to diagnose feedback-switch continuity, motor voltage, and control-board logic without bypassing the door lock safety system. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
