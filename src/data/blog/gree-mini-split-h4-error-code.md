## Gree Mini Split H4 Error Code — What It Means

H4 is an overload protection code, not a hard component failure. Gree's maintenance documentation defines H4 as a protection state triggered when the outdoor unit detects that the tube temperature has risen above the protective threshold. This is part of the H-series operational and protection codes (distinct from H5, which is IPM protection). The system enters a protective shutdown to prevent damage when abnormal temperature conditions are detected at the evaporator or condenser circuit. In most cases, H4 clears after the unit cools down and the underlying cause is corrected.

Because Gree explicitly ties this code to temperature protection, the root cause is almost always related to restricted heat transfer or abnormal refrigerant-system temperature rise. Field experience shows that dirty or blocked outdoor coils are the most frequent trigger, though fan problems, refrigerant issues, sensor faults, and control board defects can also cause H4. The code is a signal that the system is protecting itself, so the repair focus is on finding and removing the condition that is driving the temperature spike.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** Dirt, debris, bent fins, or recirculation block airflow and drive coil temperature above the protection threshold, the most common real-world trigger for H4.
- **Outdoor fan failure or reduced airflow** A failing condenser fan, obstructed discharge, or low fan speed prevents adequate heat rejection and elevates tube temperature.
- **Faulty tube temperature sensor or wiring** An open, shorted, or intermittent sensor connection sends false high-temperature signals to the control board and triggers protection unnecessarily.
- **Refrigerant charge or metering problems** Undercharge, overcharge, restriction, or a stuck metering device can produce abnormal coil temperatures that exceed the protection setpoint.
- **Outdoor control board fault** If all physical and sensor checks are normal but H4 recurs, the outdoor main board may be interpreting the protection input incorrectly or have a control-circuit defect.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the code and operating condition.** Note whether the unit is in a protection state and whether H4 clears after shutdown and cool-down, since this is a protection condition rather than a permanent component failure.
2. **Inspect the outdoor coil and airflow path.** Check the condenser coil for dirt, blockage, bent fins, debris, or recirculation, and verify that the outdoor fan is running at normal speed and moving air freely.
3. **Clean the outdoor coil thoroughly.** Use coil cleaner and a soft brush or low-pressure water to remove all dirt and obstructions from the condenser, then straighten any bent fins and clear the discharge path.
4. **Check the tube temperature sensor and wiring.** Inspect the evaporator and condenser tube temperature sensor connectors and harness for damage, corrosion, or loose contact, and test sensor resistance if accessible.
5. **Verify refrigerant system operation.** If airflow and sensors are normal, check refrigerant charge, operating pressures, and system performance for signs of undercharge, overcharge, restriction, or metering problems using standard HVAC diagnostics.
6. **Power-cycle the unit and re-test.** After correcting the suspected cause, turn off power for at least one minute, restart the system, and verify that H4 does not return during a full cooling cycle.
7. **Replace the failed component only after confirmation.** If the sensor circuit is bad, replace the tube temperature thermistor; if airflow, sensors, and refrigerant are correct but H4 persists, inspect or replace the outdoor main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if H4 returns after you have cleaned the outdoor coil and verified fan operation, if you are not comfortable working with refrigerant or electrical components, or if you suspect a sensor or control board fault. Refrigerant diagnostics require EPA certification and pressure-testing equipment. Sensor and board replacement involve live voltage and proper connector handling. A qualified tech can perform a full system check, verify tube temperatures with calibrated instruments, test sensor circuits, recover and recharge refrigerant if needed, and replace control boards safely. If the unit is under warranty, professional documentation of the repair may be required to preserve coverage.
