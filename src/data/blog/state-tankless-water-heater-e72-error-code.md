## State E72 Error Code — What It Means

The E72 code on State tankless water heaters is not explicitly defined in widely available manufacturer documentation, but based on error-code patterns in State and A. O. Smith platform units, it most likely indicates a sensor or internal fault condition. This type of code usually appears when the control board detects an issue with a temperature sensor, flame-sensing circuit, or other internal monitoring component, rather than a simple flow or ignition problem.

Because State tankless units share design architecture with A. O. Smith models, the fault may involve flame detection, gas valve control, or temperature-sensor circuits. Before ordering parts, verify the exact code definition in your specific model's service manual or rating-plate documentation, since tankless error codes vary widely by platform and firmware version.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens or venting restriction** Lint, debris, or improper vent termination can starve the burner of combustion air, triggering sensor faults or flame-detection errors.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor** Contamination on the flame rod prevents reliable flame signal, causing the control board to register a fault and lock out.
- **Low gas pressure or supply restriction** Insufficient gas flow from a partially closed valve, undersized line, or low inlet pressure can prevent stable ignition and trigger internal fault codes.
- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger or sensors** Hard-water mineral deposits coat temperature probes and heat-exchanger surfaces, causing inaccurate readings or overheating protection.
- **Failed internal sensor or wiring harness** A temperature sensor, flame-rod circuit, or control-board connection can fail due to heat stress, corrosion, or vibration, generating persistent fault codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your model number** on the unit's rating plate and download the service manual to confirm the exact definition of E72 for your specific State tankless model before proceeding.
2. **Inspect the air intake and exhaust** venting for blockages, debris, lint accumulation, or improper termination, and clear any obstructions you find.
3. **Check the inlet water filter** and isolation valves for scale, sediment, or restriction that could reduce flow below the minimum activation threshold.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the gas valve is fully open, the gas line is properly sized, and inlet pressure meets the manufacturer's specification in your manual.
5. **Examine the flame sensor or flame rod** for soot, corrosion, or loose wiring, and clean only if your service manual permits sensor cleaning without replacement.
6. **Power-cycle the unit** by turning off the gas valve and electrical disconnect, waiting two minutes, then restoring power to clear the fault code.
7. **Test sensor circuits** with a multimeter for continuity and resistance if the code returns, and inspect all harness connectors for corrosion, moisture, or poor pin fit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate model-specific documentation for the E72 code, if the fault returns after cleaning air intakes and checking gas supply, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances or testing low-voltage sensor circuits. Persistent sensor faults often require multimeter diagnostics, control-board communication, or gas-pressure measurement that are beyond typical homeowner tools. A qualified technician can verify gas inlet pressure, test flame-detection circuits, and replace internal sensors or control boards safely and in compliance with local codes.
