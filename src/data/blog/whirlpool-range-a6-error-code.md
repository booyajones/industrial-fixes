## Whirlpool Range A6 Error Code — What It Means

A6 does not appear in Whirlpool's published range error-code list. If your display shows A6, double-check the exact format. The closest Whirlpool-documented code for ranges is F7 E6, which the manufacturer defines as a cooling fan or associated wiring problem. The cooling fan runs during and after oven operation to prevent overheating of the control electronics and other components.

If your display actually reads F7 E6 or if your model documentation confirms A6 maps to a fan issue, the control has detected that the cooling fan is not operating correctly. This can mean the fan motor has failed, a connector or wire in the fan circuit is loose or damaged, or the control board is not properly driving or sensing the fan circuit. Always consult your specific model's wiring diagram and owner's manual to confirm the meaning of any code before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first when the real fault is a bad fan motor or a single loose connector at the fan. Always test the fan motor for continuity and verify wiring connections before ordering a new control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed cooling fan motor or assembly (~50%)** The motor windings burn out or the bearings seize, preventing the fan from spinning and triggering the code.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors (~25%)** Heat, vibration, or age can cause wire terminals or connectors in the fan circuit to lose contact or corrode.
- **Faulty control board (~15%)** The control may fail to send power to the fan or to correctly read the fan-status signal, even when the fan and wiring are good.
- **Obstructed or jammed fan blade (~10%)** Debris, a broken mounting clip, or a warped blade can prevent the fan from turning freely and trigger a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power at the circuit breaker** for a full minute, then restore power and observe whether the code returns immediately or during the next oven cycle.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** of the range (or the lower kick panel, depending on model) to expose the cooling fan assembly, usually mounted near the control board or oven cavity.
3. **Inspect all wire connectors** on the fan motor and trace the wiring back to the control board, looking for loose terminals, melted insulation, or corrosion.
4. **Test the fan motor for continuity** using a multimeter across the motor terminals after disconnecting the harness. A good motor will show a closed circuit (typically a few ohms to a few hundred ohms, consult your model's table).
5. **Manually spin the fan blade** by hand with power off to confirm it turns freely without binding or scraping.
6. **Reconnect power temporarily** and initiate a bake cycle to verify whether the fan receives voltage from the control. If voltage is present but the fan does not run, replace the fan assembly.
7. **Replace the control board** only if the fan motor tests good, wiring is intact, and you have confirmed the control is not sending the correct output signal to the fan circuit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if the fan and wiring both test good but the code persists, or if your range uses a complex convection or dual-fan system. A technician has the meters and experience to safely trace control signals, verify relay operation on the board, and confirm that a new control will solve the problem before you invest in the part. Also call a pro if your model documentation does not list A6 or F7 E6 and you need help identifying the exact meaning of the code displayed on your specific range.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–90 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
