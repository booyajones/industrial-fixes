## Maytag Washer F5 E3 Error Code — What It Means

F5 E3 on a Maytag front-load washer is a door lock or latch error. The machine is not getting the lock or unlock status it expects from the door lock assembly, so it stops the cycle for safety. Maytag groups F5E2 and F5E3 as door lock and latch errors in front-load washers.

In practice, the control board expects a valid signal that the door is locked or unlocked, and it is not receiving that signal. On some Maytag and Whirlpool platforms, F5E3 can mean the washer cannot lock the door or cannot unlock it, depending on the model generation. Either way, the door lock system is not reporting correctly, and the washer will not run until the fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first. Check and test the door lock assembly and inspect the wiring connector and strike alignment before spending on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty door lock assembly (~50%)** Worn internal contacts, broken plastic parts, failed solenoid, or a lock that does not physically engage or release correctly.
- **Door strike or latch misalignment (~20%)** Damaged strike, misaligned latch, cracked plastic, or the door not closing fully and seating into the lock.
- **Obstruction or residue in the lock area (~15%)** Clothing, debris, detergent buildup, or foreign material preventing the lock mechanism from moving freely.
- **Loose or corroded wiring and connectors (~10%)** Harness connections at the lock that are loose, corroded, heat-damaged, or have broken conductors.
- **Control board or UI logic issue (~5%)** Less common, but possible if the lock hardware and wiring test good and the fault persists.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** for 5 to 10 minutes to reset the control board, then restore power and check if the code clears.
2. **Inspect the door and latch area** for anything blocking full closure, including clothing, debris, or detergent residue on the strike or seal.
3. **Check strike alignment and condition** by looking for cracked plastic, a bent or damaged strike, or misalignment that prevents the latch from seating correctly.
4. **Remove the top or front panel** (consult your model's service manual) to access the door lock assembly and wiring harness at the lock.
5. **Inspect the lock connector and wiring** for loose pins, corrosion, heat damage, or broken wires, and clean or repair as needed.
6. **Run the service-mode door lock test** by entering diagnostic mode and commanding the lock to actuate; listen for a distinct click and verify the lock engages and releases.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if it does not click, does not hold, or does not report state correctly; reassemble the washer, clear the fault, and run a full test cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable removing the washer's access panels or if you have replaced the door lock assembly and the F5 E3 code persists. A pro can use service mode diagnostics to isolate wiring faults, test the control board, and verify lock signal voltage. Also call if the lock area shows signs of water damage, burn marks on connectors, or if the door strike mounting is cracked and requires frame repair. If the washer is still under warranty, contact Maytag service to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–$120 for a door lock assembly, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–$280 for service call and lock replacement.
