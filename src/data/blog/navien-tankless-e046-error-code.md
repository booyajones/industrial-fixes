## Navien E046 Error Code — What It Means

Navien error code E046 has two conflicting definitions across different models and service guides. Some units define it as Condensate Overflow, meaning the condensate drain line is clogged or the trap is full and cannot drain properly. Other units define it as Abnormality in Limit Control, meaning the high-limit safety sensor has failed or detected an overheating condition and shut down the unit to prevent damage. You must verify which definition applies to your specific model by checking the unit display interpretation, consulting your service manual, or calling Navien Technical Support at 800-519-8794 with your serial number before attempting any repair.

If the code means limit control failure, the unit is detecting a safety hazard where water temperature could rise dangerously high. If it means condensate overflow, water is not draining from the heat exchanger and the unit shuts down to prevent flooding or corrosion. Do not assume one definition fits all Navien tankless heaters.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace the high-limit sensor without verifying which E046 definition applies to the model or checking for scale buildup in the heat exchanger. Always confirm the fault definition with Navien support and test the sensor wiring and resistance before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged condensate drain line (~30%)** Debris, mold, or mineral deposits block the drain hose and prevent water from exiting the unit (applies only if E046 means condensate overflow on your model).
- **Failed high-limit sensor (~25%)** The sensor has an open or shorted circuit and falsely signals an overheating condition (applies only if E046 means limit control abnormality on your model).
- **Scale buildup in heat exchanger (~20%)** Hard water deposits restrict flow and cause actual overheating, triggering the high-limit sensor to trip.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~15%)** Poor connection at the sensor terminal or control board interrupts the signal and registers as a fault.
- **Improper drain line installation (~10%)** The condensate drain is not sloped correctly or is kinked, causing water to back up into the unit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Contact Navien Technical Support** at 800-519-8794 with your unit serial number and request the exact definition of E046 for your model. Do not proceed with repairs until you know whether the code means condensate overflow or limit control failure.
2. **Inspect the condensate drain line** from the unit to the exterior termination. Look for kinks, ice blockages, or visible debris at the outlet. Disconnect the line and flush it with water or a mild vinegar solution to clear any mold or mineral deposits.
3. **Check the condensate trap** inside the unit. Remove and clean the trap according to your service manual. make sure it reinstalls properly and drains freely.
4. **Inspect all wiring** at the high-limit sensor and control board. Look for loose, corroded, or disconnected terminals. Reconnect or clean any suspect connections.
5. **Test the high-limit sensor resistance** with a multimeter if you have one. Compare the reading to the specification in your service manual. An open circuit or very low resistance indicates sensor failure.
6. **Check for heat exchanger scale** if the fault is related to limit control. If you have hard water or the unit has not been flushed in over a year, schedule a descaling flush using a vinegar pump kit or call a technician.
7. **Replace the failed component** once diagnosed. If the sensor is faulty, order the correct replacement from Navien or an authorized parts supplier. If the drain line cannot be cleared, replace the section that is blocked. Reset the unit by turning it off for 30 minutes and restarting.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot confirm the exact meaning of E046 for your model, if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or testing sensors with a multimeter, or if the unit continues to display the error after clearing the drain line and checking wiring. A pro should also handle heat exchanger descaling if you have never performed the procedure, as improper flushing can damage internal components. If the unit shows signs of actual overheating such as steam or unusually hot water, shut off the unit immediately and call for service. Gas-fired appliances require professional diagnosis when safety sensors are involved.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
