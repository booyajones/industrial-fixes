## Bosch Oven E08 Error Code — What It Means

The E08 error code on a Bosch oven typically indicates a communication or sensor fault within the control system. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for your specific oven. In general, E08 points to a problem with one of the temperature sensors, a wiring issue between the sensor and the control board, or less commonly a fault in the main control board itself.

Because Bosch uses different control platforms across their oven line, the E08 code may relate to the oven cavity sensor, the control-panel communication loop, or another monitored circuit. The oven will usually stop heating or refuse to start when this code is active. Most E08 faults trace back to a failed sensor or a loose connector rather than the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when E08 appears, but a simple resistance test of the oven sensor with a multimeter will reveal whether the sensor is open or out of specification, saving hundreds of dollars.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~55%)** The resistance thermistor that monitors cavity temperature drifts out of range, reads open, or shorts, triggering the E08 fault.
- **Loose or corroded wire connector (~25%)** Vibration or heat causes the plug at the sensor or control board to back out or oxidize, breaking the signal path.
- **Damaged sensor wire (~10%)** The sensor lead insulation melts or breaks near the back of the oven cavity, creating an intermittent short or open circuit.
- **Faulty control board (~8%)** A solder joint or input circuit on the main board fails, preventing it from reading the sensor correctly.
- **Software glitch (~2%)** A transient power surge or brownout corrupts the control firmware, storing a false error that clears with a full power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven from the wall to make sure safe work.
2. **Remove the back access panel** (if a slide-in or wall oven) or the lower kick panel (if a range) to expose the wiring and control board.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor**, a metal probe protruding into the rear or side wall of the oven cavity with two wires running to a small plug.
4. **Unplug the sensor connector** at the back of the oven and use a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) to measure across the two sensor terminals.
5. **Compare the reading** to the specification in your model's service manual or wiring diagram (most Bosch sensors read 1,000-1,200 ohms at room temperature). An open circuit (infinite resistance) or very low resistance indicates a failed sensor.
6. **Inspect all connectors and wiring** for bent pins, corrosion, or melted insulation. Clean contacts with electronic contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
7. **Replace the oven sensor** if it is out of specification. Remove the mounting screw inside the oven cavity, pull the old sensor out, thread the new sensor into place, and reconnect the plug.
8. **Restore power** and run a test bake cycle. If E08 persists after sensor replacement and wiring checks, the control board is the remaining suspect and should be replaced or professionally diagnosed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt wiring or removing the oven from its cabinet to access the back panel. Also seek help if you have replaced the sensor and confirmed all wiring is intact but the E08 error persists, since control-board diagnosis often requires specialized test equipment and knowledge of the specific control platform. A qualified appliance technician can quickly measure signal voltages, verify board inputs, and source the correct replacement board for your model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
