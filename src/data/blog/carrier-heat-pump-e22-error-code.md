## Carrier E22 Error Code — What It Means

The E22 code on most Carrier heat pump and inverter systems indicates a high/low pressure sensor fault on the outdoor unit. The control board is receiving an invalid or out-of-range signal from the pressure-sensing circuit rather than a normal refrigerant operating condition. This points to a problem with the pressure sensor itself, its wiring, or the outdoor control board that reads the signal. The exact definition can vary by model, so always confirm against your unit's service manual before starting repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Defective pressure sensor** The high/low pressure sensor has drifted out of range or failed internally, sending an invalid signal to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring** Damaged connectors, corroded terminals, or loose harness connections between the sensor and the outdoor board interrupt the sensor signal.
- **Outdoor control board fault** The inverter or outdoor PCB misreads a good sensor signal due to internal component failure or a damaged input circuit.
- **Refrigerant system abnormality** A refrigerant leak, restriction, improper charge, or compressor problem drives pressure outside expected limits and triggers the sensor fault.
- **Power transient or control glitch** A temporary electrical disturbance or reset event causes the control to log a false fault code that may clear with a power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power cycle the system.** Turn off the thermostat, disconnect power at the outdoor disconnect or breaker, wait five minutes, then restore power and observe whether the E22 code returns.
2. **Inspect the pressure sensor and harness.** Locate the high/low pressure sensor on the outdoor unit's refrigerant line and check the connector, terminals, and wiring for looseness, corrosion, burned pins, or visible damage.
3. **Test the sensor circuit electrically.** Using your unit's service manual, verify supply voltage to the sensor, check signal output, and confirm continuity from sensor to control board with a multimeter.
4. **Compare sensor readings to service data.** If the sensor is readable, check whether its pressure or voltage output is plausibly tracking system conditions and falls within the manufacturer's range for your model.
5. **Inspect the refrigerant system.** Look for blocked outdoor coil fins, restricted airflow, kinked tubing, refrigerant leaks (oil stains or hissing), or compressor problems that could cause abnormal pressure.
6. **Replace the faulty component.** If the sensor tests out of range, replace it. If wiring and sensor are good, suspect the outdoor control board and verify according to Carrier service procedure before replacement.
7. **Run a full operational test.** After repair, cycle the system through heating and cooling modes and confirm the E22 code clears and pressure behavior remains stable across a complete cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical circuits, refrigerant systems, or multimeter diagnostics. Pressure sensor replacement and especially refrigerant system work (leak repair, charge adjustment, or compressor replacement) require EPA certification, specialized tools, and recovery equipment. If the fault returns after a power cycle and you do not have your model's service manual with sensor specifications, a professional can confirm the exact E22 definition for your unit, test the sensor circuit safely, and verify whether a refrigerant-side issue is causing the code.
