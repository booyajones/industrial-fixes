## LG Mini Split CH04 Error Code — What It Means

The CH04 error code on your LG mini split indicates the indoor unit has detected a condensate drainage problem. Water created during cooling is not draining properly. On some LG models this fault may display as FL instead of CH04. The unit detects either a blocked drain path or a high water level in the drain pan, often through a float switch or sensor. LG notes that in very high humidity or rainy conditions, temporary excessive condensate production can trigger this code even when the system continues to cool normally.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked drain line or pan** Algae, dust, or debris clogs the drain pan outlet, hose, or trap so condensate backs up and triggers the sensor.
- **Kinked or restricted drain hose** The flexible drain tubing is pinched, looped too high, or routed incorrectly and water cannot flow out by gravity.
- **Failed or stuck condensate pump** On units equipped with a pump, the pump motor has failed or the float switch inside the pump reservoir is stuck in the high position.
- **Incorrect float-switch jumper configuration** Some LG fan coils require a specific jumper or blue plug setting when no drain pump is used, and a mismatch can cause false CH04 faults.
- **High humidity or rainy weather** Temporary excessive condensate production during very humid conditions can raise the pan level enough to trip the sensor even when drainage is working.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power reset the system** by unplugging the indoor unit or switching off the circuit breaker, waiting a full 5 minutes, then restoring power to see if the code clears.
2. **Inspect the drain pan** inside the indoor unit for standing water, dirt, or slime buildup around the outlet port, and wipe or vacuum it clean if water is present.
3. **Check the drain hose routing** from the indoor unit to the termination point outside, looking for kinks, loops higher than the pan, or sagging sections that trap water.
4. **Flush the drain line** by pouring a cup of warm water mixed with a small amount of dish soap or vinegar into the pan to verify free flow at the discharge point.
5. **Test the condensate pump** (if your model has one) by pouring water into the pump reservoir until the float lifts and listening for the motor to run and move water out.
6. **Verify jumper or float-switch configuration** on fan-coil units without a pump by consulting your installation manual for the correct blue plug or jumper position on the control board.
7. **Monitor the system** for 24 hours after clearing the blockage and resetting power to confirm the CH04 code does not return during normal cooling operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the CH04 code returns immediately after power reset and drain cleaning, if you cannot locate or access the drain pan or pump, or if the float switch and jumper configuration are unclear for your specific model. Persistent faults may indicate a failed float switch, broken pump motor, or internal wiring issue that requires refrigerant-system access and electrical diagnostics beyond homeowner tools. Technicians carry model-specific service manuals with jumper tables and can pressure-test the drain system to find hidden blockages.
