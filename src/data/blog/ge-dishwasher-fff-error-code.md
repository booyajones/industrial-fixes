## GE Dishwasher FFF Error Code — What It Means

The FFF code does not appear in GE's published dishwasher fault code documentation. GE's own support materials confirm that fault codes are model-dependent and used for technician diagnosis, but FFF is not listed among standard GE dishwasher codes such as PF, C1, C3, C4, C6, C8, H2O, or leak-related faults. This suggests FFF is either a misread display, a user-reported error, or a code specific to a very limited model subset not covered in mainstream GE service literature.

If you are seeing FFF on your GE dishwasher display, first verify the exact characters shown and compare them against the fault code list in your model's service manual or wiring diagram. The closest documented fault pattern in GE-style repair literature is FTD, which indicates a failure to drain involving the drain pump, hose, filter, or pressure sensor. If your display actually reads FTD or another documented code, follow the troubleshooting steps for that specific fault. Otherwise, contact GE support or a qualified technician to confirm the code's meaning for your exact model.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the drain pump or control board without first clearing the sump filter and drain hose. Start by removing standing water, cleaning the filter assembly, and checking for kinks or clogs in the drain path before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Misread or non-standard code (~40%)** FFF is not a documented GE dishwasher fault code in manufacturer materials, so it may be a display misread, a model-specific code, or an entry error.
- **Clogged sump filter or drain hose (~25%)** Food debris, grease, or foreign objects can block the filter basket or kink the drain hose, preventing water from leaving the tub and triggering drain-related faults on similar GE models.
- **Jammed or failed drain pump (~20%)** Debris can lodge in the pump impeller or the motor can lose continuity, stopping the pump from discharging water and causing drain fault patterns.
- **Blocked garbage disposer connection or air gap (~10%)** A knockout plug left in the disposer inlet, a clogged air gap, or kinked connection hose can back up water into the dishwasher and mimic drain faults.
- **Faulty pressure sensor or clogged sensing passage (~5%)** A defective pressure switch or debris in the sensing tube can prevent the control from detecting proper drain conditions, triggering error codes on some GE dishwashers.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** and shut off the water supply valve under the sink before starting any inspection or repair work.
2. **Verify the exact code displayed** by checking the control panel in good lighting and comparing it to the fault code list in your model's service manual or on the GE support website.
3. **Remove the lower dish rack** and unscrew or lift out the sump filter assembly in the bottom of the tub. Rinse the filter under running water and clear any debris from the sump cavity.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** by pulling the dishwasher forward enough to access the hose connection at the rear. Check for kinks, blockages, or improper routing (the hose should loop up higher than the drain connection to prevent backflow).
5. **Check the garbage disposer or air gap** if present. Remove the knockout plug from a new disposer inlet if it was not already removed during installation, and flush the air gap with water to clear any clogs.
6. **Access the drain pump** by removing the lower front panel or kickplate and the pump cover if applicable. Manually spin the impeller to confirm it is not jammed by debris, and remove any foreign objects.
7. **Test the drain pump electrically** by disconnecting the wire harness and using a multimeter to check continuity across the pump motor terminals. If there is no continuity or the pump does not run when power is restored, replace the pump assembly.
8. **Restore power at the breaker** and run a rinse or drain cycle to confirm the repair. If the code persists or if FFF cannot be matched to any published GE fault, contact GE support or a qualified appliance technician for model-specific diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the exact code FFF cannot be found in any GE documentation for your model, if the control board displays random or scrambled characters suggesting a board fault, or if you have cleared all drain obstructions and tested the pump but the error persists. A technician can access model-specific service literature, run advanced diagnostics with a tech sheet or service mode, and determine whether the control board or wiring harness is at fault. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with 120-volt wiring, if the dishwasher is still under warranty, or if you do not have a multimeter and basic hand tools.
