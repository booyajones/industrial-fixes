## What this code means
The F9E2 error code on Kenmore dishwashers indicates a diverter drive fault. The control board has detected that the diverter assembly is either blocked or not indexing correctly through its spray positions during the wash cycle. The diverter is the component that routes water to different spray arms at different times, and when it cannot move properly the control board throws this fault.

On Kenmore-branded Whirlpool-family platforms the primary cause is a failed diverter relay on the main electronic control board itself, which prevents the control from properly driving the diverter motor. A mechanical obstruction in the diverter assembly is the second possibility. Check your model's tech sheet to confirm the exact meaning, because some Whirlpool-family platforms assign F9E2 to drain-path issues instead of diverter faults.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some homeowners replace the diverter motor or wash pump when the real fault is the onboard diverter relay in the control board. Verify the diverter is mechanically free and check all wiring harness connections before ordering a new control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed main control board / diverter relay (~65%)** The onboard diverter relay in the electronic control unit fails and cannot send power to the diverter motor, so the control detects no indexing movement and sets F9E2.
- **Mechanical blockage in the diverter assembly (~20%)** Food debris, broken plastic, or foreign objects jam the diverter disk or motor shaft and prevent it from rotating into the next spray position.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness connections (~10%)** Connectors at the control board or diverter motor develop corrosion or vibrate loose, interrupting the drive signal and causing the control to fault.
- **Failed diverter motor (~5%)** The diverter motor itself burns out or seizes, though the Kenmore result identifies the control-board relay as more common than motor failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dishwasher to make sure safe inspection and repair.
2. **Remove the lower dish rack and spray arm** to access the diverter assembly in the floor of the tub.
3. **Inspect the diverter disk and surrounding area** for food debris, broken plastic pieces, or foreign objects that could block rotation, and clear any obstruction you find.
4. **Check all wiring harness connectors** at the main control board (behind the door panel) and at the diverter motor for looseness, corrosion, or heat damage, and reseat or clean any suspect terminals.
5. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to see if the fault clears. If F9E2 returns immediately the control board or diverter motor is likely defective.
6. **Replace the main electronic control board** if the diverter is mechanically free and all connectors are good, because the onboard diverter relay is the primary identified cause.
7. **Verify repair** by running a full normal wash cycle and checking that the diverter indexes correctly through all spray positions without faulting.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 120 VAC wiring behind the door panel, if the diverter assembly appears physically damaged and you cannot remove it, or if you have replaced the control board and the F9E2 fault persists. A pro can cross-reference your exact model's tech sheet to confirm whether F9E2 maps to diverter drive or to a drain-path fault (some Whirlpool-family platforms differ), measure relay continuity on the old board, and test the diverter motor under load to pinpoint the failed component without trial-and-error part swaps.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $120-250 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
