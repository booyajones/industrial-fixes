## Frigidaire Range F5 Error Code — What It Means

F5 on a Frigidaire range signals an electronic oven control (EOC) fault, not a temperature sensor issue. The control board's internal watchdog or microprocessor has detected a malfunction in its own circuitry. Frigidaire's service documentation points to a failed oven control board as the root cause, and the standard remedy is a power reset followed by board replacement if the fault persists.

[Jump to Fix](#fix)

## Common Causes

- **Defective electronic oven control board** The most common cause is internal failure of the EOC, clock, or main control board itself.
- **Power interruption or transient fault** A momentary control glitch or brownout can trigger F5, and a reset may clear it without further repair.
- **Loose or corroded board connectors** Poor contact at the EOC harness terminals can mimic a board failure and cause intermittent F5 codes.
- **Burnt or damaged wiring at the control** Heat damage or rodent-chewed wires feeding the EOC will interrupt normal communication and set the fault.
- **Watchdog or relay-control fault inside the board** The control's internal self-check circuit may detect a stuck relay or microprocessor error that is not user-serviceable.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Kill power at the circuit breaker for the range and wait one to ten minutes to allow the control board to fully discharge and reset.
2. Restore power and check whether the F5 code has cleared and the oven functions normally.
3. If F5 returns immediately or within a few cycles, unplug or disconnect power again and remove the rear access panel or top console to expose the electronic oven control board.
4. Inspect all wire harness connectors at the EOC for loose pins, heat discoloration, corrosion, or pushed-back terminals and reseat any suspect connections.
5. Examine the control board itself for burnt traces, swollen capacitors, or visible component damage that confirms internal failure.
6. If wiring is intact and the fault persists, order and install a replacement electronic oven control board using your model number to match the exact part.
7. Reconnect all harnesses to the new board, restore power, and run a test bake cycle to verify that F5 does not reappear and all oven functions respond correctly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt appliances or removing control panels. A pro can verify that the fault is truly in the EOC rather than a downstream relay or wiring issue, and many have the board in stock for same-day replacement. If your range is still under warranty or if you see widespread heat damage or corrosion inside the console, professional diagnosis will save you time and prevent incorrect part orders.
