## Frigidaire Range F5 Error Code — What It Means

On many Frigidaire electric ranges and wall ovens, F5 is an electronic control failure code. It most commonly points to a fault in the electronic oven control (EOC), also called the clock or control board, rather than the oven temperature sensor. Some Frigidaire model families use F5 to indicate a watchdog or control-board logic fault, and on certain platforms the code can also flag a door latch assembly or latch-switch problem. The exact meaning depends on your model family, so always consult your unit's service literature or wiring diagram to confirm.

Because F5 is tied to the control electronics rather than a field sensor, the repair usually involves replacing the EOC board itself or addressing a wiring fault in the harness that feeds it. A power reset sometimes clears a transient glitch, but if the code returns immediately the control board or associated connectors are the leading suspects.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners assume F5 means a bad oven temperature sensor and replace it first. The sensor is tied to F2, F3, or F4 codes on Frigidaire ranges, not F5. Always power-cycle the range and inspect the control board and harness before buying parts.

## Common Causes

- **Defective EOC/control board (~60%)** Internal logic failure, watchdog fault, or component breakdown on the board is the most common root of F5 across Frigidaire-branded repair guidance.
- **Loose, damaged, or burnt wiring and connectors (~20%)** Corroded terminals, heat damage, or a backed-out harness pin at the control board or latch circuit can mimic a board failure.
- **Door latch assembly or latch switch fault (~10%)** On models where F5 monitors the latch circuit, a binding latch mechanism or failed micro-switch will trigger the code.
- **Power interruption or transient glitch (~10%)** A brief surge or brown-out can lock the control into fault mode until power is cycled for at least one minute.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker (typically two 120 V breakers for a 240 V range) and wait a full minute, then restore power and watch whether F5 returns.
2. **Pull the range** forward and remove the rear access panel or tip-down the control console (consult your model's service manual for the exact fastener locations).
3. **Inspect every connector** at the EOC board and along the main harness for heat discoloration, corrosion, or loose pins; wiggle each plug gently to confirm it is fully seated.
4. **Check the door latch assembly** (if your model has a mechanical latch for self-clean) by operating it by hand and listening for the click of the micro-switches; replace the latch or switches if binding or no click is heard.
5. **Replace the electronic oven control board** if connectors and latch are good and the code persists; transfer each wire one at a time to the new board, taking a photo before you disconnect anything.
6. **Reassemble the range**, restore power, and run a short bake cycle to verify normal operation and confirm F5 does not return.
7. **Document the repair** by keeping the old board and any photos of damaged connectors; if the new board also faults, re-check harness routing and connector seating before condemning the replacement part.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240 V wiring, if you cannot locate the exact replacement control board for your model number, or if the code persists after you have replaced the board and verified all connectors. A technician has access to the full service literature and can cross-reference the F5 fault against your specific model family to rule out less common causes such as a power board failure or a wiring fault buried in the range cabinet. Professional diagnosis is also wise if your range is still under warranty, because self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150–$300 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $200–$400.
