## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E62 Error — What It Means

The E62 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with flame sensing or ignition detection. The unit may be firing but not reliably sensing the flame, or it may be failing to establish ignition in the first place. Because error-code definitions can vary across models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover for the exact meaning on your specific model.

In most cases, the heater attempts to light but either cannot detect the flame signal or loses the signal shortly after ignition. This triggers a safety lockout to prevent unburned gas from accumulating. The problem often lies in the flame-rod circuit, ignition components, or the gas supply rather than the control board itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire control board when the real culprit is a dirty flame rod or a loose wire at the igniter. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and check all wire connections before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame-sensing rod prevent it from detecting the flame current, causing the unit to shut down after ignition.
- **Loose or corroded wire connection (~25%)** A poor connection at the flame rod, igniter, or ground wire interrupts the flame-sense signal and triggers the error.
- **Weak or intermittent gas supply (~15%)** Low gas pressure or a partially closed valve can produce a weak flame that the sensor cannot reliably detect.
- **Failed igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked or fouled igniter may produce a weak spark or no spark at all, preventing reliable ignition.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The board's flame-detection circuit or relay may fail, though this is less common than sensor and wiring issues.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

</details>

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shut-off valve to the heater before opening the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws and setting it aside to access the burner chamber and control components.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe near the burner, and disconnect its wire terminal by pulling gently.
4. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or steel wool to remove carbon buildup and oxidation, then wipe it with a dry cloth.
5. **Inspect all wire connections** at the flame rod, igniter, and ground terminal; clean any corrosion with contact cleaner and push connectors fully onto their terminals.
6. **Reconnect the flame rod** wire, replace the front cover, open the gas valve, and restore power to the unit.
7. **Test the heater** by calling for hot water; observe the ignition sequence and check that the error does not reappear; if it does, measure gas inlet pressure or replace the flame rod if cleaning did not help.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if you have cleaned the flame rod and checked all connections but the error persists, or if you suspect the control board or gas valve has failed. A technician can measure gas pressure, test the flame-sense circuit with a microamp meter, and safely diagnose control-board faults. Also call a pro if the unit is still under warranty, since DIY work may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
