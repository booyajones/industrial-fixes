## LG Washer dE1 Error Code — What It Means

The dE1 code on LG front-load washers means the door is not closed properly. The washer cannot confirm a secure door closure and will refuse to start until the condition is corrected. LG distinguishes this from dE (door unable to lock), so dE1 and dE are not identical faults.

The code is triggered most often when laundry is trapped between the door and the rubber gasket, or when the door has not been seated fully after loading. The fault may also appear if the door latch or strike is bent or broken, preventing proper engagement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the door-lock assembly or main control board when the real problem is simply a folded gasket or broken door strike. Always inspect the door opening, gasket, and strike hardware before ordering electrical parts.

## Common Causes

- **Laundry caught in the door gasket (~50%)** Clothing or towels trapped between the door and the rubber boot seal keep the door from closing fully and trigger the dE1 code.
- **Door not fully latched (~25%)** The door was not pushed firmly until the latch engaged, so the washer cannot confirm closure.
- **Bent or broken door latch or strike (~15%)** The latch or strike plate is damaged or worn, preventing the door from seating and engaging normally.
- **Loose or damaged wiring to the door lock (~7%)** The connector or harness between the door-lock assembly and main control is loose, corroded, or broken, though LG's guidance points primarily to mechanical closure issues rather than electronics.
- **Failed main control board (~3%)** The main control is no longer able to read the door-closure signal, but this is rare and should be suspected only after all door-side components test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unload the washer** and open the door. Look inside the door opening for any laundry, socks, or debris caught between the door and the rubber gasket.
2. **Wipe the gasket** clean and make sure the boot seal sits flat all the way around the opening without folds or tears that would prevent full door closure.
3. **Close the door firmly** until you hear and feel the latch click into the strike. If the door does not latch, inspect the latch tongue and strike plate for bent or broken tabs.
4. **Reset the washer** by unplugging it or switching off the breaker, then press and hold the START/PAUSE button for five seconds before restoring power.
5. **Run a test cycle** to see if the dE1 code clears. If the code returns, proceed to inspect the door hardware and wiring.
6. **Inspect the door latch and strike** for physical damage, wear, or misalignment. Replace any bent or cracked components with OEM parts.
7. **Check the wiring harness** between the door-lock assembly and the main control board for loose connectors, broken wires, or corrosion. If wiring and hardware test good but the fault persists, suspect the main control board and consult a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have replaced the door latch, strike, and gasket, verified that the door closes and latches firmly, and the dE1 code still appears. At that stage the wiring harness or main control board is the likely culprit, and diagnosis requires a multimeter and familiarity with connector pinouts. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working around electrical connectors or if your washer is still under warranty, since self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–80 in parts (gasket, latch, or strike), 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call plus parts.
