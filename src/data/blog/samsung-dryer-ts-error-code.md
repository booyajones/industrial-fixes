## What this code means
The tS code signals a thermistor or temperature-sensing fault. The control board is not seeing the expected resistance from the exhaust temperature sensor circuit during operation. This typically means the thermistor itself has failed or is reading out of range, or the dryer is overheating because airflow is restricted. Less commonly, a wiring problem or control board issue prevents the sensor from communicating properly.

The code usually appears during a cycle when the control cannot verify safe operating temperature. It may stop the dryer mid-cycle or prevent a new cycle from starting until the fault clears. If the code returns after a power reset, a hardware problem exists in the sensor circuit or the venting system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first when the real problem is a $15 thermistor or a clogged vent. Always check airflow and test thermistor resistance before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed exhaust thermistor** The temperature sensor has drifted out of specification or failed completely, causing the control to see an invalid resistance reading.
- **Restricted venting or lint buildup** Blocked exhaust ducts, crushed hoses, or a clogged lint screen cause overheating, which trips the temperature-sensing circuit.
- **Wiring or connector fault** Loose, corroded, or heat-damaged connectors between the thermistor and control board disrupt the sensor signal.
- **Open high-limit thermostat** A thermal safety device opened due to excessive heat, often indicating an airflow problem that must be corrected before replacement.
- **Control board issue** Less common, but the main control may fail to read the thermistor circuit correctly even when the sensor and wiring are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and turn off the gas valve if your model uses gas heat.
2. **Inspect airflow first.** Remove and clean the lint screen, disconnect the rear exhaust duct, and confirm strong airflow from the outdoor vent hood. Clear any crushed sections, lint buildup, or obstructions in the vent path.
3. **Access the exhaust thermistor.** Remove the top or rear panel as required for your model. The thermistor is typically mounted on the exhaust duct near the blower housing and connected by a two-wire harness.
4. **Test thermistor resistance.** Using a multimeter set to ohms, measure across the red and blue wires at room temperature. A reading near 10,000 ohms is expected. An open circuit, short, or reading far outside that range indicates a failed sensor.
5. **Inspect wiring and connectors.** Check the harness plugs at both the thermistor and control board for loose pins, corrosion, heat damage, or poor fit. Repair or replace damaged wiring.
6. **Replace the thermistor if faulty.** Disconnect the harness, remove the mounting screw or clip, and install the new sensor in the same orientation. Reconnect the wires and secure the harness.
7. **Reassemble and test.** Replace all panels, restore power, and run a short timed-dry cycle. Confirm the tS code does not return and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable disassembling the dryer cabinet or working with electrical connectors and a multimeter. Also call for help if you replace the thermistor and correct airflow restrictions but the tS code persists, which may indicate a control board fault. If the dryer shares symptoms with other error codes or fails to heat even after clearing the tS fault, professional diagnosis can save time and prevent unnecessary part replacement.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220.
