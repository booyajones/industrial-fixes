## Bosch Oven F42 Error Code — What It Means

The F42 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a problem with the door lock mechanism. This fault typically appears on self-cleaning models where the oven must lock the door during high-temperature cleaning cycles. The control board cannot confirm that the door has locked properly, or the lock will not release after a cycle.

The error may appear during a self-clean cycle, immediately after the cycle completes, or when the oven attempts to lock the door. In some cases the door remains locked and the oven becomes inoperable until the fault is cleared. The code is the control board's way of reporting that the lock motor, position switch, or wiring is not responding as expected.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board when the door lock is at fault. Always test the lock motor with a multimeter and check the wiring harness for continuity before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock motor or solenoid (~55%)** The lock motor wears out over time and cannot extend or retract the latch pin, or the internal position switch fails.
- **Broken wiring or connector (~20%)** Wires at the door lock connector can fray from repeated opening and closing of the door, or terminals corrode.
- **Damaged door catch or strike (~10%)** The metal catch on the oven door frame becomes bent or worn and the lock cannot engage fully.
- **Stuck latch mechanism (~8%)** Grease hardens or debris jams the latch rod, preventing smooth movement.
- **Control board relay failure (~7%)** The relay that powers the lock motor on the main control board develops a bad contact and cannot energize the motor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven to eliminate shock hazard.
2. **Remove the oven door** by lifting it at a 45-degree angle until the hinge arms clear the frame, then set the door on a padded surface.
3. **Access the lock assembly** by removing the screws on the front panel or control panel to expose the lock motor mounted near the top of the oven cavity.
4. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the lock motor and inspect the connector pins for corrosion, melting, or loose terminals.
5. **Test the lock motor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode by checking continuity across the motor terminals. Consult your model's wiring diagram for expected values. No continuity indicates a failed motor.
6. **Inspect the latch mechanism** for debris, rust, or bent metal. Manually slide the latch rod and confirm it moves smoothly without binding.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor shows no continuity or the mechanism is damaged. Install the new unit, reconnect the harness, and secure all panels.
8. **Reinstall the oven door** by aligning the hinge arms with the slots and pushing down until they lock into place.
9. **Restore power** and initiate a self-clean cycle to verify the lock engages and the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if testing reveals a failed control board. Control board replacement requires careful handling of sensitive electronics and proper grounding. Also call a pro if the lock assembly is accessible only by removing heavy insulation or if the oven frame must be disassembled beyond typical panel screws. High-voltage work on the oven's main power supply should always be left to a qualified service provider.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
