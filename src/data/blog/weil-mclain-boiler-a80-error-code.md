## Weil-McLain Boiler A80 Error Code — What It Means

A80 on a Weil-McLain boiler is a model-specific alarm or history code stored in the control's diagnostic memory. Weil-McLain does not publish a universal definition for A80 across all boiler families, so the exact meaning depends on your unit's control platform and model number. On Ultra and AquaBalance-style controls, Axx codes are typically stored fault records that require you to enter the diagnostics or fault-history menu and cross-reference the code in your service manual.

Because the code is model-dependent, you should identify your boiler's full model number and control type from the rating plate, then consult the service manual or contact a qualified technician. Do not repeatedly reset the boiler without diagnosing the root cause, as repeated resets can mask a safety issue or damage components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the gas valve or ignition control board before checking the flame sensor. Clean or replace the flame sensor first, a simple and inexpensive fix that solves most ignition faults.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~35%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents the control from proving flame, causing repeated lockouts.
- **Ignitor or spark electrode failure (~25%)** A cracked, fouled, or worn spark electrode will not light the gas, and the boiler locks out after multiple attempts.
- **Gas supply or valve issue (~20%)** Closed manual valve, low gas pressure, or a stuck gas valve prevents fuel from reaching the burner.
- **Dirty heat exchanger or burner (~10%)** Soot or debris on the burner or heat exchanger disrupts combustion stability and causes intermittent flame loss.
- **Air in the heat exchanger or low water (~5%)** Air pockets or low system pressure can trigger false overtemp or low-water cutoff alarms that the control logs as a fault.
- **Faulty temperature or limit sensor (~5%)** A damaged thermistor or limit switch can send incorrect signals to the control, causing nuisance lockouts.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your boiler model and control type** from the rating plate on the unit and locate the service manual or download it from the Weil-McLain website.
2. **Enter the diagnostics or fault-history menu** on the control (consult the manual for button sequence) and note any additional codes or messages stored with A80.
3. **Perform the free checks first:** confirm the gas valve is open, system water pressure is in range, the power switch is on, and the vent termination is clear.
4. **Inspect the flame sensor and ignitor** at the burner assembly. Remove the flame rod, clean it with fine sandpaper or a Scotch-Brite pad, and check the ignitor for cracks or heavy carbon buildup.
5. **Test the gas supply** by verifying that gas is present at the valve inlet and that the valve clicks open when the boiler calls for heat. If you have a manometer, measure inlet and manifold pressure against the manual's specifications.
6. **Check for combustion issues** by inspecting the burner and heat exchanger for soot, debris, or rust scale. Clean as needed or call a technician if heavy buildup is present.
7. **Correct the root cause** (replace the sensor, ignitor, or valve, or clean the heat exchanger), then reset the control once and observe a full startup cycle to confirm proper ignition and flame stability.
8. **If the fault returns after one reset**, stop and call a qualified technician. Repeated resets without fixing the underlying problem can damage the control or create a safety hazard.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician if you cannot find the service manual for your model, if you are unfamiliar with gas appliances, or if the fault persists after cleaning the flame sensor and confirming gas supply. Technicians have the tools to measure gas pressure, test flame signal with a microamp meter, and safely diagnose control board or valve failures. Always call a pro if you smell gas, see flame rollout, or hear unusual noises during ignition. Do not bypass safety switches or repeatedly reset the boiler without identifying the root cause.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
