## State Water Heater E102 Error Code — What It Means

The E102 error code is not documented in publicly available State or A. O. Smith service literature, so its exact meaning depends on your specific model and control board revision. State water heaters (part of the A. O. Smith family) use different code sets across tankless, power-vent, and standard atmospheric models. On tankless and electronically controlled units, lockout codes like this typically signal ignition failure, flame loss, airflow restriction, or sensor faults. Before attempting any repair, locate your model and serial number and download the correct service manual from the manufacturer, as the code definition and troubleshooting steps vary widely by platform.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply interruption or low pressure** Closed valve, empty propane tank, or regulator fault prevents ignition and triggers a lockout code.
- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Lint, debris, ice, or animal nests restrict combustion air or exhaust flow, causing flame loss or ignition failure.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor** Carbon buildup or a corroded flame rod prevents the board from detecting the flame, shutting down the burner.
- **Scale buildup or low flow rate** Mineral deposits in the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet screen reduce flow below the minimum threshold, preventing ignition.
- **Faulty temperature or flow sensor** An out-of-range signal from a thermistor or flow meter causes the control board to lock out and display an error.
- **Control board or wiring fault** Corrosion, loose connections, or a failed circuit on the board generates spurious codes unrelated to actual operating conditions.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Find your exact model and serial number** on the rating plate and download the service manual from the State or A. O. Smith website to confirm what E102 means for your unit.
2. **Reset the unit** by turning off power at the breaker or unplugging for 30 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the code clears or returns immediately.
3. **Check gas supply** by confirming the manual valve is fully open, other gas appliances are working, and (if propane) the tank has sufficient fuel and the regulator is not frozen.
4. **Inspect air intake and exhaust** for blockages, ice, nests, or lint at the termination point and along the vent run, then clean or clear any obstructions.
5. **Descale or flush the heat exchanger** if your unit is a tankless model and you have hard water, following the descaling procedure in your service manual with a compatible descaling solution.
6. **Examine flame sensor and ignition components** by removing the burner access cover, checking for carbon deposits on the flame rod, and cleaning with fine abrasive cloth if necessary.
7. **Test and replace sensors or control board** if all mechanical checks pass but the code persists, using a multimeter to verify thermistor resistance or flow-switch operation per the service manual, and replacing any out-of-spec parts.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate your model's service documentation, if the gas supply and venting checks do not resolve the fault, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas piping, electrical controls, or combustion appliances. Persistent or intermittent codes after basic cleaning often point to sensor drift, board faults, or heat-exchanger damage that require diagnostic tools and OEM parts lookup. A qualified technician can also verify combustion efficiency and make sure safe operation after any repair.
