## What this code means
F0 on a Frigidaire range indicates a failed electronic oven control (EOC), also called the main control board or clock. This code signals that the control module itself has malfunctioned rather than a specific sensor or keypad problem. Frigidaire uses distinct codes for other faults (F1 for shorted keypad, F30 for open temperature probe, F31 for shorted probe, F3/F20/F26 for communication errors, and F90–F94 for door lock issues), so when F0 appears the control board is the primary suspect.

The EOC manages all oven functions including temperature regulation, timer, and display. When it fails the range typically shows F0 and stops responding to commands. Unlike sensor faults that produce different codes, F0 points directly to internal control-board failure and usually requires board replacement after basic diagnostics rule out wiring or connection problems.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the oven temperature sensor first because sensor problems are easier to understand. Before ordering parts, power-cycle the range at the breaker for five minutes and check that all harness plugs on the control board are fully seated, since F0 specifically indicates control-board failure rather than a sensor fault.

## Common Causes

- **Failed EOC board (~70%)** The electronic oven control itself develops internal faults from heat cycling, power surges, or component wear and can no longer execute its self-check routines.
- **Burnt or corroded control-board terminals (~15%)** Overheated wire connectors or corroded solder joints on the EOC prevent proper signal flow and trigger a control-failure code.
- **Loose or damaged harness connections (~8%)** Vibration or installation errors can unseat the ribbon cable or power plug to the control board, causing intermittent faults that register as EOC failure.
- **Power-supply transient or voltage spike (~5%)** A brief surge on the household circuit can damage microprocessor components on the control board and leave the unit displaying F0 until the board is replaced.
- **Software glitch or corrupted firmware (~2%)** Rare cases of transient memory errors can mimic a hard failure but may clear with a full power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** supplying the range and wait at least five minutes to allow capacitors to discharge and to test whether the fault is a transient software glitch.
2. **Restore power and observe** whether F0 reappears immediately or stays cleared; if the code returns the control board has a persistent fault.
3. **Unplug or de-energize the range again**, then remove the back panel or control-console cover to access the electronic oven control board (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations).
4. **Inspect the EOC board and all connectors** for burnt terminals, melted plastic, corrosion, or loose ribbon cables; wiggle each plug gently to confirm it is fully seated.
5. **Photograph wire positions** before disconnecting anything so you can match colors and pin locations during reinstallation of the new board.
6. **Order the correct replacement EOC** using your range's full model and serial number (the board part number is usually printed on a label on the old board itself).
7. **Install the new control board**, reconnect all harnesses in the same sequence, secure the cover, restore power, and verify that F0 does not return and that all oven functions operate normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring (typically 240 V for ranges) or if the range is hard-wired rather than cord-connected. A qualified appliance technician can verify the diagnosis with a multimeter, confirm that no other faults are present, and install the replacement control board with warranty coverage. Also contact service if the new board still shows F0 after installation, since that points to a wiring fault or a defective replacement part that requires systematic troubleshooting. If your range was installed within the past 30 days Frigidaire recommends returning to the installer for warranty service rather than attempting DIY repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150–$300 in parts, 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $250–$450.
