## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E25 Error — What It Means

The E25 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame sensing or ignition issue. The unit may be failing to detect flame properly after ignition, or it may be struggling to maintain stable combustion. Because exact code definitions can vary by model and firmware version, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for the precise meaning on your specific heater.

This code can stem from problems in the gas supply, the flame sensor circuit, the igniter assembly, or ventilation that affects combustion. The heater's control board monitors the flame through a sensing rod or electrode. When that signal is weak, erratic, or absent, the board throws the E25 error and shuts down to prevent unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a flame rod coated in carbon deposits or a partially closed gas valve. Clean the flame rod and verify full gas pressure before swapping electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame sensor prevent the control board from detecting flame current, triggering a false no-flame error.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~25%)** Insufficient gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or air in the line after recent work can prevent stable ignition or cause the flame to drop out.
- **Failed or weak igniter (~15%)** A cracked or worn igniter may spark intermittently or fail to light the burner reliably, leading the board to report a flame error.
- **Faulty flame rod or wire connection (~10%)** A broken flame rod, loose wire, or corroded connector interrupts the flame signal circuit even when combustion is normal.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~7%)** Obstructed intake or exhaust piping starves combustion air or traps exhaust gases, causing unstable flame and sensor errors.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** Rarely, the board itself loses the ability to read flame current correctly, even when all other components are working.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the retaining screws, then locate the burner assembly and the thin metal flame rod extending into the combustion chamber.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon deposits, corrosion, or cracks. If dirty, gently clean the rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-abrasive pad until the metal is shiny.
4. **Check the flame rod wire** where it connects to the control board and at the rod's ceramic insulator. make sure the connection is tight and free of corrosion.
5. **Verify gas supply** by reopening the shutoff valve fully and confirming that the meter is spinning and other gas appliances light normally.
6. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside the home for blockages, nests, or ice buildup, and clear any obstructions.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then reset the unit according to your model's instructions and observe the ignition sequence. If the error persists, call a licensed technician to test gas pressure, igniter current, and flame signal with a multimeter.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error returns after cleaning the flame rod, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a faulty gas valve. Professionals have the tools to measure inlet gas pressure, test flame current with a microamp meter, and safely replace igniters or control boards. Because tankless water heaters involve both gas and electrical systems, incorrect repairs can create safety hazards or void warranties. A qualified technician can also inspect venting for code compliance and make sure combustion air ratios are correct for your installation.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
