## Gree FH Error Code — What It Means

FH stands for anti-freeze limit descending frequency. This code appears when your Gree mini split detects that the indoor evaporator coil is at risk of freezing, so the inverter automatically reduces compressor output to protect the system. FH is a protective operating state rather than a direct component failure code. The unit will limit cooling or heating capacity until the condition clears.

Unlike sensor-open fault codes in the F-series, FH indicates the control board is actively responding to temperature conditions it considers unsafe. The evaporator may be running too cold because of restricted airflow, low refrigerant, sensor reading errors, or control logic problems. The system will not resume normal operation until the underlying cause is resolved and the coil temperature returns to a safe range.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty air filters or blocked return air** Restricted airflow across the indoor coil drops evaporator temperature and triggers freeze protection even when refrigerant and sensors are normal.
- **Clogged or dirty evaporator coil** Dust and debris on the coil surface reduce heat exchange and cause the coil to run colder than designed, activating the anti-freeze limit.
- **Indoor coil thermistor fault or poor connection** A loose connector, corroded pin, or failing sensor can send incorrect temperature readings that make the board think freeze risk is present.
- **Low refrigerant charge** Refrigerant shortage causes abnormally low evaporator pressure and temperature, prompting the control to limit compressor frequency to prevent ice formation.
- **Weak or failed indoor fan motor** If the blower runs slower than commanded or stops intermittently, airflow drops and the coil temperature falls below the freeze threshold.
- **PCB or inverter control issue** The main board or inverter module may misread sensor inputs or incorrectly limit compressor speed even when all physical conditions are normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the breaker and wait two minutes, then restore power to see if FH clears after a reset and the indoor coil temperature stabilizes.
2. **Remove and inspect the air filters** on the indoor unit, clean or replace them if dirty, and check that no furniture or curtains block the return-air intake.
3. **Open the indoor unit cover** and visually inspect the evaporator coil for dust buildup, then clean the coil with approved coil cleaner and a soft brush if accessible.
4. **Check the indoor fan operation** by running the unit in fan-only mode and listening for normal airflow, then verify the blower wheel spins freely and is not loose on the motor shaft.
5. **Inspect the evaporator thermistor and connector** on the indoor coil, reseat the plug firmly, and look for corrosion or damaged pins that could cause intermittent readings.
6. **Measure thermistor resistance** with a multimeter if you have the model-specific temperature-resistance chart, and replace the sensor if the reading is open, shorted, or far out of range.
7. **Evaluate refrigerant charge and pressures** using manifold gauges if airflow and sensors test normal, and recover, leak-check, evacuate, and recharge the system to the nameplate specification if low.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if FH returns after you have cleaned filters and coil, verified the fan runs normally, and reseated sensor connectors. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification, recovery equipment, a vacuum pump, and precise charging by weight or subcool method. If sensors test in range and airflow is confirmed good but the code persists, the main PCB or inverter module may need bench testing or replacement. Do not bypass or ignore the FH protection, it exists to prevent compressor damage and refrigerant-line icing that can flood liquid back to the compressor.
