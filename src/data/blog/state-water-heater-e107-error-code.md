## State Water Heater E107 Error Code — What It Means

The E107 code on State water heaters is not explicitly documented in publicly available State technical literature, but fault codes in the 107 range across similar residential water heater platforms commonly indicate ignition failure or flame sensing problems. This means the control board attempted to light the burner but either could not establish ignition or could not confirm a stable flame. The heater enters lockout to prevent unburned gas accumulation.

Because State does not publish a unified residential fault-code table in the sources reviewed, you should locate your exact model number from the rating plate and consult the installation and service manual specific to that model. If the code recurs after a single reset, the issue is real and requires inspection of the ignition system, gas delivery, venting, and flame-sensing circuit rather than repeated resets.

## Common Causes

- **Insufficient gas supply or pressure** Low inlet pressure, a closed manual shutoff valve, or regulator failure prevents the burner from lighting or sustaining a flame.
- **Dirty or failed igniter** Carbon buildup or a cracked hot-surface igniter cannot generate enough heat to ignite the gas-air mixture.
- **Corroded or misaligned flame sensor** The flame-sensing rod may be coated with combustion deposits or positioned outside the flame envelope, so the board never confirms ignition.
- **Blocked combustion-air intake or vent** Lint, nests, or vent-pipe sag restrict airflow and prevent proper combustion, causing the flame to lift off or extinguish early.
- **Faulty gas valve or control board** Internal coil failure in the gas valve or a damaged relay on the control board can interrupt the ignition sequence before flame is established.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness** Poor connections at the igniter, flame sensor, or gas valve can break continuity during the call for heat and simulate a flame-failure condition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number** on the rating plate and download the correct State installation and service manual to confirm whether E107 appears in the fault table and what the manufacturer defines it as.
2. **Check gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve upstream of the heater is fully open, the meter or LP tank has fuel, and other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
3. **Reset the heater once** by turning the gas-control knob to Off, waiting two minutes, then rotating it back to the desired temperature setting and observing whether the burner lights without fault.
4. **Inspect the burner compartment** for dust, rust flakes, or debris on the burner surface, igniter, and flame sensor, and use a soft brush and vacuum to clean all surfaces without bending the sensor rod.
5. **Test igniter continuity** with a multimeter after disconnecting power and the igniter leads, looking for an open circuit or visible cracks in the ceramic, and replace the igniter if faulty.
6. **Examine the flame sensor** for corrosion or soot buildup, gently polish the rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a dollar bill, and confirm it sits in the flame path about three-eighths inch from the burner ports.
7. **Check vent and combustion-air piping** for blockages, proper slope, and secure terminations, then confirm that the blower or draft-inducer fan spins freely and that the pressure switch hose is clear and connected.
8. **Replace the gas valve or control board** if all ignition components, wiring, gas supply, and venting check good but the fault persists, and document the new part numbers for warranty tracking.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you smell gas, if the fault returns immediately after cleaning and resetting, or if you are uncomfortable working with live gas piping and 120 V ignition circuits. Combustion appliances require precise gas-pressure measurement, flue-draft verification, and sometimes firmware updates or board-level diagnostics that are outside the scope of basic homeowner troubleshooting. Repeated faults or intermittent lockouts also suggest a problem that needs systematic electrical testing and parts replacement rather than guesswork.
