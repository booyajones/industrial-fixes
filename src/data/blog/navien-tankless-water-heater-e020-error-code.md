## Navien E020 Error Code — What It Means

The E020 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates the control board is not receiving a proper flame signal from the flame rod (flame sensing electrode). The flame rod sits in the combustion chamber and detects the presence of flame by conducting a tiny current through the ionized gas. When the sensor cannot confirm ignition after the gas valve opens, the unit shuts down and displays E020 to prevent gas buildup.

Because flame sensing depends on a clean electrical path, even a small layer of carbon or mineral deposits on the rod can block the signal. The error can also appear if the flame rod is cracked, the wiring is loose, or the igniter and burner assembly is misaligned. Check your owner's manual for the exact troubleshooting steps for your specific model, as internal layout and component locations vary across the Navien line.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire igniter assembly or control board when only the flame rod needs cleaning. Before ordering parts, remove the front cover and inspect the flame rod for carbon buildup or white mineral deposits.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~60%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame rod insulate it from the flame and block the ionization current.
- **Loose or damaged flame rod wire (~20%)** A loose connector or frayed insulation interrupts the signal path between the flame rod and the control board.
- **Cracked or broken flame rod (~10%)** Physical damage or thermal stress can crack the ceramic insulator, causing the rod to short to ground.
- **Misaligned burner or igniter (~5%)** If the burner assembly shifts or the igniter is out of position, the flame may not surround the rod properly.
- **Failed control board (~5%)** The flame detection circuit on the main control board can fail and read no signal even when the rod is clean.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover panel** by unscrewing the fasteners (typically two or four screws) and gently pulling the panel forward.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe extending into the burner chamber, usually near the igniter electrode.
4. **Disconnect the wire connector** from the flame rod by pulling it straight off, then unscrew the mounting nut or bracket holding the rod in place.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper (400-600 grit) or steel wool, removing all carbon and mineral deposits without scratching deeply into the metal.
6. **Inspect the ceramic insulator** at the base of the rod for cracks or chips; if damaged, replace the entire flame rod assembly.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod**, tighten the mounting hardware, and firmly reconnect the wire.
8. **Replace the front cover**, restore gas and electrical power, and open a hot-water tap to test for ignition and proper operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed gas technician if you are not comfortable working around gas appliances, if the flame rod and wiring appear intact but the error persists after cleaning, or if you see other error codes cycling with E020. A technician can test the control board's flame-detection circuit, verify correct gas pressure and valve timing, and inspect the combustion chamber for alignment issues. Also call a pro if you notice yellow flames, soot buildup inside the chamber, or a strong gas smell, as these indicate incomplete combustion or a gas leak that requires immediate professional attention.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
