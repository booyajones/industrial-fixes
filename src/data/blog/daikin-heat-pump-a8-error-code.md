## Daikin Heat Pump A8 Error Code — What It Means

The A8 error code on Daikin heat pumps typically signals a communication problem between the indoor and outdoor units, or a fault with a sensor circuit. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit cover for the precise definition. In many cases the system detects a wiring issue, a loose connector, or a failed temperature or pressure sensor that prevents normal operation. The heat pump will usually shut down or enter a protective mode until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many technicians replace control boards or sensors without first checking the wiring harness and connectors. A simple visual inspection and continuity test of the cables between indoor and outdoor units often reveals the real problem and saves hundreds of dollars.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~40%)** Vibration, moisture, or poor initial installation can loosen terminal screws or corrode the low-voltage communication wires between the indoor and outdoor units.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~25%)** Indoor or outdoor thermistors can drift out of range or fail open, causing the control board to register a communication or sensor error.
- **Damaged control cable (~15%)** The multi-conductor cable running between units may be pinched, cut, or chewed by rodents, breaking signal continuity.
- **Faulty control board (~12%)** The main PCB in either the indoor or outdoor unit can develop solder cracks or component failures that interrupt communication.
- **Voltage sag or power supply issue (~8%)** Weak incoming voltage or a failing transformer can starve the control circuits and trigger intermittent faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch for both the indoor and outdoor units.
2. **Remove the service panels** from both units to access the terminal blocks and control boards.
3. **Inspect all wiring connections** at the indoor and outdoor terminal strips. Tighten any loose screws and clean terminals with electrical contact cleaner if corrosion is present.
4. **Check the control cable** running between units for cuts, pinches, or rodent damage. Repair or replace any damaged sections.
5. **Test sensor resistance** using a multimeter. Consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance values at room temperature. Replace any sensor that reads open, shorted, or out of range.
6. **Restore power** and observe the display. If the A8 code persists, measure the voltage at the control board and verify proper communication signal levels according to the wiring diagram.
7. **Replace the control board** if all wiring and sensors test good but the error remains. Start with the board that shows no output signal on the communication lines.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line-voltage wiring or if the fault persists after checking connections and sensors. Refrigerant circuit diagnostics and control board replacement require specialized tools and knowledge of heat pump operation. A technician can measure communication signals with an oscilloscope, verify refrigerant pressures, and access factory service codes that are not shown on the user display. Professional diagnosis typically costs less than replacing the wrong part and protects your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
