## What this code means
The F22 code on a Kenmore dryer indicates the control board has detected a fault in the outlet thermistor circuit. The outlet thermistor (also called the outlet thermal sensor) monitors exhaust temperature near the dryer's outlet. When the circuit is open, shorted, or reading out of range, the dryer may start briefly and then shut down with F22 displayed.

This code points specifically to the outlet thermistor, not the inlet sensor or cycling thermostat. The control expects to see roughly 10,000 ohms of resistance from the sensor at room temperature. Loose connectors, damaged wiring, or a failed thermistor will all trigger F22.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some owners replace the main control board first. Measure the outlet thermistor resistance with a multimeter (it should read about 10 kΩ at room temperature) and inspect the two red sensor wires for shorts to ground before replacing any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed outlet thermistor (~60%)** The sensor itself drifts out of spec, reads open, or develops an internal short so the control sees an invalid resistance value.
- **Loose or corroded connector (~20%)** The plug at the thermistor or the control board works loose or corrodes, breaking continuity in the circuit.
- **Damaged wire harness (~15%)** The red sensor wires are pinched, chafed, or shorted to ground inside the cabinet, especially where they pass through metal edges or around the blower.
- **Control-board sensing fault (~5%)** If the thermistor and wiring both verify good, the control board's input circuit may have failed, though this is less common.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the breaker, and wait for any capacitors to discharge before touching any wiring.
2. **Locate the outlet thermistor** near the exhaust outlet or blower housing (consult your model's wiring diagram or service manual for the exact location).
3. **Unplug the thermistor connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance across the two sensor terminals. You should see about 10,000 ohms at room temperature.
4. **Inspect the two red sensor wires** for damage, then check each wire to ground with the multimeter. If either shows continuity to ground, the harness is shorted and must be repaired or replaced.
5. **Check all connectors** at the thermistor and the control board for corrosion, bent pins, or loose fit. Clean or reseat as needed.
6. **Replace the outlet thermistor** if it reads open, shorted, or significantly outside the 10 kΩ range. Secure the new sensor in the same mounting location and reconnect the plug.
7. **Restore power** and run a short test cycle to verify the F22 code does not return. If the code persists after replacing the sensor and confirming good wiring, the control board may need replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with any electrical wiring or if you cannot locate the outlet thermistor in your dryer cabinet. A pro should also diagnose the fault if the thermistor and wiring both check out but the code returns, since control-board replacement requires careful connector work and sometimes firmware checks. If your dryer is still under warranty or you lack a multimeter and basic hand tools, professional service will save time and avoid accidental damage to connectors or circuit boards.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220.
