## Samsung Dryer dF Error Code — What It Means

The dF code on Samsung dryers signals a door-related fault. Samsung groups dF together with dC, dE, do, d0, 1 DC, and 1 dF as errors involving the door, latch, or the sensors that verify the door is secured correctly. In practical terms, the dryer is not seeing the door state it expects. The fault is usually in the door latch or strike assembly, the door switch that senses closure, or the wiring and connectors in that circuit, rather than in the drum or heater.

Because the dryer requires a secure door to start and continue operation, the control board throws dF when it cannot confirm the door is latched. Common triggers include laundry caught in the seal, a loose or damaged latch, a faulty door switch, or intermittent wiring. Samsung recommends a power reset first to rule out a temporary control glitch.

## Before You Replace Anything

Owners sometimes replace the main control board when the real problem is a worn door latch or a loose connector at the door switch. Always test the door switch circuit and inspect the latch alignment before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Laundry or debris blocking the door** A garment edge, lint, or foreign object prevents the door from closing fully and the latch from engaging.
- **Latch or strike misaligned or damaged** The plastic latch or metal strike is loose, cracked, or out of position so the switch never sees a closed state.
- **Faulty door switch** The switch that detects latch engagement is stuck, corroded, or electrically open even when the door is shut.
- **Loose or damaged wiring** Connectors at the door switch or along the harness are loose, corroded, or have broken pins that interrupt the signal.
- **Control board misreading door state** After a power interruption or surge, the board may briefly report dF even when the door circuit is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and wait 60 seconds to reset the control board and discharge residual power before opening any panels.
2. **Inspect the door seal and latch area** for laundry, lint, coins, or other debris that would prevent the door from closing fully, and clear anything you find.
3. **Close the door firmly** and check that the latch snaps into the strike without wobble or play, and that the door stays shut without you holding it.
4. **Remove the top or front panel** (consult your model's service manual for screw locations) to access the door-switch connector and the latch mounting hardware.
5. **Check the door-switch wiring** for loose or corroded connectors, damaged pins, or heat marks, and reseat or repair any connection that looks suspect.
6. **Test the door switch** with a multimeter set to continuity: with the door closed the switch should show continuity, and with the door open it should read open (consult your model's wiring diagram for the exact terminal pair).
7. **Replace the door latch, strike, or door switch** as needed, reassemble the panels, plug in the dryer, and run a test cycle to confirm dF does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared obstructions, verified the latch and strike are aligned and undamaged, tested the door switch and found it working, and the dF code still appears. At that point the fault is likely in the main control board or in wiring buried inside the cabinet. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connectors or removing panels, or if your dryer is still under warranty and DIY work would void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $100–200.
