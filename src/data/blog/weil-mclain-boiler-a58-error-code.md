## Weil-McLain A58 Error Code — What It Means

The A58 error code does not appear in Weil-McLain's official service manuals or error code lists. Weil-McLain documents codes such as A01, A02, A16, and E01 through E03, but A58 is not among them. The code you see is most likely a misread or display artifact. The most probable actual code is A01, which Weil-McLain defines as 'No burner ignition.' This means the boiler attempted to light but failed to detect a flame within the required time window.

If your display truly shows A58, verify it by accessing the contractor menu (hold UP and DOWN arrows for 10 seconds, then navigate to Diagnostics and Lockout History). If the code is actually A01, the boiler did not ignite because of no gas supply, a faulty ignition electrode, insufficient gas pressure (spec: 3.5 to 11 inches water column), a stuck gas valve, or air trapped in the heat exchanger.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the ignitor or flame sensor when the real problem is a closed gas valve or unpurged gas line. Always verify gas supply and pressure before swapping parts.

## Common Causes

- **No gas supply or closed valve (~35%)** Gas line not purged after installation, closed manual valve, or interrupted supply from the meter.
- **Faulty or dirty ignitor or flame sensor (~25%)** Electrode cracked, warped away from the burner, or covered in carbon buildup.
- **Insufficient gas pressure (~20%)** Pressure below 3.5 inches water column due to regulator failure or undersized supply line.
- **Gas valve not opening (~12%)** Gas valve solenoid stuck closed or control board not sending voltage to the valve.
- **Air trapped in heat exchanger (~5%)** Causes gurgling or sloshing noises and prevents proper combustion heat absorption.
- **Faulty control board (~3%)** Internal fault in the main controller preventing ignition sequence or flame detection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the actual error code** by accessing the contractor menu: hold the UP and DOWN arrow buttons simultaneously for 10 seconds, navigate to Diagnostics, then Errors, then Lockout History, and record the exact code displayed.
2. **Check gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve is fully open (handle parallel to the pipe). For new installations, confirm the gas line was purged of air before first firing.
3. **Inspect the ignitor and flame sensor** visually for cracks, warping away from the burner, or heavy carbon buildup. Drape the ignitor over the plastic housing (avoid contact) and call for heat to test for spark.
4. **Measure gas pressure** at the inlet of the gas valve using a manometer. Weil-McLain requires 3.5 to 11 inches water column. If pressure is low, check the regulator and supply line.
5. **Check for trapped air** by listening for gurgling or sloshing noises in the heat exchanger during a firing attempt. If present, purge the system of air using the boiler's air vents or automatic air eliminator.
6. **Test the gas valve** by verifying the control board sends voltage to the valve during ignition. If voltage is present but no gas flows, replace the gas valve.
7. **Review flame signal** with a combustion analyzer if available, or call a technician to confirm proper combustion and flame detection circuit operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician immediately if you are not trained in gas appliance service. Gas work requires special tools (manometer, combustion analyzer), knowledge of safe purging and lighting procedures, and an understanding of flame detection circuits. A technician will verify the actual error code, measure gas pressure accurately, test the ignitor and flame sensor with proper grounding, and check for carbon monoxide or incomplete combustion. Do not attempt to bypass safety controls or force ignition. If you have already replaced parts without success, a professional diagnostic will save money and prevent unsafe operation.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
