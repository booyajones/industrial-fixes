## LG Washer LE Error Code — What It Means

The LE code on LG washers stands for Locked Motor Error (or Motor Locker Error on top-load models). It appears when the tub fails to spin because the motor cannot operate properly. The drive system is either strained by too heavy a load or physically blocked by a foreign object caught in the tub or mechanism. LG designed this code to protect the motor from damage when it detects the drum cannot turn as expected.

## Common Causes

- **Overloaded washer** Too many clothes or very heavy items like comforters strain the motor beyond its normal operating range.
- **Foreign object obstruction** A coin, sock, or small item is wedged in the gap between the tub and washboard or caught in the drive mechanism.
- **Temporary control fault** A one-time software glitch or power surge can trigger the code even though the hardware is fine.
- **Motor wiring or connector issue** Damaged, loose, or corroded wires between the control board and motor prevent proper motor operation.
- **Bad hall sensor or RPS sensor** The motor position feedback circuit fails to report rotor speed accurately, causing the control board to lock out the cycle.
- **Defective motor or stator** Internal motor windings or rotor damage prevent the drum from turning freely under power.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power reset the washer.** Turn the machine off, unplug it from the wall, and wait at least five minutes before plugging it back in and starting a new cycle.
2. **Reduce and rebalance the load.** Remove half the laundry or any bulky items and retry the cycle to see if the motor can operate with a lighter load.
3. **Check for foreign objects.** Shine a flashlight into the tub and feel around the gap between the inner drum and outer tub for coins, buttons, or socks. Use long-nose pliers or tweezers to remove anything you find.
4. **Inspect motor wiring and connectors.** Unplug the machine, pull the washer away from the wall, and remove the rear or bottom access panel. Check that all connectors at the motor and control board are seated firmly and that no wires are pinched or burned.
5. **Test the hall sensor circuit (if confident with a multimeter).** With the machine unplugged, access the motor connector and set your meter to DC volts. Reconnect only the motor harness, power the machine, and manually spin the rotor by hand. You should see a pulsing reading up to about 10 V DC if the hall sensor is working.
6. **Check stator winding resistance.** Disconnect the motor from the control board and measure resistance between each pair of stator phase terminals. Readings should fall between 5 and 15 ohms. If any pair is open or far outside that range, replace the stator assembly.
7. **Call a technician if the code persists.** If the error returns after you have confirmed a light load, no obstructions, and good connections, the motor, sensor, or control board likely needs professional diagnosis and replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the LE code returns after you have completed a power reset, reduced the load, and confirmed no visible obstructions or loose wiring. Diagnosing motor feedback sensors, stator windings, and control-board drive circuits requires live voltage testing and component-level troubleshooting that can be unsafe without proper training. LG recommends professional service when basic user steps do not clear the fault. A technician will use a multimeter and the service manual to isolate whether the motor, sensor, wiring, or control board is at fault and replace only the defective component.
