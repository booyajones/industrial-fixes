## State Water Heater E120 Error Code — What It Means

The E120 code on a State tankless water heater signals an ignition or combustion fault. The burner control attempted to light the gas but could not establish a stable flame, so the unit locked out to prevent unsafe operation. This is not a temperature or flow sensor code. It means something in the ignition sequence failed, typically because air, gas, or exhaust flow was interrupted.

State tankless heaters share design roots with A.O. Smith, and the most frequently documented cause for this fault is a blocked air intake screen or restricted combustion air path. Gas supply problems and vent blockages also prevent proper ignition. Until you restore clean air flow and correct gas pressure, the heater will not fire reliably.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or intake screen** Lint, dust, or debris on the combustion air inlet restricts oxygen flow and prevents the burner from lighting or holding flame.
- **Low gas pressure or closed gas shutoff** Insufficient gas supply or a partially closed valve starves the burner and causes repeated ignition failures.
- **Restricted exhaust vent or improper termination** Blockage, separation, or recirculation of exhaust gases interrupts combustion air and trips the safety lockout.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor** The flame rod may be coated with carbon or mineral deposits, preventing the control from proving ignition even when the burner lights.
- **Clogged inlet water filter or low flow** Many tankless models require minimum water flow to initiate ignition, and a blocked filter or closed faucet can prevent startup.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off gas and power** at the shutoff valve and circuit breaker before inspecting the unit.
2. **Remove and clean the air intake screen** on the front or side of the heater, washing away lint, dust, and debris under running water.
3. **Check the vent termination** outside for blockage, nests, ice, or improper clearance that might cause exhaust recirculation, and clear any obstructions.
4. **Confirm the gas shutoff is fully open** and verify that inlet gas pressure matches the range specified on the heater's rating plate for your gas type.
5. **Inspect and clean the inlet water filter** at the cold-water connection, removing any sediment or scale that could restrict flow.
6. **Remove the flame sensor or flame rod** (consult your model's service manual for location), wipe it gently with fine abrasive pad or emery cloth, and reinstall with a secure ground connection.
7. **Restore power and gas, then reset the heater** by cycling the control or following the reset procedure in your manual. If E120 reappears immediately, stop and call a technician for combustion testing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E120 code returns after you have cleaned the air intake, confirmed gas pressure, and cleared the vent. Persistent ignition faults require combustion analysis, gas-valve circuit testing, and sometimes igniter or control board replacement. Never bypass safeties or attempt repeated manual resets without correcting the root cause. If you smell gas, see yellow or rolling flames, or hear unusual noise during ignition attempts, shut down the heater immediately and call for service.
