## Samsung Washer tC Error Code — What It Means

The tC code on your Samsung washer indicates a thermistor or temperature-sensor fault. The washer is detecting an abnormal temperature-sensor reading, so it cannot trust the water temperature measurement. In Samsung's code tables, tC is tied to the machine's temperature-sensing system rather than a mechanical wash fault. The control board is not getting a valid temperature reading from the thermistor circuit, which may be missing, implausible, or outside the expected range.

This error will typically stop the wash cycle because the machine cannot verify safe water temperature for the selected program. The thermistor is a small sensor mounted in the tub or heater assembly that changes resistance with temperature, and the control board reads that resistance to monitor water heat. When the signal is lost, shorted, or inconsistent, the tC code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when the thermistor or its wiring is the real problem. Measure the thermistor resistance at room temperature and inspect the harness for damage before replacing any board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The temperature sensor in the tub or heater assembly has failed open, shorted, or is reading unstable values.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wiring or connectors (~30%)** The harness or pins between the thermistor and main control board are loose, corroded, broken, or intermittent.
- **Main control board fault (~15%)** The PCB input circuit that reads the thermistor signal is damaged or cannot correctly interpret the sensor resistance.
- **Sensor mounting or harness retention issue (~5%)** The thermistor has shifted out of position or the harness has pulled free after a previous repair or reassembly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the washer at the wall outlet or breaker, wait 30 seconds to 5 minutes, then restart and check if the code clears.
2. **Enter diagnostic or service mode** (procedure varies by model) and read stored fault codes to confirm whether tC is present alone or with other errors.
3. **Unplug the washer** and pull it away from the wall so you can access the rear panel or top cabinet to reach the thermistor harness.
4. **Inspect the thermistor connector** at the tub heater or sensor location and at the main control board for loose pins, corrosion, wire breaks, or physical damage.
5. **Disconnect the thermistor** from the harness and measure its resistance with a multimeter at room temperature. One Samsung top-load example shows about 55–65 kΩ at room temperature, but consult your model's service manual for the exact specification.
6. **Replace the thermistor or temperature sensor assembly** if the resistance is out of range, open, shorted, or unstable when you wiggle the wires.
7. **Test or replace the main control board** if the thermistor tests good but the code persists, indicating a board input-circuit fault.
8. **Reassemble the washer**, restore power, clear any stored codes, and run a normal wash cycle or diagnostic check to confirm the tC code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the washer cabinet, measuring resistance with a multimeter, or identifying the correct thermistor location for your model. A technician has service manuals with exact resistance specifications, diagnostic software to read detailed fault logs, and experience isolating intermittent wiring faults that can be hard to spot. If you have already replaced the thermistor and checked all visible wiring but the tC code persists, the main control board input circuit is likely faulty and a pro can confirm that diagnosis before you invest in a new board. Technicians can also spot related issues such as heater faults or tub-assembly damage that may have caused the sensor to fail in the first place.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250.
