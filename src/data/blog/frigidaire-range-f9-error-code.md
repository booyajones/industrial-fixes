## Frigidaire Range F9 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 error code on a Frigidaire range typically indicates a problem with the oven door latch assembly or the door lock mechanism during a self-clean cycle. The control board has detected that the door did not lock when commanded, or that the door switch is not reporting the correct status. This safety interlock prevents the oven from operating properly and will stop the self-clean cycle or prevent normal baking modes from starting.

Because the exact definition of F9 can vary by Frigidaire model and year, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for the precise meaning on your unit. On most electric ranges the code points to the door lock motor, door position switch, or the wiring between them and the main control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the door lock motor or position switch is the actual culprit. Test continuity across the door switch terminals with a multimeter and listen for the lock motor running before buying a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door latch switch (~40%)** The micro-switch that signals door position to the control board has burned contacts or a broken actuator arm and no longer closes the circuit.
- **Door lock motor failure (~35%)** The small motor that drives the latch mechanism has burned out or seized and cannot move the bolt into the locked position.
- **Broken or binding latch linkage (~15%)** Plastic linkage arms, springs, or the latch rod itself are cracked, dislocated, or jammed with debris so the lock cannot travel its full stroke.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~7%)** The wire harness plug at the door lock assembly or control board has backed out or developed resistance that breaks the signal circuit.
- **Control board relay fault (~3%)** The relay on the main control board that energizes the lock motor has failed and no longer sends power to the latch assembly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or unplug the range to eliminate shock hazard before any disassembly or testing.
2. **Remove the oven door** by lifting it to the hinge-release position and pulling it straight out, then set it on a padded surface to access the inner door panel screws.
3. **Take off the inner door panel** by unscrewing the perimeter fasteners and gently separating the glass from the frame to expose the latch assembly and wiring.
4. **Inspect the latch mechanism** for broken springs, cracked plastic linkage arms, or a bent latch rod, and clear any grease or food debris that could bind the slide.
5. **Test the door switch** with a multimeter set to continuity mode by disconnecting the switch plug and checking that the switch closes when the latch is in the locked position and opens when unlocked.
6. **Check the lock motor** by applying direct 120 V AC (with extreme caution) to its terminals or by testing resistance across the motor windings; replace the entire door lock assembly if the motor is open or will not turn.
7. **Reassemble the door** by reversing disassembly, reconnect power, and run a short self-clean test to verify the latch locks smoothly and the F9 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the oven door removed or if voltage testing is required and you lack a multimeter and electrical safety training. A technician should also handle the repair if the control board itself shows signs of arcing, burned traces, or if multiple error codes appear in sequence, suggesting a broader electronics failure. If the range is still under warranty, contact Frigidaire or an authorized service center to preserve coverage before attempting any repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
