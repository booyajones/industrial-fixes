## Navien E146 Error Code — What It Means

The E146 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a problem with the flow sensor circuit or communication between the flow sensor and the main control board. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual for model-specific details. The heater detects an abnormal signal from the flow sensor, which measures how much water is passing through the unit. This can be caused by a faulty sensor, loose or corroded wiring, debris affecting flow, or a failed control board. The heater will typically shut down or fail to ignite until the issue is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the flow sensor immediately, but often the problem is a loose wire terminal or corroded connector. Inspect all wiring and connectors to the flow sensor before ordering a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded flow sensor connector (~40%)** Vibration or moisture can cause wiring terminals at the flow sensor to loosen or corrode, interrupting the signal to the control board.
- **Failed flow sensor (~30%)** The flow sensor itself can fail due to age, mineral buildup on the turbine, or internal electronics failure.
- **Intermittent water flow or low flow rate (~15%)** If flow is too low or erratic due to a partially closed valve, clogged inlet filter, or kinked hose, the sensor may send inconsistent readings.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main control board may have a damaged input circuit or firmware glitch that misreads the flow sensor signal.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~5%)** Wiring between the flow sensor and control board can be cut, pinched, or chafed, causing an open circuit or short.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water shutoff valves to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the heater to access the control board and flow sensor area.
3. **Locate the flow sensor** (usually mounted in-line on the cold water inlet piping) and trace its wiring harness to the control board.
4. **Inspect all connectors** at both the sensor and the board. Look for corrosion, moisture, or loose pins. Disconnect and reconnect each connector firmly.
5. **Check the wiring harness** along its length for cuts, pinches, or chafing. Repair or replace any damaged wire.
6. **Test the flow sensor** if you have a multimeter. Disconnect the sensor connector and measure resistance across the sensor terminals (consult your model's manual for expected values). Replace the sensor if readings are out of range or infinite.
7. **Reassemble and restore power**. Open the water valves and turn the breaker back on. Run a hot tap and observe whether the error clears. If the code persists, consider replacing the control board or call a professional.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if you have checked all wiring and the error persists after replacing the flow sensor. A professional can perform voltage tests on the control board, verify gas and combustion settings, and replace the board safely if needed. Also call a pro if the heater is still under warranty, as DIY repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
