## State Water Heater E93 Error Code — What It Means

E93 on State tankless water heaters signals an ignition failure or combustion-air fault. The unit cannot light or cannot prove flame. Based on manufacturer troubleshooting for State and A. O. Smith tankless platforms, the most common underlying issue is a blocked air intake screen that prevents proper combustion airflow. Other contributing factors include restricted venting, low gas supply, dirty flame sensors, or low water flow through the heat exchanger.

[Jump to Fix](#fix)

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, insects, or debris restrict combustion air and prevent ignition.
- **Restricted venting or exhaust path** Crushed, blocked, or improperly terminated vent pipe causes draft or combustion-air problems.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Closed shutoff valve, undersized gas line, or inadequate inlet pressure stops the burner from lighting.
- **Dirty flame rod or flame sensor** Carbon buildup or contamination prevents the control board from confirming flame presence.
- **Low water flow through the unit** Clogged inlet filter or low system flow can trigger ignition lockouts on tankless heaters.
- **Failed ignition circuit components** Faulty igniter, gas valve, or control board can prevent successful ignition after airflow and gas checks pass.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number** on the rating plate and look up the E93 definition in your unit's service manual to confirm the fault category.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the front or side of the unit, removing any lint, dust, or debris that restricts combustion air.
3. **Check the entire vent system** for blockage, disconnected joints, excessive length, or improper termination clearance outdoors.
4. **Confirm gas supply** by verifying the shutoff valve is fully open, the correct gas type is connected, and inlet pressure meets the rating plate requirements under firing load.
5. **Clean the inlet water filter** at the cold water connection and verify adequate flow through the unit by checking fixture flow rates.
6. **Remove and inspect the flame rod or flame sensor**, cleaning it gently if allowed by the manufacturer and checking wiring and grounding connections.
7. **Reset the water heater** and run a full ignition cycle, watching for stable flame and confirming the code does not return before returning the unit to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the code returns after cleaning intake and vent paths, or if you lack a combustion analyzer and manometer to verify gas pressure and air/fuel ratio. Incorrect venting or gas work can create carbon monoxide hazards. If flame-sensor and ignition-circuit diagnostics require board-level testing or part replacement beyond intake cleaning, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent unnecessary part swaps.
