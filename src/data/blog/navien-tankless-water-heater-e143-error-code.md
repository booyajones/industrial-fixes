## Navien E143 Error Code — What It Means

The E143 error on a Navien tankless water heater signals a problem with the exhaust temperature sensor circuit. This sensor monitors the temperature of combustion gases leaving the unit to protect against overheating and confirm proper venting. The code triggers when the control board detects an out-of-range reading, an open circuit, or a short in the sensor wiring.

The exact thresholds and diagnostics vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit's access panel. Common triggers include a failed sensor, corroded or loose connections at the sensor harness, damaged wiring, or occasionally a control board communication fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a corroded sensor connector. Check sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect all connectors before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed exhaust temperature sensor (~50%)** The sensor itself develops an internal open or short, sending no signal or an out-of-range signal to the control board.
- **Corroded or loose sensor connector (~25%)** Moisture or heat degrades the plug at the sensor, creating intermittent contact or high resistance.
- **Damaged sensor wiring (~15%)** Wires running to the exhaust sensor are pinched, melted, or broken, interrupting the signal path.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main PCB fails to read the sensor correctly even when the sensor and wiring are good.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the lower panel screws and lifting the cover away to access the internal components.
3. **Locate the exhaust temperature sensor**, typically mounted on the exhaust manifold or near the top of the combustion chamber.
4. **Disconnect the sensor harness** and inspect both the sensor terminals and the connector pins for corrosion, moisture, or damage.
5. **Measure sensor resistance** with a multimeter at room temperature and compare the reading to the table in your service manual for that model.
6. **Replace the sensor** if the resistance is out of spec or if the connector is badly corroded and cleaning does not restore continuity.
7. **Reconnect all wiring**, restore power and gas, and run a hot-water cycle to verify the error clears and the unit operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien service technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if you lack a multimeter and the skills to trace sensor circuits, or if the error persists after replacing the sensor and inspecting all wiring. Tankless water heaters involve gas combustion and require proper venting. An incorrectly diagnosed exhaust sensor issue can mask a real overheating or venting problem that poses a carbon monoxide risk.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-$400.
