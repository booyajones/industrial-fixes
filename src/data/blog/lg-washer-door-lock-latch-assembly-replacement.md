## LG Washer Door Lock Assembly Replacement — What This Part Does

The door lock assembly on an LG front-load washer is an electronically actuated safety device that latches and locks the door shut during operation. When you start a cycle, the control board sends power to the lock assembly, which mechanically engages the door strike on the door and signals back that the door is secure. If the washer does not receive the correct locked or unlocked feedback, it will not start or it will stop mid-cycle and throw a door error code.

The lock assembly fails when the internal solenoid burns out, plastic mounting tabs crack, or the wiring connector corrodes. A separate but related problem is a broken door strike on the door itself, which prevents the latch from hooking even when the electrical lock is working. Door boot misalignment or a loose wire at the lock connector can also trigger the same fault without the lock itself being bad.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **Washer will not start and displays door open or dE error** The control board is not getting the locked signal from the door lock assembly, so it refuses to run.
- **Door will not lock when you close it** You hear no click or solenoid sound when you shut the door and press Start.
- **Door will not unlock after cycle ends** The lock assembly stays engaged and you cannot open the door even after power is off for several minutes.
- **Clicking or buzzing sound at the door latch area** The solenoid is trying to actuate but the mechanism inside the lock is jammed or broken.
- **Washer starts but stops immediately with door fault** The lock engages briefly but the feedback circuit fails, so the cycle aborts within seconds.
- **Visible crack or missing piece on the door strike** The plastic strike hook on the door is broken, so the latch has nothing to grab and the washer thinks the door is open.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Unplug the washer from the wall outlet and turn off the hot and cold water supply valves.
2. Open the door and use a flat blade screwdriver to pry the inner edge of the door boot clamp away from the front panel, then pull the boot lip free to expose the lock assembly mounting area.
3. Locate the door lock assembly on the right or left side of the door opening and remove the two or three mounting screws that secure it to the front panel.
4. Pull the lock assembly forward slightly and disconnect the wire harness by pressing the release tab and pulling the connector straight off.
5. Inspect the door strike on the door itself for cracks or missing pieces and replace the strike if damaged (some models require removing the entire door or inner door panel to access the strike).
6. Position the new door lock assembly against the mounting holes, reconnect the wire harness until it clicks, and drive the mounting screws back in snug but not over-tight to avoid cracking the plastic.
7. Fold the door boot lip back over the front panel edge and press the wire clamp back into its groove all the way around the opening.
8. Restore water supply, plug in the washer, close the door, and run a drain and spin cycle to verify the lock engages, the cycle completes, and the door unlocks at the end.
9. If the door still will not lock or the error persists, check the wire connector pins for damage and verify the door strike is seated correctly in the lock.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the door boot retaining ring is rusted or seized and you cannot safely remove it without tearing the boot, if the door strike replacement requires disassembling the door and you are not comfortable with that, or if the new lock assembly still does not resolve the fault and you need electrical diagnostics on the control board or wiring harness. Also call a pro if the washer is still under warranty, because DIY repair may void coverage.
