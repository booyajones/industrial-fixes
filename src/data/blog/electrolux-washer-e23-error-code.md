## Electrolux Washer E23 Error Code — What It Means

The E23 error code on Electrolux washers indicates a drain-pump triac sensing or pump-control fault. The washer's main control board has detected a mismatch between the commanded state of the drain pump and the actual triac status on the board. This means the control board either cannot detect proper pump operation or the triac circuit that powers the pump has failed.

In practical terms, the machine expects the drain pump to respond when commanded but the sensing circuit reports something is wrong. This code points specifically to the electrical control circuit for the pump rather than a simple blockage or mechanical drain issue. Technicians diagnose this by checking the pump itself, the wiring harness and connectors between pump and board, and finally the main control board if those components pass inspection.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, assuming the triac circuit has failed. Always test the drain pump windings and inspect all pump wiring and connectors for damage or corrosion before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed drain pump (~50%)** The pump motor windings have burned out, the impeller is seized, or water ingress has damaged the pump internally so the triac cannot detect normal current draw.
- **Damaged or corroded pump wiring or connectors (~25%)** Loose pins, corroded terminals, broken wires, or heat-damaged insulation in the harness between the pump and control board cause intermittent or missing signals.
- **Failed main control board triac or sensing circuit (~20%)** The triac component or the sensing circuitry on the PCB has failed, preventing the board from properly driving or monitoring the drain pump.
- **Water damage to pump connector or PCB traces (~5%)** Leaks or condensation have allowed moisture to corrode the pump connector or control-board traces, disrupting the triac feedback signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves to isolate all power and water before opening any panels or accessing internal components.
2. **Access the drain pump** by removing the front lower panel or tipping the washer back to reach the base (consult your model's service manual for the exact location and fasteners).
3. **Inspect the pump and its connector** for visible damage, corrosion, burned odor, water stains, or melted plastic on the terminals or wiring.
4. **Disconnect the pump wiring harness** and check each wire and pin for continuity from the pump connector back to the control board connector using a multimeter.
5. **Test the drain pump electrically** by measuring resistance across the pump motor terminals (refer to your model's wiring diagram for expected values) and check that the impeller spins freely by hand.
6. **Replace the drain pump** if it fails the electrical test, shows mechanical seizure, or exhibits signs of internal water damage or burning.
7. **If the pump and wiring are good**, remove and inspect the main control board for burned traces, damaged triac components, or corrosion around the pump-output circuit, and replace the board if the triac circuit has failed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the washer's electrical system, if you cannot safely access the drain pump or control board, or if you have already replaced the pump and wiring but the E23 code persists. A technician can perform precise triac and sensing-circuit tests on the control board and has the tools to verify current draw and signal integrity. Also call a pro if you see widespread water damage inside the cabinet or if multiple error codes appear together, since that may indicate a larger system failure requiring full diagnostic testing and multiple part replacements in one visit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–120 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $180–350.
