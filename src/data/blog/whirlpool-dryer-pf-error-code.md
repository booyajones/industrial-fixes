## Whirlpool Dryer PF Error Code — What It Means

The PF code on a Whirlpool dryer stands for power failure. The dryer detected that power to the machine was interrupted or fell outside acceptable limits while a cycle was running. This code does not point to a mechanical problem inside the drum, heater, or airflow system. Instead, it signals that the control board lost line voltage or saw an unstable supply during operation.

In practice, PF can appear after a momentary outage, brownout, or utility surge. If the power interruption was brief, the code may clear with a simple reset and the cycle can restart. When PF repeats, the root cause is almost always in the power-supply path: the house circuit, receptacle, power cord, or the dryer's own control-board power section.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when PF persists, but low or fluctuating voltage at the outlet is often the real cause. Use a multimeter to measure receptacle voltage under load before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Tripped or weak circuit breaker (~30%)** A breaker that trips mid-cycle or has a loose connection at the bus bar causes intermittent power loss and triggers PF.
- **Loose or damaged power cord (~25%)** A plug that does not seat firmly, frayed wires, or heat damage at the terminals interrupts supply and throws PF.
- **Low or unstable outlet voltage (~20%)** Loose wiring at the receptacle or upstream junction can drop voltage below the control's acceptable range (one repair found only 108 V AC instead of the normal 120–125 V AC), causing PF.
- **Momentary utility outage or brownout (~15%)** A brief supply interruption from the power company registers as PF and usually clears with a reset.
- **Failing main control board (~10%)** If supply voltage is correct and stable but PF recurs, the board's internal power-supply section may be faulty (part W10654005 was identified in one Whirlpool dryer repair).

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the code.** Unplug the dryer for 60 seconds or press POWER to clear the display, then press and hold START to restart the cycle and confirm whether PF was a one-time event.
2. **Check the circuit breaker.** Open the panel, confirm the dryer breaker is fully seated and not tripped, and feel for warmth at the breaker handle or bus connection that suggests a loose terminal.
3. **Inspect the power cord and plug.** Pull the dryer out, examine the cord for fraying or heat discoloration, and verify the plug seats firmly in the receptacle with no wiggle or arcing marks.
4. **Measure outlet voltage under load.** With the dryer plugged in and the cycle running, measure line-to-line voltage at the receptacle terminals (for a 240 V dryer) or line-to-neutral (for 120 V models). Voltage should stay within the normal range (120–125 V AC on 120 V circuits, 240–250 V AC on 240 V circuits). If you see low voltage (for example 108 V AC as in one documented repair), tighten receptacle terminals or call an electrician.
5. **Test for repeat failures.** Run a complete heat cycle and watch for PF. If the code does not return and voltage is stable, the original event was transient and no part replacement is needed.
6. **Replace the main control board if PF persists with verified good supply.** When breaker, cord, and outlet voltage are confirmed correct but PF recurs, the control board's internal power section is failing. Order the board by your dryer's model number (example part W10654005 for certain Whirlpool models), disconnect power, swap the board, and transfer wire connectors carefully.
7. **Document any utility events.** If brownouts or flickers are frequent in your area, consider a whole-house surge protector or ask your utility to inspect the service drop.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are not comfortable working inside the breaker panel, if voltage measurements are confusing, or if PF persists after you have verified supply voltage and replaced the cord. An appliance technician can load-test the outlet, scope the control-board power rails, and confirm the board is the fault before you buy one. Also call an electrician if the breaker trips repeatedly, if you find burn marks at the receptacle, or if measured voltage is far outside the normal range. House wiring faults that cause PF can pose a fire risk and require permit work in many jurisdictions.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–80 in parts (cord or outlet), 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–280 service call (includes diagnosis and part if board or outlet).
