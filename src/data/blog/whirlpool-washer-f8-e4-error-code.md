## Whirlpool F8 E4 Error Code — What It Means

The F8 E4 code on a Whirlpool dishwasher signals that the float switch in the drip tray (base pan) under the machine has detected water or foam. This is a protective system designed to alert you to leaks or overflow conditions before they damage your floor or cabinet.

The code does not point to a control board fault. Instead, it means liquid has accumulated in the pan below the tub, lifting the float and triggering the alarm. Common sources include a loose water inlet fitting, an improperly routed fill hose, excess suds from the wrong detergent, or a leak anywhere in the internal plumbing.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the float switch or even the control board when the real issue is simply a loose rubber washer at the inlet elbow or water sitting in the drip tray. Always inspect and reseat the inlet connections and empty the base pan before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Leak at the water inlet connection (~40%)** The rubber washer inside the 3/4 in. elbow fitting at the fill valve may be missing, damaged, or not seated, allowing water to drip into the base pan.
- **Water already sitting in the drip tray (~25%)** Any prior leak or overflow leaves water in the base pan, keeping the float switch lifted and the code active even after the source is fixed.
- **Fill hose routed incorrectly (~15%)** If the fill hose does not follow the rear-left routing specified by Whirlpool or contacts moving parts like the motor, it can chafe and develop pinhole leaks.
- **Excess suds from wrong detergent (~10%)** Using regular dish soap or too much automatic dishwasher detergent creates foam that spills into the base pan and triggers the float.
- **Loose or disconnected float switch connector (~7%)** The float switch wire harness can work loose during installation or service, causing intermittent triggering of the code.
- **Defective float switch (~3%)** The float switch itself can fail, though Whirlpool's published guidance treats this as less common than connection and leak issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and water.** Unplug the dishwasher or switch off the breaker, then close the shut-off valve under the sink or at the connection point.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward slightly** to access the base pan and rear connections. You do not need to fully remove it from the cabinet.
3. **Remove and empty the drip tray.** Press the plastic tray snaps inward and pull the tray toward you, leaving the float switch wire attached. Pour out any water and dry the tray.
4. **Inspect the water inlet elbow fitting** at the fill valve. Remove the 3/4 in. 90° elbow, check that the rubber washer is present and undamaged, then reinstall hand-tight and add 1/4 to 1/2 turn with pliers to seal the gasket.
5. **Verify fill hose routing.** Confirm the hose exits the rear left side of the unit and does not touch the motor, lower tub, or any moving parts that could chafe it.
6. **Check the float switch connector.** Align the connector halves and push firmly until the locking tab is visible over the back of the connector housing. Make sure the power cord does not contact the motor or tub.
7. **Clear the code.** Press Cancel once to silence the alarm, then press Cancel again to clear F8 E4 from the display.
8. **Run a short test cycle** and monitor the base pan. If the code returns, recheck all connections and hose routing or call for service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have tightened the inlet connections, emptied the drip tray, verified hose routing, and cleared the code but F8 E4 returns within one or two cycles. A technician can pressure-test internal hoses, check the float switch with a multimeter, and trace leaks from the pump, spray-arm manifold, or door gasket that are not visible from the base pan. Also call if you are uncomfortable working under the dishwasher or if the unit is still under warranty, since unauthorized repairs can void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $5-15 for a replacement inlet washer or elbow if needed, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 for a service call if no parts are needed, higher if a fill hose or float switch assembly must be replaced.
