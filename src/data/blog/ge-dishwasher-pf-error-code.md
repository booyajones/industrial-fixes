## GE Dishwasher PF Error Code — What It Means

The PF code on a GE dishwasher stands for power failure. It means the control board detected that supply power was interrupted or dropped out during operation. PF is a power-interruption event, not a wash-system fault code. By itself, PF does not prove a bad pump, heater, or drain system. It indicates the control saw a loss or instability of incoming power.

The code can appear after a brief utility outage, voltage flicker, tripped breaker, or loose connection anywhere in the supply path. It can also appear if the control board or display board falsely interprets an interruption due to corrosion or a loose internal connection. The dishwasher will not resume the cycle until you clear the code and restart.

## Before You Replace Anything

Do not replace the control board or display board until you have eliminated actual power-supply problems. Check the breaker, junction-box connections, and all harness plugs first. Many PF codes are caused by a loose wire nut or tripped breaker, not a failed board.

## Common Causes

- **Brief utility outage or voltage flicker (~35%)** The home power supply dropped momentarily and the control logged the event.
- **Tripped or weak breaker (~25%)** The circuit breaker tripped during the wash cycle or has a weak internal connection causing intermittent voltage loss.
- **Loose junction-box or cord connection (~20%)** Wire nuts or hardwire connections at the dishwasher's junction box are loose, corroded, or heat-damaged.
- **Intermittent house power drop (~10%)** Another heavy load on the same circuit (such as a microwave or disposal) causes voltage sag when it runs.
- **Control-board or display-board issue (~10%)** The board falsely interprets an interruption due to corroded solder joints, a loose board-mounted fuse, or poor harness seating.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press START/CANCEL** to clear the PF code and attempt to start a new wash cycle. If the code does not return and the cycle completes, the fault was a one-time power event and no repair is needed.
2. **Check the circuit breaker** in your home's service panel. Reset it fully by turning it all the way off and then back on, even if it does not appear tripped. Confirm the breaker is tight and not sharing a heavily loaded circuit.
3. **Remove the lower access panel** (typically two screws at the bottom of the door) and locate the junction box where house power enters the dishwasher. Shut off the breaker before opening the box.
4. **Inspect all wire nuts and connections** in the junction box for looseness, heat discoloration, corrosion, or moisture. Tighten or re-make any suspect connections. Check the power cord or hardwire leads for damage.
5. **Inspect internal harnesses** by removing the door panel or control console as needed. Check the door-latch connector, user-interface ribbon cable, and all board-mounted connectors for poor seating, corrosion, or bent pins. Reseat firmly.
6. **Test for intermittent voltage drop** by running the dishwasher while monitoring the circuit. If PF appears when another appliance on the same circuit starts, move the dishwasher to a dedicated circuit or remove the conflicting load.
7. **Replace the control board or display board** only if you have confirmed stable incoming power, tight connections, and the PF code still recurs without any real power event. Field reports point to corroded solder joints or loose board-mounted fuses as intermittent causes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the PF code returns repeatedly despite resetting the breaker and inspecting the junction box, or if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V household wiring. A pro can test for intermittent voltage at the control board, trace harness faults with a multimeter, and safely diagnose whether the issue is in the house wiring, the dishwasher's internal connections, or a failing control board. Also call if the breaker trips immediately upon reset, which may indicate a short circuit or water intrusion requiring troubleshooting beyond basic DIY scope.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 in parts, 15-45 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250.
