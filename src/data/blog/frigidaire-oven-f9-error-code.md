## Frigidaire Oven F9 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 error code on a Frigidaire oven typically indicates a problem with the oven door locking mechanism or the door switch circuit. This code appears most often on self-cleaning models where the door lock assembly is part of the safety system that prevents the door from opening during high-heat cleaning cycles.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary between Frigidaire oven models and production years, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for the precise definition on your unit. In general, the F9 code points to a failed door latch motor assembly, a misaligned door strike, a faulty door switch, or a wiring issue between the door lock components and the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board when they see F9, but the door latch assembly and door switch are far more common failures. Test the door switch for continuity and listen for the latch motor before spending money on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Door latch motor assembly failure (~50%)** The motor that drives the door lock mechanism wears out or stalls, preventing the lock from engaging or releasing and triggering the F9 code.
- **Faulty door switch (~25%)** The micro-switch that signals door position to the control board can fail closed or open, causing the board to throw an error.
- **Misaligned door latch striker (~10%)** The metal striker plate on the oven door can shift or bend, preventing the latch from seating correctly and completing the circuit.
- **Wiring or connector problem (~10%)** Loose, corroded, or damaged wires between the door lock assembly and the control board interrupt the signal and generate the code.
- **Electronic control board fault (~5%)** The main control board itself can develop a failed relay or circuit that reads the door lock status incorrectly, though this is less common.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor is trying to work, so check the door striker alignment and the door switch for proper operation.<br><strong>No:</strong> The latch motor is likely dead or the wiring to it is broken, so inspect the connector and test the motor for power.</div>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error clear after a two-minute power reset at the breaker?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The issue may be intermittent wiring or a temporary glitch, so watch for recurrence and inspect connectors.<br><strong>No:</strong> The problem is consistent, pointing to a failed latch assembly, switch, or wiring that needs replacement.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you manually move the door latch mechanism by hand and does it return smoothly?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The mechanical parts are free, so focus on electrical components like the switch and motor.<br><strong>No:</strong> The latch is binding or broken, so replace the entire door lock assembly.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven to eliminate shock risk.
2. **Remove the oven door** by opening it fully, flipping the hinge locks forward, and lifting the door up and out of the hinge slots.
3. **Access the door lock assembly** by removing the screws securing the top panel or rear access panel, depending on your model layout.
4. **Unplug the wiring harness** from the door lock motor assembly and door switch, noting wire colors and positions for reassembly.
5. **Test the door switch** with a multimeter set to continuity mode to confirm it opens and closes properly when actuated.
6. **Remove the old latch assembly** by taking out the mounting screws and sliding the unit free from the oven frame.
7. **Install the new door lock assembly** in the same position, secure with screws, reconnect the wiring harness, and reinstall any panels and the door, then restore power and test a clean cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt appliance wiring or if the error persists after replacing the door lock assembly and switch. A technician can use diagnostic mode to read fault history on the control board and test the high-voltage circuits safely. Also call a pro if the control board itself needs replacement, since that repair often requires programming or recalibration specific to your oven model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
