## EcoSmart ECO 23 Error Codes — What It Means

The EcoSmart ECO 23 is a tankless water heater that displays error codes when it detects a fault. The most common codes are E1 (inlet thermistor problem), E2 (outlet thermistor problem), and E5 (insufficient temperature rise or flow rate issue). These codes tell you which sensor or performance condition is preventing the heater from working correctly.

An E1 or E2 code means the unit cannot read water temperature at the inlet or outlet. An E5 code means water is flowing too fast through the heater for it to raise the temperature enough, or the incoming water is too cold for the unit's capacity at that flow rate. Low supply voltage or incorrect wiring can also prevent the heater from delivering rated performance.

## Common Causes

- **Failed inlet thermistor** The E1 code indicates the inlet temperature sensor has failed or its wiring is disconnected.
- **Failed outlet thermistor** The E2 code indicates the outlet temperature sensor has failed or has a wiring fault.
- **Excessive water flow rate** Water moving too fast through the unit prevents adequate temperature rise and triggers the E5 code.
- **Low supply voltage** The heater requires at least 208 volts to deliver rated heating output, and low voltage reduces performance.
- **Incorrect installation wiring** Crossed wires or improper electrical connections on new installs prevent correct operation.
- **Cold water mixing at fixtures** Anti-scald valves or tempering valves downstream blend cold water back in and reduce outlet temperature.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the displayed error code** on the unit's digital display and write it down before beginning any repair work.
2. **For E1 (inlet thermistor)**, inspect the inlet sensor and its wiring for loose connections or visible damage, then consult the EcoSmart troubleshooting guide for your model before replacing the sensor.
3. **For E2 (outlet thermistor)**, check the outlet sensor and wiring for faults and follow the manufacturer's reset and diagnostic steps in the EcoSmart guide.
4. **For E5 (insufficient temperature rise)**, reduce water flow at the fixture or slightly close the inlet shutoff valve so the heater has time to raise the water temperature adequately.
5. **Verify incoming water temperature and flow rate** match your model's capacity table, because very cold inlet water or high flow exceeds the unit's ability to heat.
6. **Check electrical supply voltage** with a multimeter at the heater terminals to confirm at least 208 volts, and have an electrician verify breaker size and wire gauge if voltage is low.
7. **Inspect fixture mixing valves** and any tempering or anti-scald valves that may be blending cold water back into the hot line and reducing the temperature you feel at the tap.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician if you find low supply voltage, incorrect breaker sizing, or crossed wires, because tankless heaters draw heavy current and require correct electrical installation. Call a plumber or EcoSmart-certified technician if you have replaced thermistors according to the troubleshooting guide and the error persists, or if you continue to get E5 codes after adjusting flow and verifying inlet temperature. Any work inside the heater's electrical enclosure or replacement of heating elements should be done by a qualified technician to avoid shock hazards and to protect your warranty.
