## Maytag Dishwasher F1E1 Error — What It Means

The F1E1 code is a drain-sequence fault. Your Maytag dishwasher begins the drain sequence normally, but then stops and will not continue operating once F1E1 appears on the display. The manufacturer's guidance points to issues related to drain installation, siphoning, water supply, or a stuck overfill protection float.

The code is not a simple clog. It signals that the dishwasher started to drain but encountered a condition that prevents it from moving forward in the wash cycle. The most common root cause is incorrect drain hose installation that allows water to siphon out of the tub, confusing the control into thinking the unit is still draining or has a fill problem.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or drain pump first. Before ordering parts, check the drain hose loop height (must be at least 20 inches above the floor) and verify the float moves freely. These simple installation checks fix most F1E1 faults at no cost.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose siphoning (~50%)** The drain hose is not looped at least 20 inches above the floor or is connected below the drain trap, allowing water to siphon out continuously and triggering the fault.
- **Overfill protection float restricted (~25%)** Debris or detergent buildup prevents the float (usually in the front-right corner of the tub) from moving freely, signaling a false overfill condition that stops the cycle.
- **Water supply valve turned off (~10%)** The dishwasher cannot fill properly if the water supply shutoff valve under the sink is closed or partially closed, causing the control to halt the cycle.
- **Control board glitch (~10%)** A transient electronic fault in the control stores the error code even after the underlying condition is resolved, requiring a power reset to clear.
- **Drain system blockage or pump issue (~5%)** A partial blockage in the drain sump, pump, or hose can prevent proper draining and trigger the fault, though this is less common than siphoning or float problems.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press Cancel once** to silence the alarm and stop the error beeping.
2. **Turn off power** to the dishwasher at the breaker or unplug it, and turn off the water supply valve under the sink if accessible.
3. **Inspect the drain hose routing** under the sink. Verify that the hose connects to the waste tee or garbage disposer inlet above the drain trap and that the hose loops at least 20 inches above the floor or subfloor. If the loop is too low or the connection is below the trap, re-route and secure the hose to meet this height requirement.
4. **Check the overfill protection float** inside the dishwasher tub (typically in the front-right corner). Remove any food debris, detergent residue, or obstructions, and confirm the float moves freely up and down.
5. **Verify the water supply valve** under the sink is fully open. If it was closed or partially closed, open it completely.
6. **Reset the control** by leaving power disconnected for at least 30 seconds, then restore power.
7. **Run a short test cycle** (rinse or light wash) to see if the F1E1 code returns. If the code is gone, the issue is resolved. If it returns, inspect the drain sump, pump, and hoses for blockages or call a technician to test the control board and drain components.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have corrected the drain hose loop height to at least 20 inches above the floor, cleaned and freed the overfill float, verified the water supply is on, and completed a 30-second power reset but the F1E1 code returns on the next cycle. A technician can test for siphon behavior with instruments, inspect the drain pump and check valve for internal faults, and verify control board operation. Also call if you are uncomfortable working under the sink or inside the dishwasher tub, or if the unit is still under warranty and you want factory-authorized service.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-30 if only adjusting hose or cleaning float; $40-80 and 30-60 min if replacing drain hose. A pro service call runs about $150-250 for a service call to correct drain installation or replace float assembly.
