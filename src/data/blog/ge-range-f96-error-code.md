## GE Range F96 Error Code — What It Means

F96 on a GE range or double oven is a fan speed feedback fault. The control board is not receiving the expected signal from the upper oven cooling fan assembly. The fan uses hall sensors or a small sensor board to report motor speed back to the control, and when that feedback is missing or wrong the board throws F96. The fault can be caused by a failed blower motor, a bad sensor, a loose connector, or rarely a control board issue. The code typically appears on double-oven models where the upper cavity has its own dedicated cooling blower.

Because the exact definition can vary across GE's many product lines, always verify the code meaning in your model's service manual or wiring diagram. The repair behavior described here matches the workflows shown in GE service documentation and parts diagrams for the majority of double-oven ranges reporting F96.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some owners replace the control board first when the real problem is a seized or intermittent cooling fan motor. Before ordering a board, pull the back panel and listen to the fan during a test cycle or spin the blower by hand to check for binding or noise.

## Common Causes

- **Failed cooling fan or blower motor (~60%)** The upper oven blower motor is seized, intermittent, or no longer spinning at the speed the control expects, so the hall sensor cannot report valid feedback.
- **Bad fan speed sensor or hall sensor (~20%)** The motor still runs but the small sensor board or hall-effect sensor attached to the fan assembly has failed and cannot send speed data to the control.
- **Loose or corroded wiring and connectors (~10%)** The quick-disconnect harness to the fan, the ground wire, or a plug at the control board is loose, corroded, or making intermittent contact.
- **Missing or poor ground connection (~5%)** The fan assembly ground wire was not reconnected during a previous repair or has broken free from its mounting point, preventing proper sensor feedback.
- **Control board issue (~5%)** The main range control board has a failed input circuit for fan feedback, though this is much less common than fan or sensor failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely before starting any work.
2. **Remove the rear access panel or trim** to expose the upper oven cooling fan assembly, which is typically mounted on the back wall of the oven cavity or behind the control area.
3. **Inspect the fan harness, ground wire, and quick-disconnect plugs** for corrosion, loose connections, or damage, and reseat every connector firmly.
4. **Test the fan motor** by reconnecting power briefly and listening for operation, or use a multimeter to check for voltage at the fan connector and continuity across the motor windings when power is off.
5. **Order the correct replacement fan assembly** using your range's full model number and the GE parts diagram, because fan designs and connectors vary widely between product lines.
6. **Transfer the fan sensor or sensor board** to the new blower if the replacement design requires it, using a Torx T15H bit or the appropriate driver to remove and reinstall the sensor securely.
7. **Reinstall the fan assembly, reconnect all harnesses and the ground wire, and test the oven** before putting the back panel on, so you do not have to disassemble again if the code persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt appliance wiring, if the back panel and fan assembly are difficult to access on your built-in or slide-in range, or if the F96 code returns after you have replaced the fan and verified all connections. A pro can perform voltage and continuity tests on the fan circuit and control board inputs to pinpoint the fault without guessing at expensive parts. Also call for help if your range is still under warranty or a service plan, because unauthorized repairs can void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–180 in parts (fan assembly), 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200–400 including service call and parts.
