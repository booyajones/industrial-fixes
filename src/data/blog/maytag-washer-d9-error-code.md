## What this code means
The d9 code on Maytag washers indicates that the control board has detected incoming AC voltage below the operating threshold. One common specification cited is voltage below 90 VAC held for at least 8 seconds, though this detail comes from field sources rather than official Maytag documentation and may vary by model. The washer will not start or will halt mid-cycle to protect the motor and electronics from damage caused by undervoltage conditions.

This fault can be triggered by problems outside the machine (house power issues, outlet problems, or a weak breaker) or by internal electrical faults in the washer's power-supply path. Always verify that the washer is actually receiving stable voltage before replacing any internal parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the user interface control (UIC) or main control board first. Always measure the outlet voltage under load with a multimeter before replacing any boards. A loose neutral or outlet problem is far cheaper to fix than a board you did not need.

## Common Causes

- **House power sag or brownout** Temporary utility voltage dip, heavily loaded circuit, or a weak breaker can drop incoming voltage below the 90 VAC threshold and trigger the fault.
- **Loose or damaged power cord or plug** Intermittent contact at the plug blades, a burned receptacle, or frayed cord insulation creates voltage drop at the washer inlet.
- **Weak or faulty outlet or household wiring** A worn receptacle, loose neutral connection, or undersized branch wiring upstream of the washer delivers unstable voltage.
- **Internal harness connection problem** Loose or corroded wire harness between the RFI filter and transformer can cause intermittent voltage sensing on the control side.
- **Failed user interface control (UIC) or power-supply circuit** If supply voltage is verified good and all connections are solid, the control board itself may be misreading or failing to regulate internal voltage correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the washer or switching off the breaker, then wait one full minute to reset the control.
2. **Measure outlet voltage** with a digital multimeter set to AC volts. Plug the washer back in, start a cycle, and confirm the outlet delivers at least 110 VAC under load. If voltage is below 100 VAC, stop and call an electrician to fix the house wiring or circuit.
3. **Inspect the power cord and plug** for burned blade contacts, melted insulation, or looseness at the washer inlet and the wall receptacle. Replace the cord or receptacle if you find any damage or discoloration.
4. **Check internal harness connections** by removing the washer top or rear panel and inspecting the wire connector between the RFI filter (near the cord inlet) and the transformer. Reseat any loose connectors and look for corrosion or charring.
5. **Power-cycle and test** by plugging the washer back in and running a short cycle. If the d9 code does not return and voltage stays stable, the problem is resolved.
6. **Replace the user interface control (UIC)** if supply voltage is confirmed good, all connections are solid, and the fault persists. Consult your model's service manual for the correct part number and harness routing.
7. **Document your measurements** and any parts replaced so a service technician has a clear starting point if the code returns after your repairs.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician if your outlet voltage measures below 100 VAC under load, if you see any burning or melting at the receptacle, or if you are uncomfortable working inside your breaker panel. Call an appliance technician if you have verified stable house voltage and inspected all accessible harness connections but the d9 code still returns. Professionals have model-specific wiring diagrams and can safely trace the power-supply path from the RFI filter through the transformer to the control boards. Do not replace the UIC or any other internal board until you are certain the house power is clean and stable, or you risk destroying a new part the moment you plug it in.
