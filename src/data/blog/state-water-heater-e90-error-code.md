## State Water Heater E90 Error Code — What It Means

E90 is not a verified State Water Heaters code in manufacturer documentation. If you see E90 on your State-branded unit, the display may belong to a different OEM platform or the model is misidentified. In tankless gas water heater systems, E90 indicates a combustion abnormality where the unit cannot achieve the correct air-fuel mixture for proper combustion. The heater may struggle to move air in or out, fail to reach set temperature, or shut down repeatedly. This code appears when the control board detects poor combustion performance, whether from restricted airflow, inadequate gas delivery, or incorrect installation parameters.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted air intake** Lint, debris, leaves, or snow blocking the external intake termination or internal air filter prevents adequate combustion air from reaching the burner.
- **Insufficient gas supply pressure** Undersized gas piping, low regulator output, or shared gas lines with other appliances starve the burner of fuel and cause incomplete combustion.
- **Incorrect or obstructed venting** Improperly sized vent pipes, wrong materials, excessive length, or blocked exhaust terminations prevent combustion gases from exiting and fresh air from entering.
- **Dirty burner or heat exchanger** Buildup of soot, scale, or combustion deposits on the burner assembly or heat exchanger fins restricts airflow and reduces combustion efficiency.
- **Wrong DIP switch settings** Control board altitude or vent configuration switches not set to match the actual installation parameters cause the unit to run outside design specs.
- **Fouled combustion fan** Dust, lint, or corrosion on the fan blades or motor reduce fan speed and prevent the unit from pulling or pushing the required combustion air volume.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model and brand** by checking the rating plate on the water heater to confirm you have a State unit and note the exact model number, since E90 is not confirmed in State documentation.
2. **Inspect the air intake termination** outside the building for blockages such as leaves, snow, lint, or bird nests, and clear any obstructions from the intake pipe opening.
3. **Check the exhaust termination** for blockages or restrictions, and remove any debris that could prevent combustion gases from venting properly.
4. **Remove the front cover** and locate the internal air filter or screen near the blower intake, then clean or replace it if clogged with dust or lint.
5. **Check gas supply pressure** at the unit inlet using a manometer while the heater is firing, and verify that upstream gas piping is properly sized and the gas valve is fully open.
6. **Review the venting installation** against the manufacturer's installation manual to confirm pipe material, diameter, length, and number of elbows match approved configurations for your model.
7. **Check DIP switch settings** on the control board and compare them to the installation chart in the manual for your elevation and vent pipe setup, adjusting only if incorrect.
8. **Inspect internal components** including the burner assembly, combustion fan blades, and heat exchanger fins for soot or scale buildup, and clean as needed following manufacturer procedures.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot verify the model or if E90 persists after clearing intake and exhaust blockages. Gas pressure testing, internal burner and heat exchanger cleaning, and venting corrections require specialized tools and knowledge of combustion safety. If the unit is newly installed and throwing E90, the installation may not meet code or manufacturer specifications and must be inspected by a qualified installer. Do not attempt to adjust gas pressures, modify venting, or disassemble the burner or heat exchanger without proper training, as improper work can create carbon monoxide hazards or void warranties.
