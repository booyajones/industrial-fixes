## Weil-McLain Boiler A109 Error Code — What It Means

The A109 code is a lockout or fault indication displayed on Weil-McLain boilers. The exact meaning is not standardized across all Weil-McLain models and control platforms. Weil-McLain boilers display error numbers on the temperature display, and each code corresponds to a specific fault listed in the service manual for that model family. Without knowing your exact boiler model and control type, the A109 code could indicate a flame failure, ignition fault, temperature limit, sensor error, or airflow problem.

Because Weil-McLain uses different control boards and fault tables across their product line, you must consult your boiler's service manual or access the contractor diagnostics menu to see the full fault history and match A109 to its specific definition for your unit. Common lockout triggers on Weil-McLain boilers include loss of flame during startup, flame-sensor problems, gas supply issues, vent or air-intake obstruction, and high-temperature shutdowns caused by poor circulation or low system pressure.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the gas valve or igniter before confirming the fault. Pull the boiler's fault history from the diagnostics menu and verify gas pressure, flame-sensor condition, and system flow before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Flame loss or ignition failure (~30%)** Burner flame lights and then drops out, or ignition does not occur reliably during startup.
- **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor (~25%)** The flame-sensing electrode is coated with soot or positioned incorrectly, preventing reliable flame detection.
- **Airflow or vent obstruction (~20%)** Flue or intake is blocked by condensation, debris, ice, or a bird nest, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Weak gas supply or gas valve fault (~15%)** Gas pressure is too low or the gas valve does not open fully, preventing proper ignition.
- **Circulation or high-temperature fault (~10%)** The primary circulator is not running or system flow is poor, causing rapid temperature rise and an overtemperature lockout.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control platform.** Write down the model number from the rating plate so you can reference the correct service manual and fault-code table.
2. **Access the contractor diagnostics menu** (if available) to view the full fault history and confirm the exact definition of A109 for your model.
3. **Check boiler system pressure.** If the gauge reads below normal operating range (typically 12–25 psi when cold), add water through the fill valve until pressure is in the normal zone.
4. **Inspect the vent and air intake.** Walk outside and confirm the termination is clear of ice, snow, bird nests, or other blockage. Remove any obstruction and make sure condensate drains freely.
5. **Verify gas supply.** Confirm the manual gas shutoff valve upstream of the boiler is fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are working.
6. **Attempt a single reset.** Press the reset button on the control or power-cycle the boiler. Observe whether the burner ignites cleanly and remains lit through a full heating cycle.
7. **If the fault returns, call a qualified technician.** The technician will pull fault history, test manifold and gas pressure, inspect and clean the flame sensor and igniter, verify circulator operation and flow, and check all temperature and limit sensors against the service manual specifications.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional whenever an A109 lockout persists after you have confirmed system pressure, cleared vent obstructions, and verified gas supply. Boiler diagnostics require access to the service menu, a combustion analyzer, and gas-pressure measurement tools. Technicians can pull the full fault history to pinpoint whether the code stems from ignition failure, flame sensing, temperature rise, or a control-board issue. Because the work involves natural gas, electrical controls, and high-temperature components, and because misdiagnosis can lead to unsafe operation or expensive part swaps, professional diagnosis is the safe and cost-effective path once basic checks are complete.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
