## Weil-McLain A97 Error Code — What It Means

Weil-McLain boilers do not have an official error code labeled A97. The digits that sometimes appear as 9 or 10 on the display are not fault codes but indicators that the boiler just completed a heating cycle and is in post-purge, showing a temperature delta of around 9 to 10 °F. When homeowners report A97, they are often describing a flame-loss or ignition lockout condition where the burner lights but cannot maintain the flame and shuts down.

A flame-loss lockout means the control board detected that the burner ignited but the flame sensor did not see a stable flame signal, so the system locked out for safety. This happens when the flame sensor is dirty or misaligned, gas flow is weak or intermittent, the burner or pilot is carboned, venting is blocked, or grounding is poor due to a melted burner gasket (a known issue on the WM97+ 155 model).

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or igniter when the real issue is a dirty flame sensor or low gas pressure. Clean the flame rod with fine steel wool and verify gas pressure before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or carboned flame sensor (~40%)** Carbon buildup on the flame rod prevents the control from detecting flame even when gas is burning, triggering a lockout.
- **Weak or intermittent gas flow (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, a failing regulator, or undersized piping starves the burner and causes flame loss.
- **Dirty pilot or burner (~15%)** Carbon, dust, or debris blocking the flame path prevents stable combustion and triggers a flame-loss shutdown.
- **Vent blockage or condensation (~10%)** Bird nests, ice, debris in intake or exhaust vents, or water pooling in the flue pipe disrupts airflow and flame stability.
- **Melted burner gasket (WM97+ 155) (~10%)** A known design flaw on the largest WM97 model where the gasket melts and disrupts grounding, degrading flame-signal detection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the boiler at the service switches and gas shutoff valve before opening any panels.
2. **Check the pressure gauge** on the boiler and confirm it reads between 12 and 25 psi. If below 12 psi, open the fill valve slowly to raise pressure to about 15 psi.
3. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside the home for bird nests, ice, snow, or debris and remove any blockages.
4. **Open the boiler jacket** and locate the burner assembly, flame sensor (a small metal rod near the burner), and ignition system.
5. **Remove the flame sensor** and clean it thoroughly with fine steel wool to remove carbon and soot, then reinstall it in proper alignment so the rod sits in the flame path.
6. **Inspect the burner and pilot** for carbon buildup, dust, or debris and clean with a soft brush or compressed air.
7. **Check the burner gasket** (especially on WM97+ 155 models) for melting or damage. If melted, replace it and verify proper grounding of the burner area.
8. **Restore power and gas**, then reset the boiler and observe the ignition cycle. Listen for igniter spark, watch for a stable flame, and confirm the boiler continues to run without locking out.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot access or clean the flame sensor safely, or if the boiler continues to lock out after cleaning the sensor and clearing vents. A technician should also handle any work involving the gas valve, gas pressure testing, control board diagnostics, or vent system repairs. On WM97+ 155 models with a known gasket-melting issue, a pro can inspect grounding, replace the gasket correctly, and verify flame-signal integrity to prevent repeat lockouts.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
