## What this code means
E29 on a Bosch washer signals an abnormal or insufficient water fill. The control system has detected that the tub is not filling as expected or the fill rate is too low for the cycle to proceed. In practical terms, the machine is not receiving the water it needs during the inlet phase, and the cycle will halt until the problem is corrected.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the inlet valve or pressure switch first. Before ordering parts, check that the water supply is fully open, the inlet hose is not kinked, and the filter screen at the hose connection is clean. These simple checks solve most E29 codes and cost nothing.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screen (~35%)** Sediment, rust, or scale builds up in the fine mesh strainer at the hose-to-washer connection and restricts flow.
- **Kinked or blocked inlet hose (~25%)** The fill hose is twisted, crushed behind the machine, or has internal debris that slows the water stream.
- **Water supply tap not fully open or low pressure (~20%)** The service valve is partially closed or household supply pressure is too low to fill at the required rate.
- **Faulty pressure switch or sensing hose (~10%)** The water-level sensor does not register the fill correctly, or its small air hose is disconnected or blocked.
- **Aquastop system failure (~5%)** The integrated leak-safety valve or Aquastop hose assembly has tripped or failed and stops water entry.
- **Defective inlet valve solenoid (~5%)** The solenoid coil or mechanical valve body no longer opens fully to admit water when energized.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the water supply** at the tap behind the washer and unplug the machine from the wall outlet.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the back panel and the inlet hose connections.
3. **Disconnect the inlet hose** at the washer by turning the threaded collar counterclockwise, then inspect the small mesh filter screen inside the washer's inlet port.
4. **Clean the filter screen** with a toothbrush under running water to remove sediment, rust particles, or scale buildup, then reinstall it firmly.
5. **Inspect the inlet hose** for kinks, twists, or internal blockage. If you suspect a clog, disconnect the hose at both ends and flush it with a garden hose or replace it.
6. **Check the Aquastop system** if your model uses one. Look for signs that the safety base has filled with water (indicating a trip) or that the integrated valve is mechanically stuck. Replace the Aquastop hose assembly if it has activated or is defective.
7. **Access the pressure switch** by removing the washer's top cover (usually two screws at the back). Locate the round pressure switch and verify that its small clear air hose is firmly seated on both ends and not cracked or clogged. Reseat or replace the hose if necessary.
8. **Test the inlet valve electrically** if all upstream checks pass. Disconnect power, remove the valve from the back panel, and use a multimeter to check continuity across each solenoid coil (consult your model's wiring diagram for expected resistance). Replace the valve if the coils are open or if the valve does not open mechanically when energized.
9. **Reconnect everything**, restore water and power, and run a short fill cycle to confirm that the washer fills normally and the E29 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the filter, checked the hose and supply, and reseated the pressure-switch air line but the E29 code persists. A technician can perform electrical tests on the inlet valve and pressure switch, inspect the Aquastop assembly for hidden faults, and trace wiring and control-board signals that a homeowner cannot safely reach. Also call if you are uncomfortable working behind the machine, lifting the top cover, or using a multimeter to test electrical components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (if valve or hose needed), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 typical service call.
