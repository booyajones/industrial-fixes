## Daikin E04 Error Code — What It Means

The E04 error code on a Daikin mini split indicates that the low pressure switch has activated due to an abnormal drop in refrigerant system pressure. Daikin's service documentation describes this as an actuation of the low pressure switch (LPS) that protects the compressor from running under unsafe conditions. On systems that display subcodes, E04-01 specifically refers to the master outdoor unit low pressure switch activation. The protection trips when refrigerant pressure falls below the safe operating threshold, which prevents compressor damage but requires troubleshooting to identify and repair the underlying refrigerant circuit problem.

## Common Causes

- **Low refrigerant charge or leak** The most common cause is refrigerant escaping through leaks at flare joints, brazed connections, service valves, or coil pinholes, which drops system pressure below the safe threshold.
- **Restricted expansion valve** A blocked or malfunctioning expansion valve restricts refrigerant flow and causes abnormally low suction pressure that triggers the low pressure switch.
- **Clogged filter drier** A restricted filter drier in the refrigerant circuit creates a flow bottleneck that starves the evaporator and drops suction pressure.
- **Unopened or partially closed service valve** Service valves left closed or not fully opened during installation or service restrict refrigerant flow and cause low pressure conditions.
- **Faulty low pressure switch or sensor** The low pressure switch itself can fail or drift out of calibration and falsely trigger the E04 code even when refrigerant pressure is normal.
- **Piping blockage or restriction** Crimped copper lines, debris in the refrigerant circuit, or foreign material in the piping can block flow and create localized low pressure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the error code** by entering inspection mode on the indoor controller according to your model's procedure (typically holding a button until the display blinks, then scrolling until a long beep confirms the code).
2. **Connect a refrigerant manifold gauge set** to the service ports and measure suction and discharge pressures with the system running to verify whether refrigerant pressure is actually low or if the switch is faulty.
3. **Inspect all refrigerant connections for leaks** by checking flare fittings, brazed joints, service valves, and coils for oil staining or using electronic leak detection, since refrigerant loss is the most common root cause.
4. **Check for flow restrictions** by inspecting the expansion valve, filter drier, and refrigerant piping for blockages or crimped lines if pressures are low but no leak is immediately found.
5. **Repair any identified leaks** by re-flaring connections, re-brazing joints, or replacing damaged coil sections before attempting to recharge the system.
6. **Evacuate the system with a vacuum pump** to remove air and moisture, then recharge to the exact refrigerant mass specified on the outdoor unit nameplate using a weighing scale (never guess the charge amount).
7. **Reset the system and monitor operation** by clearing the error code, running the unit through a full cooling cycle, and verifying that pressures remain stable and the E04 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

E04 low pressure errors require refrigerant circuit diagnosis and repair that involves EPA-certified handling of refrigerants, vacuum pumps, manifold gauges, leak detection equipment, and precise charging by weight. Homeowners should call a licensed HVAC technician for this code. Attempting refrigerant work without proper certification, tools, and training risks compressor damage, personal injury from high-pressure refrigerant, environmental violations, and voiding manufacturer warranties. A qualified technician can accurately diagnose whether the problem is a leak, restriction, valve issue, or failed sensor and perform the necessary evacuation and recharge to manufacturer specifications.
