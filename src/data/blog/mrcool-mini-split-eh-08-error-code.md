## MRCOOL Mini Split EH 08 Error — What It Means

The EH 08 error code on MRCOOL mini split systems indicates a fault with the electric auxiliary heating element or its control circuit. This code appears when the system detects the electric heater is not operating correctly, whether due to a failed component, broken wiring, or sensor issue.

Because specific MRCOOL documentation for this exact code is limited, the precise meaning may vary slightly by model. Consult your installation manual or the wiring diagram on the indoor unit for model-specific troubleshooting tables. The code typically points to the electric heater circuit rather than the refrigerant heat pump itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire control board when only the heater relay has failed. Test the relay coil and contacts with a multimeter before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heater relay or contactor (~35%)** The relay that energizes the electric heating element can stick open or burn out, preventing the heater from turning on when called.
- **Defective temperature sensor (~25%)** A faulty thermistor or temperature sensor feeding incorrect data to the board can trigger a heater fault code.
- **Broken heater element (~20%)** The electric heating coil itself can develop an open circuit or internal short, causing the control board to register an error.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~15%)** Wires connecting the control board to the heater or relay can break, corrode, or vibrate loose over time.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The main PCB may have failed circuitry in the heater-control section, even when other functions still work.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and the disconnect switch near the indoor and outdoor units.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the indoor air handler to access the control board and heater assembly.
3. **Locate the heater relay** on or near the control board. Visually inspect for burn marks, corrosion, or loose wire terminals.
4. **Test the relay coil** with a multimeter set to resistance mode. A good coil typically reads a few hundred ohms. An open coil or very high resistance indicates relay failure.
5. **Check continuity of the heater element** by disconnecting its wires and measuring across the element terminals. No continuity means the element is open.
6. **Inspect the temperature sensor** (thermistor) mounted near the indoor coil. Measure its resistance and compare to the temperature-resistance chart in your service manual.
7. **Replace the failed component** (relay, heater, or sensor) and reconnect all wiring securely. Double-check wire routing against the diagram on the unit.
8. **Restore power**, clear the error code via the remote or control panel, and run a heating cycle to verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Electric heater circuits in mini splits operate at line voltage (typically 208-240 V) and require careful measurement and part replacement. If you are not comfortable working with high-voltage wiring or do not own a multimeter, call a licensed HVAC technician. A pro can also verify refrigerant charge and heat-pump operation to confirm the auxiliary heater is truly the only issue. Because MRCOOL systems may have warranty coverage, contact the manufacturer or your installing dealer before opening the unit if it is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $180-350.
