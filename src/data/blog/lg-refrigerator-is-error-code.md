## LG Refrigerator IS Error Code — What It Means

The IS or 1S error code on an LG refrigerator indicates a problem with the ice maker sensor circuit or, depending on the model, a fault with the ice maker fan motor. LG's support materials describe this as either a failed or disconnected ice maker sensor or an ice maker fan motor issue. In practice, the control board is detecting an open sensor signal, a loose connection, or an abnormal condition in the ice maker fan circuit.

Because LG uses the same code for two related but distinct components (the sensor and the fan motor), the exact meaning varies slightly by model. On most units the code points to the ice maker sensor itself. On others it can also flag a fan motor that is seized by ice buildup or mechanically obstructed. Always verify whether your display shows IS or 1S, though both typically refer to the same underlying issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire ice maker assembly when only the sensor or its wiring connector is loose. Inspect the sensor harness connection and check for ice buildup around the fan before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Disconnected or failed ice maker sensor (~50%)** The sensor plug may be loose, the connector terminals corroded, or the sensor itself electrically open, all of which trigger the IS code.
- **Ice or frost buildup around the ice maker fan (~25%)** LG identifies ice accumulation as a common cause of fan-motor-related codes because the fan blades can lock or draw abnormal current.
- **Loose, damaged, or broken wiring harness (~15%)** Wiring between the control board and the sensor or fan can be pinched, chafed, or pulled loose during door servicing or food-load shifts.
- **Ice maker fan motor failure (~10%)** The fan motor itself may have seized bearings, a shorted winding, or mechanical obstruction that prevents normal operation and raises the code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code display.** Confirm whether the panel shows IS or 1S and consult your model's user manual or LG's online support to confirm which component (sensor or fan motor) is flagged for your specific refrigerator.
2. **Power-cycle the refrigerator.** Unplug the unit or trip its dedicated circuit breaker, wait five minutes, then restore power. LG recommends this reset for fan-motor-related codes to clear transient faults.
3. **Inspect for ice or frost buildup.** Open the ice maker compartment and look for ice coating the fan blades, evaporator, or sensor area. If present, leave the refrigerator doors open long enough to fully melt the ice (this can take several hours), then close the doors and power on.
4. **Check the ice maker sensor connection.** Locate the sensor wiring harness near the ice maker assembly. Disconnect and re-seat the plug, inspect the terminals for corrosion or moisture, and verify that the wires are not pinched or cut.
5. **Test the ice maker fan (if applicable).** If your model ties the IS code to the fan motor, listen for fan noise or check that the fan spins freely by hand (with power off). A seized or noisy fan indicates motor or bearing failure.
6. **Replace the faulty component.** If the sensor connector is secure, wiring is intact, and no ice is present but the code persists, replace the ice maker sensor. If the fan does not run or is mechanically bound, replace the ice maker fan motor assembly.
7. **Re-test after repair.** Clear the error code (if your panel has a reset function) or power-cycle again. Confirm that ice production resumes and the IS or 1S code does not return within 24 hours.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the refrigerator's ice maker compartment, if the wiring harness is damaged and requires tracing back to the main control board, or if the code persists after you have replaced both the sensor and checked for ice buildup. A technician has model-specific service manuals with resistance and voltage specifications for the sensor and fan circuits, and can perform board-level diagnostics if the control board itself is misreading a healthy sensor. Also call if repeated manual defrosts are needed, which may indicate a defrost-system fault rather than a simple sensor or fan issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25–75 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
