## Daikin A6 Error Code — What It Means

The A6 error code on a Daikin heat pump or air conditioner indicates a fan motor malfunction in the indoor unit. According to Daikin's published error code charts for Europe and India, this code appears when the system detects that the indoor blower motor is not operating correctly. The fault prevents proper airflow through the evaporator coil, so the unit will shut down or fail to start until the problem is resolved.

## Common Causes

- **Broken wires or disconnected connectors** Daikin Europe documentation lists broken wires, short circuits, or disconnected connectors in the fan motor harness as primary causes of A6.
- **Jammed or obstructed blower wheel** Dust, debris, or mechanical binding can prevent the cross-flow fan from spinning freely and trigger the motor fault code.
- **Failed fan motor windings or electronics** Internal damage to the motor's windings, capacitor, or electronic commutation circuit will stop the motor from starting or running.
- **Defective motor feedback sensor** Models with electronically commutated motors use hall sensors or other feedback devices that can fail and report a motor fault even if the motor itself is intact.
- **Indoor PCB drive circuit failure** If the control board does not send proper voltage or control signals to the motor, the fan will not run and the board will log an A6 error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Power off the unit at the breaker, wait 60 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the A6 code returns. A persistent code confirms an active fault.
2. Remove the indoor unit's front cover and filter. Spin the blower wheel by hand to check for smooth rotation and listen or feel for grinding, binding, or debris.
3. Inspect the wiring harness and connectors at the fan motor. Look for loose plugs, corroded pins, damaged insulation, or burnt wire strands.
4. Use a multimeter to measure DC voltage at the motor connector when the unit attempts to run the fan. Compare readings to your model's service manual, if available, to verify proper supply and control signals.
5. Test the fan motor by disconnecting it and measuring winding resistance or applying the rated supply voltage directly, if safe to do so, to confirm the motor itself is functional.
6. Check the indoor control board if the motor tests good but receives no drive signal. Look for burnt components, damaged traces, or relay failures on the PCB.
7. Replace the defective component (motor, harness, or PCB) and clear the error code. Run the unit through a full cycle to verify normal fan operation and no code return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line voltage, if the blower wheel is difficult to access, or if you lack a multimeter and wiring diagrams for your specific Daikin model. Fan motor and PCB diagnostics require measuring live DC control circuits and interpreting feedback signals that vary by model. Professional service also ensures warranty coverage is preserved and that refrigerant lines are not disturbed during indoor unit disassembly.
