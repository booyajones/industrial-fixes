## State Water Heater E94 Error Code — What It Means

E94 on State tankless water heaters typically indicates an exhaust over-temperature condition. This code appears when the temperature of exhaust gases leaving the unit climbs above the safe operating threshold (on similar platforms, 167°F). The heater's control board detects this high exhaust temperature and shuts down combustion to prevent damage to the heat exchanger and venting system.

Because State does not publish a complete error code table publicly, you should verify the exact definition by scanning the QR code on your unit's rating plate or consulting your model-specific manual. State water heaters share platforms with A.O. Smith, and on those condensing tankless models, codes in the 94 range consistently point to exhaust overheat. The fault usually stems from restricted heat transfer or inadequate airflow rather than a control-board failure.

## Common Causes

- **Scale buildup in the heat exchanger** Hard-water deposits coat the internal tubes, reducing heat transfer and forcing exhaust temperature up past the safety limit.
- **Blocked or undersized exhaust venting** Obstructions in the vent termination, collapsed pipe, improper slope, or ice accumulation restrict exhaust flow and trap heat.
- **Restricted combustion air intake** Debris, insect nests, or lint on the intake screen starve the burner of air, producing incomplete combustion and higher exhaust temperatures.
- **Dirty or failing combustion fan** Dust and combustion residue on the blower wheel slow the fan, reducing airflow through the heat exchanger and allowing exhaust to overheat.
- **Faulty exhaust temperature sensor** A degraded thermistor can report falsely high readings even when actual exhaust temperature is normal, triggering a nuisance fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the fault returns immediately after descaling and vent cleaning, or if you lack the tools to measure sensor resistance and verify combustion airflow. A professional can perform a full combustion analysis with a flue-gas analyzer to confirm proper air-fuel ratio and verify that exhaust temperature stays within safe limits under all firing rates. If the heat exchanger is cracked or the control board needs replacement, most manufacturers require licensed installation to preserve warranty coverage. Also call for help if you smell gas, see soot around the burner, or notice the vent pipe is hot to the touch during operation.
