## Bosch Dishwasher E04 Error — What It Means

On most Bosch dishwasher models, E04 indicates a water flow or circulation problem detected by the machine's flow sensor or flow meter. The control board expects to see a certain volume of water moving through the spray system during the wash cycle. When the sensor does not confirm that flow, the dishwasher stops and displays E04. Some Bosch model lines and third-party sources list E04 as a heating fault or excess foam code, so you should always cross-check the exact meaning in your dishwasher's service documentation or on the rating plate.

The error is most often triggered by blocked spray arms, clogged filters, or limescale buildup that restricts water movement. Less commonly, a dirty or faulty flow sensor, a wiring fault, or a control-board issue can prevent the machine from reading circulation correctly. Because the code definition is not universal across all Bosch dishwashers, confirm your model number and consult the official Bosch manual if cleaning does not clear the fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or flow sensor before inspecting the spray arms and filters. Pull and clean the spray arms and filters first because debris and limescale are far more common than a failed sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged spray arms or nozzles (~50%)** Food particles, grease, and mineral deposits block the tiny jets in the spray arms so water cannot circulate properly and the flow sensor registers a fault.
- **Dirty or blocked filters and sump area (~25%)** A clogged fine filter or debris in the sump reduces water flow to the pump and spray system, triggering the circulation fault.
- **Hard-water scale buildup (~10%)** Limescale coats internal plumbing, the heating element, and spray components, restricting flow and fouling the sensor signal.
- **Faulty or dirty water flow sensor or flow meter (~10%)** The sensor may be clogged with scale or may have failed mechanically or electrically, sending incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Wiring or control-board fault (~5%)** Loose connectors, corroded terminals, or a damaged control board prevent the board from receiving valid flow-sensor signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the breaker for 10–15 minutes to clear any temporary fault in the control board.
2. **Remove the lower spray arm** by twisting or lifting it off the hub, then rinse it under running water and use a toothpick or small brush to clear every nozzle hole.
3. **Remove the upper spray arm** (if your model has one) and clean it the same way, paying close attention to any center-feed jets or side nozzles.
4. **Pull out the filter assembly** from the bottom of the tub, separate the fine mesh screen from the coarse screen, and rinse both under hot water to remove grease, food particles, and mineral deposits.
5. **Check and clean the sump area** below the filters for trapped debris, broken glass, or buildup around the pump intake.
6. **Run a descaling or dishwasher-cleaner cycle** if you have hard water or see white scale on the interior walls, heating element, or spray components.
7. **Reassemble all parts**, restore power, and run a short test cycle to confirm the E04 code does not return. If it does, inspect the flow sensor, wiring, and control board or call service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the E04 error persists after you have cleaned the spray arms, filters, and sump and run a descaling cycle. A technician can test the flow sensor with a multimeter, trace wiring faults, and access the control board safely. Also call service if you are not comfortable disassembling the dishwasher interior, if you suspect a control-board fault, or if your dishwasher is still under warranty. Because the exact meaning of E04 can vary by model, a trained Bosch technician has access to the official service documentation and diagnostic software needed to confirm the fault path and replace the correct component.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0–40 in cleaning supplies or descaler, 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250 for a service call, filter set, or sensor replacement.
