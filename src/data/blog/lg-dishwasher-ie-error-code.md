## LG Dishwasher IE Error Code — What It Means

The IE error on an LG dishwasher means the machine detected insufficient water fill. The tub did not reach the expected water level within the fill window, typically within 30 seconds to 10 minutes depending on the model. LG's support documentation confirms this code indicates the dishwasher is not receiving adequate water or the water level is too low after the fill attempt.

This is a water-inlet fault, not a drain or circulation problem. The control board is flagging an anomaly during the filling phase. The machine attempted to fill but could not achieve the correct level in the time allowed, so it stopped the cycle and displayed the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the water inlet valve immediately, but the majority of IE faults are actually supply restrictions (closed valve, kinked hose, clogged screen) that cost nothing to fix. Always check water supply, hose routing, and screen cleanliness before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Water supply valve closed or partially closed (~30%)** The shutoff valve under the sink is not fully open, restricting flow and preventing the tub from filling in the allowed time.
- **Kinked, pinched, or bent inlet hose (~25%)** The fill hose is crushed, bent sharply, or routed in a way that restricts water flow into the machine.
- **Clogged inlet valve screen or filter (~20%)** Sediment, mineral deposits, or debris have accumulated on the small mesh screen at the valve connection, blocking water entry.
- **Low household water pressure (~10%)** Home water pressure is below the required minimum of 20 PSI, so the dishwasher cannot fill quickly enough to satisfy the control.
- **Defective water inlet valve (~10%)** The valve solenoid or mechanical mechanism has failed and will not open properly even when supply, pressure, and screen are good.
- **Frozen supply line (~5%)** In cold weather or unheated spaces, the water line has frozen and is blocking flow.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the circuit breaker to work safely.
2. **Verify the water supply valve** under the sink is fully open and turn it completely counterclockwise to make sure full flow.
3. **Inspect the inlet hose** from the shutoff to the dishwasher for kinks, sharp bends, crushing, or freezing, and straighten or replace any damaged section.
4. **Remove the inlet hose** at the dishwasher connection and pull out the small mesh screen or filter, then rinse it under running water to clear sediment or mineral buildup.
5. **Check household water pressure** using a pressure gauge at a nearby faucet or hose bib. LG specifies a minimum of 20 PSI for proper operation.
6. **Test the water inlet valve** if all supply checks are good. Disconnect power, remove the valve, and test for continuity across the solenoid coil and for free mechanical opening when voltage is applied (consult your model's service manual for exact resistance values).
7. **Replace the inlet valve** if it is stuck closed, does not energize, or fails electrical testing, then reconnect hoses, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm normal fill.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified the water supply is on, the hose is clear, the screen is clean, household pressure is above 20 PSI, and the dishwasher still shows IE after a reset. A technician can test the inlet valve electrically with a multimeter, check control board signals, and confirm whether the valve solenoid is receiving proper voltage. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water connections, do not have the tools to remove and test the valve, or if the error persists after you have replaced the valve and want a second diagnostic opinion before replacing the control board.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts if valve replacement needed, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call.
