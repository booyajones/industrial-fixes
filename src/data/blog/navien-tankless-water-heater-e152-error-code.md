## Navien Tankless Water Heater E152 Error — What It Means

The E152 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates the unit detected a problem with flame sensing or ignition stability. The heater either failed to establish a flame after opening the gas valve or lost the flame unexpectedly during operation. This code protects the system by shutting down the burner when it cannot confirm safe combustion.

The error can stem from issues in the ignition circuit, gas delivery, venting, or flame detection system. Because tankless heaters rely on precise flame sensing to maintain safe operation, even minor contamination or connection problems can trigger this fault. Always verify gas supply and venting before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is a flame rod covered in carbon buildup. Cleaning the flame rod with fine sandpaper or a cloth often restores normal operation at no cost.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame rod prevent it from detecting the flame signal, causing the heater to shut down even when the burner is firing.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or closed valve (~25%)** Low incoming gas pressure or a partially closed supply valve starves the burner and prevents stable ignition.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Obstructions in the intake or exhaust pipes disrupt combustion air flow and cause flame instability or failure to light.
- **Faulty igniter (~10%)** A weak or failed igniter cannot create the spark needed to light the gas, resulting in repeated ignition failures.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~5%)** Poor electrical connections to the flame rod, igniter, or gas valve interrupt the control signals and prevent reliable ignition or flame sensing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners and carefully lifting the panel away from the unit.
3. **Locate the flame rod** near the burner assembly. It is a thin metal probe that extends into the combustion chamber.
4. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a lint-free cloth to remove carbon deposits and corrosion. Do not bend or damage the rod.
5. **Inspect the igniter** for cracks or heavy buildup. Clean it gently if dirty, or note if replacement is needed.
6. **Check all wire connections** to the flame rod, igniter, and gas valve for tightness and corrosion. Reseat any loose connectors.
7. **Reinstall the cover**, restore gas and power, and monitor the unit through a hot-water demand cycle to confirm the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if the flame rod and igniter appear clean but the error persists, or if you suspect low gas pressure or venting problems. Professionals have the tools to measure gas pressure, test flame current, and inspect the heat exchanger for cracks or blockages. Any work involving gas lines, combustion safety, or internal control boards should be handled by a qualified technician to avoid carbon monoxide hazards and make sure warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
