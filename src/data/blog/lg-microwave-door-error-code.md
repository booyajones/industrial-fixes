## What this code means
LG's DOOR error indicates a problem with the door not being closed or the door latch not being engaged. The control system does not detect the door-latch/switch circuit as properly engaged, so it treats the condition as an open door and blocks operation to prevent microwave energy from escaping.

In practical terms, the oven is seeing an open-door condition from its safety interlock system. This can be a true mechanical obstruction, a latch that is cracked or misaligned, or a failed micro-switch in the door interlock assembly. LG's published guidance says to check for packing material or foreign objects, close the door firmly, reset power, and call for service if the code remains.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first, but the real fault is almost always mechanical: a failed interlock switch, a cracked latch hook, or door sag. Test switch continuity and inspect latch alignment before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or misaligned door interlock switch (~50%)** Technicians commonly find a micro-switch with a broken plunger, a sticky actuator, or one that no longer makes contact when the door closes, causing the oven to think the door is open.
- **Cracked, bent, or worn door latch hook (~25%)** If the plastic latch is damaged or the hook no longer reaches the interlock switch, the unit may intermittently or consistently show DOOR even when you close it firmly.
- **Door sag or misalignment (~15%)** A heavy over-the-range door can sit low on one side so the latch no longer lines up with the switch assembly, preventing reliable actuation.
- **Packing material, food debris, or rack obstruction (~7%)** LG's first instruction is to check for anything that physically prevents the door from latching, including foam, cardboard, or a rack positioned wrong.
- **Bent door or frame from impact (~3%)** Repeated slamming or a hard knock can permanently deform the door or cabinet frame and prevent the latch from engaging the switch.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and leave it disconnected for 2-3 minutes to reset the control board, then plug it back in and test.
2. **Inspect the door opening** for any packing material, food debris, or racks that could block the door from closing flush against the cabinet.
3. **Open and close the door firmly** several times and watch whether the latch hook visibly engages the switch assembly in the cabinet frame.
4. **Check door alignment** by observing whether the door sits level or sags on one side, and whether it requires lifting or pushing to start the oven.
5. **Remove the outer cabinet** (unplug first and discharge the high-voltage capacitor) to access the door interlock switch stack and latch actuator.
6. **Test each door switch electrically** using a multimeter set to continuity, confirming that each switch clicks and makes contact when the actuator is pressed.
7. **Replace the failed switch or latch component** with the exact part for your model, restore any displaced actuator parts, reassemble the cabinet, and test the oven through multiple open/close cycles.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the DOOR error persists after you have checked for obstructions, reset power, and confirmed the door closes smoothly. Microwave repair involves discharging a high-voltage capacitor that can store a lethal charge even when the unit is unplugged, and the door interlock system is a safety-critical assembly that must be tested and reassembled correctly. Technicians have the meters, part cross-references, and experience to diagnose whether the fault is a switch, latch, actuator, wiring, or structural misalignment, and they carry liability insurance for work on high-voltage appliances.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-250.
