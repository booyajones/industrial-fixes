## What this code means
The F6E3 error code on a KitchenAid dishwasher means the machine has detected foam or suds in the tub. When suds are present, the dishwasher may not operate properly or may not fill with water. The suds-detection system triggers this code to prevent damage and poor wash results.

KitchenAid identifies this as a suds or foam condition rather than a hard electrical failure. The code points first to detergent-use issues. In practice, the root issue is almost always chemical sudsing from the wrong detergent, too much detergent, or a missing rinse-aid cap. A failed sensor or control board is only suspected if the code persists after the tub is fully de-sudsed and correct detergent practices are followed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or pressure sensor when F6E3 appears. First confirm suds are actually present in the tub and run the oil-and-empty-cycle cleanup before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Wrong detergent used (~50%)** Laundry soap, hand soap, or other non-dishwasher detergent creates excessive suds that trigger the F6E3 code.
- **Too much dishwasher detergent (~30%)** Overdosing even the correct detergent will produce foam the sensor interprets as a suds condition.
- **Rinse-aid cap not replaced (~10%)** Leaving the rinse-aid dispenser cap off after refilling can allow excess rinse aid to enter the tub and contribute to sudsing.
- **Residual suds from previous wash (~8%)** Suds from an earlier cycle may still be present in the sump or filter area, re-triggering the code on the next start.
- **Faulty suds-detection sensor or wiring (~2%)** If the code returns after thorough suds cleanup and correct detergent use, the sensing system or control logic may be misreading the condition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cancel the current cycle** and open the dishwasher door to inspect the tub for visible foam or suds.
2. **Remove all dishes** and use a plastic cup or container to scoop out as much foam as possible from the tub and sump area.
3. **Check the rinse-aid dispenser cap** and confirm it is fully installed and seated. Replace it if it was left off.
4. **Add 2 to 3 tablespoons of vegetable cooking oil** directly into the bottom of the empty tub to help break down the remaining suds.
5. **Run a full wash cycle** with no detergent and no dishes. The oil will suppress foam and allow the machine to drain and rinse the tub clean.
6. **Follow with a second full cycle**, this time adding your normal dishwasher detergent but still loading no dishes, to flush out any remaining oil and suds residue.
7. **Resume normal use** with the correct automatic dishwasher detergent, dosed according to the product label and your water hardness. If F6E3 returns, contact a service technician to test the suds-detection system and control.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the F6E3 code persists after you have removed all suds, run two full empty cycles (one with oil, one with detergent only), confirmed the rinse-aid cap is in place, and switched to the correct dishwasher detergent in the proper dose. At that point the suds-detection sensor, its wiring, or the control board may be misreading the condition. A technician can test the sensing system and determine whether a component replacement is needed. KitchenAid's published guidance for F6E3 centers on the suds condition itself rather than a specific part test matrix, so diagnosis beyond suds cleanup requires service-level tools and access to your model's wiring diagram.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-15 (cooking oil and time), 2-4 hours total cycle time. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call if suds cleanup does not resolve.
