## State Water Heater E173 Error — What It Means

The E173 error code does not appear in any published State Water Heater documentation or quick-reference tables. It is likely a misread code, a proprietary code for a specific sub-model, or a display error. Common State codes include 11 (ignition failure), 14 (overheating), and 24 (flow sensor or clogged filter), but E173 is absent from all known lists.

Before troubleshooting, confirm the exact code by checking your unit's display carefully. The code may actually be E1, 17, 73, or E17. Use the QR code on the rating plate to access your specific model's manual and troubleshooting section. If the unit is not igniting or running properly, the most common issues with State gas water heaters involve the thermocouple or flame sensor, gas supply problems, or vent blockages.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas control valve when a dirty flame sensor or thermocouple is the real problem. Clean the sensor with fine sandpaper or a rag before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Misread or non-existent code (~40%)** The display may show a partial code or the code may be E1, 17, 73, or E17 rather than E173.
- **Dirty flame sensor or thermocouple (~35%)** Carbon buildup on the sensor prevents the unit from detecting the pilot flame and causes ignition failures.
- **Gas supply or vent blockage (~15%)** Insufficient gas pressure or a plugged air intake or exhaust vent prevents proper ignition or causes overheating.
- **Failed gas control valve or driver circuit (~10%)** The gas valve or its electronic driver may fail and prevent the unit from opening gas flow to the burner.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off gas and power** at the shutoff valve and circuit breaker or disconnect before starting any work.
2. **Verify the error code** by checking the display carefully. Use the QR code on the unit's rating plate to access the official manual and confirm whether E173 is listed for your model.
3. **Remove and clean the flame sensor or thermocouple** by unscrewing it from the burner assembly and gently sanding the tip with fine sandpaper or wiping it with a clean rag until shiny.
4. **Check gas supply and vent components** by confirming the gas valve is fully open, the air intake screen is not blocked, and the exhaust vent is clear of debris or nests.
5. **Reinstall the sensor** and restore gas and power. Observe the ignition sequence and check whether the unit stays lit and the error clears.
6. **Test the pressure switch** (if accessible) by listening for a click within 5 seconds of the unit calling for heat. A stuck-closed switch or no click points to a failed switch or gas valve driver.
7. **Consult the manual troubleshooting section** for any model-specific codes or procedures. If the error persists after cleaning and checking vents, call a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the error code persists after cleaning the flame sensor and checking gas supply and vents, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. A technician can test the gas valve driver circuit, pressure switch, and electronic control board with specialized tools. Also call a pro if you smell gas, hear unusual noises, or see flame rollout. Gas work and sealed combustion diagnostics require training and proper instruments to perform safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
