## Frigidaire Oven F91 Error Code — What It Means

The F91 error code on a Frigidaire electric range or oven means the Electronic Oven Control (EOC) attempted to unlock the oven door more times than allowed and still did not see a successful unlock. Frigidaire groups F90 through F94 together as door lock issues, with F91 specifically indicating an unlock failure after repeated attempts.

The code typically appears when the door lock motor cannot move the latch to the unlocked position, the lock switch fails to report the correct state back to the control, or an obstruction prevents the mechanism from moving freely. The control expects feedback from the door lock switch confirming the door is unlocked, and when that signal does not arrive after multiple tries, it sets the F91 fault and halts operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the Electronic Oven Control first, but the actual fault is usually in the door lock assembly or its wiring. Always test the lock motor, switch, and harness before condemning the EOC.

## Common Causes

- **Door lock assembly obstruction or binding (~35%)** Debris, a stuck cam, or a bent rod prevents the lock from moving freely to the unlocked position.
- **Defective door lock motor (~30%)** The motor cannot drive the latch all the way to the unlocked position, so the control never receives the unlock signal.
- **Faulty lock-position switch (~20%)** The door lock switch or micro switch fails to report the correct unlocked state back to the EOC.
- **Damaged or loose wiring (~10%)** Broken wires, loose terminals, or heat damage in the harness between the EOC and lock assembly interrupt feedback signals.
- **Failed Electronic Oven Control (~5%)** The EOC itself has failed and cannot correctly command or read the lock assembly, but this is rare when the lock and wiring test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker for at least 30 seconds to clear any false door-lock errors.
2. **Restore power** and check whether the F91 code returns or whether the oven operates normally after the reset.
3. **Open the oven door** and visually inspect the door lock assembly for debris, a stuck cam, or a bent rod that could bind the mechanism.
4. **Command a lock and unlock cycle** (if your model allows manual control through the service menu or by starting a self-clean cycle and canceling it) and watch whether the motor, cam, and rod move smoothly and fully.
5. **Check wiring and connectors** between the Electronic Oven Control and the door lock assembly for loose terminals, broken wires, or heat damage.
6. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor does not move the cam all the way, the lock switch does not report the correct position, or the mechanism binds despite cleaning.
7. **Replace the Electronic Oven Control** only after the door lock assembly and wiring have been confirmed good and the F91 code still returns on every unlock attempt.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet with the power on, if your multimeter checks show confusing results, or if you have replaced the door lock assembly and checked all wiring but the F91 code still returns. A technician can safely isolate whether the EOC itself has failed, perform voltage and continuity tests at the lock motor circuit, and replace the control board if needed. Also call a pro if the lock mechanism is physically broken or if you find heat damage to the main wiring harness that requires soldering or full harness replacement.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
