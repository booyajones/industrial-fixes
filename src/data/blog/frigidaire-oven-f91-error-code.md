## Frigidaire Oven F91 Error Code — What It Means

Frigidaire groups F91 with F90, F92, F93, and F94 as door lock issues. F91 specifically means the electronic oven control did not receive the expected feedback from the door latch system when it tried to lock or unlock the door. The control sent a command to the lock motor but did not see confirmation that the latch moved to the correct position.

This code appears most often during or after a self-clean cycle, when the oven relies on the door lock to prevent opening at high temperatures. It can also occur if the lock assembly is binding, if wiring between the control board and the lock is damaged, or if the position switch inside the lock assembly fails to report status correctly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control board first. Before swapping the board, test the door lock motor with a multimeter (it should read about 2,000 ohms on the brown and white wires) and check wiring continuity (near 0 ohms on good wires). Most F91 codes trace to the lock assembly or its wiring, not the board.

## Common Causes

- **Door lock assembly failure (~55%)** The lock motor, gears, or latch mechanism inside the assembly has worn out, stalled, or failed so it cannot move or report position.
- **Mechanical binding or obstruction (~20%)** Baked-on residue, heat distortion, or a misaligned door prevents the latch from moving freely, so the control never sees a completed lock or unlock.
- **Wiring harness damage (~15%)** Loose, broken, or open wiring between the electronic oven control and the door lock assembly interrupts the feedback circuit.
- **Lock position switch fault (~7%)** The switch or feedback sensor inside the lock assembly that tells the control whether the door is locked or unlocked has failed.
- **Electronic oven control board issue (~3%)** The control board itself cannot read or process the lock feedback signal correctly, though this is less common when hardware and wiring test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range before opening any panels or touching wiring.
2. **Let the oven cool fully** if the code appeared during or after self-clean, because the unit may remain in a lock state until internal temperatures drop below the safety threshold.
3. **Reset the appliance** by removing power for about 30 seconds, then restore power. Frigidaire states this clears many false error codes. If F91 does not return and the door unlocks, monitor for recurrence.
4. **Verify door alignment and latch access** by closing the door and inspecting the latch striker area for baked-on residue, warping, or obstruction. Do not force the door or pry the latch with tools.
5. **Access the rear or console wiring** by removing the back panel or control console cover. Locate the door lock assembly connector and inspect for loose, corroded, or damaged pins and wires.
6. **Test the door lock motor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. Disconnect the lock assembly connector, then probe the brown and white wires at the motor spades. A working motor reads about 2,000 ohms. If you see OL (open line) or a very low reading, replace the lock assembly.
7. **Check wiring continuity** from the lock connector back to the control board. Each wire should read near 0 ohms end-to-end. Repair or replace any open or damaged harness sections.
8. **Replace the electronic oven control board** only if the lock assembly tests good (about 2,000 ohms), wiring continuity is intact, and the code persists after reassembly and a power reset.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a service technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet, if you cannot safely disconnect power at the breaker, or if you lack a multimeter and do not want to buy one for a single repair. Also call a pro if you have replaced the door lock assembly and verified wiring continuity but F91 returns, because the control board replacement requires matching the exact board revision to your model and safely handling ribbon connectors. If your range is still under warranty, contact Frigidaire or an authorized service center before opening any panels.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $60-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
