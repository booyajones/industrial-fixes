## Bosch ER2 Oven Error — What It Means

ER2 on a Bosch oven indicates a mains connection or power supply problem. The control board is not receiving proper voltage from the house electrical supply. This is not a temperature sensor fault. Multiple Bosch repair sources list ER2 as a faulty mains connection, electrical connection problem, or electric network connection failure. The code can appear after a power interruption or because of wiring issues between the house supply and the oven's control board.

The exact meaning can vary slightly by model family (wall oven, range, or double oven), so always verify the code definition in your specific model's owner manual or wiring diagram. Bosch's U.S. owner-support materials for ranges emphasize checking wiring and power supply when power-related faults occur.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first. Instead, kill power at the breaker and inspect the terminal block behind the oven for loose screws, burned wires, or corrosion before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded terminal block connections (~40%)** The screw terminals where the house supply connects to the oven can work loose over time or show corrosion and heat damage, causing intermittent or low voltage to the control board.
- **House supply problem (~30%)** A missing leg of 240 V supply, undervoltage, tripped breaker, or incorrect wiring at the receptacle or disconnect will trigger ER2 because the oven cannot draw proper power.
- **Damaged mains harness or connector (~15%)** The wiring harness between the terminal block and the control or power board can develop an open, pinch, or burned connector that interrupts supply voltage.
- **Control or power board fault (~10%)** If incoming supply and all connections test good, the control module or power board itself may have failed and cannot process the mains input correctly.
- **Transient electronics fault after power interruption (~5%)** Sometimes ER2 appears after a brief power outage and clears with a simple reset, indicating the control board logged a supply event but no hardware is damaged.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the exact code** and model number from the oven's data plate so you can verify the code definition for your specific appliance family.
2. **Turn off power at the breaker** before you touch any wiring or open panels. Do not rely on the oven's control panel power button.
3. **Pull the oven away from the wall** and remove the rear access panel to expose the terminal block where the house supply connects to the appliance.
4. **Inspect the terminal block and wiring** for loose screw terminals, heat discoloration, burned insulation, or corrosion on the conductors.
5. **Measure incoming voltage** at the wall receptacle or disconnect and at the appliance terminal block to confirm proper 240 V supply (consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected voltage).
6. **If supply and terminals are good, reset the appliance** by leaving the breaker off for five minutes, then restoring power and checking if the code clears.
7. **If ER2 persists after reset and all wiring checks good**, the control module or power board is the remaining likely cause and should be tested or replaced by a qualified technician.
8. **After repair, restore power and test** the oven under a normal heat cycle to confirm the code does not recur and all functions operate correctly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional for any Bosch ER2 error. Diagnosing and repairing this code requires working with high-voltage 240 V supply, opening the oven's rear panel, measuring live voltage at the terminal block, and tracing internal harnesses. An appliance technician or electrician has the tools and training to safely verify whether the problem is in the house supply, the terminal connections, or the control board. If you see any burned wires, smell heat damage, or the breaker trips repeatedly, do not attempt repair yourself. A mis-wired terminal block or shorted harness can cause fire or shock. Let a qualified technician isolate the fault, make the repair, and recheck operation under load.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
