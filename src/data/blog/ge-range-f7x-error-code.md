## GE Range F7 Error Code — What It Means

The F7 error code on a GE range or oven indicates a keypad or membrane switch fault. The electronic control is detecting a stuck button, multiple keys being pressed at the same time, or a short circuit in the touch panel or ribbon cable interface. This fault prevents normal operation of the oven controls.

The code typically points to either a failed keypad assembly or, less commonly, a faulty main control board. Technicians isolate the two by disconnecting the keypad ribbon cable from the control board and checking whether the fault clears. If F7 disappears with the keypad unplugged, the keypad itself is the problem. If F7 remains even with the keypad disconnected, the control board has failed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first when the real culprit is a dirty or failed keypad. Disconnect the keypad ribbon and power on the range. If the error clears, you've saved the cost of an unnecessary board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or shorted membrane keys (~45%)** Buttons on the keypad are electrically stuck closed or shorted together, often from spills, wear, or residue buildup.
- **Contaminated or loose ribbon cable contacts (~25%)** Oxidation, grease, or poor seating at the ribbon connector between keypad and control board creates intermittent shorts.
- **Failed keypad or touchpad assembly (~20%)** Age, heavy use, or high heat from self-clean cycles degrades the membrane switch or glass touch panel assembly.
- **Faulty electronic control board (~10%)** The control board itself has developed a fault in the keypad input circuit, even when the keypad is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill all power** to the range by switching off the circuit breaker or unplugging the unit from the wall outlet.
2. **Remove the control panel cover** by taking out the screws along the top edge or side clips, depending on your GE model, to expose the control board and keypad assembly.
3. **Locate the ribbon cable** that connects the keypad to the control board and carefully release the locking tab or lift the connector clip to disconnect it.
4. **Clean the ribbon contacts** on both the cable and the board socket using a clean pencil eraser or electrical contact cleaner, then blow out any dust and reseat the connector firmly until it clicks.
5. **Restore power** and observe whether the F7 code reappears without the keypad connected, or after reconnecting if you cleaned the contacts.
6. **Replace the keypad assembly** if the error cleared when disconnected, or **replace the control board** if F7 remained even with the keypad unplugged.
7. **Reassemble the control panel**, restore power, and test every button to confirm normal operation and that no keys are sticking.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with the control panel disassembly or if your range is hardwired and you cannot safely disconnect power. A technician can also perform resistance and continuity checks on the keypad and control board to confirm the diagnosis before ordering parts. If you have already replaced both the keypad and control board and F7 still appears, the wiring harness or a less common board failure may be involved, and a service call will save you further guesswork and expense.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–150 for keypad assembly or $100–200 for control board, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on part and labor.
