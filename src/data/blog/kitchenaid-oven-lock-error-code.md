## KitchenAid Oven Lock Error Code — What It Means

KitchenAid uses two different lock-related displays that are often confused. If your oven shows LOC, Lock, or Locked, the control lock feature has been activated and has disabled the touchpads. This is not a fault code. It is a user-activated lock state. If your display shows F9 or F9E0, the oven has detected a door lock failure. The control is not seeing the expected response from the door lock system after attempting to lock the oven, and it may retry the lock sequence several times before displaying the fault.

LOC is resolved by holding the START or ENTER button for 5 seconds to unlock the control panel. F9 and F9E0 are actual failure codes that indicate a problem with the door lock switches, wiring, latch mechanism, or control board. The oven will not operate normally until the lock system fault is repaired.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first when seeing F9 or F9E0, but the real cause is usually a stuck door lock switch or binding latch. Inspect the door lock assembly and wiring before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or failed door lock switch (~40%)** The lock switch does not signal the control board that the door has locked or unlocked, causing the F9 or F9E0 fault to appear.
- **Binding or jammed door latch mechanism (~25%)** The latch assembly is physically stuck or obstructed and cannot move fully into the locked position.
- **Damaged or loose wiring to the lock assembly (~20%)** Corroded terminals, broken wires, or loose connectors prevent the lock circuit from completing properly.
- **Control lock activated by accident (~10%)** The LOC display means the control lock feature is on, not a failure, and is unlocked by holding START for 5 seconds.
- **Failed oven control board (~5%)** If the lock switches and wiring are intact and the fault returns after reset, the control board may be defective.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify which code is displayed.** If the screen shows LOC, Lock, or Locked, the control lock is on. Hold START or ENTER for 5 seconds to unlock. If the display shows F9 or F9E0, you have a door lock fault and should continue.
2. **Disconnect power at the circuit breaker.** Turn off the breaker feeding the range or wall oven and wait 1 minute. This is required before accessing any internal components.
3. **Remove the door and access the lock assembly.** On most KitchenAid ovens the door lock mechanism is mounted above the oven cavity or behind the control panel. Consult your model's service manual for the exact location and removal steps.
4. **Inspect the door lock switches and latch.** Check that the latch moves freely and is not binding or obstructed. Look for a stuck micro-switch on the latch assembly. Test each switch terminal and connector for corrosion or looseness.
5. **Check all wiring and connectors to the lock system.** Verify that the harness between the control board and the door lock assembly is intact, with no broken wires, burned terminals, or disconnected plugs.
6. **Replace the door lock assembly if damaged.** If the switches are stuck, the latch binds, or the wiring is damaged at the assembly, replace the entire door lock mechanism. If all mechanical parts and wiring are intact and the fault returns after power is restored, the control board is the likely cause.
7. **Restore power and test.** Turn the breaker back on and monitor the display for 1 minute. If the F9 or F9E0 code does not return, the repair is complete. If the code reappears immediately, recheck your work or replace the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt appliance wiring or if you cannot safely access the door lock assembly. A qualified appliance technician has the tools to test the lock circuit, measure switch continuity, and verify that the control board is sending the correct signals to the latch motor. If the fault returns after you have replaced the lock assembly and checked all wiring, the control board likely needs replacement, and a pro can confirm the diagnosis before you invest in that part.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
