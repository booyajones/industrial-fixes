## Samsung Dryer t5 Error Code — What It Means

The t5 code (also displayed as tS, tC, or tC5 on some Samsung dryer models) is a temperature-sensing error. Samsung groups these under temperature errors that signal an abnormal temperature-sensor reading or an overheating condition in the temperature circuit. The dryer has detected that the thermistor or temperature sensor is reading outside normal range, or that airflow restrictions are causing the drum to overheat.

This is not a mechanical failure of the drum or motor. It is a safety warning tied to how the dryer measures and regulates heat. The code can appear during a cycle or prevent the dryer from starting, and it often clears after the machine cools and power is cycled.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but airflow restriction (lint buildup or crushed vent hose) is far more common. Always clean the lint screen and inspect the entire vent path before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged lint screen or filter** Lint, fabric softener residue, or other buildup restricts airflow and causes the drum to overheat, triggering the temperature error.
- **Restricted or kinked exhaust vent hose** A crushed, sagging, kinked, or lint-packed vent hose behind the dryer blocks hot air from escaping and trips the sensor.
- **Blocked exterior vent termination** Lint accumulation at the outdoor vent cap, or a bird nest or flap stuck closed, prevents proper exhaust and overheats the drum.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor** The thermistor (typically mounted on the blower housing or duct) can drift out of spec, short, or open, sending a false reading to the control board.
- **Damaged sensor harness or connector** Wiring between the thermistor and control board can develop broken wires, corroded pins, or loose connectors that mimic a sensor failure.
- **Open high-limit or safety thermostat** If airflow is poor, a one-time or resettable safety thermostat may open to prevent fire, and the control board reads this as a temperature fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker, then wait 60 to 120 seconds to allow the control board to reset and the drum to cool.
2. **Remove and clean the lint screen** thoroughly under running water if fabric softener residue is present, then dry it completely before reinstalling.
3. **Inspect the vent hose** behind the dryer for crushing, kinks, sagging, or visible lint buildup, and straighten or replace the hose if needed.
4. **Check airflow at the exterior vent** by running the dryer on high heat and verifying strong, steady exhaust at the outdoor termination, then clear any blockage at the vent cap or duct run.
5. **Test the thermistor circuit** by unplugging the dryer, disconnecting the thermistor harness (usually a two-wire plug on the blower housing or duct), and measuring resistance across the thermistor terminals with an ohmmeter (expect approximately 10,000 ohms at room temperature).
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the thermistor to the control board for chafed insulation, broken wires, or corroded connector pins, and repair or replace damaged sections.
7. **Check safety thermostats** (high-limit or cycling thermostats) by testing for continuity across each pair of terminals, replacing any that are open if airflow is verified as normal and the thermistor tests good.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connectors or multimeters, if the thermistor and all wiring test normal but the code persists, or if you suspect the control board is at fault. A technician can perform a full diagnostic of the temperature circuit, check safety thermostats with the proper equipment, and rule out rare board-level failures. Also call a pro if the exterior vent run is very long, routed through walls or the roof, or if you cannot safely access the vent termination to verify airflow.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (thermistor or vent hose), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 service call and part replacement.
