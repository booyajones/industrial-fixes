## Carrier Error Code 20 — What It Means

On most Carrier furnace control boards, error code 20 means the pressure switch did not close when expected, opened when it should have stayed closed, or the draft and vent circuit is out of range. The pressure switch proves that the inducer motor is pulling enough draft through the vent before gas valve opening. When the switch fails to respond correctly, the board locks out and throws code 20. The underlying problem is usually not the switch itself but a blockage, drainage issue, or inducer motor fault that prevents proper airflow and draft.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted venting** Obstructions in the flue pipe, sagging vent sections, or termination caps clogged with debris prevent the inducer from establishing proper draft.
- **Condensate trap or drain blockage** Water backs up into the pressure switch tubing or vent path when the trap or drain line is clogged, falsely signaling the board.
- **Inducer motor failure or weak performance** A worn motor with failing bearings or reduced RPM cannot develop the draft pressure needed to close the switch.
- **Pressure switch tubing problem** Pinched, cracked, disconnected, or water-filled hoses prevent the switch from sensing draft correctly.
- **Stuck or faulty pressure switch** The switch itself may be stuck open, stuck closed, or electrically out of spec and unable to respond to draft changes.
- **Wiring or control board fault** Loose terminals, corroded connectors, or a defective board input can prevent the board from reading the switch signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number and code chart** on your furnace's control board literature, because Carrier uses different fault tables across product families and code 20 may have a different meaning on some models.
2. **Inspect the entire venting system** for blockages, collapse, improper slope, or pooled condensate in horizontal runs, and clear or re-pitch any problem sections.
3. **Check and clear the condensate trap and drain line** to confirm water is flowing freely and not backing up into the pressure tubing or vent path.
4. **Test the inducer motor** by listening for bearing noise, checking current draw during startup, and confirming it spins up and pulls strong draft.
5. **Inspect the pressure switch tubing** for kinks, cracks, disconnections, or water inside, and blow out or replace any damaged hose.
6. **Test the pressure switch** for proper open/close response during inducer operation and measure continuity across the terminals to confirm it is not stuck or electrically open.
7. **Cycle the furnace through several starts** after correcting any airflow or drainage issues, and monitor the code to confirm the switch now proves correctly and the fault clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the venting system requires disassembly or major re-pitching, or if the fault persists after you have cleared drains and confirmed inducer operation. A technician has the combustion analyzers and manometers needed to verify draft pressure, measure gas valve operation safely, and diagnose intermittent control board faults. If the pressure switch tests good and the inducer runs correctly but the board still throws code 20, the problem may be in the board's pressure-switch input circuit and requires board-level diagnosis or replacement.
