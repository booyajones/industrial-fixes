## Electrolux Dryer E28 Error Code — What It Means

The E28 error code on Electrolux dryers typically signals a communication fault between the main control board and other components, or an internal control system issue. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer cabinet for your specific unit.

This code usually appears when the control board cannot communicate properly with sensors, the user interface, or the motor control. It may also indicate a problem with the control board itself or the wiring harness that connects the various electrical components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first, but loose connector pins or damaged wiring harnesses cause most E28 codes. Inspect and reseat all harness connections before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose wiring harness connector (~40%)** Vibration and heat cycles can work connector pins loose over time, breaking communication between boards.
- **Damaged or chafed wiring harness (~25%)** Wires rubbing against sharp metal edges or melted insulation from heat create intermittent shorts or open circuits.
- **Failed main control board (~20%)** Internal component failure or moisture damage to the board prevents proper signal processing and communication.
- **Faulty user interface board (~10%)** The touchpad or display control board can fail and stop communicating with the main control.
- **Power supply or voltage issue (~5%)** Low voltage or inconsistent power from the outlet can cause control boards to throw communication errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker to prevent electrical shock during inspection.
2. **Remove the control panel** by taking out the screws securing the top or front panel (location varies by model) to access the control boards and wiring.
3. **Inspect and reseat all connectors** on the main control board and user interface board, looking for loose pins, corrosion, or burn marks on the plastic housings.
4. **Trace the wiring harnesses** between boards and check for pinched, chafed, or melted insulation, especially where wires pass through metal brackets or near the heating element.
5. **Test the control board** by checking for visible damage such as burnt resistors, blown capacitors, or moisture stains; replace the board if damage is evident.
6. **Reassemble the dryer** and restore power, then run a test cycle to confirm the error has cleared.
7. **Monitor for recurrence** over the next few loads to make sure the repair holds and no intermittent fault remains.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or cannot locate the source of the fault after inspecting wiring and reseating connectors. A technician can use a multimeter to test continuity and voltage at each connector, pinpoint intermittent faults that are hard to see, and safely replace control boards with the correct part numbers for your model. Also call a pro if you find evidence of a short circuit or melted wiring that suggests a deeper electrical problem.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
