## Maytag Dishwasher F3E2 Error Code — What It Means

The F3E2 code on a Maytag dishwasher signals a water temperature sensing fault in the thermistor or temperature sensor circuit. The control board has detected the thermistor circuit is open, shorted, or reading out of the expected range, so it cannot reliably monitor water temperature during the wash cycle. This is the same family of codes used across Whirlpool-branded platforms and points directly to a problem in the heater temperature-sensing loop.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor** The sensor itself has gone out of range, open, or shorted internally and no longer provides valid resistance readings to the control.
- **Broken or damaged wiring harness** A conductor in the sensor lead has broken, corroded, or pulled loose at the connector, interrupting the circuit back to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded connector** The plug at the thermistor, heater assembly, or control board has corroded pins or poor contact, causing intermittent or total circuit failure.
- **Heater assembly or heater-circuit fault** If the sensor tests good but the code persists, a fault in the heater element circuit can also trigger the same temperature-sensing error.
- **Main control board fault** When the sensor, wiring, and heater all test good, the control board itself may have a failed input circuit or internal relay issue.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. {'text': '**Unplug the dishwasher** or turn off the breaker before any testing or disassembly to eliminate shock hazards.'}
2. {'text': '**Remove the lower access panel** or toe-kick and locate the heater assembly and thermistor connector, usually mounted on or near the heating element at the bottom of the tub.'}
3. {'text': '**Inspect the wiring harness and connector** for burned pins, corrosion, loose terminals, or broken conductors, especially at the thermistor lead where a broken wire is a documented common cause.'}
4. {'text': "**Unplug the thermistor connector and measure resistance** across the sensor terminals with a multimeter set to ohms. One field example showed a healthy sensor reading approximately 9.8 kΩ to 14.8 kΩ over a room-temperature range, but consult your model's service manual for the exact table."}
5. {'text': '**Replace the thermistor** if the resistance is infinite, zero, or far outside the expected range for ambient temperature.'}
6. {'text': '**Check continuity from the sensor pins back to the control board** if the sensor tests good, repairing or replacing any open or intermittent wire segments.'}
7. {'text': '**Reassemble, restore power, and run a test cycle** to verify the code clears and the dishwasher heats normally.'}

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical testing under the machine, if the wiring and thermistor both test in range but the code returns, or if you lack a multimeter and service manual with resistance tables. A pro can efficiently isolate whether the fault lies in the heater circuit or the control board and will have model-specific diagnostic routines and OEM part cross-references to avoid unnecessary part swaps.
