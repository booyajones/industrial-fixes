## KitchenAid Dishwasher F5E1 Error — What It Means

F5E1 on a KitchenAid dishwasher is a door latch or door switch fault. The control is not receiving a valid "door closed and latched" signal, so the dishwasher will not start or run a cycle normally. Field service documentation describes this as a door switch failure or door latch assembly issue.

The code often appears if the door is not fully closed within about four seconds after pressing Start or Resume, or if the latch mechanism or switch inside the door assembly is defective or misaligned. The dishwasher expects the door-switch signal to change state when the latch engages, and F5E1 indicates that signal is either missing or intermittent.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real problem is a worn latch assembly or a loose wire connector. Test the door switch for continuity (it should read approximately zero ohms when closed) before buying electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Defective door latch assembly or door switch (~50%)** The latch mechanism or embedded switch no longer closes the control circuit correctly, so the control never sees the door as latched.
- **Door not fully closed or obstructed (~20%)** A dish handle, utensil, or misaligned rack prevents the door from closing squarely enough to engage the latch.
- **Misaligned door or worn hinges (~15%)** Sagging hinges or a twisted door frame cause the latch strike and latch tongue to miss each other, so the switch never closes.
- **Loose, corroded, or disconnected wiring (~10%)** The harness connector at the latch or control board has worked loose or a terminal is corroded, breaking the signal path.
- **Control board interpretation issue (~5%)** Less commonly, the control board misreads an intermittent latch signal even when the mechanical parts are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dishwasher before any inspection or testing.
2. **Verify the door closes fully** and nothing is blocking the latch or the door seal, then press Start to confirm the error still appears.
3. **Inspect the door latch assembly and strike** for cracks, looseness, or misalignment by opening the door and examining the latch tongue and the receiver on the tub frame.
4. **Remove the inner door panel** by taking out the screws around the perimeter of the door (consult your model's service sheet for screw locations).
5. **Locate the door latch and switch assembly** on the inside of the door panel and disconnect its wire harness.
6. **Test the door switch for continuity** using a multimeter set to ohms: with the latch manually closed the switch should read approximately zero ohms, and open it should read infinite resistance.
7. **Replace the door latch assembly** if the switch reads open or very high resistance when closed, or if the latch mechanism is visibly broken or worn, then reassemble the door and restore power to test.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the door panel, if the dishwasher is still under warranty, or if you have replaced the latch assembly and verified all wiring and the fault persists. Further electronic control diagnosis may require a service-mode test or control-board replacement, and a technician can also check for intermittent grounding or control-circuit faults that a multimeter alone will not catch. If the door hinges are severely bent or the tub frame is cracked, a pro can assess whether the dishwasher is economical to repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–90 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call plus parts.
