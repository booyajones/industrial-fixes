## Bosch Dishwasher E19 Error Code — What It Means

The E19 error code on a Bosch dishwasher indicates a water circulation problem. The machine detects that water is not flowing through the spray arms or the pump system as expected. This can stem from a blockage in the sump, a clogged filter, debris in the impeller, or a failed circulation pump. The control board throws the code when the pressure switch or flow sensor confirms that circulation has stopped or fallen below the threshold needed to clean dishes properly.

Because circulation depends on both the drain path being clear and the pump being free to move water, the fault can appear at several points in the hydraulic loop. The dishwasher may drain normally yet still show E19 if the wash pump itself cannot build pressure. Always start with the easiest mechanical checks before testing electrical components or replacing the pump.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the circulation pump when the real culprit is a piece of broken glass, a toothpick, or food debris jamming the impeller. Remove the filter basket and inspect the sump by hand before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked sump or impeller (~45%)** Broken glass, bones, fruit pits, or small utensils lodge in the sump cavity or wedge the wash pump impeller, stopping circulation.
- **Clogged filter screen (~20%)** The fine mesh filter under the spray arm fills with food particles and grease, choking flow to the pump inlet.
- **Failed circulation pump (~18%)** The motor winding burns out or the seal leaks, preventing the pump from building enough pressure to satisfy the flow sensor.
- **Kinked or obstructed drain hose (~10%)** A sharp bend or accumulation in the drain line creates back-pressure that the machine interprets as a circulation fault.
- **Faulty pressure switch or flow sensor (~5%)** The sensor that monitors circulation pressure drifts out of calibration or its diaphragm chamber clogs, sending a false low-flow signal to the board.
- **Wiring fault to the circulation pump (~2%)** A loose connector or corroded terminal interrupts power to the pump motor, mimicking a mechanical failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water supply valve under the sink.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** enough to access the toe-kick panel or lower front cover, then remove the screws and lift it away.
3. **Remove the lower dish rack**, unscrew the lower spray arm by turning counterclockwise, and lift out the cylindrical filter basket from the floor of the tub.
4. **Inspect the sump cavity** below the filter for broken glass, bones, plastic fragments, or any object that might block the impeller. Remove debris by hand and wipe the cavity clean.
5. **Reach into the sump opening** and try to spin the wash pump impeller by hand. It should rotate smoothly with slight resistance. If it is jammed, work the obstruction free or note that the pump bearings may be seized.
6. **Check the fine mesh filter** and the coarse screen for food buildup. Scrub both under hot running water with a soft brush and reinstall them in the correct order.
7. **Restore power and water**, then run a short rinse cycle. If the E19 clears, the blockage is resolved. If the code persists, test the circulation pump wiring with a multimeter or replace the pump assembly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you have cleared all visible blockages, confirmed the impeller spins freely, and cleaned the filter yet the E19 code returns. The technician will test the circulation pump motor windings, measure the flow sensor signal, and check the control board for relay faults. Also call if you are uncomfortable working around 120-volt wiring or if the pump replacement requires tilting the dishwasher and disconnecting multiple hose clamps in a tight space. A professional visit typically includes a pressure test of the spray arm circuit and a diagnostic readout from the onboard computer.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
