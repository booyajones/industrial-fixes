## State Water Heater E84 Error Code — What It Means

The E84 error code on State tankless water heaters (an A. O. Smith brand) typically indicates an ignition failure or flame-proving fault. The unit cannot establish or verify a flame during startup. This class of fault is most commonly triggered by air intake blockage, exhaust vent restrictions, low gas supply, or a contaminated flame sensor. The control board shuts down the burner and displays E84 to prevent unsafe operation. In some cases the code may also appear if water flow is too low or if scale buildup inside the heat exchanger affects combustion conditions.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, or debris on the inlet filter restricts combustion air and is the most common field cause of ignition failure in State and A. O. Smith tankless units.
- **Obstructed exhaust vent or termination** Ice, animal nests, or improper installation of the vent pipe prevent exhaust gases from escaping and trigger a flame-proving fault.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor** Carbon deposits or mineral buildup on the flame rod prevent the control from sensing ignition even when a flame is present.
- **Low gas supply or closed valve** Insufficient gas pressure, a partially closed manual shutoff, or an empty propane tank stops the burner from lighting.
- **Low water flow or clogged inlet filter** If flow drops below the minimum threshold or the inlet screen is plugged with sediment, the unit will not fire and may log an ignition error.
- **Scale buildup in the heat exchanger** Hard water deposits inside the exchanger can disrupt flame shape or sensing, especially in units that have not been descaled on schedule.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and reset** the unit by turning off the electrical disconnect or unplugging for 30 seconds, then restore power and attempt a hot-water call to see if the fault clears.
2. **Inspect the air intake screen** on the exterior or inside the unit housing, remove any lint or debris by hand or with a soft brush, and rinse if the manufacturer allows.
3. **Check the exhaust vent and termination** for blockages, ice buildup, or improper pitch, and clear any obstructions or correct any sagging pipe runs.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming that the manual gas shutoff valve is fully open, checking that the propane tank (if applicable) has fuel, and listening for gas flow when the unit calls for heat.
5. **Remove and clean the flame sensor or igniter assembly** by disconnecting power and gas, unbolting the burner access cover, gently removing the rod, and wiping it with fine sandpaper or a clean cloth until the metal is bright.
6. **Check water flow and inlet filter** by confirming that at least one fixture is open to full flow, inspecting the cold-water inlet screen for sediment, and flushing or replacing the filter if clogged.
7. **Test after each fix** by running hot water for two to three minutes and watching the display; if E84 returns after clearing intake and sensor, escalate to a licensed technician for gas-valve, flow-sensor, or control-board diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E84 code returns after you have cleared the intake, cleaned the flame sensor, and verified gas supply. Persistent ignition faults often require combustion analysis, gas-valve replacement, or control-board swap, all of which involve working on live gas lines and high-voltage components. Any time you smell gas, hear hissing, or see physical damage to the burner assembly, shut off gas and power immediately and contact a professional before attempting further troubleshooting.
