## Samsung Washer C6 Error Code — What It Means

The C6 error code on Samsung front-load and top-load washers indicates a problem with the water temperature sensing system. The washer's control board is not receiving the expected signal from the thermistor (temperature sensor), or the heating element circuit is reporting a fault. This prevents the machine from properly heating water during warm or hot wash cycles.

The exact meaning can vary slightly between Samsung models, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the panel for model-specific details. In general, the code points to the thermistor, its wiring harness, or less commonly the control board itself. The washer may still run cold-water cycles but will stop or refuse to start when a heated cycle is selected.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first, but a simple continuity test of the thermistor and inspection of its connector catches most C6 faults at a fraction of the cost.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty or drift thermistor (~50%)** The temperature sensor embedded in the sump or tub has failed or drifted out of its resistance range, sending incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded thermistor connector (~25%)** The wiring plug at the thermistor has vibrated loose or developed corrosion, breaking the signal path intermittently.
- **Damaged thermistor wiring harness (~15%)** The wire bundle running from the sensor to the control board is pinched, cut, or shorted against the metal cabinet.
- **Failed control board (~8%)** The main PCB has lost the ability to read the thermistor signal due to a burnt trace or failed input circuit.
- **Heating element short or open circuit (~2%)** A shorted or open heater triggers a protective fault that the control board reports as a temperature error on some models.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves.
2. **Remove the lower front or rear access panel** using a screwdriver or nut driver, depending on your model.
3. **Locate the thermistor**, a small sensor with two wires, usually clipped to the sump hose or embedded in the tub wall near the heater.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** and inspect both halves for corrosion, bent pins, or moisture. Clean with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Test the thermistor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. Compare the reading to your model's specification table (typically 10-50 kΩ at room temperature).
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the reading is infinite, zero, or far outside the expected range. Clip or thread the new sensor into the same mounting location.
7. **Reconnect the wiring harness**, ensuring a firm click, then replace the access panel, restore power and water, and run a test cycle on hot to verify the code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if the thermistor and wiring both test good but the code persists. A failing control board requires specialized diagnostics and soldering skills, and misdiagnosis can lead to wasted money on unnecessary parts. Professionals also carry model-specific schematics and can quickly isolate faults in the heating element circuit or board input stages that are difficult to trace without a service manual.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
