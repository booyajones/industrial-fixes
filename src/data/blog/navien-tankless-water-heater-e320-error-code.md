## Navien E320 Error Code — What It Means

The E320 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates that the exhaust temperature has exceeded safe operating limits. The unit detects dangerously high temperatures in the exhaust system and shuts down to prevent damage or unsafe conditions. This code protects the heat exchanger and venting components from overheating.

The error typically points to either a physical blockage restricting airflow through the venting system or a sensor that is reading incorrectly. When exhaust gases cannot escape properly, heat builds up inside the unit. The exhaust temperature sensor monitors this condition and triggers the code when thresholds are crossed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the exhaust temperature sensor first, but a visual inspection of the vent pipe for blockages, nests, or improper termination usually reveals the real cause and costs nothing.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked exhaust vent pipe (~40%)** Debris, bird nests, ice, or lint restrict exhaust flow and trap heat inside the unit.
- **Failed exhaust temperature sensor (~25%)** The sensor reads incorrectly high or sends a false signal to the control board.
- **Improper venting installation (~15%)** Vent pipe is too long, has too many elbows, or lacks proper pitch for condensate drainage.
- **Partially blocked heat exchanger (~10%)** Scale or sediment buildup on the heat exchanger restricts airflow and raises exhaust temperature.
- **Faulty combustion fan (~7%)** The fan spins too slowly or intermittently, reducing exhaust velocity and causing heat to back up.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~3%)** Poor electrical connection at the exhaust sensor causes intermittent high readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the water heater at the electrical breaker and the gas shut-off valve before beginning any work.
2. **Inspect the outdoor exhaust termination** by going outside and visually checking the vent outlet for bird nests, leaves, ice, snow, or other debris and remove any blockages you find.
3. **Check the entire exhaust vent run** inside the building for proper slope, sags where condensate could pool, disconnected joints, or crushing and repair or reinstall as needed.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the water heater and locate the exhaust temperature sensor, which is typically mounted on the exhaust collector or flue pipe near the top of the unit.
5. **Test the exhaust temperature sensor** by disconnecting the wiring harness and using a multimeter to measure resistance across the sensor terminals, then compare the reading to your model's specification table.
6. **Replace the exhaust temperature sensor** if the resistance reading is out of range or if the sensor shows visible corrosion or damage at the mounting point.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore power and gas, and run a hot water tap to initiate a heating cycle and verify that the E320 code does not return and exhaust temperatures remain normal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you find no obvious blockages and the sensor tests within normal range, as the problem may involve the heat exchanger, combustion fan, or gas valve. Professional work is also needed if the venting system requires reconfiguration to meet code or if the unit has not been serviced in several years and may need a heat exchanger flush. Any work involving gas line connections, combustion analysis, or internal flue components should be handled by a trained technician with the proper tools and safety equipment.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
