## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E16 Error — What It Means

The E16 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters generally indicates a flame failure or ignition problem. The unit has attempted to light the burner but has not detected a stable flame or has detected a flame condition outside normal parameters. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent gas buildup or unsafe operation.

Because the exact definition of error codes can vary by model and firmware version, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for the precise meaning on your specific heater. Common underlying issues include interrupted gas supply, insufficient combustion air, blocked venting, a dirty or failing flame sensor, or a faulty ignition assembly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve first, but a dirty flame sensor is usually the culprit. Clean the sensor rod with fine sandpaper or steel wool before buying parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~40%)** Mineral deposits, soot, or corrosion on the flame rod prevent it from detecting the flame signal, causing the heater to shut down.
- **Gas supply issue (~25%)** Closed or partially closed gas valve, low gas pressure, or empty propane tank stops the burner from lighting or maintaining a flame.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Obstructed intake or exhaust pipes reduce combustion air or trap exhaust gases, causing flame instability or shutdown.
- **Failed igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A worn or broken igniter cannot generate the spark needed to light the gas, or a cracked ceramic insulator causes grounding.
- **Faulty gas valve (~7%)** A stuck or failing gas valve does not open fully or modulate correctly, preventing proper flame establishment.
- **Control board issue (~3%)** A malfunctioning circuit board may fail to send ignition signals or misread flame sensor input, triggering false errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater before any work.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the access panel screws to expose the burner chamber and flame sensor.
3. **Locate the flame sensor rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner. It may be held by a single screw or clip.
4. **Clean the flame sensor** by gently sanding the rod with fine-grit sandpaper or steel wool until shiny, removing all oxidation and soot. Do not bend the rod.
5. **Inspect intake and exhaust vent pipes** outside the home for blockages such as bird nests, leaves, ice, or debris. Clear any obstructions.
6. **Check the gas supply valve** is fully open. If you have propane, verify the tank is not empty and the regulator is functioning.
7. **Reinstall the cover**, restore gas and power, and attempt to run hot water to see if the unit fires and the error clears.
8. **If the error persists**, call a licensed technician to test gas pressure, inspect the igniter and gas valve, and check control board operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame sensor and checking vents does not resolve the error, or if you suspect a problem with the gas valve, ignition module, or control board. Gas work requires proper licensing, leak testing, and combustion analysis to make sure safe operation. A professional can measure gas pressure, test flame current with a multimeter, and replace sealed components under warranty if applicable.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
