## What this code means
HC4 is a heating or overheating fault code on Samsung dryers. Samsung groups HC4 with HC, hE, and 1 HC, all of which indicate the dryer is overheating. The code triggers when the internal temperature exceeds safe limits, usually because hot air cannot escape properly. On electric dryers, Samsung also notes that low voltage supply can cause insufficient heating and contribute to the fault, though restricted airflow remains the primary trigger in the field.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the thermistor or heating element first. Before ordering parts, clean the entire vent path and exterior hood, then check airflow at the outside vent during a test cycle-most HC4 codes clear once airflow is restored.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged lint screen or residue buildup (~40%)** Lint or dryer-sheet residue on the screen blocks airflow and traps heat inside the drum.
- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~35%)** Lint accumulation in the duct, crushed flex pipe, kinked runs, or too many elbows prevent hot air from escaping to the outside.
- **Overloaded drum (~10%)** Cramming too many clothes into one load limits air circulation and can cause localized overheating.
- **Failed thermistor or thermostat (~7%)** If airflow is good, an out-of-range thermistor or stuck high-limit thermostat will trigger a false overheating alarm.
- **Low supply voltage (electric models) (~5%)** Samsung states that abnormal or low voltage can cause heating errors and contribute to improper temperature behavior.
- **Shorted heating element or control board fault (~3%)** A heating element shorted to chassis or a failing relay on the main board can cause actual overheating even when airflow is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dryer** and allow it to cool completely, then unplug it or turn off the circuit breaker.
2. **Remove and inspect the lint screen.** If you see residue or a waxy film from dryer sheets, wash the screen with warm soapy water, scrub gently, rinse, and let it dry fully before reinstalling.
3. **Disconnect the exhaust duct** at the back of the dryer and inspect the transition hose and the wall duct for lint buildup, kinks, or crushing; clean or replace as needed.
4. **Check the exterior vent hood** and remove any lint, bird nests, or debris blocking the flapper; confirm the flapper opens easily.
5. **Reconnect everything, restore power, and run a timed dry cycle** with a small load; go outside and verify strong airflow at the vent hood.
6. **If HC4 returns after airflow is confirmed good,** check supply voltage on electric models (consult your model's service manual for the expected range), then test the thermistor, high-limit thermostat, and heating element for out-of-range readings or shorts to chassis.
7. **Replace the failed component** and retest with a normal load; if all sensors and heater test good but the code persists, suspect the main control board or heat relay.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you have cleaned the entire vent system and the exterior hood shows strong airflow but HC4 still appears, or if you are not comfortable using a multimeter to test thermistor resistance and heating-element continuity. Also call if the dryer shares a long or complex duct run with another appliance, if the vent termination is on the roof, or if you suspect low supply voltage and want the electrical service verified. A technician can perform precise resistance checks on the thermistor and thermostats, test the heating element for shorts to chassis, and confirm the control board is cycling the heater relay correctly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in vent cleaning supplies or replacement duct, 30-90 min; $20-80 for thermistor or thermostat if airflow is clear. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for vent cleaning and component diagnosis.
