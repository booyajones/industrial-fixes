## State Water Heater E159 Error Code — What It Means

E159 on State tankless water heaters indicates a combustion or ignition-related lockout. While State does not publish a specific definition for this code in widely available service documentation, it belongs to the same error family used by A. O. Smith tankless platforms, where similar codes flag flame failure or ignition problems. The unit has detected that it cannot establish or maintain a stable flame, so it locks out to prevent unsafe operation.

This fault typically stems from restricted airflow, inadequate gas supply, or a failed ignition component. Because State and A. O. Smith share tankless control architecture, the diagnostic approach mirrors that for ignition faults in the shared platform. Always confirm the exact model number and refer to the unit's service literature, since code definitions can vary across product lines.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, or debris on the combustion air intake restricts airflow and prevents proper ignition.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Closed gas valve, low inlet pressure, or undersized gas piping starves the burner and causes flame failure.
- **Failed ignition transformer** A weak or dead transformer cannot generate the spark needed to light the burner.
- **Restricted exhaust venting** Obstructed or improperly routed vent pipes back-pressure combustion air and trigger a lockout.
- **Scale or contamination in the heat exchanger** Mineral deposits or soot interfere with flame sensing or gas flow and disrupt combustion stability.
- **Faulty gas valve or wiring** A stuck valve or loose connection prevents gas from reaching the burner even when the igniter sparks.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the model number** from the rating plate and confirm the E159 code on the display panel, then consult the model-specific service manual to verify the code definition for your unit.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the front or side of the heater, removing any lint, pet hair, or debris that could restrict combustion air.
3. **Check the exhaust vent termination** outside for blockage, snow, or improper clearance, and verify that the vent piping is routed according to the installation manual with no sags or obstructions.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve upstream is fully open, then measure inlet gas pressure under firing conditions to make sure it meets the rating-plate specification.
5. **Test the ignition transformer** by observing for spark at the burner during a call for heat, or use a multimeter to check for output voltage across the transformer leads during the ignition cycle.
6. **Inspect the burner and flame sensor** for soot, scale, or corrosion, and clean gently with a soft brush if contamination is present.
7. **Clear the fault code** by cycling power or following the reset procedure in the manual, then run a full hot-water draw and monitor for stable ignition and no re-occurrence of E159.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate a visible blockage in the intake or vent, if you are unfamiliar with measuring gas pressure or working with live ignition circuits, or if the code returns after you have cleared the intake and confirmed gas supply. Combustion and ignition diagnostics require gas-line work and electrical testing that can be hazardous without proper tools and training. A technician can also access factory service bulletins and model-specific fault trees that are not published in consumer materials, which is especially important for codes like E159 that lack a universal published definition.
