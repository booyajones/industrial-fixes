## Navien Tankless Water Heater E243 Error — What It Means

The E243 error on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a problem with the flow sensor circuit or the sensor itself. The control board is not receiving the expected signal from the flow sensor, which monitors water movement through the heat exchanger. This prevents the unit from firing properly because the system cannot confirm adequate flow to heat safely.

The error can stem from a faulty flow sensor, damaged wiring between the sensor and the control board, loose connections, or occasionally a board-level fault. Because the flow sensor is an electronic component exposed to water flow and debris, it tends to wear or drift out of calibration over time, making it the most common culprit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a bad flow sensor or corroded connector. Always test continuity and voltage at the sensor connector before swapping the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed flow sensor (~55%)** The flow sensor itself has degraded, drifted out of spec, or suffered internal electrical failure, sending no signal or a garbled signal to the board.
- **Corroded or loose wiring connection (~25%)** The connector at the flow sensor or at the board has corroded contacts, moisture intrusion, or a loose pin that breaks the signal path.
- **Damaged sensor harness (~10%)** The wire bundle between the flow sensor and control board has been pinched, cut, or abraded, creating an open circuit or short.
- **Control board fault (~7%)** The input circuit on the control board that reads the flow sensor has failed, though this is less common than sensor or wiring issues.
- **Debris or air in the flow path (~3%)** Sediment or air bubbles interfere with the sensor paddle or turbine, causing intermittent or erratic readings that trigger the error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the isolation valves on both the cold inlet and hot outlet lines.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to access the internal components and locate the flow sensor, typically mounted on the cold-water inlet line near the heat exchanger.
3. **Inspect the sensor connector** and wiring harness for any signs of corrosion, moisture, or loose pins. Clean contacts with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
4. **Disconnect the flow sensor** connector and use a multimeter to check continuity or resistance across the sensor terminals, consulting your model's service manual for expected values.
5. **If readings are out of spec or open**, replace the flow sensor by unscrewing it from the water line. Use thread sealant or a new gasket as specified by the manufacturer.
6. **Reconnect all wiring**, restore power and water flow, and run a hot-water tap to verify the unit fires without error.
7. **Document the repair** and monitor the system for the next few cycles to confirm the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with water connections under pressure, if multimeter testing is beyond your skill level, or if replacing the flow sensor and inspecting wiring does not clear the error. A professional can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board, check for intermittent faults under load, and safely handle any gas-line or venting issues that may complicate access to the sensor. If the unit is still under warranty, professional service ensures you do not void coverage by performing unauthorized repairs.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
