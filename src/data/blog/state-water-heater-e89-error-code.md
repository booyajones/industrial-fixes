## State Water Heater E89 Error Code — What It Means

The E89 error code on State tankless water heaters indicates a combustion or ignition fault. The heater is unable to establish or maintain ignition, which typically traces back to restricted airflow, low gas pressure, or low water flow through a dirty inlet filter. This fault belongs to the same family of ignition-related codes common to State and A. O. Smith tankless platforms.

Because ignition requires a precise balance of gas, air, and water flow, E89 can appear whenever any of those inputs falls outside normal range. The most frequent culprit in the field is blocked air intake screens, though gas supply issues and fouled water filters also trigger this code. If basic cleaning does not restore operation, the fault usually involves the flame sensor, gas valve, or venting path and requires professional diagnosis.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, or debris on the intake grilles restricts combustion air and prevents ignition.
- **Dirty inlet water filter** A clogged filter reduces flow below the minimum required to allow the gas valve to open.
- **Low gas pressure or interrupted gas supply** Inadequate pressure at the unit or a closed isolation valve stops the burner from lighting.
- **Contaminated or faulty flame sensor** Carbon buildup or sensor failure prevents the control board from confirming ignition.
- **Blocked or improperly installed venting** Exhaust restrictions or incorrect vent termination interfere with the combustion air path.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Note the exact model and serial number** from the data plate so you can reference the correct service manual and fault table for your platform.
2. **Power off the unit** at the service disconnect or breaker, then turn off the gas isolation valve upstream of the heater.
3. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the front or side of the cabinet, removing any lint, dust, or blockage that restricts airflow.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** at the cold-water connection, flushing any sediment or scale that restricts flow.
5. **Restore gas and power**, then verify that the gas isolation valve is fully open and that household gas pressure is normal at other appliances.
6. **Check the flame sensor or flame rod** for corrosion or carbon deposits, clean it with fine abrasive cloth, and reinstall it securely.
7. **Inspect the vent termination and exhaust path** for obstructions, ice, or improper installation, and confirm that all joints are sealed and meet clearance requirements.
8. **If the error persists**, record any additional fault codes displayed and contact a licensed technician to test gas-valve operation, combustion air settings, and control-board diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if cleaning the air intake and inlet filter does not clear E89, or if you lack the tools to verify gas pressure and test the flame sensor. Combustion faults that persist after basic cleaning usually involve the gas valve, ignition control board, or heat-exchanger blockage. All of these require metered diagnostics and safe handling of gas connections. A qualified technician will also check for code revisions or service bulletins specific to your serial-number range and confirm that venting complies with the installation manual.
