## Navien E185 Error Code — What It Means

The E185 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically signals a problem with internal communication or sensor circuits. Because Navien uses different fault codes across models and the exact meaning of E185 can vary, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel for the precise definition on your unit. In general, this code family points to flow sensor malfunctions, wiring faults, or control board communication failures that prevent the heater from correctly reading water flow or temperature.

The error may appear intermittently or lock out the heater entirely. It often arises after power interruptions, voltage spikes, or when connectors work loose over time. Before replacing any parts, verify that incoming power is stable and all harness plugs are fully seated.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the flow sensor or control board first. Instead, inspect every connector on the flow sensor harness and control board for corrosion or poor seating, which solves most E185 faults at zero cost.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~40%)** Vibration and humidity cause harness plugs at the flow sensor or control board to oxidize or back out slightly, breaking continuity.
- **Failed flow sensor (~25%)** The hall-effect flow sensor can fail internally, sending no signal or erratic pulses to the control board.
- **Control board communication fault (~20%)** The main PCB loses sync with daughter boards or sensors after a power surge or component aging.
- **Voltage spike or power interruption (~10%)** Temporary overvoltage or a brownout corrupts the board's firmware registers, requiring a full power cycle to clear.
- **Damaged wire harness (~5%)** Rodents, sharp edges, or installation error can nick insulation and create intermittent shorts in sensor wiring.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws along the bottom edge and lifting the panel free.
3. **Locate the flow sensor**, usually a cylindrical device mounted inline on the cold-water inlet pipe with a two- or three-pin connector.
4. **Disconnect and inspect the flow sensor connector** for green corrosion, moisture, or bent pins. Clean contacts with electronics spray if needed.
5. **Reseat every connector** on the main control board and any daughter boards, pressing firmly until you hear or feel a click.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then press the reset button on the control panel or cycle power again to clear the fault.
7. **Run hot water** at a fixture for two minutes and watch the display. If E185 returns, proceed to sensor or board replacement as diagnosed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with 120V electrical connections, if the error persists after reseating connectors and replacing the flow sensor, or if you suspect the control board needs replacement. Gas and electrical work on tankless heaters must meet local codes, and warranty coverage often requires professional installation of control boards. A technician can also use diagnostic software to read detailed fault logs and test communication between boards.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
