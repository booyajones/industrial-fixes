## Navien Tankless E308 Error — What It Means

The E308 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a flame loss or ignition failure during operation. The unit attempted to ignite or maintain a flame but was unable to sustain combustion. This triggers a safety shutdown to prevent gas buildup or unsafe operation.

The code typically appears when the flame sensor (flame rod) cannot detect consistent flame, when gas supply is interrupted, when combustion air is restricted, or when ignition components fail. The unit will lock out and require a reset after troubleshooting the underlying issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the entire ignition board when the actual problem is a dirty or loose flame rod. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and check the wire connection before replacing any electronic components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensing rod prevents it from detecting the flame signal, causing the unit to shut down even when combustion is occurring.
- **Low or interrupted gas pressure (~25%)** Insufficient gas supply pressure, a partially closed valve, or an issue with the gas meter or regulator starves the burner and prevents stable ignition.
- **Blocked or restricted air intake (~15%)** Lint, debris, or ice blocking the combustion air intake reduces oxygen supply and causes flame instability or failure to ignite.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition assembly (~10%)** A weak or failed igniter cannot produce a strong enough spark to light the gas reliably, or the grounding path has degraded.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~5%)** Corroded connectors or broken wires at the flame rod or igniter interrupt the signal path and cause intermittent or complete failure.
- **Control board malfunction (~5%)** The main circuit board may fail to process the flame signal correctly or fail to send the ignition command, though this is less common than sensor issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and turn off the gas supply valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws and carefully lifting the panel to access the burner chamber and ignition components.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon deposits, corrosion, or physical damage. Use fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to gently clean the rod surface until it is shiny.
4. **Check the flame rod wire connection** at the rod base and at the control board. Tighten or reseat any loose connectors and look for signs of corrosion or fraying.
5. **Examine the igniter** for cracks or heavy carbon buildup. Clean carefully or replace if damaged. Verify the spark gap meets manufacturer specifications.
6. **Verify gas supply pressure** using a manometer at the inlet test port. Consult your model's service manual for the correct inlet and manifold pressure values.
7. **Reassemble the unit** and restore power and gas. Run a hot water tap and observe the ignition sequence. If the error persists, call a licensed technician for further diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod and checking vents does not resolve the error, or if you suspect gas pressure problems. Professionals have manometers to measure inlet and manifold pressure accurately, combustion analyzers to verify safe operation, and the training to diagnose control board and gas valve faults. Any work involving gas connections, pressure testing, or circuit board replacement should be performed by a qualified service provider to maintain warranty coverage and safety compliance.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
