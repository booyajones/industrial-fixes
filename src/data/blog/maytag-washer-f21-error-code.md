## Maytag F21 Error Code — What It Means

F21 on a Maytag washer means the machine detected a long drain time or drain timeout condition. The tub did not empty fast enough during the cycle. In Whirlpool and Maytag-branded front-load platforms, the code is commonly treated as a drainage fault rather than a single-component failure. The washer is reporting that water is taking too long to leave the tub, and that points first to a restriction or failure in the drain path rather than automatically to the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump or control board first. Before buying any part, clean the drain filter and coin trap, check the drain hose for kinks, and inspect the sump hose for debris.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain pump filter or coin trap (~35%)** Lint, coins, socks, or debris accumulate in the filter or trap and block water flow out of the tub.
- **Kinked, pinched, or blocked drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose between the washer and standpipe is crushed, bent, or clogged with lint or debris.
- **Obstructed sump hose or pump inlet (~15%)** Debris is caught in the rubber sump hose or strainer path leading into the pump, restricting flow.
- **Weak, seized, or failed drain pump motor (~15%)** The pump motor does not run or runs too slowly to evacuate water in the allotted time.
- **Excess suds from non-HE or overdosed detergent (~5%)** Too much detergent or the wrong type creates foam that interferes with draining and sensing.
- **Blocked or disconnected pressure switch tube (~5%)** The air trap or pressure transducer tube is clogged, cracked, or disconnected, so the control cannot confirm the tub is empty.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and prepare towels and a shallow pan for residual water before opening the drain access.
2. **Access the drain filter or pump area** through the lower front panel or service panel, depending on your model.
3. **Open the drain filter or coin trap** slowly to let water drain into your pan, then remove the filter and clean out lint, coins, socks, or other debris. Reinstall the filter securely.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** from the washer to the standpipe for kinks, crushing, blockages, or improper installation. Straighten or replace the hose if needed.
5. **Check the sump hose and pump inlet** for trapped debris. On many failures, debris is found in the rubber sump hose or strainer path leading to the pump.
6. **Spin the pump impeller** by hand through the filter opening. It should turn freely without binding. If it is broken or seized, replace the pump assembly.
7. **Test the drain pump electrically** if the flow path is clear and the code persists. Consult your model's service data for resistance or voltage values, then replace the pump if it does not run correctly.
8. **Inspect the pressure switch or pressure transducer tube** and air trap for blockage, cracks, or disconnection. Clean or replace the tube and trap as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you have drained the filter, cleared all hoses, and confirmed the pump runs but the code still appears. That pattern suggests a pressure switch, control board, or motor issue that requires model-specific diagnostic tools and service data. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with residual water, lifting the front panel, or testing electrical components with a multimeter. A technician can perform voltage and resistance checks on the pump motor, pressure transducer, and wiring harness to isolate the fault and avoid replacing parts by trial and error.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
