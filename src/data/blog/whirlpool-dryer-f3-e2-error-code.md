## Whirlpool Dryer F3 E2 Error — What It Means

The F3 E2 error code on many Whirlpool dryer models typically indicates a problem with the thermistor or temperature-sensing circuit. The dryer's electronic control board is receiving an out-of-range signal from the temperature sensor, which monitors the air temperature inside the drum. This can mean the sensor itself has failed, the wiring to the sensor is damaged or loose, or in some cases the control board is misreading a good signal.

Because error code definitions can vary by model and year, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the dryer cabinet to confirm the exact meaning for your appliance. In general, F3 E2 points to the dryer's inability to accurately monitor heat, so it will stop the cycle to prevent overheating or unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, but a simple resistance check of the thermistor with a multimeter usually pinpoints the real fault and costs much less.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty thermistor (~55%)** The temperature sensor has drifted out of spec or opened completely, sending no signal or an erratic signal to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded thermistor connector (~25%)** The push-on connector at the thermistor or the harness plug has come loose, gotten wet, or corroded, breaking the circuit.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** A wire in the harness running to the thermistor has been pinched, cut, or melted by heat over time.
- **Failed electronic control board (~8%)** The control board's temperature-input circuit has failed and cannot read the sensor signal even when the sensor is good.
- **Incorrect thermistor installed (~2%)** A previous repair used the wrong thermistor with a different resistance curve, causing the board to read an out-of-range value.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off the gas supply if you have a gas model.
2. **Remove the front or rear access panel** depending on your dryer configuration (consult your model's service manual for the correct panel).
3. **Locate the thermistor**, usually clipped to the blower housing or attached to the exhaust duct near the heating element; it is a small cylinder or disc with two wires.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** and use a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) to measure across the sensor's two terminals; compare the reading to your model's specification sheet.
5. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the thermistor back to the control board for any cuts, pinches, or signs of heat damage; wiggle the connector while measuring resistance to check for intermittent breaks.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if it reads open (infinite resistance) or far outside the expected range; press the new sensor firmly into its mounting clip and reconnect the harness.
7. **Reassemble the dryer panels**, plug the machine back in, and run a timed-dry test cycle to confirm the F3 E2 error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with a multimeter or disassembling dryer panels. Also call a tech if you have replaced the thermistor and inspected all wiring but the F3 E2 code persists, since that points to a control-board fault that requires diagnostic software or a board swap. If your dryer is still under warranty, contact Whirlpool or an authorized service center before attempting any repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
