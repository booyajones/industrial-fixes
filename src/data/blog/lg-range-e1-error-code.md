## LG Range E1 Error Code — What It Means

The E1 code is not consistently documented in LG's official U.S. range error code list. However, LG does confirm that error code 1 (without the E prefix) indicates a thermistor error. If your display shows E1, it most likely represents a temperature-sensing fault related to the oven thermistor or sensor circuit, though the exact meaning may vary by model or region. The thermistor is a temperature probe that monitors oven heat and sends signals to the control board. When this circuit fails, the range cannot regulate temperature safely and throws the error.

Without your exact model number, it is difficult to confirm whether E1 is a factory code or a display variant. LG's official guidance for range error codes is to power cycle the unit and reset the circuit breaker. If the code returns, a component in the thermistor circuit has likely failed.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor** The thermistor probe inside the oven can fail due to age, heat cycling, or internal element damage.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections** The harness plug at the sensor or control board can loosen or corrode over time, breaking the signal path.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** Wires running from the sensor to the board can fray, short, or break if pinched behind panels or near hot surfaces.
- **Faulty main control board** The control board's thermistor input circuit can fail and misread sensor signals even when the probe is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait 30 seconds, then restore power to reset the control board.
2. **Check the display** when the range powers back on. If E1 or 1 does not reappear immediately, the fault may have been transient.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** (a metal probe projecting into the oven cavity, usually on the rear wall) and unplug its connector behind the range.
4. **Inspect the connector and wiring** for burns, corrosion, or loose pins. Clean contacts with electrical cleaner and reseat firmly.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter at room temperature. Consult your model's service manual for the correct ohm reading (typically 1,000–1,100 Ω at 70 °F, but verify your spec).
6. **Replace the oven sensor** if resistance is out of range or if the wire is damaged. Secure the new probe with the mounting bracket and reconnect the harness.
7. **Restore power and run a test bake cycle** at 350 °F for 15 minutes. If the code returns, the main control board may need replacement by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the error returns after you have replaced the oven sensor and verified all wiring, or if you are not comfortable working inside a live 240 V appliance. A pro can perform board-level diagnostics, confirm the exact code definition for your model, and access factory service bulletins. If your range is still under warranty, contact LG support before attempting any repair to avoid voiding coverage.
