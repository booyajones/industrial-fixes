## What this code means
The SE error code (sometimes displayed as 5E on older digital panels where the digit looks like the letter S) tells you the washer has detected a slow-draining fault. The control board has determined that water is not leaving the tub at the expected rate during the drain cycle. The machine will pause, stop the cycle, and display the code to alert you before attempting to restart.

Samsung explicitly states that SE or 5E indicates a drain problem where the washer cannot remove water fast enough. This is almost always a mechanical blockage or installation error, not a failed pump motor. The fault triggers when the pump runs but water level stays too high for too long.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump motor first, but over 90% of SE codes are fixed by cleaning the pump filter or clearing a kinked hose. Always clean the filter and check hose routing before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged pump filter (~60%)** The filter accumulates lint, coins, pet hair, and small items, blocking water flow to the pump.
- **Kinked or crushed drain hose (~20%)** The external drain hose is twisted, pinched behind the machine, or clogged internally by debris.
- **Incorrect drain hose height or depth (~10%)** The hose is installed too low (causing siphoning) or pushed too far into the standpipe (sealing off air flow and preventing proper drainage).
- **Excessive suds from detergent (~5%)** Using too much HE detergent or standard (non-HE) detergent creates foam that blocks the drain sensor or physically restricts water flow.
- **Obstructed pump impeller (~3%)** Debris has bypassed the filter and jammed the pump impeller inside the pump housing.
- **Failed drain pump motor (~2%)** The pump motor has failed or the impeller blades are broken (requires electrical testing to confirm).

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and turn off the water supply valves.
2. **Place towels and a shallow pan** on the floor in front of the lower access door to catch residual water.
3. **Open the small access door** at the lower front of the washer (usually on the right side).
4. **Locate the small black rubber drain tube** next to the filter, unclip it, remove the cap, and let water drain into the pan. Re-cap and clip the tube when empty.
5. **Remove the pump filter** by twisting it counterclockwise and pulling it straight out. Inspect the filter and the housing cavity behind it for coins, lint, hair, and debris. Rinse the filter under warm water and scrub clean.
6. **Reinstall the filter** by inserting it into the housing and twisting clockwise until it clicks or snugs. Do not overtighten.
7. **Inspect the external drain hose** for kinks, twists, or internal clogs. Straighten any kinks and verify the hose is positioned 28-96 inches above the floor and inserted no more than 8 inches into the standpipe.
8. **Plug the washer back in**, run a rinse-and-spin cycle with no load, and watch for proper drainage and no error code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the filter, cleared the hose, verified correct hose height and depth, and the SE code persists. A pro can test the drain pump motor electrically, inspect the pump housing for hidden obstructions, and check the control board for false fault signals. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with residual water, accessing the lower front panel, or removing and reinstalling the pump filter. If the pump motor needs replacement and you lack confidence in disconnecting wiring harnesses or handling the pump assembly, a service call will save time and prevent damage to hose clamps or electrical connectors.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
