## Bosch Washer F19 Error Code — What It Means

Bosch publishes F19 (also displayed as E19 on some models) as 'Heating time has been exceeded.' The washer control has timed out waiting for the water to reach target temperature during the wash cycle. The machine is trying to heat the water but the heating circuit is either not working or taking too long, so the control board flags the fault and stops the cycle.

This is a heating-system fault, not a drain or door issue. The error indicates that either no heat is being generated or the sensor cannot confirm that temperature is rising at the expected rate. The fault can be caused by a problem in the heater itself, the sensor that monitors water temperature, the wiring that connects them, or the control board relay that powers the heater.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real culprit is a failed heating element or corroded connector at the heater. Use a multimeter to test heater continuity and sensor resistance before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Heating element failure (~40%)** The electric heater has failed open or shorted, so no heat is generated and the timer expires.
- **Temperature sensor fault (~25%)** The thermistor is out of range or reading incorrectly, so the control never sees the temperature rise even if the heater works.
- **Limescale buildup on the heater (~15%)** Hard-water deposits insulate the element from the water, slowing heat transfer and extending heat-up time beyond the timeout.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~10%)** Burned spade terminals, oxidized connectors, or broken wires at the heater or sensor prevent proper electrical contact.
- **Control board or relay failure (~7%)** The relay or triac on the main board that switches heater power has failed, so voltage never reaches the element.
- **Low mains voltage (~3%)** Reduced household supply voltage lowers heater wattage and extends heat-up time enough to trigger the timeout.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the washer.** Unplug the machine for two minutes, then plug it back in and run a hot cycle to see if the code returns. Bosch recommends a restart before deeper diagnosis.
2. **Unplug and access the heater compartment.** Most Bosch front-loaders place the heater and thermistor at the front lower edge of the outer tub, behind the lower front panel or through the rear access panel depending on model.
3. **Inspect heater and sensor wiring.** Look for burned, loose, or corroded spade connectors on the heating element terminals and the thermistor plug. Clean or replace damaged connectors.
4. **Test the heating element.** Disconnect both heater wires and measure resistance across the element terminals. A typical washer heater reads 20–50 ohms (consult your model's service sheet). Also check from each terminal to ground (tank or chassis) to confirm no short to earth.
5. **Test the thermistor.** Unplug the sensor and measure its resistance at room temperature. Compare the reading to the manufacturer's thermistor table for your model. An open circuit or wildly incorrect reading means the sensor has failed.
6. **Check for limescale.** If the heater tests good electrically but shows heavy white or gray buildup, descale it or replace it. Scale reduces heat transfer and extends warm-up time.
7. **Replace the defective component.** Install a new heater, thermistor, or wiring harness as needed. Clear any stored fault code by unplugging the washer for a few minutes, then test with a hot wash cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with mains-voltage components inside a washer, if you lack a multimeter or do not know how to test resistance safely, or if both the heater and sensor test within specification yet the code persists (which points to a control-board fault). Technicians have service manuals with exact resistance tables, relay-test procedures, and the tools to verify water-level pressure switches and wiring integrity across the harness. If your washer is still under warranty, contact Bosch support before opening panels, since DIY disassembly may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on the part.
