## What this code means
The F19 (or E19) error code on a Bosch washer means the heating time has been exceeded. The machine's control system expected the wash water to reach temperature within a set window, but heating did not complete in time. Bosch's official support pages confirm this code indicates a heating fault, not a drain or fill problem.

In practical terms, your washer is flagging that something in the heating circuit is preventing normal water temperature rise during a hot or warm wash cycle. The machine will usually stop mid-cycle and display the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board first, assuming an electronics fault. Always test the heating element and temperature sensor with a multimeter before buying any board. Most F19 codes trace to a failed heater or sensor, not the electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element (~50%)** The heater coil is open, shorted, or damaged and cannot warm the water, so the timer expires before temperature is reached.
- **Defective temperature sensor (thermistor/NTC) (~25%)** The sensor is out of spec or intermittent, so the control never sees the correct temperature feedback and times out.
- **Wiring or connector fault (~10%)** Loose, corroded, or burnt terminals at the heater or sensor disrupt the circuit and prevent proper heating or signal.
- **Limescale buildup on heater (~10%)** Heavy scale on the element insulates it from the water and slows heat transfer, causing the heating cycle to exceed the allowed time.
- **Control-board fault (~5%)** The electronics module fails to supply power to the heater or misreads sensor feedback, even when the heater and sensor test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves.
2. **Access the heater assembly** by removing the rear or front lower panel (consult your model's service manual for exact location, often at the base of the tub).
3. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** at the heater and temperature sensor for burn marks, corrosion, looseness, or heat damage. Clean or re-seat any suspect terminals.
4. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter set to resistance. Disconnect the heater leads and measure across the element terminals. Consult your model's service data for the expected resistance value. Replace the heater if it reads open (infinite resistance) or shorted (near zero).
5. **Test the temperature sensor (NTC thermistor)** by measuring resistance across its terminals at room temperature. Compare to the model-specific specification. Replace the sensor if it is out of range or shows an intermittent reading.
6. **Check for limescale buildup** on the heater surface and in the tub area. If heavy scale is present, descale the heater using a citric-acid or vinegar solution, or replace the heater if scale cannot be removed.
7. **Reassemble the panels**, restore power and water, clear any stored fault codes (consult your user manual), and run a monitored hot-water wash cycle to verify that the machine heats normally and the F19 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with electrical components or if you lack a multimeter and service documentation to test the heater and sensor safely. A technician can verify the exact fault with proper test equipment, confirm model-specific resistance values, and replace the correct part without trial-and-error. Also call if the heater and sensor both test within specification but the code persists, because that points to a control-board or wiring fault that requires deeper diagnostic tools. Bosch's official guidance recommends contacting Bosch Support or an authorized service provider for proper assessment of F19 heating faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–90 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280.
