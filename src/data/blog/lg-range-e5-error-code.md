## LG Range E5 Error Code — What It Means

On LG ranges where E5 is documented, the code indicates a thermistor or temperature-sensing fault. LG's official support materials identify this as a problem with the oven sensor (thermistor) that monitors cavity temperature. The sensor may be shorted, open, or sending an invalid signal to the control board. Not every LG range model uses E5 for this function, so consult your specific model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the exact meaning before ordering parts.

The error can appear during preheat, bake, or self-clean cycles when the control board expects a valid resistance reading from the sensor but receives an out-of-range or missing signal. In some cases the code will clear after a power cycle, but if the underlying sensor or wiring fault remains, E5 will return as soon as the oven attempts to heat.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first when the real culprit is the oven sensor. Before ordering a new board, visually inspect the sensor probe and its wiring harness for damage, and check connector pins for corrosion or looseness.

## Common Causes

- **Defective oven sensor (thermistor) (~55%)** The probe may be shorted, open, or drifting out of the expected resistance range, causing the board to reject the signal and throw E5.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~20%)** The plug between the sensor harness and the control board can develop oxidation, push back, or loose pins that intermittently break the circuit.
- **Damaged sensor wiring (~12%)** Wires routed near the oven cavity or hinge can be pinched, melted, or chafed through insulation, creating a short or open circuit.
- **Failed main control board sensor input (~10%)** The PCB circuit that reads the thermistor can fail even when the sensor itself tests good, misinterpreting a valid signal as a fault.
- **Incorrect sensor for the model (~3%)** Installing a thermistor with the wrong resistance curve after a previous repair will generate E5 because the board expects a specific resistance table.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait 30 seconds, then restore power and press the range's power button to see if the code clears.
2. **Pull the range forward** (disconnect gas if applicable, or unplug electric) to access the rear panel or lower rear access cover where the sensor mounts.
3. **Locate the oven sensor probe**, typically a thin metal rod protruding into the oven cavity from the back wall, with a two-pin connector on the exterior side of the cavity or at the control board.
4. **Inspect the sensor and harness** for burns, cracks in the ceramic insulator, pinched wires, or push-back at the connector. Wiggle the connector to check for looseness or corrosion on the pins.
5. **Disconnect the sensor plug** and, if you have your model's resistance chart, use a multimeter on the ohms scale to measure across the sensor terminals at room temperature. If the reading is open (infinite), shorted (near zero), or far outside the expected range, replace the sensor.
6. **Install the new oven sensor** by threading it through the mounting bracket, securing the bracket screws, and plugging the harness connector firmly onto the board or junction.
7. **Restore power**, run a bake cycle at 350 °F for 10 minutes, and verify that the oven heats normally and E5 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working behind the range, if your unit is gas and you lack experience safely disconnecting the supply line, or if the new sensor does not resolve E5 and you suspect the main control board. A pro can test the board's thermistor-input circuit with a decade box or known-good sensor, confirm the correct resistance curve for your model, and replace the board if necessary. Also call for service if the sensor wiring is routed inside the oven cavity insulation or welded sheet metal that requires disassembly beyond removing a simple rear panel.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–60 for sensor, 20–40 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250 service call including part.
