## Maytag Dishwasher F7E2 Error Code — What It Means

The F7E2 error code on a Maytag dishwasher signals a wash motor or circulation pump fault. The control board has detected that the wash motor is not operating correctly. This code points to the circulation system that sprays water during the wash cycle, rather than a drain-only problem. Maytag's first instruction is to cycle power to reset the control, and if the code returns, the fault is usually tied to the motor or pump assembly itself.

The control monitors the wash motor and throws F7E2 when it sees no feedback, incorrect speed, or physical obstruction preventing normal operation. The fault can be mechanical (debris jamming the impeller), electrical (corroded connectors or a failed motor winding), or electronic (a control board or feedback sensor issue). Because the same code letter combinations appear on other Whirlpool-family appliances for different faults, always confirm you are working on a dishwasher and not a washer or dryer.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but a wash motor with a seized bearing or corroded connector is far more common. Always inspect the sump for debris and test the motor wiring before buying a new control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed wash motor or circulation pump assembly (~40%)** The motor windings can burn out, bearings seize, or the impeller crack, preventing the motor from spinning or drawing current correctly.
- **Debris or obstruction in the sump or impeller area (~25%)** Food particles, broken glass, or other foreign objects can jam the impeller and prevent the motor from turning freely, triggering the fault.
- **Corroded or damaged wiring and connectors (~20%)** Water exposure can corrode the motor harness plug or inline connectors, creating high resistance or an open circuit that the control interprets as a motor fault.
- **Faulty speed sensor or feedback circuit (~10%)** Some models use a Hall-effect sensor or tachometer signal to confirm motor speed, and a failed sensor can cause F7E2 even when the motor runs.
- **Main control board fault (~5%)** If the motor, wiring, and feedback components all test good, a failed relay or driver circuit on the control board can prevent motor operation and log F7E2.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the dishwasher** by turning off the circuit breaker or unplugging the unit for at least 60 seconds, then restore power and run a test cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Remove the lower dish rack, spray arm, and filter assembly** to expose the sump area at the bottom of the tub.
3. **Inspect the sump and impeller** for debris, broken glass, or any object that could jam the wash motor, and clear any obstructions you find.
4. **Check that the impeller spins freely** by rotating it gently by hand. If it binds or feels gritty, the motor bearing may be seized.
5. **Disconnect power, then unplug the motor harness connector** (usually found under the dishwasher near the motor) and inspect the pins for corrosion, water damage, or looseness. Clean or replace the connector if needed.
6. **Test the wash motor windings** with a multimeter set to resistance. Consult your model's wiring diagram for expected values. An open circuit or very high resistance indicates a failed motor.
7. **Replace the circulation pump or wash motor assembly** if the motor tests bad or the impeller will not turn. Reconnect all hoses and wiring, then run a test cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are not comfortable working under the dishwasher or if the motor tests good but the code returns after you clear obstructions and clean connectors. A technician can perform electrical tests on the control board, check for proper voltage at the motor during a cycle, and diagnose feedback sensor faults that require a wiring diagram. Also call if you find water pooling under the unit, which may indicate a leaking pump seal or hose that should be addressed at the same time.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $120–$250 in parts (circulation pump assembly), 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200–$400 including labor and parts.
