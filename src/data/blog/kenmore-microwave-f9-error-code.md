## Kenmore Microwave F9 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 error code appears on Kenmore microwave models that use an inverter board to regulate power. It signals that the inverter system is not operating correctly. The inverter is the high-frequency power supply used in these units to control magnetron output and heating performance.

This fault is specific to inverter-equipped Kenmore microwaves. On models without an inverter, a different diagnostic path applies. The code typically prevents the microwave from heating or running at all until the underlying problem is corrected.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the inverter board without first testing the door interlock switches. A failed door switch can mimic inverter symptoms and costs far less to replace. Always verify door-switch continuity before ordering the inverter.

## Common Causes

- **Failed inverter board (~70%)** The inverter board develops shorted components or fails internally, preventing the microwave from generating the high-frequency power needed to run the magnetron.
- **Defective door interlock switch (~20%)** One of the door switches fails or loses continuity, which the control board interprets as a safety fault and throws the F9 code on some models.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~5%)** A poor connection between the inverter board and the main control or between door switches and the harness can trigger the fault.
- **Control-board misread (~5%)** In rare cases the main control board itself sends the F9 code incorrectly even when the inverter and switches are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and wait two full minutes to allow stored high-voltage capacitors to discharge and to reset the control board.
2. **Plug the unit back in** and check whether the F9 code reappears immediately or only when you attempt to start a cook cycle.
3. **Confirm your model uses an inverter board** by checking the model-number plate and cross-referencing with an online Kenmore parts diagram or service bulletin.
4. **Access the door-switch assembly** by removing the outer cabinet or control panel (consult your model's disassembly guide) and locate the three or four door interlock switches mounted near the latch.
5. **Test each door switch for continuity** with the door closed and open using a multimeter, noting which terminals should close and open according to your wiring diagram.
6. **Inspect the inverter board** for burn marks, swollen capacitors, or obvious damage, and check all wire-harness connectors for corrosion or loose pins.
7. **Replace the inverter board** if door switches test good and the board shows signs of failure or if the fault persists after all other checks, then reassemble and verify normal heating and operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Microwave repair involves high-voltage capacitors that can store lethal charge even when unplugged. If you are not trained in appliance disassembly and capacitor discharge procedures, call a qualified service technician. A pro will safely verify door-switch operation, test the inverter board output, and perform a post-repair microwave-leakage check to confirm the unit is safe to use. Inverter boards and high-voltage harnesses require careful handling and proper grounding during installation.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on model and inverter board price.
