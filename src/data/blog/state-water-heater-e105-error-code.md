## State Water Heater E105 Error Code — What It Means

E105 is not a universally documented error code across all State water heater models, and the exact meaning depends on your specific unit. State products (manufactured by A. O. Smith) use different diagnostic codes for tankless, hybrid heat-pump, and tank-style units. If your heater is a tankless or power-vent model, E105 most likely indicates an ignition failure or combustion-related shutdown caused by air supply problems, gas delivery issues, or venting restrictions.

Before attempting any repair, locate your exact model number and consult the service manual or diagnostic table on the unit itself. Many ignition-related codes share similar root causes: blocked air intake screens, restricted exhaust vents, low gas pressure, or insufficient water flow triggering a safety lockout. Combustion faults can also result from scale buildup on heat exchangers or faulty flame sensors, especially in units that have been running for several years without descaling service.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted air intake** Lint, dust, insect nests, or debris on the intake screen starve the burner of combustion air and force an ignition lockout.
- **Obstructed exhaust vent or flue** Crushed termination caps, bird nests, ice buildup in winter, or improper vent pipe slope prevent exhaust gases from leaving and trigger a safety shutdown.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Partially closed gas valve, air in the line after a utility interruption, or inadequate gas pressure to the unit prevent ignition.
- **Low water flow or dirty inlet filter** Clogged inlet screen or insufficient flow rate prevent the unit from firing because minimum-flow safeties are not satisfied.
- **Scale or mineral buildup on heat exchanger or flame sensor** Hard-water deposits insulate sensors from flame contact or reduce heat transfer, causing intermittent or failed flame detection.
- **Faulty flame sensor or ignition component** Corroded flame-rod connections, worn igniter electrode, or failed sensor circuit prevent the control board from confirming ignition even when gas and air are present.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Write down your complete model number** from the rating plate on the water heater and locate the diagnostic code chart in your installation manual or on the inside of the front cover to confirm what E105 means for your specific unit.
2. **Turn off gas and power** at the appliance shutoff valve and circuit breaker, then let the unit cool for at least ten minutes before inspecting any combustion components.
3. **Remove and clean the air intake screen** by unscrewing or unclipping the inlet filter housing, rinsing the screen under running water, and checking for any cracks or damage that would allow unfiltered air into the blower.
4. **Inspect the exhaust vent and intake termination** outside the building for obstructions, crushed pipe, bird or wasp nests, ice, and proper clearance from windows or other openings, then check all indoor vent joints for disconnection or sag.
5. **Check the inlet water filter** by shutting off the cold supply valve, relieving pressure from a nearby hot tap, unscrewing the filter housing at the water inlet, and rinsing the mesh screen free of sediment.
6. **Restore gas and power**, then press the reset button or power-cycle the unit and observe whether it ignites normally and whether the error clears after one or two heating cycles.
7. **If the code returns immediately or after a few cycles**, turn off the unit and call a licensed technician to test gas pressure at the valve, inspect the flame sensor for fouling, check igniter and flame-rod continuity, and descale the heat exchanger if scale buildup is suspected.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the error persists after you have cleaned the intake and inlet filters, confirmed proper venting, and verified that gas is flowing to other appliances. Combustion diagnostics require a manometer to measure gas pressure, a multimeter to test flame-sensor microamp signals, and sometimes a descaling pump to remove scale from the heat exchanger. Any work involving gas-line connections, pressure testing, or control-board replacement should be performed by a qualified professional to protect your warranty and meet local codes. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building immediately and call your utility or emergency services before attempting further diagnosis.
