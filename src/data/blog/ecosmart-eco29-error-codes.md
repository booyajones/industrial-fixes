## EcoSmart ECO 29 E1 Error — What It Means

The E1 code on an EcoSmart tankless water heater indicates a thermistor or temperature sensor failure. When the thermistor fails, the LED display shows E1 and the unit will not function correctly until the sensor is replaced. The thermistor monitors water temperature and sends that data to the control board. Without a working sensor, the heater cannot regulate output temperature safely.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor** The temperature sensor itself has gone out of spec or stopped communicating with the control board.
- **Loose or corroded connector** The plug between the thermistor wire and the control board may have worked loose or developed corrosion over time.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** The red wire running from the thermistor to the board can be pinched, cut, or burned by nearby hot surfaces.
- **Inadequate electrical supply** Low or unstable voltage at the unit can cause erratic behavior and make sensor faults appear intermittently.
- **Control board issue** If the board cannot read sensor data correctly, it may report E1 even when the thermistor is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the breaker** feeding the water heater and verify the unit is fully de-energized with a meter.
2. **Remove the front cover** to access the internal control board and thermistor connections.
3. **Locate the thermistor** and trace the red wire back to its plug on the control board.
4. **Unplug the sensor** from the board and inspect both the plug and socket for corrosion, burn marks, or loose pins.
5. **Check electrical supply** by measuring across the black and red terminals on the board (should read 208 to 240 V depending on your service panel).
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the E1 code is present and connections are clean (the manufacturer specifies sensor replacement for E1).
7. **Restore power** and run hot water to verify the code clears and the unit maintains target temperature.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or electrician if you are not comfortable working with 240 V circuits or if the E1 code persists after replacing the thermistor and verifying all connections. If your voltage reading at the heater terminals is 0.00 or well below 208 V, the issue may be upstream at the breaker panel and will require electrical troubleshooting. EcoSmart technical support can also guide you through further diagnostics if the control board or other components are suspect.
