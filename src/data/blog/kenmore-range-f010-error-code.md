## Kenmore F10 Error Code — What It Means

The F10 code on a Kenmore range or oven signals a runaway temperature or over-temperature fault. The electronic oven control (EOC) has detected that the oven temperature has climbed past the setpoint or is heating uncontrollably. This fault can appear during normal baking or during the self-clean cycle.

The control board shuts down heating to protect the appliance and displays F10. A simple power reset may clear a transient fault, but if the code returns the same day or the next time you use the oven, a component has usually failed and needs replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control board first. Before buying a new board, test the oven temperature sensor with a multimeter in ohms mode. A sensor out of the 1,000–1,300 ohm range at room temperature is the actual problem in the majority of F10 calls.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (probe) (~50%)** The resistance drifts out of the normal 1,100-ohm range at room temperature, sending false high-temperature readings to the control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring or loose connector (~20%)** Frayed wires, burned terminals, or a loose plug between the sensor and the control board create an intermittent or open circuit that mimics runaway temperature.
- **Failed electronic oven control (EOC) with stuck relay (~20%)** A shorted or stuck heating relay on the control board continues to energize the bake or broil element even after the set temperature is reached.
- **Blocked airflow or excessive heat buildup (~7%)** Poor ventilation around the range, a blocked convection fan, or heavy insulation damage can cause an actual over-temperature condition that trips the fault.
- **Self-clean cycle heat stress (~3%)** The high heat of self-clean can push marginal sensors, wiring, or control relays over the edge and trigger F10 during or immediately after the cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Let the oven cool** completely, then restore power and observe whether the F10 code clears on its own.
3. **Power-cycle the range** by removing power for 1–2 minutes to reset the control board, then plug back in or flip the breaker.
4. **Remove the oven temperature sensor** from the back wall of the oven cavity (usually two hex screws or a bracket) and disconnect the wire plug.
5. **Test sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature (around 70–75°F) the sensor should read close to 1,100 ohms. Replace the sensor if it reads below 1,000 ohms, above 1,300 ohms, or shows open/OL.
6. **Inspect the sensor harness** for frayed insulation, burned terminals, or loose connectors between the sensor and the control board. Repair or replace damaged wiring.
7. **Replace the electronic oven control board** if the sensor and wiring both test correctly and the F10 code returns immediately after a power reset.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if the range is hardwired and you cannot safely isolate power, or if the fault persists after you have replaced both the sensor and the control board. A technician can perform a full circuit trace, check for high-voltage shorts, and verify that the bake and broil elements are not drawing excessive current. Gas ranges with electronic ignition also require a technician to check gas valve relays and make sure no gas-safety interlocks have failed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
