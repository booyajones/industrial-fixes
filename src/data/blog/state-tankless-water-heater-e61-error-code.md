## State Tankless Water Heater E61 Error Code — What It Means

The E61 error on State tankless water heaters indicates a combustion fan or blower RPM fault. The control board has detected that the fan is not reaching or reporting the expected speed, so the unit locks out for safety. This prevents improper combustion or exhaust gas buildup by stopping the heater from firing until the airflow problem is resolved.

The heater relies on the combustion fan to move air through the burner chamber and exhaust system. When the board cannot confirm the fan is spinning correctly, it assumes an unsafe air or exhaust condition exists and triggers the E61 code to protect your home.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked exhaust vent or intake termination** Debris, bird nests, ice, or a bent termination cap can restrict airflow and prevent the fan from reaching target RPM.
- **Worn or failing combustion fan motor** The blower motor may be seized, have worn bearings, or simply be unable to spin fast enough to satisfy the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring between fan and PCB** Broken connectors, corroded pins, or damaged wires interrupt the fan speed signal to the control board.
- **Dirt or debris inside the fan housing** Dust, lint, or foreign objects can jam the impeller or slow the blades enough to trigger the fault.
- **Faulty main control board** The PCB may misread a good fan signal or fail to send proper voltage to drive the motor correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the water heater at the breaker and gas shutoff valve before opening any panels or touching components.
2. **Inspect the exhaust termination** outside your home for visible blockages, nests, ice, or damage to the cap, then clear any obstructions and check that the vent pipe is straight and unkinked.
3. **Remove the front cover** and visually inspect the combustion fan housing and blades for dirt, debris, or anything preventing free rotation, then clean the impeller and housing if needed.
4. **Check all wiring and connectors** between the fan motor and the control board for loose pins, corrosion, breaks, or heat damage, then reseat or repair any faulty connections.
5. **Test the fan motor electrically** using a multimeter to measure resistance or continuity across the motor leads, then compare your reading to the spec in your model's service manual if available.
6. **Verify control signals** at the fan connector with the unit powered on (gas still off) to confirm the board is sending voltage to the motor and receiving a speed feedback signal, or replace the board if signals are absent.
7. **Replace the failed component** (fan motor, harness, or PCB) identified by your tests, then restore power and gas, clear the error code, and run the heater through a full heat cycle to confirm normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water heater technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the exhaust vent runs through walls or the roof and you cannot safely inspect the full length, or if you have replaced the fan motor and checked all wiring but the E61 code returns. A qualified technician has the tools to measure fan RPM, verify control board outputs, and safely diagnose faults in the combustion air and exhaust system. Any work involving gas connections or venting modifications should always be performed by a professional to meet local codes and protect your safety.
