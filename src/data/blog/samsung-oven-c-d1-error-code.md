## Samsung Oven C-d1 Error Code — What It Means

The C-d1 code on a Samsung oven or range signals a door lock mechanism problem. The control board has detected that the door lock actuator cannot complete its movement, the position switch is reporting the wrong state, or the latch is physically obstructed. This fault commonly appears after a self-clean cycle or when the oven tries to lock the door for high-heat operation and the mechanism becomes stuck or fails to respond correctly.

The code does not indicate a heating element or temperature sensor failure. Instead it points directly to the mechanical and electrical components of the door lock assembly, including the motor that drives the latch, the microswitch that confirms latch position, and the wiring between the lock and the main control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but the lock motor or switch usually fails first. Test the lock motor and switch for continuity and proper movement before replacing any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or jammed lock motor actuator (~40%)** The lock motor cannot drive the cam and latch through its full travel, often because grease has hardened or the motor itself has worn out.
- **Faulty door lock position switch (~25%)** The microswitch in the lock assembly does not change state correctly when the latch moves, so the control board sees a mismatch between the commanded position and the feedback signal.
- **Debris or grease blocking the latch (~20%)** Carbonized food, spills, or hardened grease physically prevent the latch hook or rod from moving freely, which stops the actuator from completing its cycle.
- **Damaged wiring or connectors (~10%)** Broken wires, loose pins, or corroded connectors between the main control and the lock assembly interrupt the motor power or switch feedback.
- **Main control board lock circuit fault (~5%)** The control board fails to deliver the correct signal to the lock motor or misreads the switch feedback, though this is less common than mechanical or switch failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker and wait 60 seconds, then restore power to attempt a reset of the lock actuator and clear the code.
2. **Open the oven door** and inspect the door lock latch, hook, and surrounding frame for any visible debris, grease buildup, or obstructions, then clean thoroughly with degreaser and a stiff brush.
3. **Remove the rear access panel** (or the appropriate service panel for your model) to expose the door lock motor assembly, which is typically mounted near the top of the oven cavity behind the control panel.
4. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the lock motor and use a multimeter to test continuity across the motor terminals, then reconnect and observe whether the motor drives the cam when power is restored (the motor should turn the cam firmly without grinding or stalling).
5. **Test the position microswitch** by manually moving the lock cam to each position and checking for a change in continuity across the switch terminals (a good switch will open or close cleanly at each end of travel).
6. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** between the lock assembly and the main control board for broken strands, melted insulation, or corroded pins, and repair or replace any damaged sections.
7. **Replace the lock motor assembly or switch** if testing shows a failed component, then reassemble the panels, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring behind the oven panels, if the lock assembly is difficult to access on your model, or if you have replaced both the lock motor and switch and the C-d1 code persists. A pro can perform precise continuity and voltage tests on the main control board's lock circuit, verify that the board is sending correct commands to the lock motor, and replace the control board if the lock output has failed. Technicians also have model-specific service manuals and wiring diagrams that show exact connector pinouts and resistance specifications, which can speed diagnosis when multiple components are suspect.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
