## Kenmore Range F07 Error Code — What It Means

The F07 error code on a Kenmore range typically indicates a problem with the control panel or keypad. The control board has detected that a button or key is stuck, shorted, or continuously registering a signal when it should not be. This prevents normal operation because the control believes a command is being held down. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the panel for model-specific details.

In many cases, the issue is not a failed part but rather debris, grease, or moisture under the membrane keypad causing a false signal. Less often, the control board itself has failed or a connector has worked loose. The range will usually not allow you to operate it until the fault clears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real problem is just a dirty or damaged membrane keypad. Clean the keypad thoroughly and inspect for cracks or stuck keys before spending money on a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or shorted button (~50%)** Grease, food debris, or a damaged membrane under a key causes a continuous signal that the control reads as a fault.
- **Moisture or spill under the keypad (~20%)** Liquid that has seeped behind the touchpad creates a short circuit between contacts, triggering the error.
- **Failed membrane keypad (~15%)** The flexible circuit inside the keypad has cracked or worn out, sending false signals even when no button is pressed.
- **Loose or corroded ribbon connector (~10%)** The flat cable linking the keypad to the control board has shifted or oxidized, causing intermittent or false signals.
- **Faulty main control board (~5%)** The control board itself has a component failure that misreads the keypad input or generates the error internally.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the range** or flip the dedicated breaker to cut all power before opening any panels.
2. **Remove the control panel.** Depending on your model, this usually means taking out screws along the top edge or sides of the panel, then lifting or tilting it forward to access the back.
3. **Inspect the membrane keypad** for visible damage, stuck keys, or residue. Press each button to confirm it returns freely.
4. **Clean the keypad surface** with a soft damp cloth and a drop of mild dish soap, then dry it completely. Pay attention to the edges where spills collect.
5. **Check the ribbon cable connector** where the keypad plugs into the control board. Unplug it, look for corrosion or bent pins, wipe the contacts gently with isopropyl alcohol, and reseat it firmly.
6. **Reassemble and restore power.** If the error persists, replace the membrane keypad first since it is the most common culprit and less expensive than the control board.
7. **Test the range** by selecting a burner or oven function. If F07 returns immediately, and you have already replaced the keypad, the main control board is likely at fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the control panel or if the range uses a direct hardwired connection instead of a plug. A technician can test the keypad and control board with a multimeter to confirm which has failed, saving you from buying the wrong part. If the error returns after you have replaced both the keypad and the control board, the wiring harness or a less common component may be the cause, and a pro with diagnostic tools and experience will resolve it faster.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
