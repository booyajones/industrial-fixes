## Daikin Mini Split LC-02 Error — What It Means

The LC-02 error code on a Daikin mini split system signals a communication problem between the indoor air handler and the outdoor condensing unit. The two units exchange information continuously over control wiring, and when that signal is interrupted, degraded, or missing, the system shuts down to protect itself.

This code typically points to wiring issues rather than a failed component. The control cable may be loose, damaged, or incorrectly terminated. In some cases, electrical noise from nearby equipment or a fault in the indoor or outdoor control board can also trigger the fault. Because the exact definition of LC codes can vary by model series, consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram to confirm the precise meaning for your unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many technicians replace the indoor or outdoor control board first, assuming a circuit failure. Always inspect and reseat all control wiring terminals and check for visible damage before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded control wiring connections (~45%)** Terminal screws at the indoor or outdoor unit work loose over time, or corrosion builds up on the wire ends, breaking the communication link.
- **Damaged control cable (~25%)** The multi-conductor cable running between units may be pinched during installation, chewed by rodents, or degraded by UV exposure if routed outdoors without conduit.
- **Incorrect wiring or polarity (~15%)** Installers sometimes reverse polarity on the control wires or land them on the wrong terminals, especially after a service call or board replacement.
- **Electrical noise interference (~8%)** High-voltage wiring, motor starters, or other equipment near the control cable can induce noise that corrupts the signal between units.
- **Failed indoor or outdoor control board (~7%)** A component failure on either board can prevent proper communication, though this is less common than wiring issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or disconnect switch serving both the indoor and outdoor units.
2. **Remove the cover** on the indoor unit and locate the control board and terminal block where the control wiring lands.
3. **Inspect and reseat** each control wire at the indoor terminal block, checking for tightness and any signs of corrosion or burn marks.
4. **Trace the control cable** from the indoor unit to the outdoor unit, looking for pinches, cuts, or areas where insulation is damaged.
5. **Open the outdoor unit** electrical compartment and check the control wire terminals on the outdoor board, tightening screws and cleaning any corrosion.
6. **Verify wiring polarity** by comparing the actual wire colors and terminal positions to the wiring diagram inside each unit or in the installation manual.
7. **Restore power** and observe whether the LC-02 code reappears. If it clears, monitor the system over the next few cycles to confirm stability.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the electrical compartments of either unit, if the wiring diagram is unclear, or if the code persists after you have checked and corrected all visible wiring issues. A technician can measure communication voltage, test board outputs with a multimeter, and safely handle any high-voltage circuits that share the same compartment. Also call a pro if you suspect a board has failed, since proper diagnosis requires signal tracing and board-level testing that goes beyond simple visual inspection.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in wire or terminals, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
