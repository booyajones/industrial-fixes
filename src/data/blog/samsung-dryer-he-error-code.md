## Samsung Dryer hE Error Code — What It Means

The hE (or HC) code on a Samsung dryer signals a heating-related fault. The control board is detecting either insufficient heating, overheating, or invalid temperature-sensor feedback from the heater circuit. In practical terms, the dryer is not reaching the expected temperature during a cycle, or it is exceeding safe temperature limits and triggering protection.

The code appears on both electric and gas models. It does not mean the door is open or the load is unbalanced. The control is specifically monitoring the heater system and has flagged a problem with how heat is being generated, sensed, or controlled.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or heating element first. Before buying parts, check airflow by disconnecting the vent and running a timed cycle empty. Most hE codes clear once airflow is restored.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted airflow** A clogged lint screen, blocked external vent, crushed flex duct, or excessively long vent run prevents proper air movement and causes abnormal heating or overheating.
- **Lint-screen contamination** Fabric softener residue can coat the mesh and reduce airflow even when the screen appears clean.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor** The sensor provides temperature feedback to the control, and when it fails or drifts out of spec the board flags a heating error.
- **Open thermal fuse or thermostat** Safety devices in the heater circuit open when they sense overheating or abnormal conditions, cutting power to the heating element and triggering the code.
- **Failed heating element (electric) or gas valve/igniter (gas)** A broken or grounded heating element, or a faulty gas-ignition component, prevents normal heating and generates the error.
- **Control board or relay fault** If all field components test good and airflow is correct, the main control board or its heater drive relay may be driving the heater incorrectly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** or turn off the circuit breaker to cut power before inspecting any components.
2. **Remove and inspect the lint screen.** If you see any fabric softener residue or film, wash the screen with warm soapy water, rinse thoroughly, and let it dry completely.
3. **Disconnect the vent hose** from the back of the dryer and check for lint buildup or crushing. Inspect the entire vent path to the outside, including the external vent hood, and clear any blockages.
4. **Restore power and run an empty timed dry cycle** (no load) for 10 minutes to see if the code returns. If the code clears, the problem was airflow.
5. **Test the thermistor** by unplugging the dryer, locating the thermistor on the heater housing or blower duct, disconnecting its connector, and measuring resistance with a multimeter. Compare the reading to the service-manual value for your exact model.
6. **Check the thermal fuse and thermostats** for continuity. These are typically mounted on the heater housing or blower housing and should show continuity when cold. Replace any device that is open.
7. **Inspect the heating element** (electric dryers) by accessing the heater housing, disconnecting the element, and testing for continuity across its terminals and for short-to-ground between each terminal and the chassis. On gas dryers, verify the igniter glows and the gas valve opens during a cycle.
8. **Examine all wiring and connectors** at the heater assembly, thermistor, thermostat, and control board for signs of overheating, corrosion, or loose connections. Repair or replace damaged harnesses.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical wiring or multimeter testing, if you have a gas dryer and suspect an ignition or valve problem, or if you have cleared all airflow restrictions and tested the thermistor, thermal fuse, and heating element and the code still appears. A pro can perform voltage and signal tests on the control board, verify gas pressure and ignition timing on gas models, and diagnose intermittent wiring faults that are hard to catch during a static test. If the dryer overheats to the point of scorching clothes or emitting a burning smell, stop using it immediately and have it serviced.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in parts (thermistor or thermal fuse if needed), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
