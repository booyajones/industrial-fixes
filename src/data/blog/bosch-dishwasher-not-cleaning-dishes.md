## Bosch Dishwasher Not Cleaning Dishes — What's Happening

When a Bosch dishwasher is not cleaning dishes properly, it usually means poor wash performance from insufficient water distribution or cleaning action. This is typically not a fault code issue. According to Bosch, the most common causes are detergent tablets that have not dissolved, spray arms that are blocked or cannot rotate, a blocked filter restricting circulation, improper loading that interferes with spray patterns, or the wrong program or detergent for the soil level.

Bosch also notes that heavily soiled dishes with burnt-on food may require a higher-temperature or more intensive program to achieve proper cleaning. The symptom often appears as food residue remaining on dishes, film or spotting, or inconsistent wash results across the load.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Detergent tablet not dissolving** The tablet remains in the dispenser or tray instead of releasing into the wash, leaving dishes without effective cleaning chemistry.
- **Spray arm nozzles blocked** Food debris or mineral buildup clogs the small spray holes, reducing water pressure and coverage across the dish load.
- **Spray arms not rotating freely** Dishes or utensils obstruct the spray arms, preventing them from spinning and distributing water throughout the tub.
- **Filter blockage at base of tub** A clogged filter restricts circulation and recirculates dirty wash water, reducing cleaning effectiveness.
- **Improper loading blocking spray patterns** Large items, nested dishes, or overcrowding prevent water and detergent from reaching all surfaces.
- **Wrong program or detergent choice** A light cycle or incorrect detergent type cannot handle heavy soil or burnt-on residue.
- **Low water fill or insufficient water level** The dishwasher underfills, which reduces spray pressure and may prevent the heater from energizing correctly during the wash.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Run a normal cycle or service test and confirm the complaint, then check whether the detergent tablet fully dissolved and whether wash coverage appears normal.
2. Remove the upper spray arm and lower spray arm, inspect the outlet nozzles for blockage, and clear any debris with a cocktail stick or small pick.
3. Rinse both spray arms under running water, reinstall them, and verify they clip into place correctly and rotate freely without obstruction.
4. Remove the lower rack, unlock and remove the filter assembly at the base of the tub, then separate the cylindrical filter and wash both parts under running water with a small brush.
5. Reinstall the filter assembly and verify the loading pattern so dishes do not block spray paths or the detergent dispenser area.
6. Confirm the correct detergent type and amount, and select a hotter or more intensive cycle if the load is heavily soiled or has burnt-on residue.
7. If cleaning remains poor, check water fill level by running a cycle and observing the tub at about five minutes in (water should be visible near the top of the round filter handle area).
8. If the water level is low or spray pressure appears weak, inspect the fill valve and circulation pump operation, then escalate to electrical and mechanical component testing if water delivery or heating is not correct.

## When to Call a Pro

If cleaning performance does not improve after cleaning the spray arms and filter, verifying proper loading, and selecting the correct cycle, the issue may involve low water fill, circulation pump problems, or heating element failure. A technician can measure actual water level during fill, test spray pressure and pump operation, verify heater function, and perform electrical diagnostics that require disassembly and meter work. Call a pro if you are not comfortable with those checks or if the problem persists after all maintenance steps.
