## Navien E340 Error Code — What It Means

The E340 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a problem with the combustion fan system. The unit has detected that the fan is not operating correctly or not delivering adequate airflow to support safe combustion. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent unsafe operation. The exact meaning can vary slightly across Navien models, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for model-specific details. Common triggers include electrical faults in the fan circuit, mechanical failure of the fan motor itself, blockages in the air intake or exhaust path, or control board communication errors.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board thinking it is faulty when the actual problem is a failed fan motor or a loose connector at the fan. Test fan voltage and continuity before ordering a circuit board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed combustion fan motor (~40%)** The fan motor windings burn out or bearings seize, preventing the fan from spinning or reaching the required speed.
- **Loose or corroded wiring at the fan connector (~25%)** Vibration or moisture causes connections to loosen or corrode, interrupting power or feedback signals to the control board.
- **Blocked air intake filter or vent (~15%)** Dust, lint, or outdoor debris restricts airflow, causing the fan to work harder or the pressure switch to signal a fault.
- **Faulty control board (~12%)** The circuit board may fail to send the correct voltage to the fan or misread fan feedback signals.
- **Defective pressure switch (~8%)** The pressure switch that confirms adequate airflow may stick closed or fail to signal the board, triggering a fan error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the Navien unit by unscrewing the retaining screws to access the internal components.
3. **Locate the combustion fan** assembly, typically mounted near the top or side of the unit, and inspect all electrical connectors for tightness and corrosion.
4. **Remove and clean the air intake filter**, usually found on the exterior or just inside the intake pipe, rinsing it with water and allowing it to dry completely.
5. **Test the fan motor** for continuity using a multimeter across the motor terminals with power off. If the motor shows infinite resistance, it is open and must be replaced.
6. **Check the pressure switch** and its tubing for blockages, cracks, or disconnections. Blow through the tubing to confirm it is clear.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore power and gas, and observe the fan during startup. If the error persists after cleaning and checking connections, replace the fan motor or call a technician to test the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, testing electrical components with a multimeter, or disassembling the heater. A pro should also handle control board replacement, firmware updates, or any situation where the cause remains unclear after basic checks. Because tankless water heaters involve both gas and electrical systems, improper repairs can create safety hazards or void your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
