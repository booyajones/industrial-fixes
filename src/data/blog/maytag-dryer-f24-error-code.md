## Maytag Dryer F24 Error Code — What It Means

Maytag's official dryer help page lists F24 in its error-code index but does not publish the exact fault definition. The manufacturer-recommended recovery is a power reset: disconnect power for 5 minutes, restore power, then run a Timed Dry cycle and watch for recurrence. Because the code meaning is not disclosed in public Maytag dryer documentation, you must consult your model's tech sheet or service manual to confirm the specific circuit the code monitors.

In the field, technicians report that F24 on certain Maytag and Whirlpool dryer models is associated with a temperature-sensor or inlet-thermistor circuit fault. If your model's tech sheet confirms that definition, the error typically means the control board cannot read a valid signal from the dryer's thermistor due to an open sensor, damaged wiring, or a corroded connector. Always verify the exact fault definition for your dryer model before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board when F24 appears, but a failed thermistor or a loose connector is far more common. Check the sensor's resistance at room temperature and inspect every connector in the circuit before condemning the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed inlet thermistor or temperature sensor (~50%)** The sensor reads open or out of range, so the control cannot regulate heat correctly.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged connector to the sensor (~25%)** Vibration or moisture degrades the plug pins or socket, breaking the signal path.
- **Broken or chafed wire in the sensor harness (~15%)** A wire rubs against a sharp edge or overheats, opening the circuit to the control board.
- **Failed electronic control board (~10%)** The sensor input circuit on the board itself has failed, even though the sensor and wiring test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the breaker, then wait 5 minutes to clear any stored fault.
2. **Restore power and start a Timed Dry cycle** for at least one minute to see if the code returns immediately or stays cleared.
3. **Retrieve your dryer's model number and service manual** to confirm the exact definition of F24 and locate the inlet thermistor on your model's wiring diagram.
4. **Access the thermistor** by removing the lower front panel or rear duct cover (consult your service manual for sensor location) and unplug its connector.
5. **Measure the thermistor's resistance** at room temperature with a multimeter set to ohms, and compare the reading to the tech-sheet specification for your model.
6. **Inspect the sensor harness and connector** for broken wires, corrosion, pushed-back pins, or heat damage, and repair or replace any damaged wiring.
7. **Replace the thermistor** if it reads open, shorted, or out of range, then reassemble the dryer, restore power, and run a full heat cycle to verify the code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you cannot locate your model's service manual or wiring diagram, if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, or if the error persists after you have replaced the thermistor and repaired all visible wiring faults. A technician has the tools to test the control board's sensor-input circuit and can confirm whether the board itself has failed. Also call a pro if you discover signs of overheating, such as melted insulation or scorched connectors, since those conditions may indicate a separate airflow or electrical problem that must be corrected to prevent future failures.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 for a thermistor, 30–60 minutes. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call including sensor and labor.
