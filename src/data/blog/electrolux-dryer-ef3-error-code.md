## Electrolux Dryer EF3 Error Code — What It Means

EF3 on an Electrolux dryer signals that the dryer reached its maximum allowed drying time for the selected cycle but the load was still wet or damp. The control board monitors moisture sensors and cycle duration. When an automatic dryness cycle runs too long without detecting the target dryness level, it throws EF3 and stops. This is a drying-performance fault, not a single broken part.

The code typically points to restricted airflow preventing heat and moisture from escaping, or to dirty or faulty moisture sensors that cannot accurately read dryness. Less often, weak heating or control-board faults can allow a cycle to run too long. The dryer is protecting itself and alerting you that normal drying could not be completed in the expected time window.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the moisture sensor assembly or control board first. Instead, pull and clean the entire exhaust path and wipe down the sensor bars in the drum. Most EF3 faults clear after restoring proper airflow and cleaning sensor surfaces.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust venting (~50%)** Lint buildup in the duct, transition hose, or outside vent cap traps heat and moisture inside, so clothes stay wet and the cycle times out.
- **Dirty or obstructed lint screen (~20%)** A clogged lint trap reduces airflow through the drum and exhaust path, slowing evaporation and extending drying time beyond the maximum.
- **Dirty or damaged moisture sensor bars (~15%)** Residue, fabric-softener film, or lint on the sensor strips in the drum prevents accurate moisture detection, so the control never sees dry and times out.
- **Moisture sensor harness or wiring damage (~10%)** A broken wire, corroded connector, or loose terminal between the sensor bars and the control board disrupts the dryness signal.
- **Faulty moisture sensor assembly (~3%)** If the sensor bars themselves are worn or internally failed, the control cannot measure moisture correctly and the cycle will time out.
- **Defective electronic control board (~2%)** When the sensor path is intact but the board misinterprets or ignores the dryness readings, the cycle can run too long and throw EF3.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the wall or circuit breaker before any inspection or repair work.
2. **Remove and clean the lint screen** thoroughly under running water to clear any fabric-softener residue, then let it dry completely.
3. **Disconnect the exhaust duct** from the back of the dryer and check both the dryer's exhaust port and the entire duct run for lint blockage. Vacuum or brush out all lint, and inspect the outside vent cap for obstructions or crushed sections.
4. **Locate the moisture sensor bars** inside the drum (usually two flat metal strips near the lint screen or on the rear wall). Wipe them clean with a soft cloth dampened with white vinegar or rubbing alcohol to remove any film, then dry them.
5. **Inspect the moisture sensor wiring harness** at the drum mounting. Look for damaged insulation, loose connector pins, or corrosion. Reconnect securely if loose.
6. **Restore power and run a test cycle** on an automatic dryness setting with a small damp load. Monitor exhaust airflow at the outside vent and check that the dryer heats and stops when the load is dry.
7. **Replace the moisture sensor assembly** if the sensor bars are physically damaged or if cleaning and wiring checks did not clear the code. If the sensor tests good and airflow is normal, consult your model's wiring diagram and consider control-board replacement or professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared all lint, cleaned the sensor bars, verified wiring connections, and confirmed normal heating but EF3 still appears. A pro can measure sensor resistance, test the control-board inputs with a multimeter, and access the service-mode diagnostics built into your model. Also call if you are uncomfortable disconnecting ducting, working behind the dryer, or testing electrical circuits. If the dryer is a washer-dryer combo and you see water-related symptoms alongside EF3, that machine may need leak testing and inlet-valve diagnosis that is easier for a trained service tech.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts (vent hose or sensor cleaner), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250 service call plus parts if sensor or board replacement needed.
