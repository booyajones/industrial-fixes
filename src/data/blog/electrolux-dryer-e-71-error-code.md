## What this code means
E71 on an Electrolux dryer indicates an outlet thermistor fault. The dryer's control board is receiving an invalid signal from the outlet or control thermistor circuit, which monitors the exhaust air temperature. When this sensor circuit fails, the dryer cannot safely regulate drying temperature and posts the E71 code.

The fault is most commonly caused by a defective outlet thermistor itself, damaged or corroded wiring between the sensor and the control board, or loose connectors. Less often, the electronic control board itself is at fault if it can no longer read a valid sensor signal even when the thermistor and wiring test good.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first. Always test the outlet thermistor resistance with a multimeter (should read about 5,000 ohms at room temperature) and inspect the harness for loose pins or corrosion before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty outlet thermistor (~50%)** The outlet temperature sensor has failed internally and no longer reports a valid resistance, so the control board cannot calculate exhaust temperature.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring and connectors (~30%)** Vibration, heat, or moisture has broken wires, corroded connector pins, or loosened the plug between the outlet thermistor and the main control board.
- **Defective electronic control board (~15%)** The control board's thermistor input circuit has failed, causing it to read invalid signals even when the sensor and harness test within specification.
- **Intermittent connector contact (~5%)** Oxidation or dirt on the thermistor connector pins creates a high-resistance connection that appears as an out-of-range reading to the board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off any gas supply if your model is gas-fired, then wait five minutes for residual charge to dissipate.
2. **Remove the rear panel or top panel** (consult your model's service manual) to access the outlet thermistor, which is typically mounted on the blower housing or near the exhaust duct outlet.
3. **Inspect the thermistor wiring harness and connector** for loose pins, cracks, heat discoloration, corrosion, or breaks, and repair or replace any damaged sections.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure the resistance across the thermistor terminals at room temperature (about 70 °F).
5. **Compare your reading to the specification** of approximately 5,000 ohms at room temperature. If the reading is open (infinite), very low, or far outside that range, replace the outlet thermistor.
6. **Reconnect all wiring**, reassemble the dryer, and run a test cycle. If E71 returns and the thermistor and harness tested good, replace the electronic control board.
7. **Clear the error code** (if needed) by pressing and holding the start button or cycling power, then verify normal operation through a complete dry cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if your multimeter test shows conflicting results, or if replacing the thermistor and inspecting the wiring does not clear the E71 code. A pro can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board's thermistor input circuit, check for intermittent faults under heat and vibration, and confirm that the blower housing and exhaust path are not creating false temperature readings. Also call a pro if you see evidence of arcing, burnt wiring, or repeated code returns after parts replacement, since those symptoms can indicate a deeper electrical fault or a short in the main harness.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
