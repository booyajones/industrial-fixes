## Navien E250 Error Code — What It Means

The E250 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates an exhaust overheating condition. The unit's sensors have detected that exhaust gases are exiting at dangerously high temperatures, which can signal restricted airflow, blocked venting, or a combustion problem. The heater will typically shut down to prevent damage or unsafe operation.

This code often points to ventilation problems rather than internal component failures. Because proper venting is critical for both safety and performance, it is important to inspect the entire exhaust path and air intake system before replacing any internal parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust thermistor or flame rod first. Before ordering parts, inspect the entire vent run for blockages, ice buildup, or crushed piping, which are far more common and cost nothing to fix.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~40%)** Bird nests, ice, snow, or debris in the vent terminal or piping prevent hot gases from escaping, causing overheating.
- **Crushed or improperly sloped vent piping (~20%)** Sagging or kinked vent pipe traps exhaust gases and creates back pressure that raises temperatures.
- **Blocked air intake (~15%)** Restricted combustion air supply starves the burner, leading to incomplete combustion and hotter exhaust.
- **Faulty exhaust thermistor (~15%)** The sensor that monitors exhaust temperature may drift out of calibration or fail, triggering false overheating alarms.
- **Improper vent sizing or configuration (~10%)** Vent piping that is too long, has too many elbows, or uses incorrect diameter creates excessive resistance.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Inspect the exhaust vent terminal** outside for blockages such as bird nests, ice, snow, leaves, or other debris and remove any obstructions.
3. **Check the air intake screen** and piping for blockages, lint, or damage and clean or clear as needed.
4. **Trace the entire vent run** from the heater to the outside terminal, looking for crushed pipe, loose joints, sags, or condensate pooling in low spots.
5. **Verify proper vent slope** by consulting your installation manual to confirm the piping rises continuously toward the outside and has no horizontal or downward runs that trap exhaust.
6. **Restore power and gas**, reset the heater according to the user manual, and observe a full heating cycle to confirm the error does not return.
7. **If the code persists** after clearing all visible obstructions and verifying proper slope, call a qualified technician to test the exhaust thermistor and inspect internal combustion components.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you cannot safely access the vent terminal, if the vent piping requires structural modifications, or if the error returns after you have cleared all obstructions. Professionals have combustion analyzers to measure exhaust gas composition and temperature, tools to test thermistors and gas valves, and the training to diagnose complex airflow or burner issues. Because this code involves both gas combustion and safe venting of exhaust, a qualified technician should handle any work beyond basic cleaning and visual inspection.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
