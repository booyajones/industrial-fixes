## LG Mini Split Blowing Warm Air — What's Happening

When your LG mini split blows warm air instead of cooling, you are seeing a symptom rather than a specific fault code. The unit may be running but not delivering cold air, or it may feel like room-temperature or slightly warm air is coming from the indoor head. LG documents that low refrigerant (fault code CH38 or F4) is the most relevant coded failure for this complaint, but many warm-air cases happen without any error code at all.

If no code is displayed, the problem is usually operational or mechanical. The remote may be set to the wrong mode, the filter or coil may be clogged, or the outdoor unit may not be communicating with the indoor head. In heating mode, the system may also pause warm air delivery temporarily during a defrost cycle, which LG lists as normal operation.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Incorrect mode or setpoint** The remote is in fan-only, auto, or heat mode, or the target temperature is set higher than room temperature, so the system never calls for cooling.
- **Dirty air filter or blocked indoor coil** A clogged filter or debris on the indoor evaporator restricts airflow and reduces heat transfer, making the discharge air feel warm.
- **Low refrigerant (CH38 / F4)** LG identifies code CH38 or F4 as low refrigerant charge or refrigerant loss, which prevents the evaporator from absorbing heat and results in warm or room-temperature air.
- **Blocked outdoor coil or fan** Dirt, leaves, or debris on the outdoor condenser coil or a stopped outdoor fan prevent heat rejection and can cause the system to deliver warm air or shut down on high pressure.
- **Communication fault (CH05 / E0 / CH53 / CH66)** LG lists these codes as communication errors between the indoor and outdoor units or wiring and piping connection issues, which can stop the compressor or outdoor unit from responding to cooling commands.
- **Defrost cycle (heating mode only)** During heating operation, the outdoor coil periodically frosts and the system pauses warm air delivery to melt ice, which LG documents as normal defrost operation.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the operating mode and setpoint on the remote or wall controller, confirm the unit is set to Cool and the target temperature is at least 5°F below room temperature.
2. Check the indoor display or remote for any fault codes (CH38, F4, CH05, E0, CH53, CH66) and write them down before resetting power or clearing the display.
3. Remove and inspect the indoor air filter, clean or replace it if dirty, then visually check the indoor coil fins for dust or blockage.
4. Inspect the outdoor unit for debris around the coil, verify the outdoor fan is spinning when the system runs, and clear any obstructions from the condenser.
5. Check that both the indoor and outdoor circuit breakers are on and that the communication wiring between units is secure at the terminal blocks.
6. If the unit is in heating mode and the outdoor coil is iced, allow the defrost cycle to complete (typically 5 to 15 minutes) before diagnosing further.
7. If no code is present and airflow is clean, measure refrigerant pressures at the service ports and check operating temperatures to detect undercharge or a refrigerant leak, especially if code CH38 or F4 was displayed.
8. After cleaning filters and coils or repairing wiring or refrigerant leaks, run the system in cooling mode and confirm cold air discharge, compressor operation, and stable indoor temperature drop.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if the unit displays code CH38, F4, or any communication fault (CH05, E0, CH53, CH66), or if cleaning the filter and coil does not restore cooling. Refrigerant leak detection, pressure testing, and refrigerant charging require EPA certification and specialized tools. A professional will also diagnose compressor, reversing valve, or electronic control board failures that can mimic warm-air symptoms without triggering a code.
