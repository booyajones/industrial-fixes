## Samsung Microwave E-24 Error — What It Means

Samsung defines E-24 as an over-temperature error. The control board has detected that the microwave's internal temperature exceeded the safe limit during operation. Samsung's UK support also associates this code with a fire-sense or overheat condition. The microwave will shut down to protect its electronics and cavity from heat damage.

The code typically appears when heavy use, poor airflow, or a blocked vent allows heat to build up beyond the threshold the temperature sensor monitors. In some cases moisture contamination or a failed sensor can also trigger the fault even when the cavity is not actually overheated.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first, but a blocked vent or failed thermal sensor are far more common. Always power-reset, clear vents, and verify the sensor resistance (if you have the service manual spec) before swapping the board.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked venting or poor airflow (~40%)** Grease, dust, or an obstruction in the vent path traps heat inside the microwave cavity and raises the measured temperature above the limit.
- **Residual heat from heavy use (~25%)** Running the microwave continuously or operating it in an already-hot kitchen can heat-soak the cavity and trip the over-temperature threshold.
- **Moisture contamination of electronics (~15%)** Steam or liquid spills that reach the control area or sensor wiring can cause false high-temperature readings or short the sensor circuit.
- **Failed or drifting thermal sensor (~12%)** The temperature sensor itself may read high or open, signaling an over-temperature condition when the cavity is actually cool.
- **Damaged sensor wiring harness (~5%)** Chafed, pinched, or corroded wiring between the thermal sensor and the control board can produce intermittent or incorrect temperature signals.
- **Faulty control board (~3%)** A less common cause is a main control board that misreads the sensor input or has a defective temperature-monitoring circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** or switch off the dedicated breaker and wait a full 60 seconds to reset the control board.
2. **Let the unit cool** for at least 15 minutes if it has been in heavy use or the cavity feels warm to the touch.
3. **Inspect all vent openings** on the top, sides, and rear of the microwave for dust, grease, or any obstruction, and clear them with a soft brush or damp cloth.
4. **Verify clearance** around the microwave according to your installation manual (typically 3 inches on sides and rear for over-the-range models) to prevent heat buildup.
5. **Check for moisture** around the control panel, door seal, and interior ceiling. If you see condensation or signs of a recent spill, unplug the unit and allow it to dry completely before retesting.
6. **Restore power** and run a brief test cycle with a cup of water to see if E-24 returns. If the code is gone, the fault was heat or a transient glitch.
7. **If E-24 persists**, contact Samsung service or a qualified technician to test the thermal sensor resistance, inspect the sensor wiring harness, and evaluate the control board using the model-specific service manual.
8. **Once repaired**, clean the vents monthly and avoid back-to-back heavy cycles to prevent the error from recurring.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the E-24 code returns after you have cleared the vents, allowed the microwave to cool, and performed a power reset. Diagnosing a thermal sensor requires comparing its resistance to the service-manual specification at room temperature, which most homeowners do not have access to. A technician will use a multimeter to test the sensor, trace the wiring for shorts or opens, and verify that the control board correctly interprets the sensor signal. Because the microwave operates at high voltage (typically around 2,000 volts inside the magnetron circuit), any work beyond cleaning vents and resetting power should be left to a qualified appliance repair technician. If moisture contamination is suspected and drying the unit for 24 hours does not resolve the fault, a pro can safely disassemble the unit to inspect and dry the control area.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
