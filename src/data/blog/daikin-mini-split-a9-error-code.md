## Daikin A9 Error Code — What It Means

A9 on a Daikin mini split indicates a malfunction of the electronic expansion valve (EEV) circuit in the indoor unit. The EEV meters refrigerant flow into the evaporator coil, and when the A9 code appears the indoor unit cannot drive or read the valve correctly. Refrigerant metering stops working normally and the system shuts down to protect itself. Daikin's fault-code chart defines A9 specifically as an electronic expansion valve problem, which means either the valve coil, the indoor control board that powers it, or the wiring between them has failed.

## Common Causes

- **Failed EEV coil or valve assembly** The electronic expansion valve coil may have an open circuit, the valve mechanism may be stuck, or internal windings have burned out.
- **Defective indoor unit PCB** The control board that drives the EEV may have failed and can no longer send correct signals to the valve.
- **Loose or damaged connector** The EEV connector at the indoor unit PCB may be partially unseated or corroded, breaking the control circuit.
- **Broken relay cable or harness** The wiring between the indoor board and the expansion valve may have a short circuit, open circuit, or physical break.
- **Transient electrical noise** A power surge or control upset may trigger A9 once, and the unit may return to normal after a power cycle if no hard fault exists.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power to the indoor unit** at the breaker or disconnect switch, wait ten seconds, then restore power and observe whether the A9 code returns and whether the unit resumes normal operation.
2. **Remove the front cover and access panel** of the indoor unit to expose the electrical box where the PCB and EEV connector are located.
3. **Inspect the EEV connector on the indoor PCB** and confirm it is fully seated with no corrosion, moisture, or bent pins, then press firmly to reseat if necessary.
4. **Trace the wiring harness** from the PCB to the electronic expansion valve body on the refrigerant line and check for pinched, cut, or burned wires, shorts to ground, or open circuits with a multimeter continuity check.
5. **Replace the relay cable or repair the harness** if you find any broken or shorted wires, ensuring all connections are clean and tight.
6. **Replace the indoor unit PCB** if all wiring and connectors check good but the A9 code persists after power cycling, following Daikin's service procedure that identifies the board as the next likely fault.
7. **Replace the EEV coil or complete expansion valve assembly** if a new PCB and verified-good wiring do not clear the code, as the valve mechanism or coil itself is the remaining failed component.

## When to Call a Pro

A9 repair involves live electrical diagnostics, refrigerant lines, and surface-mount control boards inside a sealed indoor unit. If you are not EPA-certified or do not have multimeter skills and Daikin service documentation for your model, call a licensed HVAC technician. Misdiagnosing the fault can lead to replacing the wrong part, and improper handling of the expansion valve or PCB can damage refrigerant circuits or create refrigerant leaks that are expensive to repair. Professional techs have access to Daikin's full fault-code tables, resistance specs for the EEV coil, and the correct replacement part numbers for your serial number.
