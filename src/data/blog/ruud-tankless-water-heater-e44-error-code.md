## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E44 Error Code — What It Means

The E44 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters generally indicates a problem with the temperature sensing system or a safety lockout condition. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the inside panel for your specific unit. The error usually appears when the system detects temperatures outside normal operating ranges or when a temperature sensor is reporting faulty readings. This code is designed to protect the heat exchanger and internal components from damage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the entire control board when the actual problem is a loose sensor wire or a failed temperature sensor. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect all sensor connections before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Temperature sensor failure (~45%)** The thermistor or temperature sensor has drifted out of specification or failed completely, sending incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~25%)** Connections at the sensor terminals or control board have become loose, corroded, or intermittent due to heat cycling or moisture.
- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger (~15%)** Mineral deposits restrict water flow or insulate sensors from accurate temperature readings, causing erratic behavior.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main control board has developed a defect in the temperature monitoring circuit or memory corruption.
- **Voltage irregularities (~5%)** Low incoming voltage or electrical noise on the power supply causes the control board to misread sensor values or trigger false safety alarms.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shut-off valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and set the cover aside.
3. **Locate the temperature sensors** on the inlet and outlet pipes and at the heat exchanger. Note the wire colors and photograph the connections.
4. **Disconnect one sensor** at a time and use a multimeter set to resistance mode to measure across the sensor terminals. Consult your model's resistance table for expected values at ambient temperature.
5. **Inspect all sensor wiring** from the sensors to the control board for signs of heat damage, corrosion, or loose pins. Reseat every connector firmly.
6. **If a sensor reads out of range**, order the correct replacement sensor for your model and install it with heat-safe dielectric grease on the threads.
7. **Reassemble the cover**, restore gas and power, and monitor the display. If the E44 returns immediately, test the control board or call a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working near gas lines, if the error persists after replacing sensors and checking all connections, or if you suspect a control board failure. Professionals have the tools to test gas valve operation, measure combustion parameters, and safely diagnose board-level faults. Also call a pro if your unit is still under warranty, as DIY repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
