## Navien E216 Error Code — What It Means

The E216 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with the combustion air supply or exhaust system. The unit detects that proper airflow is not being established, which prevents safe ignition or sustained operation. This code can be triggered by restrictions in the venting, a failing combustion fan, or pressure switch problems that monitor airflow.

Because tankless heaters rely on precise air-to-fuel ratios, any interruption in the intake or exhaust path will cause the unit to shut down and display this code. The safety system is designed to prevent incomplete combustion or backdrafting, so the heater will not operate until the airflow fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the combustion fan motor when the real problem is a blocked vent pipe or a dirty fan blade. Inspect the entire vent run and clean the fan housing before ordering a new motor.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~40%)** Ice, debris, or condensate pooling in the vent pipe prevents proper exhaust flow and triggers the error.
- **Combustion fan failure (~25%)** The fan motor or blades wear out or become obstructed, reducing airflow below the safe threshold.
- **Air pressure switch fault (~20%)** The pressure switch that confirms airflow may stick, leak, or fail to close, signaling an airflow problem even when the fan runs.
- **Intake air blockage (~10%)** The fresh-air intake terminal is covered by snow, leaves, or other material, starving the combustion chamber of oxygen.
- **Improper vent installation or length (~5%)** Vent piping that exceeds maximum length, has too many elbows, or slopes incorrectly causes back-pressure and airflow errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve to work safely.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vent terminals** for both intake and exhaust. Remove any ice, snow, leaves, or debris blocking the openings.
3. **Remove the front service panel** and locate the combustion fan assembly, usually mounted at the top or side of the heat exchanger.
4. **Check the fan blades** for dust, debris, or obstructions. Spin the fan by hand to confirm it rotates freely without binding.
5. **Inspect the vent pipe** from the unit to the exterior for proper pitch (typically a quarter-inch per foot downward slope toward the outside) and check for condensate pooling or sags.
6. **Test the air pressure switch** by disconnecting its rubber sensing hose and gently blowing into it to hear a click, or use a multimeter to check for continuity when the switch is activated.
7. **Power the unit back on** and observe the fan during startup. If it fails to spin or spins weakly, replace the combustion fan motor. If the fan runs normally but the code persists, replace the pressure switch or inspect the wiring harness for loose connections.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the vent system requires modification or re-pitching, or if you have replaced both the fan and pressure switch and the code still appears. A technician can perform combustion analysis, measure airflow with a manometer, and diagnose intermittent electrical faults in the control board or wiring harness. Also call a pro if the vent run exceeds the maximum length specified in your installation manual, as code-compliant venting may require additional parts or design changes.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts if a fan or pressure switch is needed, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-400 depending on diagnosis and part replacement.
