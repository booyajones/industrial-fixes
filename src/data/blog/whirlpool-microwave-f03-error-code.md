## Whirlpool Microwave F03 Error Code — What It Means

When your Whirlpool built-in microwave displays F03, the unit has detected a problem with the Electronic Oven Control (EOC) or the associated wiring between the control and other circuits. Whirlpool's official documentation states this code points to a possible control board fault or a wiring issue that prevents the EOC from communicating properly.

If you own an over-the-range microwave, Whirlpool uses the code F3H instead of F03, and that variant specifically points to the humidity thermistor or its wiring rather than the main control. Always confirm your appliance type and model number before diagnosing, because Whirlpool assigns different meanings to similar codes across product families.

## Common Causes

- **Failed Electronic Oven Control board** The EOC itself has an internal fault or intermittent electronic failure that triggers the F03 code.
- **Damaged or loose wiring harness** Broken insulation, loose connectors, pinched wires, or corroded terminals interrupt communication between the control and circuits.
- **Bad humidity thermistor (over-the-range models only)** If your unit is an OTR microwave displaying F3H, the humidity sensor or its wiring is out of range or open.
- **Overheated or burned connector pins** High resistance at plug contacts or heat damage at the EOC harness can mimic a control board failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your model type** by checking the rating plate to confirm whether you have a built-in microwave (F03 = EOC fault) or an over-the-range unit (F3H = humidity thermistor fault).
2. **Power-cycle the microwave** by switching off the circuit breaker for 1 minute, then restoring power and watching for 1 minute to see if the code clears and operation resumes.
3. **Inspect all wiring at the control board** for loose plugs, damaged insulation, pinched harness sections, or corrosion at the EOC and related connectors, since Whirlpool explicitly lists associated wiring as a likely cause.
4. **Pull the control board forward** (after disconnecting power) and examine the back of the board and the harness plug for burn marks, pushed-out pins, or melted plastic that would cause an open circuit.
5. **Test the suspect circuit with a multimeter** according to your model's technical sheet if the code returns after reset, focusing on continuity and resistance values for the EOC and any sensors listed for your specific model.
6. **Replace the identified component** (EOC board, humidity thermistor, or damaged harness section) with the manufacturer part number from your model's service manual or parts diagram.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to confirm the code does not return and all microwave functions operate normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the F03 code returns after a power reset and you are not comfortable working inside live or recently live 120 V circuits. Control board replacement requires removing the outer case, disconnecting multiple harnesses, and verifying proper grounding. If you lack a multimeter or your model's technical sheet with resistance tables, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent incorrect part orders. Whirlpool's official consumer guidance recommends professional service when the code persists after the initial reset.
