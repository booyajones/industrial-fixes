## Electrolux Washer EF1 Error Code — What It Means

The EF1 error code on an Electrolux washing machine signals a drain system blockage. The machine detects that water is not exiting the tub, usually because the drain filter, drain pump, or drain hose is clogged, kinked, or blocked by debris. This is a drain-specific fault, distinct from the EF1 code on Electrolux dryers (which relates to vent or moisture sensor issues). The washer will pause mid-cycle and refuse to spin or complete the drain phase until the obstruction is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump when the real problem is a clogged filter or kinked hose. Always pull and clean the filter and straighten the drain hose before buying a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain filter (~50%)** Lint, coins, buttons, or small debris accumulate in the filter housing and block water flow.
- **Kinked or crushed drain hose (~25%)** The drain tube is bent, twisted, or pressed against the floor or wall, restricting drainage.
- **Blocked drain pump impeller (~15%)** Foreign objects lodge in the pump impeller, preventing it from spinning and moving water out.
- **Drain hose submerged too deep (~5%)** The hose end sits more than 12 inches deep in the standpipe, creating a siphon or backflow issue.
- **Failed drain pump (~5%)** The pump motor or impeller is seized or burned out and cannot move water even when clear of debris.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply to prevent electrical shock or flooding.
2. **Locate the drain filter** at the front bottom of the machine behind a small access panel.
3. **Remove the filter** by turning it counterclockwise and clean it under warm water with a soft brush to remove lint, debris, and small objects.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** for kinks, twists, or crushing where it exits the machine or enters the standpipe, and straighten any bends.
5. **Check the drain pump impeller** by removing the pump cover (if accessible) and clearing any debris lodged in the blades.
6. **Test the drain pump** with a multimeter for resistance (typically 100-300 Ω, but consult your model's technical sheet), and replace the pump if it shows no continuity or is seized.
7. **Reconnect all components**, plug in the machine, and run a drain-only or rinse-spin cycle to verify the error clears and water exits properly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared the filter and hose, tested the pump with a multimeter, and the error persists. A pro can check for a failed pressure sensor, control board issue, or wiring fault that mimics a drain blockage. Also call if you are uncomfortable tilting the machine to access the pump or do not own a multimeter. Most EF1 errors are straightforward DIY fixes, but persistent codes after cleaning warrant a diagnostic visit to avoid replacing parts by trial and error.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if a filter or hose is damaged, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-200 service call.
