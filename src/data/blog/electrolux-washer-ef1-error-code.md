## Electrolux Washer EF1 Error Code — What It Means

The EF1 code on an Electrolux washer signals a drain fault. The machine has detected that water is not draining from the tub as expected. This happens when the drain path is restricted or obstructed, preventing normal water evacuation during the drain or spin cycle.

The blockage is typically in the drain filter, drain hose, or drain pump. The code tells you the machine cannot complete its drain sequence because something is physically stopping the flow or the pump cannot move water. The tub may still hold standing water when the error appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump before checking the filter and hose. Pull and rinse the filter first and inspect the drain hose for kinks or clogs before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Drain filter blocked (~50%)** Lint, coins, buttons, or small debris collect in the filter housing and restrict water flow out of the tub.
- **Drain hose kinked or crushed (~25%)** The hose is pinched behind the washer or twisted at the standpipe, preventing water from leaving.
- **Drain hose internally clogged (~10%)** Lint or fabric fibers build up inside the hose wall and create a blockage even when the hose looks straight.
- **Drain pump impeller obstructed (~10%)** A sock, hairpin, or other object is jammed in the pump impeller or volute, stopping the pump from spinning.
- **Drain pump weak or failed (~5%)** The pump motor runs but does not generate enough pressure to move water, or the pump is seized and will not turn.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply, then pull the unit away from the wall to access the rear panel and drain hose.
2. **Locate the drain filter access panel** on the lower front of the washer (consult your model's manual for exact location) and place towels underneath to catch residual water.
3. **Open the filter cover and slowly unscrew the filter**, tilting it to drain trapped water into a shallow pan, then rinse the filter under running water and inspect the housing for coins or debris.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** where it exits the back of the washer and follows the standpipe or laundry sink, straightening any kinks and feeling along the length for hard spots or internal clogs.
5. **Disconnect the drain hose** from the washer outlet and the standpipe, then flush it with a garden hose or run a plumber's snake through if you feel resistance.
6. **Check the drain pump inlet** by shining a flashlight into the filter cavity and turning the impeller by hand to confirm it spins freely and has no socks or foreign objects jamming it.
7. **Reinstall the filter and hose**, reconnect power, and run a drain or rinse-spin test cycle to verify the tub empties completely and EF1 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the drain filter, hose, and pump impeller are all clear yet EF1 persists, or if you are not comfortable accessing the pump motor and testing it for continuity. A pro will also check the pressure switch and wiring harness to rule out a false drain signal. If the pump needs replacement and the unit is built-in or difficult to move, a service call saves time and avoids damage to cabinetry or flooring.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (if a hose or filter is damaged), 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250 including labor and a pump if needed.
