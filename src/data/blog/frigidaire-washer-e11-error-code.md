## What this code means
E11 on a Frigidaire washer means the machine is taking too long to fill with water, a long fill fault. The control board expects the washer to reach the required water level within a set time window. When that does not happen, it stops the cycle and displays E11. This code points to a problem in the water inlet or level detection circuit. It is not primarily a drain fault, although improper drain setup can allow siphoning that prevents the tub from filling.

The code appears on Frigidaire and Electrolux front-load platforms. It tells you to check the water supply path, the inlet valve, the inlet hoses and screens, and the pressure sensing system. Occasionally wiring or control board faults can trigger E11, but those are less common than physical restrictions in the fill circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first, thinking the code means an electronics failure. In reality, most E11 faults trace to clogged inlet screens, kinked hoses, or a weak water inlet valve. Always clean the inlet screens and verify house water pressure before buying any part.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens (~35%)** Sediment and debris from the house supply collect in the fine mesh screens where the fill hoses attach to the washer, restricting water flow.
- **Low house water pressure or partly closed supply valves (~25%)** If the hot or cold shutoff valve is not fully open, or if home pressure is weak, the washer cannot fill quickly enough.
- **Kinked or pinched inlet hoses (~15%)** Hoses that are bent sharply behind the washer or trapped between the machine and the wall restrict flow.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~15%)** A weak solenoid coil, sticking valve, or internal restriction in the valve assembly prevents adequate water from entering the tub.
- **Siphoning from improper drain setup (~7%)** If the drain hose is inserted too far into the standpipe or the standpipe is too short, water drains out as fast as it enters, preventing net fill gain.
- **Faulty pressure switch or pressure hose (~3%)** A split, clogged, or disconnected pressure hose, or a defective pressure sensor, can make the control think the tub is not filling even when water is entering.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the water supply** at both hot and cold shutoff valves behind the washer and unplug the machine from power.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the inlet hoses and drain hose at the rear. Inspect the inlet hoses for kinks, pinches, or collapse and straighten any bends.
3. **Remove both inlet hoses** from the back of the washer and inspect the small mesh screens inside the inlet ports. Use needle-nose pliers to pull the screens out carefully. Rinse them under running water and use a soft brush to remove sediment. Reinstall the screens and reconnect the hoses.
4. **Check the drain hose setup** to confirm the standpipe is at least 30 inches high and the drain hose is not pushed in more than a few inches. Siphoning occurs when the hose is inserted too far or the standpipe is too low.
5. **Restore water and power** and run a fill or rinse cycle to see if the washer fills normally. If it does, the code should clear automatically. If not, proceed to the inlet valve test.
6. **Test the water inlet valve** by unplugging the washer, removing the top or rear panel to access the valve, and checking for visible debris or damage. If the valve does not open fully when energized or flow is weak, replace the valve assembly. Consult your model's service manual for specific resistance and voltage values.
7. **Inspect the pressure hose and pressure switch** if the washer fills adequately but the code persists. Look for a split, clog, or loose connection in the small air hose running from the tub to the pressure sensor. Test or replace the pressure switch if the hose is intact and the fault remains.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the inlet screens, verified adequate house water pressure, inspected all hoses for kinks and siphoning, and replaced the water inlet valve but the E11 code still appears. At that point the fault likely lies in the pressure sensing circuit, wiring harness, or control board. Diagnosing those components requires a multimeter, service-manual specifications, and experience tracing control signals. A pro can also confirm that the fill rate meets the manufacturer's flow requirements and can access model-specific diagnostic modes to pinpoint sensor or board faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
