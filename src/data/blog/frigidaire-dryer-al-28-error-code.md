## Frigidaire AL-28 Dryer Error — What It Means

AL-28 is a drying-performance alarm that tells you the dryer is taking too long to complete a cycle or is not reaching the expected temperature rise. Frigidaire does not publish AL-28 as an official error code in available support documentation, but service reports and community evidence point to a heat-cycle timeout or heating-system failure. In practical terms, this code is treated as a heating problem on either an electric heater circuit or a gas burner circuit, not a door latch or control board cosmetic issue.

The dryer runs but does not generate enough heat to dry clothes in the normal time window, which triggers the alarm. This is different from a complete no-heat situation. The machine is detecting that the cycle is dragging on too long, which usually means the heater is weak, failed, or the airflow path is restricted enough to mimic a heater fault.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or open heating element** The electric heating element is broken, partially open, or has a damaged coil that prevents full heat output.
- **Gas burner ignition or flame fault** On gas models, the igniter, gas valve, or burner assembly components are not generating a proper flame or are cycling off prematurely.
- **Grounded or damaged heater circuit wiring** Wiring or terminals in the heater circuit are damaged, corroded, or shorted to ground, which is a known Frigidaire heat fault pattern.
- **Restricted airflow or clogged venting** Lint buildup in the exhaust duct, blower housing, or external vent restricts airflow and causes extended dry times that trigger the alarm.
- **Open thermal safety device** A thermal fuse, high-limit thermostat, or other safety cutoff has opened due to overheating or age and is preventing the heater from running.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault** by running a heated automatic cycle and confirming the dryer does not reach normal drying performance within the expected time for a typical load.
2. **Identify fuel type** (electric or gas) because the diagnostic path and components differ between the two.
3. **Inspect the heating element** on electric models for visible breaks, damage, or poor terminal connections, and use a multimeter to check for continuity and ground faults.
4. **Check gas burner operation** on gas models by observing ignition, flame presence, and cycle behavior during a heat cycle, and inspect the igniter and gas valve components for wear.
5. **Clear and inspect airflow paths** by removing and cleaning the lint filter, checking the blower housing and exhaust duct for obstructions, and verifying the external vent is not collapsed or clogged.
6. **Test suspect components** with a meter for continuity and resistance, including thermal safety devices and wiring connections in the heater circuit.
7. **Replace the failed component** (heater, igniter, thermal fuse, or wiring), reassemble the dryer, and run a full heated cycle to confirm normal temperature rise and dry time before returning to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt electric circuits or natural gas components, if you cannot safely access the heater or burner assembly, or if you have replaced the obvious parts and the AL-28 alarm returns. A technician will have the meter skills and service manual data to trace intermittent ground faults, check gas pressures, and verify proper cycle timing. If the dryer is under warranty or you are unsure of your fuel type or model configuration, professional diagnosis is the safer path.
