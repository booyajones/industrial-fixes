## Frigidaire F13 Error Code — What It Means

Frigidaire's F13 error on ranges and ovens indicates a control-memory fault. Frigidaire documents it as "E Prompt Failure" on range error-code guides and "EEPROM Failure" on electric range support articles. In practical terms, the electronic control cannot reliably read or validate its stored program data, so the appliance may not operate normally or may stop functioning entirely.

The fault usually stems from a temporary control glitch after a power surge or interruption, or from a failed main control board with corrupted EEPROM data. On some models with a separate user-interface board, poor connector contact or a damaged ribbon cable between the control and display can also trigger F13.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the display or keypad assembly first. Inspect and reseat all ribbon cables and connectors between the control board and display before replacing any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed electronic oven control (EOC) board (~40%)** Corrupted EEPROM data or internal logic failure on the main control board prevents reliable program and data storage.
- **Temporary control glitch (~35%)** A power surge, voltage irregularity, or brief outage can scramble the control's internal memory and trigger F13 until the system is reset.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~15%)** Poor contact between the control board and its interface components or harnesses can break the communication path and mimic a memory fault.
- **User interface or keypad ribbon-cable issue (~10%)** On models with a separate display board, a damaged or partially seated ribbon cable can prevent the control from validating its data.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** to the range at the circuit breaker or by unplugging it, and wait 1-5 minutes to perform a full reset of the electronic control.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether F13 returns immediately or the range operates normally. If the code stays cleared, the fault was transient and no further action is needed.
3. **Turn off power again** and remove the control-panel assembly or rear access panel to expose the electronic oven control board and its connectors.
4. **Inspect all wire harnesses, ribbon cables, and connectors** for loose pins, corrosion, moisture, or heat damage. Reseat every connector firmly and check for broken or charred wires.
5. **Test the range** by restoring power. If F13 reappears, the electronic oven control board has failed and must be replaced.
6. **Order the correct replacement control board** by entering your range's full model and serial number into Frigidaire's parts lookup or a major appliance-parts supplier.
7. **Install the new control board** by disconnecting power, transferring all wire connectors to matching terminals on the new board, securing the board in its mounting bracket, and reassembling the panel before testing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt circuits or removing the control panel on your range. A pro can perform voltage and continuity checks on the control board and interface path, confirm the exact failure point, and source the correct model-specific board. Professional diagnosis is also warranted if F13 returns after you have already replaced the control board, since that may indicate a wiring fault or a separate issue with the user-interface assembly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150-300 in parts (control board), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $250-450 including service call and labor.
