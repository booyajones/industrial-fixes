## Gree Mini Split F8 Error Code — What It Means

On Gree mini splits, the F8 error code indicates overload current descending frequency protection. This means the inverter control board has detected abnormally high electrical current and is reducing compressor frequency to prevent damage to the system. It is a protective response, not a single-component sensor failure.

Some videos incorrectly describe F8 as a pipe coil sensor fault, but Gree's published error code list clearly identifies F8 as an overload current and frequency-limit condition. The root cause is typically an upstream problem that forces the compressor to draw excessive current, such as restricted airflow, electrical supply issues, dirty coils, or mechanical load problems.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or dirty coils** The indoor or outdoor coil is clogged with dust or debris, causing poor heat exchange and forcing the compressor to work harder and draw more current.
- **Restricted airflow** Clogged filters, blocked vents, or a failed fan prevent adequate air movement across the heat exchangers, raising system load and current draw.
- **Power supply or wiring problems** Low voltage, loose terminals, or damaged wiring create resistance and heat, leading to abnormal current readings that trigger overload protection.
- **Refrigerant overcharge or restriction** Too much refrigerant or a blockage in the system drives up head pressure and compressor load, resulting in high current draw.
- **Compressor or inverter board failure** A mechanically failing compressor or a faulty inverter control board can draw excessive current or misreport current levels, triggering F8.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker and wait two minutes before inspecting or resetting to allow capacitors to discharge and the fault to clear.
2. **Check and clean all filters** on the indoor unit and remove any obstructions blocking the indoor or outdoor vents and coil surfaces.
3. **Inspect the outdoor coil** for dirt, grass, leaves, or other debris and clean thoroughly with coil cleaner and a soft brush or low-pressure water.
4. **Verify supply voltage** at the outdoor disconnect and indoor unit with a multimeter, confirming it matches the nameplate rating and that all wire connections are tight and free of corrosion.
5. **Test outdoor fan operation** by restoring power and observing whether the fan spins freely and moves adequate air across the condenser coil.
6. **Monitor current draw** with a clamp meter on the compressor and fan circuits during operation, comparing readings to the nameplate specifications to identify abnormal load.
7. **Call a licensed technician** if voltage, airflow, and coils are normal but the F8 code persists, as inverter board diagnostics or refrigerant system work require specialized tools and training.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified clean coils, good airflow, correct supply voltage, and tight wiring connections but the F8 code still appears during operation. Diagnosing inverter board faults, measuring true compressor current under load, and checking refrigerant charge require specialized meters and EPA certification. Do not replace the control board or compressor based only on the code. A qualified technician will isolate the actual failed component through systematic electrical and refrigerant-side testing.
