## Bosch Oven E100 Error Code — What It Means

The E100 error code on Bosch ovens typically indicates a communication or electronic control fault, though the exact meaning can vary by model and production year. Because Bosch uses alphanumeric codes across many different oven platforms, E100 may point to a temperature sensor issue, a control board malfunction, or a wiring fault between components. Always consult your specific model's service manual or the wiring diagram inside the oven to confirm what E100 means for your unit.

In general, E100-series codes signal that the oven's main control board has detected a problem it cannot resolve on its own. This can range from a loose connector or corroded wire to a failed sensor or a fault in the control board itself. The oven will usually disable heating until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but a loose or corroded connector or a failed temperature sensor is often the real culprit. Check all connectors and test sensor resistance before spending on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded connector (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen or corrode the plug between the temperature sensor and the control board, breaking communication and triggering the code.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~30%)** The oven cavity or broiler sensor may drift out of spec or open-circuit, causing the control to flag an error.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires can chafe against sharp edges or melt near the heating element, creating shorts or open circuits.
- **Faulty main control board (~15%)** The electronic control board itself may fail due to power surges, component wear, or heat exposure.
- **Software glitch or memory fault (~5%)** A one-time electronic upset can log a phantom error that clears after a full power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the oven if possible to make sure safe work.
2. **Remove the oven door and racks**, then take off the back service panel or side panels to access the control board and sensor wiring.
3. **Inspect all connectors** at the control board, temperature sensor, and any relays. Look for corrosion, melted plastic, or loose pins. Reseat any suspect plugs.
4. **Test the temperature sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance. Compare the reading at room temperature to the spec in your service manual or wiring diagram.
5. **Check wiring continuity** by probing each wire from sensor to board. Look for breaks, shorts to ground, or chafed insulation near the element.
6. **Replace the faulty component** (sensor, connector, or control board) as indicated by your tests. If the sensor is bad, note its mounting bracket and terminal style before ordering.
7. **Reassemble the oven**, restore power, and run a test bake cycle. Clear any stored codes by holding the appropriate button combination (consult your manual) or by power-cycling again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside a live electrical cabinet, if testing the sensor and connectors reveals no obvious fault, or if the error persists after replacing the sensor and the control board is suspect. High-voltage connections and delicate surface-mount components on the board require care. A technician will have manufacturer diagnostics and can source exact-match parts quickly. Also call for help if the oven shares a junction box with other 240-volt appliances and you are unsure how to isolate the circuit safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
