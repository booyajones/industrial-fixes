## Amana Dryer F3 E3 Error Code — What It Means

F3 E3 on an Amana dryer signals an inlet thermistor fault. The inlet thermistor is a temperature sensor that monitors air entering the heating system. When the control board detects an abnormal reading from this sensor (open circuit, short, or out-of-range resistance), it throws F3 E3 and halts the cycle to prevent unsafe operation.

Amana's published troubleshooting starts with a simple power reset: shut off the breaker for five minutes, restore power, and run a timed dry cycle for five minutes to see if the fault clears. If F3 E3 returns, the inlet thermistor or its wiring is the problem. This code does not point to the exhaust thermistor, heating element, or blower. It isolates the issue to the inlet sensor circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first when F3 E3 appears. Always test the inlet thermistor resistance and inspect its connector before swapping the board, since a faulty sensor or broken wire is far more common.

## Common Causes

- **Failed inlet thermistor (~65%)** The inlet thermistor itself develops an open or short circuit, sending invalid readings to the control board.
- **Damaged wiring or connector (~20%)** Broken wires, corroded pins, or a loose plug at the thermistor harness prevent proper signal transmission.
- **Transient control glitch (~10%)** A temporary power surge or static event causes the control to misread the sensor until a full reset clears the fault.
- **Control board misread (~5%)** The main electronic control board fails to interpret a good thermistor signal correctly, though this is rare when the sensor tests in spec.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cut power at the circuit breaker** and wait five full minutes to discharge the control board and clear any transient fault.
2. **Restore power** and select a timed dry cycle (no heat setting needed). Let the dryer run for five minutes while you watch the display.
3. **Check whether F3 E3 returns.** If the code stays off through a complete cycle, the problem was temporary and no part replacement is required. Monitor the next few loads.
4. **Unplug the dryer** (or turn the breaker off again) if the fault reappears. Pull the dryer away from the wall and remove the lower front or rear access panel to locate the inlet thermistor, usually mounted on the blower housing or duct near the heating element.
5. **Inspect the thermistor connector and wiring** for burn marks, broken wires, pushed-out pins, or corrosion. Wiggle the plug gently to confirm it is fully seated. Repair or replace any damaged harness segments.
6. **Disconnect the thermistor plug** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance across the thermistor terminals. Consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected resistance at room temperature. An open (infinite resistance) or very low reading (near zero) confirms a bad sensor.
7. **Replace the inlet thermistor** if it tests out of range or if you see physical damage. Snap or screw the new sensor into the mounting bracket, reconnect the plug, and reassemble the panel. Restore power and run a full timed dry cycle to verify F3 E3 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you lack a multimeter to test the thermistor, or if the error persists after you have replaced both the inlet thermistor and verified all wiring is intact. A pro can quickly isolate whether the control board is misreading a good sensor or whether a hidden break exists in the harness. Also call for help if your dryer shares a 240 V circuit with other appliances and you are unsure how to safely de-energize the correct breaker.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call with part.
