## Navien Tankless Water Heater E183 Error — What It Means

The E183 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates the unit is detecting a problem with water flow or pressure that prevents proper operation. The exact definition can vary slightly between models, so consult your owner's manual for the precise meaning on your unit. In general, this code appears when the heater cannot sense adequate flow through the heat exchanger or when flow-related sensors report values outside normal range.

The unit will not fire or will shut down to protect itself from damage. Common triggers include clogged inlet screens, insufficient incoming water pressure, a failing flow sensor, air in the lines, or a problem with the circulation system if the unit is tied into a recirculation loop. Because tankless heaters require a minimum flow rate to ignite, anything that restricts flow or confuses the sensor can trip this code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flow sensor or circuit board when the real problem is simply debris in the inlet screens or low incoming water pressure. Clean the screens and verify supply pressure before ordering any sensor or board.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screens (~50%)** Sediment and debris accumulate on the fine mesh screens at the cold-water inlet, reducing flow below the threshold needed for ignition.
- **Insufficient incoming water pressure (~20%)** Municipal supply pressure too low or a partially closed shut-off valve starves the heater of the flow rate it needs to operate.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~15%)** The sensor that measures gallons per minute drifts out of calibration or fails, sending incorrect signals to the control board.
- **Air trapped in the system (~10%)** Air pockets in new installations or after maintenance prevent steady flow and confuse the sensor.
- **Recirculation pump or valve issue (~5%)** If your system includes a recirculation loop, a stuck check valve or failing pump can disrupt normal flow patterns.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the isolation valves on both the cold inlet and hot outlet.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to access the service valves, which typically have built-in filter screens.
3. **Unscrew the filter caps** on the cold-water inlet valve using a wrench, then pull out the fine mesh screens and rinse them under running water to remove sediment.
4. **Inspect the screens** for tears or heavy scale buildup. Replace the screens if they are damaged or cannot be fully cleaned.
5. **Reinstall the screens and caps**, tighten snugly, then open the isolation valves slowly to pressurize the system and watch for leaks.
6. **Restore power** and open a hot-water tap fully for two to three minutes to purge any air from the heat exchanger.
7. **Observe the display** while a tap is running. If the heater fires and the error does not return, the screens were the problem. If E183 persists, test or replace the flow sensor or call a technician to measure actual flow rate and sensor voltage.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien service technician if cleaning the inlet screens and bleeding air does not clear the error, if you measure incoming water pressure below your model's specification and cannot remedy it yourself, or if you suspect the flow sensor or circuit board has failed. A technician can use a flow meter and multimeter to verify gallons-per-minute at the inlet and check sensor voltage in real time. Also call a pro if the heater is still under warranty, since DIY repairs on certain internal components may void coverage. Gas-fired components and venting are best left to certified professionals.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts (if cleaning screens does not solve it, a flow sensor typically costs $30-40), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
