## Frigidaire Oven F93 Error Code — What It Means

The F93 code indicates that the electronic oven control (EOC) commanded the door to lock, but the door did not reach the locked state within the allowed time window. The control board is waiting for a confirmation signal from the lock system that the latch has fully engaged, and that signal never arrives.

This is a door lock system fault. Frigidaire groups F93 with other door lock issue codes (F90 through F94). The fault can be mechanical (a jammed or obstructed latch), electrical (a failed lock motor, switch, or wiring), or occasionally a control board problem.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control board first. Check the door lock assembly for physical obstructions, binding, and proper motor operation before replacing the EOC.

## Common Causes

- **Jammed or obstructed door lock mechanism (~40%)** Debris, bent cam rods, or a warped latch bracket physically prevent the lock from reaching its locked position.
- **Failed door lock motor or latch assembly (~30%)** The motorized latch mechanism no longer drives the lock cam or rod smoothly, so the lock never fully extends.
- **Faulty lock switch or microswitch (~15%)** The lock reaches position but the microswitch fails to signal the control, so the EOC times out waiting for confirmation.
- **Damaged wiring or connectors (~10%)** Broken, loose, or corroded wiring between the EOC and the lock assembly interrupts the command or feedback signal.
- **Defective electronic oven control (EOC) (~5%)** If the lock hardware, switch, and wiring all test good, the control board itself may be failing to interpret the lock feedback correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or by unplugging the oven for at least 30 seconds to reset the control and clear any false code.
2. **Remove the oven door** (if needed for access) and take off the inner door panel or lower access panel to expose the door lock assembly and latch mechanism.
3. **Inspect the latch path** for debris, grease buildup, bent cam rods, or any physical obstruction that would prevent the lock from moving freely through its full travel.
4. **Cycle the lock mechanism** by hand (power off) and observe whether the motor-driven cam or rod moves smoothly and whether the latch reaches its full locked position without binding.
5. **Test the lock switch or microswitch** by checking that it clicks and changes state when the lock moves to the locked position, and inspect the wiring connectors and harness for loose, broken, or corroded pins.
6. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor does not drive the latch, the microswitch does not actuate, or any mechanical component is broken or worn.
7. **Replace the electronic oven control (EOC)** only if the lock mechanism, switch, and all wiring test good and the fault persists after reassembly and a full power cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working inside the oven door, if disassembly requires removing the control panel or door hinges, or if you have tested the lock assembly and wiring and still cannot isolate the fault. A pro has the wiring diagrams, multimeter skills, and experience to trace lock-circuit voltages and confirm whether the EOC or the lock hardware is at fault. If the oven is still under warranty, contact Frigidaire or an authorized service center before attempting repairs yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
