## Frigidaire Oven F93 Error Code — What It Means

F93 is part of Frigidaire's door lock error group (F90 through F94). The manufacturer's published support materials classify F93 as a door lock issue without providing a more granular public definition. Third-party repair references commonly interpret F93 as a maximum door lock time exceeded or door motor mechanism failure, meaning the oven's control board detected that the door lock motor did not complete its lock or unlock cycle within the expected time window.

This error typically appears when the oven is trying to lock the door for a self-clean cycle or when it is attempting to unlock the door afterward. The lock mechanism uses a small motor or actuator to slide a latch into place. When that motor stalls, binds, or fails to signal completion, the control board times out and displays F93. The error can also appear if the door is misaligned, the latch is obstructed, or the wiring between the lock assembly and the control board is damaged.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Before ordering a new board, inspect the door lock motor for binding, test its wiring for continuity, and verify the latch moves freely by hand.

## Common Causes

- **Sticking or failed door lock motor/actuator (~50%)** The small motor that drives the latch can bind from grease buildup, wear out electrically, or fail to send a position signal back to the control.
- **Binding latch or strike (~20%)** The metal latch arm or the strike receiver can become bent, corroded, or obstructed by debris, preventing smooth travel and causing the motor to time out.
- **Misaligned door or latch assembly (~15%)** If the oven door has shifted on its hinges or the lock assembly mounting has loosened, the latch may not engage the strike properly and the lock cycle cannot complete.
- **Lock switch or sensor circuit failure (~10%)** The lock position switch or door-closed switch may be open, shorted, or corroded, so the control cannot confirm latch position even when the motor moves.
- **Wiring damage between lock assembly and control (~5%)** Wires in the harness can break, connectors can loosen, or insulation can melt near the oven cavity, interrupting the lock motor or switch signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker for at least 30 seconds to reset the control board, then restore power and press the unit's CANCEL or UNLOCK button if your model has one.
2. **Let the oven cool completely** if the error appeared during or after a self-clean cycle, since the latch may still be thermally bound and will not release until the cavity temperature drops below the lock threshold.
3. **Inspect the door alignment** by opening and closing the door to confirm it sits square in the frame and that the latch arm on the door frame lines up with the strike hole in the oven cavity.
4. **Remove the door lock assembly cover** (usually inside the top front of the oven cavity or behind the control panel on the back of the door, depending on your model) and visually check the latch arm for obstructions, bent metal, or grease buildup that could cause binding.
5. **Test the lock motor circuit** by disconnecting the harness plug at the lock assembly and using a multimeter to check for continuity through the motor windings and across any lock-position switches. One field reference reports a cold resistance around 200 Ω across the PTC terminals of the lock motor, though this is not a manufacturer-published Frigidaire specification.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** between the lock assembly and the control board for broken wires, loose connectors, or heat damage, paying close attention to any routing near the oven cavity where insulation can melt.
7. **Replace the failed component** (lock motor assembly, lock switch, or harness) if testing reveals an open circuit, short, or mechanical failure. If all lock-side parts test good and the fault persists after reassembly, suspect the control board and consult a technician or replace the board as a last step.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside the oven cabinet, if you cannot locate or access the lock assembly after consulting your model's service manual, or if all mechanical and electrical tests of the lock components pass yet the F93 error persists. A technician has model-specific wiring diagrams and can perform control-board diagnostics that are difficult without a service manual. Also call a pro if you discover melted wiring or burnt connectors that suggest a deeper electrical fault, since those conditions can indicate a short circuit that may damage a new lock motor or control board if not traced and repaired first.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–$150 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–$300.
