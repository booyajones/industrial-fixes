## State Tankless Water Heater E71 Error — What It Means

An E71 error code on a State tankless water heater is not reliably documented in manufacturer service literature for all State models, but it typically indicates an ignition or flame-proving failure in the gas valve or solenoid circuit family. State tankless units share platform architecture with A. O. Smith, and codes in this range point to problems where the unit cannot ignite the burner or cannot confirm flame presence after ignition. This can stem from air-supply issues, gas-supply problems, water-flow restrictions, vent blockages, or dirty flame-sensing components.

Because the exact definition of E71 varies by model, always pull the service manual or wiring diagram for your specific State unit before proceeding. The fault may involve a solenoid valve circuit (similar to Rinnai code 71 solenoid faults) or moisture intrusion around the combustion chamber, but those interpretations are not verified across all State tankless platforms.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or gas valve first. Check and clean the air intake screen, flame rod, and inlet water filter before replacing any electronic or gas-supply component.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake (~30%)** Lint, dust, or debris on the intake screen starves the burner of combustion air and prevents ignition or flame proving.
- **Dirty flame sensor or flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or soot on the flame-sensing electrode prevents the control board from confirming flame presence, triggering a lockout.
- **Low or restricted water flow (~15%)** A clogged inlet filter or low supply pressure stops the unit from reaching minimum flow to fire the burner.
- **Restricted exhaust vent (~10%)** Blockage or improper venting creates back-pressure that prevents ignition or causes flame loss after startup.
- **Low gas pressure or inadequate gas supply (~10%)** Undersized gas line, partially closed valve, or low utility pressure prevents the burner from lighting or holding flame.
- **Solenoid valve or gas valve circuit fault (~10%)** A failed solenoid coil or control-board output to the gas valve interrupts the ignition sequence or flame-holding circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact State model number** and consult the service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the meaning of E71 for your unit.
2. **Turn off power and gas** at the shutoffs before performing any inspection or cleaning.
3. **Inspect and clean the air intake screen or filter** for lint, dust, pet hair, or other debris that restricts combustion air.
4. **Remove and clean the flame rod or flame sensor** using fine sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon buildup or soot.
5. **Check and clean the inlet water filter** at the cold-water inlet to the heater; low flow will prevent ignition.
6. **Inspect the exhaust vent pipe** for blockage, nests, or improper termination that could cause back-pressure.
7. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the shutoff valve is fully open and that other gas appliances operate normally; low gas pressure requires a plumber or gas technician.
8. **Test the gas valve and solenoid circuits** using a multimeter per the manufacturer procedure if all other checks pass; replace the gas valve or control board only after confirming the circuit fault.
9. **Restore power and gas, then reset the unit** and observe the ignition sequence; if E71 returns, call a licensed technician for circuit-level diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician for any E71 repair that involves the gas valve, solenoid circuits, control board diagnostics, or suspected moisture intrusion in the combustion chamber. Gas work requires proper licensing, leak testing, and combustion-air verification. If you have cleaned the air intake, flame sensor, and inlet filter and the error persists, professional circuit-level diagnostics and gas-pressure testing are necessary to avoid unsafe operation or misdiagnosis.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350.
