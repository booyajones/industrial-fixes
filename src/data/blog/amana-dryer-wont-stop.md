## Amana Dryer Won't Stop — What's Happening

When an Amana dryer won't stop, it means the machine keeps running after the cycle should have ended or does not shut off when it's supposed to. The dryer may run continuously or fail to advance through the cycle properly, staying on indefinitely until you manually turn it off or unplug it.

This is usually a problem with either the airflow system causing overheating and faulty cycle detection, or a control component that isn't advancing or terminating the run signal. It's not typically an error code on most Amana dryers, just abnormal behavior where the machine doesn't recognize when to stop.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Restricted airflow from clogged vent or lint buildup** This is the most common cause when an Amana dryer won't stop, because blocked vents prevent proper air circulation and interfere with temperature and moisture sensing that tells the dryer when to shut off.
- **Defective main control board** A failed electronic control board can lose the ability to terminate power to the motor and heating circuit at the correct time, especially after airflow problems are ruled out.
- **Faulty timer** On mechanical-timer models, a bad timer motor or worn contact set can prevent the cycle from advancing or stopping correctly.
- **Stuck start button or control switch** If the start switch remains mechanically stuck or electrically closed, it can keep sending a continuous run signal to the dryer.
- **Faulty door switch** A door switch that fails in the closed position or sends an incorrect signal can create abnormal run and stop behavior, though this more often causes no-run issues.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm whether the problem happens on timed-dry cycles, auto-dry cycles, or both to separate timer and control faults from moisture-sensing and airflow faults.
2. Pull and clean the lint filter, then disconnect the dryer and inspect the full length of the exhaust duct and outside termination for heavy lint buildup or blockage.
3. Reconnect the dryer and run a short test cycle to verify it heats properly and does not obviously overheat, watching for normal temperature rise and air movement at the vent outlet.
4. If airflow is clear and heating is normal, unplug the dryer and access the control panel to inspect the timer or main control board for signs of arcing, burned contacts, or stuck components.
5. Check the start button for mechanical sticking and test the door switch with a multimeter for correct continuity when the door is closed and open.
6. Isolate the suspected timer, control board, or switch and perform continuity or function testing per the service manual.
7. Replace the failed component, reassemble the dryer, and run a complete cycle to verify it shuts off at the proper time without overrunning.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you've cleaned all venting and the dryer still won't stop, or if you're not comfortable testing live voltage on control boards and timer assemblies. A pro can safely isolate whether the fault is in the timer, main control, or a sensing circuit, and replace the correct part without risking shock or further damage to the dryer.
