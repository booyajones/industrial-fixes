## Navien E300 Error Code — What It Means

The E300 code on a Navien tankless water heater typically signals that the unit has detected an abnormal condition related to overheating, flame sensing, or combustion. The exact definition can vary by model and firmware version, so always consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific meaning on your unit. In general, the code triggers when the control board senses conditions that could pose a safety risk, such as excessive heat buildup, inconsistent flame signal, or airflow restrictions. The heater will lock out to prevent damage or unsafe operation until the underlying problem is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame rod or igniter first when the real problem is a dirty inlet filter or blocked exhaust vent. Clean both the inlet screen and inspect the vent termination outdoors before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted water flow (~35%)** A clogged inlet filter or partially closed service valve reduces flow through the heat exchanger, causing the unit to overheat and trip the sensor.
- **Blocked or restricted vent (~25%)** Debris, ice, or improper venting prevents exhaust gases from leaving, starving the burner of air and triggering a safety lockout.
- **Failing flame rod (~20%)** Carbon buildup or a weakened flame-rod sensor sends erratic signals to the board, which interprets them as unsafe combustion conditions.
- **Faulty overheat sensor (~10%)** The high-limit thermistor may read incorrectly or fail open, causing the board to believe the heat exchanger is too hot even when it is not.
- **Gas supply or pressure issue (~7%)** Low gas pressure or a partly closed gas valve can produce an unstable flame, leading to erratic sensing and code lockouts.
- **Control board glitch (~3%)** Firmware bugs or electrical noise can occasionally set the code without a true fault, resolved by a power reset or board update.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve to the heater.
2. **Remove and clean the inlet filter screen** at the cold-water connection using a soft brush and running water.
3. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vent pipes** from end to end, checking for obstructions, damage, or improper pitch that allows condensate to pool.
4. **Check the flame-rod assembly** inside the burner chamber for carbon deposits or corrosion; wipe gently with fine emery cloth if accessible.
5. **Test the overheat sensor** with a multimeter if you have a wiring diagram showing its location and resistance values; replace if out of specification.
6. **Verify gas supply pressure** at the inlet to the unit using a manometer; consult your model's installation manual for the correct range.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then restart the heater and monitor the display for code recurrence or normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if free checks do not clear the code, if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, or if diagnostics point to the flame rod, overheat sensor, or control board. These components require disassembly of the combustion chamber and careful testing to avoid misdiagnosis. Gas-pressure testing and any work on the sealed combustion system should always be performed by a licensed professional to maintain warranty coverage and make sure safe operation.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
