## Whirlpool Refrigerator Running Constantly — What's Happening

A Whirlpool refrigerator running constantly is not a fault code by itself. It is a symptom that the cabinet is not reaching setpoint efficiently, so the control keeps calling for cooling. The compressor and fans may run for long periods while the system tries to pull the compartment down to the target temperature.

Whirlpool's troubleshooting guidance frames this as the refrigerator still working to satisfy the thermostat. The most common causes are warm setpoint or incorrect controls, door seal leakage, dirty condenser coils, blocked airflow, high ambient temperature, or a cooling system or control problem. Some models also have a Demo or Showroom mode that can affect normal cooling behavior and must be ruled out early.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Dirty condenser coils** Whirlpool and service guidance both identify dirty coils as a common reason the unit runs constantly because heat cannot be rejected efficiently.
- **Door seal or gasket leakage** Debris or a cracked gasket prevents full contact around the door perimeter, letting warm air into the cabinet continuously.
- **Temperature setting too warm or controls set incorrectly** Whirlpool says to set the controls correctly and, if needed, turn the temperature colder and wait 24 hours for the cabinet to stabilize.
- **Demo or Showroom mode enabled** Whirlpool notes that some models have a Demo or Showroom mode that can affect normal cooling behavior and must be ruled out early.
- **Poor airflow, blocked vents, or improper loading** Food or packaging blocking internal vents or overpacking the cabinet disrupts circulation and forces the compressor to run longer.
- **High ambient temperature or installation near heat sources** Whirlpool says most units are designed for locations between 55°F and 110°F and should not be installed near ovens or radiators.
- **Condenser fan or compressor fault** Service troubleshooting for Whirlpool refrigerators emphasizes checking compressor operation and the condenser fan, because a failed fan can leave the cabinet hot and force extended runtime.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the unit is not in Demo or Showroom mode and confirm cooling is enabled.
2. Check the temperature control setting and turn it to a colder setting if needed, then wait 24 hours to see if runtime improves.
3. Inspect the door gasket for cracks or deformation and clean any debris from the gasket and mating surfaces to confirm full contact around the perimeter.
4. Confirm the ambient temperature is between 55°F and 110°F and that the refrigerator is not installed next to heat sources or lacks adequate clearance for ventilation.
5. Unplug the unit and clean the condenser coils with a vacuum and brush to remove dust and debris.
6. Check that internal vents are not blocked by food or packaging and that the cabinet is not overpacked in a way that disrupts circulation.
7. Verify the compressor is running and the condenser fan is moving air at the rear of the refrigerator.
8. If the problem persists after basic checks, test the temperature sensors, wiring, and main control board for faults, or check for evaporator icing or defrost circuit problems if cooling is weak.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the refrigerator still runs constantly after cleaning the coils, confirming proper door sealing, checking airflow, and verifying correct control settings. Compressor, condenser fan, temperature sensor, defrost circuit, and main control board diagnosis all require test equipment and refrigerant system knowledge. If the evaporator is iced over or the cabinet will not cool despite continuous runtime, the problem is in the sealed system or electronic controls and a professional should diagnose and repair it.
