## Daikin Mini Split LC-01 Error — What It Means

The LC-01 error code on Daikin mini split systems typically indicates a communication failure between the indoor air handler and the outdoor condensing unit. The two units exchange control signals and sensor data through low-voltage wiring, and when that signal is interrupted or corrupted, the system cannot operate safely.

This code may appear on startup or during operation. The indoor unit's display will show LC-01 and the system will shut down to prevent improper operation. The fault can stem from damaged wiring, loose terminals, voltage issues, or a failed control board in either unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many technicians replace the indoor control board when the actual problem is a pinched or corroded wire at the outdoor unit terminal block. Inspect all field wiring and terminals under both covers before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded terminal connections (~40%)** Vibration, weather exposure, or improper torque during installation can cause communication wires to loosen or corrode at the terminal blocks.
- **Damaged communication wiring (~25%)** The low-voltage control wires between units can be nicked, pinched, or chewed by pests, breaking the signal path.
- **Voltage drop or power supply issue (~15%)** Insufficient or unstable incoming power to either unit can prevent the control boards from establishing a stable communication link.
- **Failed indoor control board (~10%)** The main PCB inside the air handler can develop solder cracks or component failures that prevent it from sending or receiving signals.
- **Failed outdoor control board (~10%)** The outdoor unit's control board can fail due to moisture intrusion, power surges, or component wear, interrupting communication.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the indoor unit display light up and respond to the remote?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The indoor board has power and basic function; focus on the communication wiring and outdoor unit connections.<br><strong>No:</strong> Check the indoor unit's power supply and circuit breaker; the board may not be receiving voltage at all.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the outdoor unit running or attempting to start when you call for cooling?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Communication may be intermittent; inspect connectors and wire insulation along the entire run for damage.<br><strong>No:</strong> The outdoor unit is not receiving the start command; check outdoor power, disconnect switch, and wiring at the outdoor terminal block.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are all terminal screws on both units snug and free of corrosion?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Wiring connections are sound; the fault likely lies in a control board or power supply issue requiring a technician.<br><strong>No:</strong> Tighten loose terminals and clean corrosion with contact cleaner, then test the system again.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to both the indoor and outdoor units at their respective circuit breakers and wait at least two minutes to allow capacitors to discharge.
2. **Remove the cover** from the indoor air handler to access the main control board and terminal block, noting the wire colors and positions.
3. **Inspect all communication wire terminals** at the indoor unit for tightness, corrosion, or burnt marks, and check that wires are seated fully in their connectors.
4. **Access the outdoor unit** service panel and locate the low-voltage terminal block, then check the same wiring connections for damage or looseness.
5. **Trace the communication wiring** between units along its entire path, looking for pinches, cuts, staple damage, or rodent activity that could break continuity.
6. **Restore power** to both units and observe the startup sequence, watching for the LC-01 code to reappear or clear.
7. **Contact a licensed HVAC technician** if the error persists after confirming all wiring is secure, as further diagnosis requires multimeter testing of control board signals and possibly board replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical panels or if the error persists after checking all accessible wiring connections. Diagnosing LC-01 often requires a multimeter to measure control voltages and signal continuity between boards, and many Daikin systems use proprietary communication protocols that need specialized diagnostic tools. A technician can also identify whether the fault lies in a control board, power supply transformer, or outdoor inverter circuit. Refrigerant work is not usually needed for this code, but the repair involves live electrical testing and may require ordering OEM control boards that must be programmed or configured to match your system.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
