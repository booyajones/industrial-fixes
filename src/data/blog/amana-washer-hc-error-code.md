## Amana HC Error Code — What It Means

HC stands for Hot/Cold reversed on Amana top-load HE washers. The code appears at the end of the wash cycle when the washer detects that the cold supply hose is connected to the hot inlet valve and the hot supply hose is connected to the cold inlet valve. This is an installation or service issue, not a failure of the motor, drain system, or door lock.

Amana describes HC as a hose-connection problem. The washer's temperature sensors recognize that water temperatures do not match the expected inlet during the cycle. The prescribed fix is to turn off the water supply, reverse the hoses to the correct ports, and run another cycle to verify the correction.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes suspect a failed control board or inlet valve when they see an error code. Before replacing any internal parts, simply check the rear hose connections and swap them if they are reversed.

## Common Causes

- **Hot and cold hoses reversed (~85%)** The hot supply hose is connected to the cold inlet and the cold hose is connected to the hot inlet, either from original installation or after recent service work.
- **Water supply not fully open (~10%)** One or both shutoff valves behind the washer are partially closed, which can confuse the temperature reading and complicate diagnosis even though it is not the direct meaning of the HC code.
- **Damaged or kinked supply hoses (~3%)** A crushed or bent inlet hose restricts flow and may prevent the washer from sampling the correct water temperature, leading to a false HC reading.
- **Installation or configuration error (~2%)** If the code persists after hoses are corrected, the next concern is usually a model-specific setup issue rather than a failed electronic component, based on Amana's troubleshooting path.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off both water shutoff valves** behind the washer by rotating them fully clockwise.
2. **Pull the washer away from the wall** enough to see the rear inlet ports and the attached hot and cold hoses.
3. **Identify the hot and cold inlet ports** on the washer (typically marked H and C) and note which hose is connected to each.
4. **Disconnect both supply hoses** from the washer inlets using an adjustable wrench or pliers, and place a towel underneath to catch any residual water.
5. **Swap the hoses** so the hot supply hose connects to the hot inlet (H) and the cold supply hose connects to the cold inlet (C), then hand-tighten each connection and snug with a wrench (do not overtighten).
6. **Turn both shutoff valves fully counterclockwise** to open the water supply and check for leaks at the connections.
7. **Run a complete wash cycle** and confirm that the HC code does not reappear at the end of the cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the HC code persists after you have verified the hoses are connected to the correct ports, both shutoff valves are fully open, and the hoses are undamaged. A persistent code may indicate a model-specific installation or configuration problem that requires Amana service documentation or diagnostic equipment. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working behind the washer, if the inlet ports or valves show signs of corrosion or leakage, or if you need the rear panel opened to inspect internal wiring or sensors.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0 (no parts needed if hoses are simply reversed), 5-10 min. A pro service call runs about $80-150 service call (though most technicians will simply swap the hoses).
