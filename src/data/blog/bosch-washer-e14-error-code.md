## Bosch Washer E14 Error Code — What It Means

The E14 error code on Bosch washing machines typically indicates a problem with the electronic control module or associated sensors. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific definition. In general, this code points to a communication fault, voltage irregularity, or sensor reading that the control board cannot process.

The fault often stems from loose connections, moisture intrusion on circuit boards, or a failing control module itself. Because Bosch uses different control architectures across model families, the E14 code may signal different specific faults depending on your washer's production year and series.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the entire control module when the real problem is a loose connector or oxidized contact. Inspect and clean all harness plugs at the control board and reseat them firmly before buying a new module.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded harness connectors (~40%)** Vibration during spin cycles can work connectors loose over time, and moisture from leaks or condensation can corrode contacts at the control module.
- **Failed main control module (~25%)** The electronic control board itself can develop solder cracks, capacitor failures, or relay faults that prevent normal operation.
- **Intermittent power supply or voltage fluctuations (~20%)** A tripped breaker, loose outlet, or household voltage drop can cause the control board to register faults and throw error codes.
- **Faulty sensor or thermistor (~10%)** Temperature or water-level sensors that send erratic readings can trigger control faults and communication errors.
- **Moisture intrusion on circuit boards (~5%)** Water entering the control compartment from a leak or high humidity can create shorts and phantom signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the washer or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the top or rear panel** to access the control module compartment (fastener locations vary by model).
3. **Inspect all wiring harness connectors** at the control board for looseness, bent pins, or green corrosion.
4. **Disconnect each connector** one at a time and spray contacts with electrical contact cleaner, then reseat firmly until you hear a click.
5. **Check for moisture** inside the control compartment and dry any damp areas with a clean cloth or compressed air.
6. **Reinstall the access panel** and restore power, then run a test cycle to see if the error clears.
7. **Replace the main control module** if the error persists and all connections are clean and tight, matching the module part number to your washer's model and serial tag.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the control compartment or if testing with a multimeter is required to isolate sensor faults. A technician can measure voltage at the board, test sensor continuity, and verify that replacement parts match your specific Bosch model. If the washer is still under warranty, contact Bosch service to avoid voiding coverage. Also call a pro if you find evidence of a leak that has damaged multiple electrical components, as that repair may involve both plumbing and electrical work.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-180 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
