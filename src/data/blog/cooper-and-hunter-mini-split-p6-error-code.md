## Cooper & Hunter Mini Split P6 Error — What It Means

P6 is a pressure-protection fault code on Cooper & Hunter mini splits. It means the system detected refrigerant pressure outside the normal operating range or a malfunction in the low-pressure or high-pressure switch circuits. The control board enters protection mode to prevent compressor damage when suction or discharge pressure is too low or too high.

This code can appear because the refrigerant charge is incorrect, there is a restriction in the system, a pressure switch has failed, or the wiring to the switch is damaged. The Cooper & Hunter troubleshooting guidance emphasizes checking actual system pressures first, then testing the pressure switches themselves if gauge readings are normal.

## Common Causes

- **Incorrect refrigerant charge** Undercharge or overcharge creates abnormal suction or discharge pressure that triggers the protection circuit.
- **Failed pressure switch** The low-pressure or high-pressure switch may be stuck open, intermittent, or completely failed.
- **Refrigerant restriction or flow blockage** A clogged filter-drier, kinked line, or metering device problem causes pressure to fall outside normal limits.
- **Wiring or connector fault at the pressure switch** Corroded pins, loose connectors, or broken harness wires prevent the board from reading the switch correctly.
- **Control board pressure-input circuit malfunction** If switches and wiring test good but the code persists, the board's input circuit may be reading incorrectly.
- **Airflow or coil condition affecting pressure** Dirty filters, blocked coils, or fan issues can contribute to pressure abnormalities during operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault code** on the indoor unit display and write down the exact code to confirm it is P6, not a different error.
2. **Attach refrigerant gauges** to the service ports and measure suction and discharge pressures while the unit attempts to run, comparing readings to the expected range for your ambient and load conditions.
3. **Inspect for refrigerant leaks** if pressures are low, check for overcharge if pressures are high, and look for restrictions such as kinked lines or clogged filter-driers if pressures are unbalanced.
4. **Test the pressure switches** by isolating each switch circuit and checking continuity across the switch terminals, following the Cooper & Hunter service procedure for your model.
5. **Inspect wiring and connectors** at both the low-pressure and high-pressure switches and at the control board for corrosion, loose pins, or damaged insulation.
6. **Replace the faulty pressure switch** if continuity testing shows it is open or behaves intermittently, or replace the control board if the switch circuit tests good but the fault remains.
7. **Clear power** by turning off the breaker for two minutes, restore power, and run the system through a full cycle to confirm the P6 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not EPA-certified to handle refrigerant, if you do not own a manifold gauge set and recovery equipment, or if you are uncomfortable working with live electrical circuits. Diagnosing P6 requires measuring live refrigerant pressures, testing low-voltage switch circuits, and potentially adding or removing refrigerant. If the fault returns after you replace a switch or if pressures remain abnormal with no obvious leak or restriction, a technician with Cooper & Hunter service training can perform advanced board-level diagnostics and make sure the system is charged to the correct superheat and subcooling targets.
