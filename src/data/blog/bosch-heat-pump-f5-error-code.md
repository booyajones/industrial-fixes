## Bosch Heat Pump F5 Error Code — What It Means

The F5 error code on Bosch heat pumps has different meanings depending on your product line. In Bosch Industrial HVAC systems, F5 indicates a T6A temperature sensor error. In some Bosch ductless mini-split indoor units, F5 (displayed alongside E3) points to a fan speed too low or too high condition. Because Bosch uses different code sets across product families, you must check your specific model's manual to interpret F5 correctly before starting any repair work.

## Common Causes

- **Open or shorted temperature sensor** The T6A sensor or equivalent temperature sensor has failed internally and no longer provides a valid resistance reading to the control board.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged sensor wiring** Wiring or connectors between the sensor and PCB are loose, corroded, rubbed through, or have damaged pins that interrupt the signal.
- **Faulty control board input circuit** The PCB sensor input stage is damaged and reads the fault even when the sensor and wiring test correctly.
- **Fan motor or airflow restriction (E3/F5 fan-speed fault)** If your unit uses the fan-speed version of F5, the indoor or outdoor fan is failing, blocked, or not sending proper feedback to the board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the breaker and confirm your model number and fault table in the installation or service manual to determine whether F5 is a sensor error or fan-speed fault.
2. **Inspect sensor connectors and wiring** for looseness, corrosion, bent pins, or damaged insulation on both the sensor end and the PCB connection.
3. **Disconnect the suspect sensor** (T6A or equivalent) and measure its resistance with a multimeter, checking that it is not an open circuit or dead short.
4. **Warm the sensor** with your hand and watch the resistance change, a good thermistor will shift resistance predictably with temperature change.
5. **Replace the sensor** with the exact same resistance type if it fails resistance checks or does not respond to temperature, ensuring the connector seats fully.
6. **Check the control board sensor input** if the sensor tests good but the fault persists, measure board-side reference voltage only if you have the model-specific service chart.
7. **If F5 is the fan-speed version**, inspect the indoor and outdoor fan motors, blades, filters, and coil for blockage or damage, and verify fan feedback signals before replacing the fan motor or board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line voltage, if you cannot locate or access the sensor on your model, or if the sensor and wiring both test correctly but the fault code persists. Technicians have model-specific resistance tables, board-level diagnostics, and refrigerant certification if the repair requires opening the sealed system. Also call a pro if your unit displays the fan-speed version of F5 and you find no obvious airflow blockage, since fan control faults often require board-level troubleshooting and feedback signal tracing.
