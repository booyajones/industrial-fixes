## State Water Heater E171 Error Code — What It Means

The E171 code on State tankless water heaters signals an ignition or flame-proving failure during startup. The unit is attempting to light but cannot establish or confirm combustion. This is a safety lockout that prevents gas flow until the problem is corrected.

The control board has detected that the ignition sequence did not produce a stable flame signal within the expected time window. The heater will not operate until you clear the underlying cause and reset the fault. Because State is an A. O. Smith brand, your specific model's manual (accessible via the QR code on the rating plate) will provide the exact code definition and troubleshooting steps for your unit family.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or dirty filter** Lint, dust, or debris on intake screens or filters restricts combustion air and prevents ignition or stable flame-proving.
- **Insufficient gas supply or low gas pressure** Undersized gas piping, a partially closed shutoff valve, or inadequate meter capacity starves the burner during light-off.
- **Restricted venting or exhaust blockage** Blocked, kinked, or over-elbowed exhaust vent piping causes poor draft and combustion-air imbalance that trips the flame sensor.
- **Dirty or corroded ignition and flame-sensing components** Carbon buildup or mineral deposits on the ignition electrode or flame rod prevent reliable spark and flame-signal continuity.
- **Poor grounding or loose wiring connections** A weak ground path or corroded harness connectors interrupt the flame-proving circuit and trigger a false failure.
- **Failed gas valve, ignition transformer, or control board** Internal component failure prevents the control from energizing ignition or commanding the gas valve, even when all inputs are correct.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the fault code and model family** by checking the display and locating the rating plate to reference your unit's specific manual (scan the QR code if present).
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake filter and screens** on the exterior of the heater, removing any lint, dust, or debris that could restrict combustion air flow.
3. **Verify the gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve is fully open, then measure inlet gas pressure under firing conditions and compare the reading to your model's specification table in the manual.
4. **Check the exhaust and intake venting** for blockages, excessive length, too many elbows, or damaged terminations that could create back pressure or draft problems.
5. **Remove and clean the ignition electrode and flame sensor** (consult your manual for locations) using a fine abrasive pad or soft brush to remove carbon, corrosion, or mineral scale.
6. **Inspect all ground wiring and harness connectors** at the gas valve, control board, and chassis ground points for tight, clean contact and repair any corroded or loose terminals.
7. **Test the gas valve and ignition outputs** using a multimeter or by listening for spark and gas-valve click during a call for heat; if the control commands ignition but the component does not respond, replace the failed part.
8. **Descale the heat exchanger** if you see heavy mineral buildup or if the unit has a history of hard water; scale can interfere with combustion and flame stability.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas connections, if you lack a manometer to verify gas pressure, or if cleaning intake components and resetting the unit does not clear the E171 code. A professional should handle any gas-valve or control-board replacement, measure combustion parameters, and confirm safe venting and grounding. If the heater has been serviced repeatedly for the same fault, a pro can perform a full combustion analysis and check for installation issues such as undersized gas piping, inadequate venting, or electrical grounding defects that are outside the scope of basic homeowner troubleshooting.
