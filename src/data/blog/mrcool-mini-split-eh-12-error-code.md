## MRCOOL Mini Split EH 12 Error — What It Means

The EH 12 error code on MRCOOL mini split systems indicates a problem with the electric auxiliary heating element or its control circuit. This code appears when the system detects a fault in the electric heater operation, which may involve wiring issues, a failed relay, a faulty heating element, or a sensor problem. Because the exact definition can vary slightly between MRCOOL models, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the indoor unit for model-specific details.

The auxiliary heater is an optional component that provides supplemental heat during very cold weather or when the heat pump alone cannot maintain the desired temperature. When this code appears, the unit typically continues to run in heat-pump mode but the electric heater will not activate, reducing overall heating capacity.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many users replace the heating element when the real issue is a failed relay or loose connector at the control board. Check all wiring connections and test the relay before ordering a new heater.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~35%)** Vibration or heat cycling can cause terminals at the heater element or relay to loosen, breaking the circuit and triggering the fault.
- **Failed auxiliary heater relay (~30%)** The solid-state or electromechanical relay that energizes the heater can fail due to high current or voltage spikes, preventing heater operation.
- **Burned-out heating element (~20%)** The resistance coil inside the auxiliary heater can open or short, causing the control board to detect an abnormal current draw.
- **Faulty temperature or current sensor (~10%)** A sensor monitoring heater temperature or current can drift out of calibration or fail, sending incorrect signals to the control board.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The indoor unit's main control board may fail to supply the correct voltage or signal to the heater circuit, often after a power surge.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or disconnect switch for both the indoor and outdoor units and wait 60 seconds before beginning any work.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** by lifting the front panel and unscrewing any fasteners that secure the lower service panel to access the control board and wiring.
3. **Inspect all wire connections** at the auxiliary heater terminal block, the relay, and the control board, looking for loose terminals, corrosion, or burn marks.
4. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter set to resistance mode by disconnecting power and measuring across the element terminals (consult your model's table for the expected ohm value).
5. **Check the relay** by verifying continuity across the coil and the switched contacts, or by swapping it with an identical relay if your board has multiple relays of the same type.
6. **Measure voltage** at the heater terminals with power restored and the system calling for auxiliary heat to confirm the control board is sending the correct signal.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (wiring harness, relay, heating element, or control board) and reassemble the unit, then restore power and run a full heating cycle to verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt circuits or if basic checks do not reveal an obvious loose connection. Electric auxiliary heaters operate at line voltage and pose a serious shock hazard. A technician has the tools to measure current draw, verify control board logic, and safely test live circuits. Professional diagnosis is also recommended if the heating element tests good but the code persists, as this points to a control board or sensor issue that requires schematic-level troubleshooting.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $180-400.
