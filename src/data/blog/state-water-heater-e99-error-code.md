## State Water Heater E99 Error Code — What It Means

E99 is not a universally documented error code in State's published materials, but available evidence points to an internal control or hardware lockout. The closest match in technical sources describes E99 as a hardware fault requiring a reset and, if persistent, mainboard replacement. Because State water heaters share platforms with A.O. Smith and the exact meaning varies by model (gas storage, gas tankless, hybrid electric), you must confirm the code definition against your unit's installation manual and data plate. Until you verify the model-specific meaning, treat E99 as a control system shutdown that prevents normal operation.

## Common Causes

- **Control board or internal hardware fault** The only direct E99 reference in technical sources identifies it as a hardware error requiring power-cycling and, if unresolved, control board replacement.
- **Dirty or failed thermocouple or flame sensor** On State gas models, contamination or failure of the flame-proving components is a common shutdown cause emphasized in manufacturer training material.
- **Gas control valve or gas sensor valve issue** Gas delivery or control valve problems will force the system into lockout, a frequent root cause on State and A.O. Smith gas platforms.
- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Tankless units using the State platform will shut down if the combustion air intake screen or exhaust path is obstructed.
- **Temperature sensor or button stuck closed** A stuck temperature-adjustment button or faulty sensor can create a lockout condition on gas storage models.
- **Gas supply interruption** Low or interrupted gas pressure will prevent ignition and trigger fault codes on both storage and tankless gas water heaters.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Identify your exact model number and platform (gas storage, gas tankless, or hybrid electric) from the data plate and locate the corresponding installation manual to confirm the E99 definition for your unit.
2. Power-cycle the water heater by turning off the gas valve or circuit breaker, waiting two minutes, then restoring power to clear any transient fault.
3. Check for lockout indicators on the display or status light, then inspect the thermocouple or flame sensor for soot, corrosion, or damage and clean the sensor tip with fine steel wool if contamination is visible.
4. Verify gas supply by confirming other gas appliances are operating normally, then check that the manual gas shutoff valve to the water heater is fully open.
5. Inspect the air intake screen and exhaust vent termination for blockages (bird nests, lint, ice, or debris) on tankless models and clear any obstructions.
6. Test the unit by initiating a call for hot water and observing whether ignition occurs and the error clears; if E99 returns immediately, proceed to component-level testing.
7. Replace the control board if the error persists after all sensors, gas supply, and venting have been verified, as a hardware fault on the mainboard is the documented remedy for persistent E99 conditions.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if the error returns after power-cycling and basic sensor cleaning, if you are unfamiliar with gas appliance work, or if your unit is still under warranty. Control board diagnosis requires a multimeter and familiarity with low-voltage DC circuits, and gas-valve replacement involves shutting off gas supply, disconnecting pipes, and verifying proper operation after installation. Because E99 is not clearly defined in State's published code lists, professional diagnosis ensures you replace the correct component and avoid repeat service calls.
