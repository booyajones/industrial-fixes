## LG Mini Split CH46 Error Code — What It Means

The CH46 error code on LG mini split systems indicates a fault with the outdoor unit suction-line temperature sensor. The control board is reading an abnormal signal from the suction thermistor, usually because of an open circuit, short circuit, loose connector, or a sensor that has drifted out of its expected resistance range. This sensor monitors refrigerant temperature on the suction line and feeds that data to the outdoor PCB for proper system operation.

When the board detects a reading that falls outside normal parameters, it throws CH46 and may shut down the outdoor unit to protect the compressor. The problem is typically electrical rather than mechanical, so your focus will be on the sensor itself, its wiring, and the connector at the outdoor board.

## Common Causes

- **Disconnected or loose sensor plug** The thermistor connector at the outdoor PCB has worked loose due to vibration, corrosion, or poor seating during a previous service call.
- **Damaged sensor lead or harness** The wire between the suction sensor and the board has been pinched, cut, or corroded, breaking continuity or creating an intermittent short.
- **Failed suction thermistor** The sensor element itself has degraded and now reads open circuit, short circuit, or resistance values outside the expected thermistor curve.
- **Poor sensor contact with the suction line** The thermistor has shifted or lost thermal contact with the pipe, causing erratic temperature readings that the board interprets as a sensor fault.
- **Failed outdoor PCB input circuit** If the sensor and wiring both test good, the input circuit on the outdoor control board may have failed and cannot process the sensor signal correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and isolate the unit** at the disconnect or breaker, then wait five minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening any panels.
2. **Locate the outdoor suction sensor** on the larger refrigerant line (suction line) leaving the outdoor unit, usually near the service valve or coil exit.
3. **Inspect the sensor placement and attachment** to the pipe to confirm it is still firmly clipped or strapped and making full thermal contact with the line.
4. **Check the sensor wiring and connector** back to the outdoor PCB for corrosion, loose pins, breaks in the wire, or damaged insulation.
5. **Disconnect the sensor from the PCB** and use a digital multimeter in resistance mode to measure the thermistor across its two leads.
6. **Compare the resistance to the thermistor curve** for your model. LG sensors in this family typically read around 10 kΩ at 10°C and 4 kΩ at 30°C, but consult your service manual for exact values.
7. **Replace the sensor** if it reads open, short, or out of range. If the sensor tests good and wiring is intact, replace the outdoor main PCB.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable working inside the outdoor unit or measuring thermistor resistance, call a licensed HVAC technician. Refrigerant circuit work is not involved in a sensor replacement, but misdiagnosing the PCB and replacing it unnecessarily can cost several hundred dollars. A tech can also verify that the fault is truly CH46 and not a similar code, cross-check the sensor against the thermistor table for your exact model, and confirm that no other system faults are present before ordering parts.
