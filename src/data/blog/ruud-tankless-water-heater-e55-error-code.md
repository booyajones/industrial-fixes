## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E55 Error Code — What It Means

The E55 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a combustion-related fault. This means the unit has detected a problem with flame sensing, ignition, or combustion air flow. The heater will shut down to protect itself and prevent unsafe operation.

Because the exact definition of error codes can vary across different Ruud tankless models, consult your owner's manual or the label inside the unit's service panel for the precise meaning. In general, E55-range codes point to issues with gas delivery, venting, flame detection circuitry, or the combustion chamber itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame sensor or igniter when the real culprit is a blocked vent or low gas pressure. Check vent termination and gas supply pressure before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted vent piping (~35%)** Debris, ice, bird nests, or improper vent installation can starve the combustion chamber of air or prevent exhaust gases from escaping, triggering a flame fault.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low inlet gas pressure, a partially closed manual valve, or high demand from other appliances can prevent proper ignition or stable combustion.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~20%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing electrode prevents the control board from confirming ignition, shutting the burner down as a safety measure.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition transformer (~10%)** A weak or broken igniter cannot produce a reliable spark, leading to repeated ignition failures and combustion errors.
- **Failed control board or wiring fault (~10%)** Corroded connectors, damaged wiring, or a failing circuit board can mimic combustion problems even when gas and air delivery are normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the manual gas shutoff valve.
2. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vents** outside the building for blockages, bird nests, ice, or debris. Clear any obstructions and confirm both pipes are properly pitched per the installation manual.
3. **Check the gas supply valve** at the heater and upstream at the meter. Turn valves fully open and verify other gas appliances in the home operate normally.
4. **Power-cycle the heater** by turning the circuit breaker off, waiting one minute, then restoring power. Attempt to run hot water and observe whether the error returns.
5. **Remove the front cover** and locate the flame sensor rod inside the burner chamber. Inspect for heavy carbon buildup or corrosion. Do not touch internal components if you are not trained in gas appliance service.
6. **Call a licensed technician** if the error persists. A pro will measure inlet gas pressure, test the flame sensor with a multimeter, inspect the igniter, and verify control-board voltage signals.
7. **Document the error history** by noting how often the code appears and under what conditions (cold starts, high-demand periods). This information helps the technician diagnose intermittent faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional whenever the E55 code persists after clearing vents and confirming gas supply. Tankless water heaters operate with natural gas or propane under pressure, and troubleshooting combustion faults requires measuring gas pressure, inspecting flame-sensing circuits, and working safely around high-voltage ignition components. A licensed technician has the tools to test inlet pressure, clean or replace the flame sensor correctly, verify grounding, and inspect the heat exchanger for scale or corrosion that can disrupt combustion. Attempting internal repairs without proper training risks gas leaks, carbon monoxide hazards, and voiding your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
