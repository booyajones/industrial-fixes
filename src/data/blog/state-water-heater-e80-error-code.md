## State Water Heater E80 Error Code — What It Means

The E80 code on a State water heater indicates an over-temperature or overheating lockout condition. The control has detected that water temperature has climbed above the unit's safety limit and has shut the heater down to prevent damage or scalding. This is a protective shutdown, not a nuisance fault.

The heater will remain locked out until the condition is corrected and the control is reset. The code does not clear itself. Repeated lockouts after reset point to an underlying mechanical or sensor problem that must be addressed, not simply reset each time it appears.

## Common Causes

- **Scale and mineral buildup on heat-transfer surfaces** Heavy lime or calcium deposits insulate the tank bottom and trap heat, causing localized overheating that trips the safety limit.
- **Blocked or restricted venting on gas models** Obstructed exhaust, improper vent installation, or debris in the combustion air path can cause incomplete combustion and excessive heat retention.
- **Failed temperature sensor or thermistor** A faulty sensor can falsely report an overheat condition even when actual water temperature is normal, triggering a false lockout.
- **Restricted airflow or inadequate combustion air** Insufficient fresh air supply to the burner can reduce combustion efficiency and lead to elevated tank temperatures beyond safe limits.
- **Faulty control board interpreting sensor input incorrectly** The electronic control may misread temperature data due to internal failure, causing premature or erroneous over-temperature shutdown.
- **Actual sustained overheating from thermostat malfunction** A stuck or failed thermostat may allow the burner or element to fire continuously, driving water temperature above the cutoff threshold.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician immediately if the E80 code returns after a single reset, if you smell gas or see evidence of incomplete combustion (soot, yellow flame), or if you are not comfortable working with gas lines, electrical controls, or high-temperature water systems. Recurring over-temperature lockouts indicate a real mechanical or control failure that will not resolve on its own. A technician can perform combustion analysis, sensor testing, and tank inspection with the proper tools and safely isolate the root cause rather than cycling the unit on and off.
