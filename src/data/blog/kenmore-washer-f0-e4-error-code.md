## Kenmore Washer F0 E4 Error Code — What It Means

F0 E4 is a water-temperature fault on Whirlpool-built Kenmore top-load washers. The control board has detected that incoming wash or rinse water is hotter than expected, so it limits operation and displays the code. Whirlpool defines F0 E4 as wash water temperature too hot, and the Kenmore fault guide specifies high water temperature in the rinse cycle. The code does not mean the washer is broken. It means the machine is seeing water it considers too warm, either because the plumbing delivers hot water when cold is called for or because the temperature sensor is reporting an out-of-range signal.

In the field, the most common real cause is that hot and cold fill hoses are reversed at the washer inlet or at the wall valves. If the plumbing is correct, the fault shifts to the thermistor (temperature sensor), the wiring harness between the sensor and control board, or the control board itself misreading the temperature signal.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or thermistor first. Always verify that the hot hose connects to the home hot-water valve and the cold hose to cold before buying any part.

## Common Causes

- **Hot and cold hoses swapped (~60%)** The hose on the washer's HOT inlet is connected to the home's cold-water valve, or vice versa, so the wrong temperature water enters each cycle.
- **Hot supply water entering unexpectedly (~15%)** The wall valve labels are wrong, plumbing was recently changed, or both hoses draw from the hot line.
- **Thermistor out of range or failed (~15%)** The temperature sensor is open, shorted, or reading resistance far from the expected 50 kΩ at 77 °F, causing the control to see a false high-temperature signal.
- **Harness or connector problem (~7%)** Wiring between the thermistor and control board is damaged, corroded, or has a loose terminal that produces an erratic temperature reading.
- **Control board misreading sensor (~3%)** After all plumbing and sensor checks pass, the main control board interprets the thermistor signal incorrectly and sets the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and pull it forward so you can see the back panel and the fill-hose connections at the inlet valves.
2. **Verify hose routing** by tracing each hose from the washer inlet (marked HOT and COLD) to the wall shut-off valves, confirming that the hose on the HOT inlet connects to the home's hot-water valve and the COLD inlet to the cold valve.
3. **Test the supply** by briefly opening each wall valve into a bucket to confirm that the water temperature matches the valve label, then swap hoses if they were reversed.
4. **Reset the control** by unplugging the washer for 5 minutes, then plug it back in and run a short rinse or wash cycle to see whether the F0 E4 code returns.
5. **Locate and test the thermistor** (consult your model's service manual for the exact location, often near the inlet-valve assembly or tub) by disconnecting the two-wire plug and measuring resistance across the sensor terminals; it should read about 50 kΩ at 77 °F.
6. **Inspect the thermistor wiring harness** and connectors for corrosion, bent pins, or chafed insulation, and repair or replace any damaged sections.
7. **Replace the thermistor** if it reads open (infinite resistance), shorted (near zero), or far from 50 kΩ at room temperature, then retest the washer; if the code persists after all checks, replace the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have corrected the hose connections and reset the washer but the F0 E4 code returns every cycle, or if you are not comfortable using a multimeter to test the thermistor resistance. A pro will verify the supply-water temperature, measure the thermistor in-circuit, check the harness continuity pin-by-pin, and determine whether the control board is at fault. Also call if you find scorched connectors or suspect a wiring short, since those conditions can damage the new part if not fully repaired first.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if thermistor or hose needs replacement, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220 service call.
