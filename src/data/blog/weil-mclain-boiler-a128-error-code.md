## Weil-McLain Boiler A128 Error Code — What It Means

A128 does not appear in published Weil-McLain fault-code tables for common residential series. Weil-McLain control platforms vary by model and year, so the exact meaning depends on your boiler's control board and series. Read the model nameplate and CP number, then consult the service manual for that specific series. Many Weil-McLain controls store a fault history that a technician can pull from the diagnostics menu to identify the actual failure rather than guessing from the display.

Until you confirm the code definition, treat any lockout as a safety issue. Do not keep resetting the boiler. The most common causes of boiler lockouts across all models are low system pressure, ignition or flame-sensing faults, condensate-drainage blockage on condensing units, and failed temperature or low-water sensors.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace a control board or ignition module when the real problem is a shorted thermostat wire, a clogged condensate trap, or a sensor reading out of range. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect low-voltage wiring and condensate drainage before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Low system pressure or water level (~30%)** Pressure below 12 psi can trigger a low-water cutoff or pressure switch, and one source advises filling to about 15 psi for typical residential systems.
- **Ignition or flame-sensor fault (~25%)** Dirty flame sensors, failed igniters, or wiring issues prevent the boiler from proving flame and cause a lockout.
- **Condensate-drainage blockage (~20%)** Frozen drain lines, sludge in the trap, or improper slope are common on condensing boilers and can trigger pressure-switch faults.
- **Failed temperature or low-water sensor (~15%)** A thermistor or low-water-cutoff sensor reading out of range will lock out the boiler even when water level and pressure are correct.
- **Low-voltage short or thermostat wiring issue (~10%)** A shorted thermostat wire or corroded connection can pull down control voltage and mimic other faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power and gas** to the boiler before opening any panels or touching components.
2. **Record the exact model number and CP number** from the boiler nameplate so you can find the correct fault-code table in the service manual.
3. **Check the system-pressure gauge.** If it reads below 12 psi, open the fill valve slowly until the gauge reaches about 15 psi, then close the valve and watch for leaks.
4. **Access the control's diagnostics menu** by following the button sequence in your manual, and write down any stored fault codes and the date-time stamps to share with your technician.
5. **Inspect the condensate trap and drain line** on condensing models for blockage, sludge, or ice, and clear any obstruction.
6. **Call a qualified boiler technician** to test ignition components, flame sensors, and thermistors with a multimeter, pull resistance readings, and confirm which part has failed before replacing anything.
7. **Do not repeatedly reset the boiler** if the fault returns immediately. Repeated lockouts indicate a real safety issue that needs diagnosis, not a reset.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician immediately if you cannot find A128 in your model's manual, if the fault returns after you refill system pressure, or if you are not confident working with gas, electrical diagnostics, or control-board menus. Boiler faults often involve multiple interacting safeties, and misdiagnosis wastes money on parts that were never broken. A technician will pull the fault history from the control, test sensor resistance and voltage, inspect the flame path, and confirm the actual failed component before replacing anything. Do not keep resetting a boiler that locks out repeatedly. Repeated lockouts mean a safety device is doing its job, and bypassing or ignoring it can lead to dangerous conditions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
