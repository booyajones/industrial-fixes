## Navien Tankless Water Heater E223 Error — What It Means

The E223 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a communication or operational issue related to the flow sensor circuit. This code appears when the control board cannot receive proper signals from the flow sensor, which monitors water movement through the unit. The error may stem from faulty wiring, a failed sensor, or debris interfering with the sensor's operation.

Because the exact definition of error codes can vary between Navien models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the diagnostic chart inside the unit's cover for the precise meaning on your specific heater. The flow sensor is a common point of failure on tankless water heaters and is usually accessible without major disassembly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board thinking it is defective when the real issue is a failed or disconnected flow sensor. Test continuity and voltage at the sensor connector before spending money on a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Flow sensor failure (~45%)** The sensor's internal Hall effect component or impeller can fail from wear or scale buildup, preventing accurate flow detection.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~30%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen the connector pins or corrode contacts at the sensor or control board, breaking the signal circuit.
- **Debris or scale blocking the sensor (~15%)** Sediment or mineral deposits can jam the sensor impeller or coat the magnetic pickup, stopping flow detection even when water is moving.
- **Control board communication fault (~10%)** A software glitch or failed input circuit on the control board can misread or ignore valid sensor signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the water supply valves to depressurize the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the heater by unscrewing the lower panel fasteners and lifting the cover away from the mounting clips.
3. **Locate the flow sensor**, typically a cylindrical component mounted in-line on the cold water inlet pipe near the heat exchanger.
4. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the flow sensor by pressing the locking tab and pulling the connector straight off.
5. **Inspect the connector** and sensor pins for corrosion, moisture, or bent terminals. Clean with electrical contact cleaner if needed and allow to dry.
6. **Remove the flow sensor** by unscrewing the mounting bolts or union nuts (consult your model's service manual for the exact method) and pull the sensor from the pipe.
7. **Examine the impeller and sensor body** for scale, debris, or damage. Flush with white vinegar or descaling solution if mineral buildup is present, then rinse with clean water.
8. **Test the sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode. Spin the impeller by hand and watch for a pulsing resistance reading. No change indicates a failed sensor.
9. **Install the new or cleaned sensor** with new O-rings or gaskets (if supplied), tighten the union nuts to finger-tight plus one quarter-turn with a wrench, and reconnect the wiring harness.
10. **Restore water and power**, open a hot-water tap, and observe the display. The E223 error should clear within 10 seconds if the sensor is functioning properly.
11. **Run the heater** for several minutes and check for leaks around the sensor housing before replacing the front cover.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas or electrical connections, if the error persists after replacing the flow sensor and inspecting all wiring, or if you discover leaks or corrosion elsewhere in the system. Professionals have factory scan tools that can read live sensor data and pinpoint intermittent faults the basic display cannot show. If your heater is still under warranty, unauthorized repairs may void coverage, so check your warranty terms before opening the unit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $60-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
