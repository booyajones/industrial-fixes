## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E95 Error — What It Means

The E95 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition. The unit has attempted to ignite but either failed to establish a flame or the control board cannot sense that a flame is present. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent gas buildup or unsafe operation.

Because error code meanings can vary between Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, consult your specific owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover to confirm the exact definition for your model. Common triggers include gas supply interruptions, dirty flame sensors, faulty ignition components, or blocked venting that prevents proper combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the gas valve or igniter when the real culprit is a flame rod covered in mineral scale or soot. Visually inspect and clean the flame rod with fine steel wool or sandpaper before ordering any gas components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Mineral deposits, soot, or corrosion on the flame sensor prevent it from detecting the flame even when ignition occurs.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low inlet gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an empty propane tank stops the burner from lighting reliably.
- **Failed igniter (~15%)** A cracked ceramic insulator or broken wire in the spark igniter prevents the unit from creating a flame.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructions in the exhaust or intake vent pipes cause incomplete combustion and flame dropout.
- **Faulty gas valve (~7%)** A stuck solenoid or failed gas valve coil prevents gas from reaching the burner even when the control board calls for heat.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** Internal relay or circuit failures in the main control board can misread flame signals or fail to energize the igniter.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the manual gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by loosening the retaining screws; consult your model's service manual for the exact panel location.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a small metal probe near the burner assembly, and inspect it for white mineral buildup, black soot, or corrosion.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or steel wool until the metal surface is shiny, taking care not to bend or damage the rod.
5. **Check the igniter** for cracks in the ceramic and verify that the electrode gap matches the specification in your manual.
6. **Inspect vent pipes** for blockages, bird nests, or debris; confirm that the termination caps are clear and that intake and exhaust are not reversed.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and initiate a hot-water demand to test for ignition and stable flame.

## When to Call a Pro

Gas-fired appliances require careful attention to combustion safety, venting, and pressure regulation. If you are uncomfortable working around gas lines, lack a multimeter to test igniter voltage and flame-rod microamps, or cannot identify the correct clearances and settings in your service manual, call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician. A professional can measure inlet gas pressure, verify proper venting, and use combustion analyzers to confirm safe operation. Any time you smell gas or suspect a leak, leave the area immediately and contact your gas utility or emergency services.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
