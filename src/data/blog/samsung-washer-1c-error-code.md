## What this code means
The 1C error code (sometimes displayed as 1E or E7 on certain models) indicates a water-level sensor fault. The washer's control board is not receiving the expected signal from the pressure-sensing system. Samsung lists this code under water-level sensor problems, which include incorrect connections at the sensor terminal, a folded or kinked hose running to the sensor, or a hose clogged with foreign material.

The water-level sensor uses air pressure inside a tube connected to the tub to determine how much water is inside. If that tube is blocked, pinched, or disconnected, or if the sensor's wiring is loose, the control board cannot measure the water level accurately and triggers the 1C fault. Leaks, excess suds, or a failed sensor itself can also produce the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water-level sensor first. Instead, unplug the machine, pull it forward, and inspect the small air tube running from the tub to the sensor for kinks, blockages, or loose connections before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Folded, kinked, or blocked pressure hose (~40%)** The small air tube from the tub to the sensor can be pinched behind the drum, kinked at a bend, or clogged with lint or detergent residue.
- **Loose or corroded sensor terminal connection (~25%)** The plug at the water-level sensor can work loose from vibration or develop corrosion that interrupts the signal to the control board.
- **Excessive suds from too much detergent (~15%)** Oversudsing can confuse the pressure-sensing system and make the washer read an abnormal water level, triggering the fault.
- **Water leak under the machine or at hose connections (~10%)** A leak at the inlet valve, drain pump seal, or tub boot can change the actual water level and throw off the sensor reading.
- **Failed water-level sensor (~10%)** The sensor diaphragm or internal switch can fail, preventing it from sending a correct pressure signal even when the hose and wiring are intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves at the wall.
2. **Pull the machine forward** away from the wall so you can access the rear panel and underneath the cabinet.
3. **Inspect for leaks** around the inlet hoses, drain hose, pump, and tub boot. Tighten clamps and replace any torn or cracked hoses.
4. **Locate the small air tube** (pressure hose) that runs from a port on the outer tub or sump to the water-level sensor, usually a round plastic component mounted on the control-panel area or side of the cabinet.
5. **Check the tube** for kinks, pinches, or blockages. Remove it at both ends, blow through it to confirm it is clear, and look inside for lint or detergent buildup. Rinse with warm water if needed.
6. **Inspect the sensor terminal** where the wire harness plugs into the water-level sensor. Unplug the connector, check pins for corrosion or bent contacts, clean with electrical contact cleaner if dirty, and reseat firmly.
7. **Run an empty rinse cycle** with the washer plugged back in and the panels still open to watch for suds. If heavy foam appears, continue running rinse cycles until the water is clear, then correct your detergent type and dosage.
8. **Reassemble and test** a normal wash cycle. If the 1C code returns after hose and connection repairs, replace the water-level sensor with the correct part for your model and retest.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have straightened the pressure hose, cleaned the sensor connections, eliminated suds, checked for leaks, and the 1C code still appears after a new sensor is installed. The control board may have a fault in the sensor-input circuit, or the tub or sump may have a crack that is allowing air into the pressure system. A pro can also verify correct sensor resistance and signal voltage if you lack a multimeter and the wiring diagram for your exact model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts (sensor or hose clamps), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call.
