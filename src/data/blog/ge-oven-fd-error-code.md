## GE Oven FD Error Code — What It Means

FD on a GE oven or range signals a probe circuit fault. GE support documents state that this code typically indicates the meat probe or the probe receptacle needs to be replaced. Independent repair sources describe FD more specifically as a shorted meat probe or a fault somewhere in the probe and receptacle path.

The code appears when the control board detects an abnormal reading from the meat-probe circuit. This can be caused by a defective probe with an internal short, a damaged or contaminated receptacle at the oven wall, or pinched and corroded wiring between the receptacle and the control. In less common cases the control board itself may be at fault, but GE's published meaning for FD centers on the probe and receptacle side of the circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a shorted probe or corroded receptacle. Always unplug the probe and inspect both the probe cord and the receptacle terminals before ordering any control.

## Common Causes

- **Defective meat probe (~50%)** An internal short or broken conductor inside the probe itself triggers the fault and is the most frequently documented cause of FD.
- **Damaged or contaminated probe receptacle (~30%)** Corrosion, burnt terminals, condensation, or physical damage at the jack where the probe plugs in can create a false fault signal.
- **Pinched or broken probe wiring (~15%)** The harness between the receptacle and the control can be pinched during installation or corroded over time, making the control read a circuit failure.
- **Control board issue (~5%)** If the code persists after disconnecting the entire probe circuit and inspecting all wiring, the control board may be interpreting a phantom fault.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the FD code disappear when you unplug the meat probe from the oven receptacle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The probe itself is likely defective and should be replaced.<br><strong>No:</strong> The receptacle, its wiring, or the control board is at fault. Inspect the receptacle for damage and corrosion next.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Do you see any burns, corrosion, or bent pins inside the probe receptacle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Replace the receptacle. Clean any corrosion on the surrounding harness connector before reconnecting.<br><strong>No:</strong> Check the wiring harness between the receptacle and control for pinches or breaks. If the harness is intact and the code still appears with the probe unplugged, suspect the control board.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the probe cord cut, kinked, or showing heat damage anywhere along its length?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Replace the meat probe. Do not attempt to tape or splice a damaged probe cord.<br><strong>No:</strong> The internal sensor in the probe may be shorted even if the cord looks fine. Replace the probe and retest.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker before touching any probe or receptacle components.
2. **Unplug the meat probe** from the receptacle on the oven wall and wait thirty seconds to see if the FD code clears from the display.
3. **Inspect the probe and its cord** for cuts, kinks, bent contacts, heat damage, or any visible contamination on the metal tip.
4. **Inspect the receptacle** where the probe plugs in for corrosion, burnt terminals, condensation inside the jack, or loose mounting.
5. **Check the receptacle wiring harness** by gently wiggling the connector at the back of the receptacle and tracing the harness toward the control for pinches or breaks.
6. **Replace the defective component** (probe, receptacle, or harness) based on your inspection findings.
7. **Restore power** at the breaker, run a test bake cycle without the probe plugged in, then plug in the new probe and verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the FD code remains after you have replaced both the probe and the receptacle and verified that all wiring is intact. That scenario points to a control-board fault or a hidden harness break inside the oven cabinet, and both require disassembly and multimeter tracing. Also call a pro if your oven is still under warranty or if you are uncomfortable working around line-voltage connections at the control. A qualified appliance technician can isolate the fault with a meter, order the correct part for your model, and test the full probe circuit in one visit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 for probe or receptacle, 20–40 minutes. A pro service call runs about $100–200 service call plus parts.
