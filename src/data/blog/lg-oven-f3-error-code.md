## LG Oven F3 Error Code — What It Means

On LG range and oven products, F3 is logged as a Touch Pad Sensor Key Error. The control board detects an invalid or stuck key input from the touch panel or membrane keypad circuit. LG also documents that F-3 on microwave and lightwave ovens can occur when foreign substances are around the touch button panel. In practical terms, the control sees a key that is being held down continuously, multiple keys pressed at once, or signal noise from contamination on the keypad sensor array.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when the real fault is a failed or contaminated touch pad assembly. Always clean the panel, perform a power reset, and test the keypad harness connector before swapping the board.

## Common Causes

- **Liquid, grease, or food debris on the touch panel (~50%)** Moisture or sticky residue bridges sensor traces and causes the control to see phantom or stuck key presses.
- **Failed touch pad or membrane keypad assembly (~25%)** The flexible sensor sheet or internal traces crack, delaminate, or short out, sending constant or false signals to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness connector (~15%)** The plug between the touch panel and the main control board works loose or develops corrosion, creating intermittent signals or noise.
- **Defective main control board (~8%)** The microprocessor or keypad scan circuit on the control board misreads or fails to debounce keypad signals properly.
- **Transient power disturbance or electronic noise (~2%)** A voltage spike or surge can latch a false error that clears after a complete power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press Power or Clear/Off** to dismiss the error, then observe whether it returns immediately.
2. **Clean the touch panel** with a soft damp cloth and mild dish soap, paying attention to edges and seams where liquid can wick under the overlay, then dry thoroughly.
3. **Turn off the circuit breaker** that supplies the range for 30 seconds to 2 minutes to reset the control board and clear any latched fault.
4. **Restore power** and test the oven through a short bake cycle to see if F3 reappears.
5. **Unplug the range** (or lock out the breaker and tag it), remove the control panel cover, and inspect the ribbon cable or harness connector between the touch pad and the control board for looseness or corrosion.
6. **Press each key on the touch pad** one at a time while watching for stuck or unresponsive areas, or disconnect the touch pad harness and see if the error clears (indicating a shorted keypad).
7. **Replace the touch pad assembly** if cleaning and connector checks do not resolve the fault, or replace the main control board only after verifying the keypad and wiring are intact.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable removing the control panel cover, working near line voltage inside the range console, or diagnosing which of the two boards (touch pad vs. main control) has failed. A pro will use a multimeter to check continuity across each key trace and can quickly isolate whether the keypad flex circuit is shorted or the control board scan circuit is at fault. Also seek service if the error persists after you have cleaned the panel, performed a full power reset, and verified all harness connections, because intermittent electronic faults can require waveform analysis or board-level repair that is beyond typical DIY scope.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–120 in parts (touch pad or membrane keypad), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300 including service call and part.
