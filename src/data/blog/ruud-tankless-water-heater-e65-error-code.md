## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E65 Error — What It Means

The E65 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater generally indicates a flame detection or combustion problem. The unit is either failing to ignite, losing flame during operation, or sensing abnormal combustion conditions. Because tankless heaters rely on precise gas-air mixture and flame sensing circuits, this code can stem from fuel delivery issues, airflow restrictions, or sensor malfunctions.

The exact meaning of error codes can vary slightly between model families, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for your specific unit. In most cases, the heater will lock out and refuse to supply hot water until the fault is cleared and the unit is reset.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flame rod or igniter when the real problem is a gas supply restriction or dirty burner orifices. Check gas pressure and inspect the burner chamber for debris before ordering sensor parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted venting (~35%)** Lint, bird nests, ice, or debris in the intake or exhaust vent pipes can starve combustion air or prevent exhaust gases from leaving, triggering flame-fault detection.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~25%)** Insufficient gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or air in the gas line after a recent outage will prevent stable ignition or cause flame dropout.
- **Dirty or fouled flame rod (~20%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing electrode prevents the control board from confirming ignition, even when a flame is present.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition transformer (~10%)** A weak spark or failed igniter assembly will prevent the burner from lighting reliably, leading to repeated fault codes.
- **Clogged burner orifices or heat exchanger (~10%)** Scale, dust, or insect debris inside the burner ports or heat-exchanger fins disrupts the flame pattern and triggers combustion faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve.
2. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vents** outside for blockages, nests, ice, or debris; remove any obstructions and make sure termination caps are secure.
3. **Open the heater's service cover** and visually check the burner chamber for dust, scale, or insect nests; use a soft brush or compressed air to clean accessible surfaces.
4. **Locate the flame rod** (a thin metal probe near the burner) and gently wipe it with fine sandpaper or a clean cloth to remove carbon buildup.
5. **Restore gas and power**, then initiate a demand by opening a hot-water tap; observe the ignition sequence through the viewing window if your model has one.
6. **If the error returns**, measure incoming gas pressure with a manometer (consult your model's installation manual for the required range) or call a qualified technician to test the gas valve, igniter, and flame-sensing circuit.
7. **Document the error history** using the unit's diagnostic display if available; recurring codes may point to a specific sensor or control-board fault that requires professional tools to isolate.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if free checks do not resolve the E65 code, if you smell gas, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas-line connections. Diagnosing flame faults often requires a manometer to measure gas pressure, a multimeter to test ignition voltage and flame-rod microamperage, and familiarity with combustion airflow requirements. Because tankless heaters are sealed-combustion appliances and involve both gas and high-voltage ignition circuits, professional service is the safest route when the cause is not obvious. A technician can also inspect the heat exchanger for scale buildup that may require descaling or replacement under warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
