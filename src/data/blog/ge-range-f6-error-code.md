## GE Range F6 Error Code — What It Means

On GE ranges and wall ovens, the F6 error code signals a problem with the keypanel (also called the keypad or touchpad). GE's published fault-code guidance states that this code usually means there is a problem with the keypanel and that it often needs replacement. The keypanel is the membrane-switch interface you use to select oven functions and temperatures.

The fault typically occurs when a button is stuck, the touchpad membrane has failed, the ribbon connector between the keypad and control board is loose or damaged, or less commonly when the electronic control board input stage misinterprets signals from an otherwise good keypad. A power reset clears temporary faults, but if F6 returns the keypanel is the most common replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the oven control board first. Before doing so, inspect the ribbon connector for looseness or damage and test by disconnecting the keypad to see if the fault clears.

## Common Causes

- **Failed keypad or touchpad membrane (~50%)** The membrane-switch layer degrades over time, causing buttons to stick, short together, or send continuous signals that trigger F6.
- **Stuck or shorted button (~20%)** Liquid spills, food debris, or physical damage can cause a single button to remain closed and send a constant signal to the control board.
- **Damaged ribbon connector or harness (~15%)** The flexible ribbon cable or connector pins between the keypad and control board can become loose, corroded, or heat-damaged, interrupting communication.
- **Electronic control board input failure (~10%)** The input stage of the oven control board can fail and misinterpret valid keypad signals as a fault, even when the keypad itself is good.
- **Intermittent keypad self-triggering (~5%)** A partially failed touchpad may trigger randomly without button press, especially in humid conditions or after temperature swings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** for at least one minute, then restore power and observe whether F6 returns immediately or after a short time.
2. **Inspect the keypanel surface** for stuck buttons, liquid spills, cracks, or areas that feel continuously pressed.
3. **Unplug the range or shut off the breaker again**, remove the front control panel or back panel (depending on model), and locate the ribbon cable or wire harness connecting the keypad to the oven control board.
4. **Inspect the ribbon connector** for looseness, bent pins, corrosion, heat discoloration, or damage, and reseat or replace the connector if any issues are found.
5. **Replace the keypanel assembly** if the connector is good and the fault persists (most GE models use a plug-in keypad that unclips from the control panel frame).
6. **Reconnect all wiring**, reassemble the control panel, restore power, and test all oven functions to confirm F6 is cleared.
7. **If F6 returns after keypanel replacement**, disconnect the new keypad ribbon and observe whether the fault clears, which indicates a faulty oven control board input stage requiring board replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range's electrical compartment, if the fault persists after replacing the keypanel and you need to test or replace the oven control board, or if your range is still under warranty (unauthorized repairs can void coverage). GE's fault-code guidance recommends trained service for fault-code diagnosis. A technician has model-specific wiring diagrams, can perform isolation tests between the keypad and control board, and carries the correct replacement parts for your exact model. Professional diagnosis is also wise if you find heat damage or evidence of an electrical short, since those conditions may point to a larger wiring or control-board issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–150 in parts (keypanel assembly), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300 including service call and keypanel.
