## GE Oven Display Not Working — What's Happening

When your GE oven display is completely blank, dim, or dead, you are looking at a failure somewhere in the display, keypad, or control board circuit. The display board itself may have failed, or the main control board is not sending power to it. In some cases the keypanel or control assembly is defective. If your oven is showing a fault code F0 instead of a blank screen, GE states that code usually means a problem with the keypanel and often requires keypanel replacement.

A blank display is not a fault code. It means one component in the chain (display board, main control board, ribbon harness, or keypad) has lost power or communication. If the display was working and went dark suddenly, the control board or display board has likely failed. If the display flickers or comes back briefly after a power cycle and then goes dark again, that points strongly to a failing control board rather than a loose connection.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Failed display board** The LED or display circuitry itself has burned out or lost internal power regulation.
- **Main control board not supplying power to the display** The electronic range control has failed and is no longer energizing the display board.
- **Loose or dirty ribbon cable between keypad and control board** The flat ribbon connector has worked loose, collected grease or moisture, or has a damaged contact pin.
- **Bad keypanel or membrane switch** A stuck or shorted button condition or internal keypad failure can prevent the display from initializing.
- **Control board relay or logic failure after an overheat or electrical fault** A surge, self-clean cycle overheat, or component aging has damaged the control board processor or relay circuit.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power to the oven at the circuit breaker before opening the unit or disconnecting any harness.
2. Remove the control panel cover and locate the ribbon cable running from the keypad/display to the main control board.
3. Disconnect the ribbon cable at both ends, inspect the contacts for contamination or damage, clean with contact cleaner if needed, and reseat firmly.
4. With the keypad ribbon disconnected from the control board, use a multimeter to measure resistance across terminals 13 and 14 on the keypad side: you should read open (OL) at rest and approximately 150 Ω when the OFF button is pressed.
5. If the keypad test fails, replace the keypanel assembly.
6. If the keypad tests correctly, restore power and check whether the display board is receiving supply voltage from the main control board (consult your model service manual for the exact voltage spec).
7. If the display board is not receiving power, replace the main control board.
8. If the display board is receiving power but remains blank, replace the display board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if you cannot safely access the control board and ribbon connectors, or if the keypad resistance test and ribbon reseating do not restore the display. Control board and display board diagnostics require live voltage checks in some models, and mishandling the ribbon connectors or static discharge can damage expensive electronics. A technician has the model-specific service manual, the correct voltage specs, and the tools to verify power supply rails before replacing parts. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
