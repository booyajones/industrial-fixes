## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E45 Error — What It Means

The E45 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with combustion or flame sensing. The unit has detected a condition that prevents safe operation, such as inconsistent flame signal, inadequate gas flow, or an exhaust venting issue. The heater will shut down to protect itself and the home.

Because the exact meaning of E45 can vary across different Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit for the precise definition. In general, this code points to a fault in the combustion process that the control board has flagged as unsafe.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame sensor or gas valve first, but many E45 codes trace back to blocked intake or exhaust vents. Inspect and clear all venting before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted venting (~35%)** Exhaust or intake pipes clogged by debris, ice, or incorrect installation prevent proper airflow and combustion.
- **Dirty or failing flame sensor (~25%)** Carbon buildup on the flame rod or a worn sensor prevents the board from confirming ignition.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~20%)** Partially closed valve, regulator issue, or insufficient gas pressure starves the burner and causes flame dropout.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition sequence (~10%)** A weak spark or failed ignition component prevents reliable lighting and triggers the error.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main PCB may misread flame signals or fail to manage the ignition cycle correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the heater at the circuit breaker and the manual gas shutoff valve before any inspection.
2. **Inspect both intake and exhaust vents** outside the home for blockages, bird nests, ice, or improper termination; clear any obstructions and confirm vent pipes are sloped correctly for condensate drainage.
3. **Check the gas supply** by verifying the manual valve is fully open and testing another gas appliance in the home to confirm adequate pressure.
4. **Power-cycle the unit** by leaving power and gas off for at least 60 seconds, then restore both and attempt to run hot water to see if the error clears.
5. **Remove the front cover** (with power and gas still off) and visually inspect the flame sensor rod for heavy carbon deposits or corrosion; clean gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth if accessible.
6. **If the error persists**, call a licensed technician to measure gas pressure at the valve, test the flame sensor with a multimeter, inspect the igniter, and check the control board for fault logs.
7. **Document the error pattern** including when it occurs (startup, mid-cycle, after extended use) to help the technician diagnose intermittent faults quickly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional immediately if you smell gas, if the error returns after clearing vents and power-cycling, or if you are uncomfortable working around gas lines and high-voltage ignition systems. Tankless water heater combustion diagnostics require a manometer to measure gas pressure, a multimeter to test flame sensors and igniters, and an understanding of the unit's control logic. Technicians can also retrieve detailed fault logs from the control board that pinpoint intermittent issues not visible during a single test. Any work involving gas connections, burner removal, or control board replacement should be handled by a licensed plumber or HVAC contractor to maintain warranty coverage and safety.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
