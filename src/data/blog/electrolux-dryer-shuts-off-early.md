## Electrolux Dryer Shuts Off Early — What's Happening

When an Electrolux dryer starts, runs for 5 to 10 seconds, then shuts off, the control board is usually detecting a motor-sensing fault rather than a simple timer issue. The fault code tied to this behavior is E53, which means the control is not seeing the motor centrifugal switch change state as expected during operation.

The centrifugal switch inside the motor assembly is designed to signal the control board that the drum is spinning. If the switch fails, stays open, or the wiring connection is interrupted, the control thinks the motor is not running properly and shuts the dryer down for safety. Less often, the problem is a faulty main control board or a power-supply issue that mimics a motor fault.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Failed or intermittent centrifugal switch inside the motor** The switch does not close or change state when the motor spins, so the control board does not receive the expected feedback signal and stops the cycle.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded motor harness connections** The motor plug, harness terminals, or wires back to the control can become loose, heat-damaged, or contaminated, breaking the feedback circuit.
- **Drive motor winding or internal mechanical fault** A motor winding problem or internal mechanical issue can prevent the centrifugal switch from closing properly or cause the motor to overheat and shut down.
- **Main control board fault** The control board may fail to interpret the motor feedback signal correctly even when the motor and switch are working normally.
- **Improper supply voltage at the terminal block** Missing or low voltage on one leg of the 240 VAC supply can cause the motor to run weakly or the control to misread motor operation.
- **Motor overload or overheating from restricted airflow** A clogged vent, drum obstruction, or heavy load can cause the motor to overheat and trigger an internal thermal cutoff or fault condition.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the dryer and verify supply voltage at the terminal block or wall outlet with a multimeter: you should see 240 VAC line-to-line and approximately 120 VAC from each hot leg to neutral.
2. Enter diagnostic mode or pull fault-code history for your model to confirm whether code E53 or another motor-related fault is stored in the control.
3. Remove the front or rear panel as needed for your model to access the drive motor, motor harness, and control connections.
4. Inspect the motor plug, harness, and all terminals for looseness, heat marks, corrosion, or broken wires, and repair or replace any damaged connectors or conductors.
5. Disconnect the motor harness and test the centrifugal switch circuit at the motor terminals with an ohmmeter: the circuit should read open at rest and change state when the switch mechanism is manually engaged.
6. If the switch does not change state or the motor windings show an open or shorted reading, replace the drive motor assembly.
7. If the motor and wiring test good, check continuity from the motor harness back to the control-board connector to rule out a broken wire in the cabinet harness.
8. If all motor-side tests pass but the dryer still shuts off early, replace the main control board and retest the cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable working with 240 VAC at the terminal block, testing motor circuits with a multimeter, or removing cabinet panels to access internal components, call a qualified appliance technician. A pro can pull diagnostic codes, isolate the motor-feedback circuit, and safely replace the motor assembly or control board. If the dryer continues to fault after you have verified voltage and inspected connections, professional diagnosis will save you from replacing the wrong part.
