## Frigidaire Washer Won't Agitate — What's Happening

A Frigidaire washer that won't agitate fills with water normally but the basket or agitator does not perform the wash-motion cycle, or it starts and then stops during agitation. This symptom is not a single error code. It points to a mechanical, drive, lid-switch, or control problem rather than a water-system fault.

On top-load Frigidaire units, the complaint usually narrows to the drive train, agitator hardware, motor or actuator system, or control board rather than the fill or drain circuits. Front-load models may show similar no-wash-motion symptoms if the motor, control board, or door-lock interlock fails.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Worn agitator dogs or internal agitator parts** The agitator repair kit includes internal ratcheting parts (dogs) that commonly wear out and prevent the agitator from engaging, causing a no-agitate complaint.
- **Failed motor coupler** The coupler connects the motor to the transmission on direct-drive models and can break or strip, preventing both agitation and spin.
- **Broken or loose drive belt** Belt-driven Frigidaire washers lose agitation and spin if the belt slips off, stretches, or breaks.
- **Motor failure** The drive motor may run weakly, hum without turning, or fail completely, stopping all mechanical motion including agitation.
- **Transmission or gearcase problem** Internal transmission gears can strip or seize, blocking power transfer from the motor to the agitator and basket.
- **Lid switch or door interlock not satisfied** If the lid-switch circuit or door-lock system does not close properly or signal the control, the washer will not start the agitate cycle for safety.
- **Control board or actuator issue** The main control board or shift actuator may fail to command the agitate cycle even when the mechanical path is intact.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the washer and verify the symptom by noting whether the machine filled but never began wash motion, or whether an error code appeared on the display.
2. Enter diagnostic mode by holding Spin Speed and Soil Level buttons together for about 10 seconds (on models with this feature) and check for stored fault codes that may clarify the root cause.
3. Inspect the agitator assembly by lifting or unscrewing the cap, removing the agitator, and examining the internal dogs or ratchet components for wear, cracks, or stripped teeth.
4. Check the drive system by removing the cabinet or rear panel and inspecting the belt for breaks or slack (belt-drive models) or the coupler for cracks or missing rubber pieces (direct-drive models).
5. Test the motor by disconnecting power, checking electrical connections, and verifying that the rotor turns freely by hand and that the motor windings are not open or shorted (use a multimeter if available).
6. Confirm the lid switch or door lock by manually operating the switch with the door open and listening for a click, or by checking continuity across the switch terminals when the lid is closed.
7. Inspect the transmission or gearcase for leaking grease, unusual noise, or seized movement by rotating the input shaft by hand with the belt or coupler removed.
8. Run a manual agitate command in diagnostic mode after each repair to verify that the mechanism engages under test conditions before returning the washer to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable disassembling the cabinet, testing electrical components with a multimeter, or handling the drive train. Transmission or gearcase replacement requires special tools and alignment, and control-board diagnosis depends on schematic interpretation and live voltage checks. A qualified appliance technician can run factory diagnostics, identify stored fault codes, and safely verify motor, actuator, and board operation before recommending parts.
