## What this code means
F4 on a KitchenAid range indicates a problem with the Electronic Oven Control or the wiring connected to it. On KitchenAid wall ovens the same code points to the Oven Cavity Control or its wiring. This is a control-side fault, not a user-settable condition or a simple sensor reading. The exact interpretation can vary by model family, so always check your specific model's tech sheet or wiring diagram.

Some third-party sources mention a shorted oven temperature sensor as a cause of F4, but KitchenAid's own help pages do not list that explanation. The manufacturer documentation focuses on the control board and associated wiring. A power transient or loose connector can also trigger the fault and leave it latched in memory until you reset the unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the oven temperature sensor first because third-party sites list it, but KitchenAid's own pages point to the control board or wiring. Power-cycle the unit and inspect connectors before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed Electronic Oven Control board (~50%)** The main control board has internal component failure and needs replacement.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness connector (~25%)** A plug at the control board is not fully seated, or the pins are corroded and causing intermittent contact.
- **Heat-damaged or pinched wiring (~15%)** Insulation has melted or a wire is pinched behind the control assembly, creating a short or open circuit.
- **Power transient or surge (~10%)** A brief voltage spike left the fault latched in memory and a reset clears it.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker panel for the range and wait a full minute to allow the control to discharge and reset.
2. **Restore power** and observe the display for about one minute to see if the F4 code returns immediately.
3. **Record your exact model number** from the data plate (usually inside the oven door frame or on the rear panel) and download the tech sheet or wiring diagram from KitchenAid or an appliance parts supplier.
4. **Remove the back panel or access cover** for the control area (unplug or lock out the breaker first) and inspect every wiring connector at the Electronic Oven Control for loose pins, corrosion, or heat discoloration.
5. **Check the wire harness** along its full run for pinched insulation, melted plastic, or burn marks that suggest a short circuit.
6. **Test components** according to the wiring diagram if your model's tech sheet identifies specific test points or sensor resistance values for F4.
7. **Replace the Electronic Oven Control** if wiring is intact and the fault persists after a power reset, or call a qualified appliance technician to perform the replacement and verify proper voltage supply to the new board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if the fault returns after a power reset and you cannot locate visible wiring damage, or if you have already replaced connectors and the code persists. The control board sits behind high-voltage connections and the replacement requires verifying line voltage and proper grounding. A qualified appliance service tech will have the model-specific wiring diagram, the correct test meter procedures, and the OEM or equivalent control board in stock or on order. Professional replacement usually includes a labor warranty and a functional test of all oven modes to confirm the repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200–400.
