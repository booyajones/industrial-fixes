## Daikin A5 Error Code — What It Means

The A5 error code on Daikin split-system and heat-pump indoor units signals that the freeze-up protector has activated. This is a protective shutdown that occurs when the indoor coil temperature drops low enough to risk ice formation. The unit is designed to stop operation before the coil actually freezes and suffers damage.

This is not a component-failure code but a condition alarm. The system has detected that heat transfer across the indoor coil is insufficient, usually because air is not moving properly over the coil or because a sensor is reporting dangerously low temperatures. Daikin pairs A5 with related indoor faults including A6 (fan motor fault), C4 (heat exchanger temperature sensor fault), and C9 (suction air temperature sensor fault), so multiple codes may appear together.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted indoor airflow** Dirty or clogged air filters, blocked return grilles, or a dust-covered indoor coil prevent warm air from reaching the heat exchanger and drive coil temperature down.
- **Indoor fan motor failure or wiring fault** If the indoor fan does not start, runs too slowly, or has a broken wire or connector, airflow drops and the coil quickly reaches freeze-protection temperature.
- **Faulty heat-exchanger temperature sensor** A failed or out-of-calibration coil thermistor can report incorrect low temperatures to the control board and trigger freeze protection even when the coil is not actually cold.
- **Faulty suction air temperature sensor** The sensor that monitors return-air temperature may drift or fail, causing the board to miscalculate coil risk and activate the A5 alarm.
- **Refrigerant charge or circuit fault** Low refrigerant, a stuck expansion valve, or another refrigeration problem can reduce coil pressure and temperature enough to trip the freeze-up protector.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the breaker or disconnect switch, wait 60 seconds, then restore power and check whether the A5 code clears after the system restarts.
2. **Remove and inspect the indoor air filter.** If the filter is visibly dirty or has not been changed in more than 30 days, replace it with a clean filter of the correct size and rating.
3. **Check all return and supply grilles** for blockages such as furniture, curtains, or closed dampers, and verify that no obstructions are within two feet of the indoor unit.
4. **Open the indoor unit cover** (after powering off again) and visually inspect the indoor coil for dust, pet hair, or debris buildup, then clean the coil with approved coil cleaner and a soft brush if dirty.
5. **Verify indoor fan operation** by powering the unit back on in fan-only mode and listening for normal fan sound, then visually confirm the fan wheel is spinning freely and at full speed.
6. **Test the heat-exchanger and suction-air thermistors** with a multimeter set to resistance mode, comparing measured values against your model's service data (many Daikin NTC sensors read near 10 kΩ at 25 °C, but consult your model's table for exact specs).
7. **Inspect all wire connectors** at the indoor fan motor, thermistors, and indoor PCB for corrosion, loose pins, or damage, and re-seat any suspect connectors before testing again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the A5 code returns after you have cleaned the filter and coil and confirmed the fan is running, or if you see multiple fault codes (A6, C4, C9) displayed at the same time. Thermistor testing and refrigerant-circuit diagnosis require a multimeter, service manual resistance tables, and manifold gauges. Any refrigerant work must be performed by an EPA-certified technician. If the indoor fan does not run at all or the PCB shows no output voltage to the fan, the repair involves live AC voltage and should be handled by a qualified service provider.
