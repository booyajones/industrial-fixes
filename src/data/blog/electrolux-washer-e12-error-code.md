## Electrolux Washer E12 Error Code — What It Means

E12 is a water-inlet fault on Electrolux washers. The machine expected to see the water level rise during the rinse fill step but did not detect it. Electrolux groups E10, E11, and E12 together as fill failures, with E12 specifically occurring when the washer cannot fill during the rinsing phase.

The control board monitors the fill process and throws E12 when it does not register enough incoming water within the expected time window. This can happen because water is not reaching the machine, the inlet valve is not opening, or the machine cannot confirm that fill has occurred.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the inlet valve first without confirming water supply and inlet screen blockages. Always verify that both supply taps are fully open, hoses are not kinked, and inlet screens are clean before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or restricted water supply (~40%)** Supply taps not fully open, low household pressure, or kinked inlet hose stops water from reaching the machine during rinse fill.
- **Blocked inlet hose screens (~25%)** Sediment or debris clogs the mesh strainers at the hose connection points, restricting flow below the threshold the control expects.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~20%)** The valve solenoid does not open or the valve body is clogged internally, so no water enters even when the control sends a fill signal.
- **Reversed hot and cold supply lines (~10%)** After installation or a move, hot and cold hoses are swapped, causing the washer to call for rinse water from the wrong valve.
- **Wiring or connector issue (~5%)** A loose terminal, corroded connector, or damaged harness to the inlet valve prevents the control signal from reaching the valve.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and water** at the wall outlet and supply taps before starting any work.
2. **Verify both supply taps** behind the washer are turned fully open and that household water pressure is adequate.
3. **Inspect the inlet hoses** for kinks, crushing, or other visible damage that would restrict flow.
4. **Remove both inlet hoses** from the back of the washer and check the mesh screens inside the inlet ports for sediment or debris, then clean or replace as needed.
5. **Confirm correct hose installation** by verifying that hot and cold lines are connected to the correct inlets as marked on the machine.
6. **Run a diagnostic or test rinse cycle** to see if the code clears after addressing supply and hose issues.
7. **Check the inlet valve electrically** by inspecting the wiring harness and connectors for damage, loose terminals, or corrosion.
8. **Replace the water inlet valve assembly** if water supply and hoses are good but the machine still will not fill during rinse, then clear the code and retest.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified that both supply taps are open, inlet screens are clean, and hoses are in good condition but E12 persists. A pro can run the factory diagnostic mode to confirm the stored fault, measure whether the control is sending a signal to the inlet valve, and safely replace the valve or trace a wiring fault. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water connections or if the washer requires disassembly beyond removing the rear panel to access the valve.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
