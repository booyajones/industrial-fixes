## What this code means
E12 is a water inlet or fill fault. The control board expects the machine to fill with water during the wash or rinse cycle, but it detects no flow or insufficient flow. Electrolux groups E10, E11, and E12 together as fill-related faults. The washer will not continue the cycle until the fill problem is corrected.

In some cases the machine may lock for several minutes after the fault appears. The code does not always mean a failed part. Supply issues, kinked hoses, and clogged inlet strainers account for the majority of E12 cases in the field.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the inlet valve first without checking the tiny mesh screens inside the hose fittings. Pull both hoses and inspect the screens for sediment before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screen or filter (~40%)** Sediment and mineral deposits from the water supply collect in the mesh strainers at the inlet hose connections or valve inlets, restricting flow below the threshold the control expects.
- **Kinked or blocked inlet hose (~25%)** The hose is pinched behind the machine, crushed by the cabinet, or internally collapsed, preventing adequate water flow to the fill valve.
- **Water supply taps not fully open or low household pressure (~15%)** One or both shut-off valves at the wall are only partially open, or the home supply pressure has dropped below the minimum the washer needs to fill in the allotted time.
- **Defective water inlet valve (~12%)** The solenoid coils inside the valve have failed open or the valve body is clogged or mechanically stuck, so it does not open when the control sends the command.
- **Reversed hot and cold connections after installation (~5%)** If the machine was recently installed or moved, swapped hot and cold lines can cause the washer to timeout or fault during certain cycles that expect a specific temperature inlet.
- **Faulty pressure switch, sensor, or wiring (~3%)** The level-sensing circuit cannot confirm the machine is filling, even if water is entering, or a broken wire interrupts the valve command signal.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>When you remove the inlet hoses, does water flow freely and strongly from both wall taps?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The supply is good. Inspect the mesh screens in the hose ends and the valve inlets for debris or sediment and clean them.<br><strong>No:</strong> Turn both taps fully counterclockwise. If flow is still weak, the problem is household supply or a partially closed valve, not the washer.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are the inlet hoses kinked, crushed, or twisted anywhere along their length?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Straighten or reposition the hoses so they have a smooth, gradual bend. Replace any hose that is damaged or permanently crimped.<br><strong>No:</strong> The hoses are clear. Move to checking the inlet screens and the inlet valve itself.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After cleaning the screens and verifying supply, does the machine still show E12 when you restart a cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The inlet valve solenoids or the pressure sensor circuit are suspect. Test the valve with a meter or replace it if you have verified all upstream supply and hose conditions.<br><strong>No:</strong> The fault is cleared. Run a full cycle to confirm the repair.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the water** supply at both hot and cold taps behind the washer and unplug the machine from the wall outlet.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the inlet hoses at the back panel. Have a shallow pan and towel ready for residual water in the lines.
3. **Disconnect both inlet hoses** from the back of the washer and from the wall taps. Inspect each hose along its entire length for kinks, crushing, or internal blockage.
4. **Remove and inspect the inlet screens** inside the hose fittings and the valve inlets on the washer. Use needle-nose pliers or a small pick to lift out the mesh strainers, then rinse them under running water and scrub with a soft brush to remove sediment.
5. **Reconnect the hoses** to the washer and wall taps, hand-tight plus a quarter turn with pliers. Turn the water taps fully on and check for leaks at both connections.
6. **Plug the washer back in** and start a rinse or test cycle. Listen for the inlet valve to click and confirm water flows into the drum within 30 seconds.
7. **If E12 persists**, turn off the water and power again, then test the inlet valve solenoids with a multimeter or replace the valve assembly if all upstream checks passed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the inlet screens, verified strong water flow at the taps, replaced the inlet valve, and the E12 code still appears. At that stage the fault is likely in the pressure sensor circuit, the wiring harness, or the main control board. A pro has the service manual, wiring diagrams, and meters to trace the signal path and pinpoint whether the problem is a sensor, a broken wire, or a failed board output. Also call if you are not comfortable working with plumbing connections or if the machine was recently installed and you suspect incorrect hot and cold hookup, since Electrolux identifies reversed lines as a known installation issue for fill faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220.
