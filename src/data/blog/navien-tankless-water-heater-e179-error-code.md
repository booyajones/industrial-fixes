## Navien Tankless Water Heater E179 Error — What It Means

The E179 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a communication fault or sensor signal problem within the unit's control system. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel for the specific definition on your unit.

This code often points to loose or corroded wiring connections, a failed sensor (such as temperature or flow sensors), or interference in the communication bus between the control board and peripheral components. Because Navien units rely on constant feedback from multiple sensors to modulate burner output and maintain temperature, any break in that signal chain will trigger a fault code and shut down the heater.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real problem is a single corroded connector or failed sensor. Inspect all wiring harnesses and test sensor resistance with a multimeter before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wire connection (~35%)** Vibration, moisture intrusion, or poor initial installation can cause connectors at the control board or sensors to work loose or develop corrosion, breaking the signal path.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~25%)** Inlet, outlet, or heat-exchanger temperature sensors can drift out of range or fail open, sending invalid data to the control board.
- **Failed flow sensor (~20%)** The flow sensor or its wiring may be damaged or fouled with debris, preventing the board from reading flow rate correctly.
- **Control board firmware glitch (~10%)** A transient power surge or software bug can lock the board in a fault state that a power cycle will clear.
- **Main control board failure (~10%)** The printed circuit board itself can develop solder cracks, capacitor failures, or relay problems that corrupt communication signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and lifting it away.
3. **Inspect all wire harness connectors** on the main control board and each sensor (inlet, outlet, heat exchanger, flow) for looseness, bent pins, or green corrosion.
4. **Reseat any loose connectors** firmly and clean corroded terminals with contact cleaner and a small brush.
5. **Use a multimeter** set to resistance (ohms) to test each temperature sensor by unplugging it and measuring across its terminals; compare readings to the service manual's temperature-resistance table.
6. **Check the flow sensor** for debris or damage; if accessible, remove and rinse it under water, then reinstall.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then run hot water to see if the error returns; if it does, replace the sensor or control board identified by your tests.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you lack a multimeter and the service manual, or if your tests do not isolate a single failed sensor. Tankless water heaters integrate gas control, high-voltage ignition, and water flow in a single unit, and misdiagnosis can lead to expensive part replacements or unsafe operation. A technician can run factory diagnostic routines, verify gas pressure and combustion, and order the correct part under warranty if your unit is still covered.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
