## What this code means
The tE2 code on a Samsung washer signals a temperature-sensing fault in the thermistor or temperature sensor circuit. Samsung groups tE, tE1, tE2, tE3, tC1, tC2, and tC3 as temperature sensor errors. The control board has detected a problem with the feedback from the sensor that monitors water or tub temperature, so it cannot trust the reading and stops the cycle to prevent incorrect heating or other issues.

This code points to a failure in the sensor itself, its wiring and connectors, the heater assembly on models that heat wash water, or the control board's input circuit. It is not a drain fault, water-level issue, or (as some online videos mistakenly claim) a fan duct sensor problem. The fix centers on electrical diagnostics of the thermistor, harness, and heater path.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Test the thermistor's resistance with a multimeter and inspect all connectors and wiring before spending money on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Defective temperature sensor or thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor fails open, shorted, or drifts out of specification so the control board sees no valid signal.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring or connector (~30%)** Vibration, moisture, or heat breaks the wire, corrodes a terminal, or unseats the plug between the sensor and control board.
- **Heater assembly or heater circuit fault (~10%)** On models with an inline heater, a burned element, damaged terminal, or loss of continuity can trigger the temperature error.
- **Main control board input circuit issue (~10%)** If the sensor, harness, and heater all test good, the control board's temperature input may be damaged or faulty.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the thermistor connector fully seated and free of corrosion or moisture?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Good wiring reduces one variable. Move to resistance testing the sensor itself.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean and reseat the connector, or replace damaged terminals. Clear the code and test a cycle before ordering parts.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the thermistor's resistance read open (infinite), zero, or far outside the expected range for room temperature?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The sensor is bad. Replace the thermistor and retest.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor may be good. Check continuity of the wiring harness and inspect the heater if your model uses one.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>If your washer has a heater, does the heater element show continuity and intact terminals?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Heater is likely fine. If sensor and harness also passed, suspect the control board input circuit.<br><strong>No:</strong> Replace the heater assembly and clear the code.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and pull it away from the wall so you can access the back or bottom panel.
2. **Remove the access panel** (back, lower front, or top depending on model) to expose the temperature sensor, usually clipped to the tub sump or heater housing.
3. **Inspect the thermistor connector** for corrosion, moisture, pushed-back pins, or damage, and make sure the plug is fully seated.
4. **Disconnect the sensor** and use a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) to measure across the thermistor's two terminals at room temperature, then compare the reading to your model's service specification or resistance chart (consult your wiring diagram if you do not have the chart).
5. **Check wiring continuity** from the sensor plug back to the control board connector, wiggling the harness at flex points to reveal intermittent breaks.
6. **If your model has a heater**, measure continuity across the heater element and inspect terminals for burn marks or breaks.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (sensor, harness, or heater), reassemble the panels, plug in the washer, clear the code if needed, and run a test cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with the washer unplugged and disassembled, if you lack a multimeter or do not know how to interpret resistance readings, or if the thermistor, harness, and heater all test good but the code persists (pointing to a control board fault that requires cross-checking multiple circuits). A pro can also access Samsung's service mode and resistance tables for your exact model, saving you diagnostic guesswork and the risk of ordering the wrong sensor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250.
