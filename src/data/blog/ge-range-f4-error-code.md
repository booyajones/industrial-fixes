## GE Range F4 Error Code — What It Means

The F4 error code on a GE range signals an oven temperature sensor fault. The control board is reading the sensor circuit as open, shorted, or outside the expected resistance range. GE support guidance directs technicians to the oven sensor first, along with a basic power reset.

The sensor is a small probe mounted inside the oven cavity that monitors temperature. When the resistance reading falls outside normal limits (typically around 1,080 to 1,100 Ω at room temperature), the control throws the F4 code and may disable oven operation to prevent runaway heating or inaccurate baking temperatures.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first. Before buying a new control, measure the sensor resistance directly with a multimeter at room temperature (should read around 1,080–1,100 Ω). If the sensor reads open, shorted, or far out of range, replace the sensor instead.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~65%)** The sensor probe has drifted out of calibration, developed an internal open, or shorted, causing the control to see resistance outside the expected 1,080–1,100 Ω range at room temperature.
- **Damaged or loose sensor wiring (~20%)** The harness between the sensor and control board may have a pinched wire, melted insulation, loose connector, or abrasion that breaks continuity or creates an intermittent short.
- **Corroded or loose connector at the sensor or board (~10%)** Oxidation, moisture, or vibration can create high resistance or an open circuit at the plug terminals, making the control misread the sensor.
- **Control board misreading a good sensor (~5%)** If the sensor and wiring both test correctly but the code persists, the electronic range control itself may have a faulty analog-to-digital converter or reference circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Switch off the range circuit breaker** for 60 seconds to reset the control, then restore power and check whether the F4 code returns.
2. **Disconnect power at the breaker** before opening any panels or touching wiring.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor**, usually a metal probe with two wires protruding through the rear wall of the oven cavity.
4. **Unplug the sensor connector** and inspect both the sensor terminals and the harness plug for corrosion, melted plastic, or loose pins.
5. **Measure the sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms, probing the two sensor terminals at room temperature (expect roughly 1,080–1,100 Ω).
6. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if the reading is open (infinite resistance), shorted (near zero), or significantly outside the 1,080–1,100 Ω range.
7. **Reassemble, restore power, and test** the oven through a bake cycle to confirm the F4 code does not return and the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240 V wiring, if you cannot locate the sensor or safely access the rear panel, or if the sensor and wiring both test good but the F4 code continues to appear after a power reset. A qualified appliance repair tech can verify the control board input circuit, check for chassis shorts, and confirm whether the electronic range control needs replacement. Also call if the oven shows signs of arcing, smoke, or other electrical damage that may indicate a more serious fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 20–40 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
