## Maytag Dishwasher E7 Error Code — What It Means

The E7 error code on a Maytag dishwasher indicates the drying system is malfunctioning and the unit is not reaching the required temperature. This typically means a heating-related component has failed or the incoming water is too cold. Note that Maytag's official support page for E7 focuses on resetting the control rather than defining the fault, so always confirm the exact meaning for your model before replacing parts. If the code appears immediately after a power outage or interruption, it may be a control glitch rather than a true hardware failure.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element** The element that raises water temperature during wash and dry cycles can burn out or develop an open circuit, preventing proper heating.
- **Faulty thermostat** The thermostat monitors water and interior temperatures and will trigger the error if it reads incorrectly or fails to close its contacts.
- **Low incoming water temperature** If the supply water is below approximately 120°F, the dishwasher may not reach target temperature and will flag a heating fault.
- **Blocked vent assembly** A clogged or obstructed vent prevents moisture from escaping during the dry cycle, causing the control to detect incomplete drying.
- **Control board glitch** Temporary electrical interference or a brief power interruption can corrupt the control's memory and throw a false E7 code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cycle power for 30 seconds.** Unplug the dishwasher or flip the breaker off for at least 30 seconds, then restore power and test a full cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Verify incoming water temperature.** Run hot water at the sink nearest the dishwasher and check that it reaches at least 120°F before starting a cycle.
3. **Test the heating element for continuity.** Disconnect power, remove the lower access panel, locate the element terminals, and use a multimeter to confirm the element is not open.
4. **Inspect the thermostat.** Check the thermostat for continuity at room temperature and verify it is mounted securely against the tub where it can sense heat accurately.
5. **Check the vent assembly for blockage.** Open the dishwasher door and locate the vent (usually on the inner door panel), then clear any debris or buildup that could trap moisture inside.
6. **Run a diagnostic cycle.** Consult your model's service sheet to enter diagnostic mode and observe whether the heating element energizes and the thermostat closes during the cycle.
7. **Replace the failed component.** If testing confirms a bad element, thermostat, or vent mechanism, install the correct replacement part for your model and reset the code by cycling power.

## When to Call a Pro

If the code returns after a power reset and you are uncomfortable working with 120 V wiring or testing live circuits, call a qualified appliance technician. Also bring in a pro if you have confirmed the heating element and thermostat both test good but the dishwasher still will not heat, since the fault may lie in the control board or wiring harness. Any situation involving water leaks near electrical components or a burning smell during operation requires immediate professional diagnosis to prevent fire or shock hazards.
