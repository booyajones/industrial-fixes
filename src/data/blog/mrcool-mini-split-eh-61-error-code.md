## MRCOOL EH 61 Error Code — What It Means

The EH 61 (or E5-EH61) error code on your MRCOOL mini split indicates a problem with the evaporator coil temperature sensor, known as the T2 sensor, inside the indoor air handler. This sensor monitors the temperature of the evaporator coil and sends that data to the control board. When the board detects that the sensor is open-circuit, shorted, disconnected, or reporting an invalid value, it triggers the EH 61 fault and shuts down the system to protect the equipment.

The code does not necessarily mean the sensor itself has failed. Often the issue is a loose or corroded connector, damaged wiring, or debris blocking airflow and causing abnormal readings. In some cases the indoor control board may be at fault if the sensor and wiring test good but the board still cannot read the signal correctly.

## Common Causes

- **Disconnected or loose sensor connector** The T2 sensor plug may have vibrated loose or was not fully seated at the indoor control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** Frayed insulation, pinched wires, corrosion, or a break in the harness can cause an open or short circuit.
- **Failed T2 evaporator coil temperature sensor** The sensor itself may have failed internally and now reads open, shorted, or out of range.
- **Dirty evaporator coil or restricted airflow** Dust, debris, or a clogged filter can cause abnormal coil temperatures that push the sensor reading out of bounds.
- **Faulty indoor control board** If the sensor and wiring are good but the board cannot process the signal, the PCB may need replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off all power** to the mini split at the breaker and wait at least 15 minutes to allow the system to fully reset.
2. **Remove the indoor unit's front cover** and locate the evaporator coil and the T2 sensor, which is clipped or taped to the coil fins.
3. **Inspect the sensor connector and wiring harness** for loose plugs, corrosion, frayed insulation, or any physical damage, and reseat the connector firmly on the control board.
4. **Disconnect the T2 sensor from the board** and use a multimeter set to resistance mode to measure across the sensor leads. An open (infinite) reading indicates a failed sensor.
5. **Check the evaporator coil and air filter** for dust, debris, or blockages, and clean or replace as needed to restore proper airflow.
6. **Inspect the indoor control board** for burn marks, corrosion, or loose terminal connections. Look for any signs of moisture damage.
7. **Restore power and test** the system. If the code returns after confirming the sensor, wiring, and coil are good, replace the indoor PCB. If the code clears, monitor the system for 24 hours to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside electrical equipment, if you cannot locate the T2 sensor or control board, or if the fault returns after you have verified the sensor and wiring. A technician has the tools to measure sensor resistance against the manufacturer's curve, check refrigerant pressures and system operating conditions, and safely replace the indoor PCB if needed. If your system is under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.
