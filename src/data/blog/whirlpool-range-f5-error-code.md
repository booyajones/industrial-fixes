## Whirlpool Range F5 Error Code — What It Means

On a Whirlpool range, the F5 or F5 E1 error code points to a problem with the main oven door latch assembly, the door switch, or the wiring that connects them to the control board. This is not a heating or temperature fault. The control has detected that the door latch is not reporting the correct position, either because the latch mechanism is stuck or broken, the door switch has failed, or the wiring has come loose or shorted.

Whirlpool's product help identifies this code specifically as a main oven door latch system fault and its feedback circuit. The code will often appear during the self-clean cycle (when the door must lock) or after a cycle if the latch does not fully release. In the field, technicians find the door latch assembly itself is the most frequent culprit, followed by the door switch and then wiring issues.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first when they see an error code, but the F5 fault almost always lives in the latch assembly, door switch, or wiring. Test those mechanical parts and connections before spending money on electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or sticking door latch assembly (~55%)** The motor-driven latch mechanism binds, the plastic linkage breaks, or the motor burns out so the latch cannot report its position to the control.
- **Failed door switch (~20%)** The micro-switch that senses door closure or latch position develops burned contacts or a broken actuator arm and sends no signal.
- **Loose, damaged, or shorted wiring (~15%)** Vibration pulls a connector loose, heat degrades insulation, or a wire breaks inside the harness between the latch assembly and the control board.
- **Misaligned switch actuating mechanism (~7%)** The door hinges sag or the latch linkage shifts so the switch plunger is never fully pressed, even when the door is closed.
- **Control board interpretation problem (~3%)** The control circuitry that reads the latch and switch signals develops a fault, though Whirlpool guidance emphasizes checking the mechanical components first.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait one full minute, then restore power to clear any transient fault and confirm the code persists.
2. **Remove the oven door** (lift straight up on most Whirlpool models after opening to the broil-stop position) to access the latch assembly and door switch more easily.
3. **Inspect the door latch assembly** for physical damage, broken plastic tabs, or a latch rod that will not slide freely, and listen for motor hum during a self-clean start to confirm the motor tries to move.
4. **Check the door switch** by locating the small micro-switch near the door strike or latch, verifying the actuator arm moves freely, and using a multimeter to test for continuity when the switch is pressed and open when released.
5. **Examine all wiring and connectors** in the latch and switch circuit for looseness, heat damage, frayed insulation, or corrosion, and reseat every connector firmly.
6. **Replace the door latch assembly** if it binds, the motor does not turn the latch rod, or any part of the mechanism is visibly broken.
7. **Replace the door switch** if it shows no continuity change or if the contacts are pitted and burned, and verify the new switch actuates correctly when the door closes.
8. **Reinstall the oven door** and run a short bake cycle to confirm the code does not return and the latch operates normally during a self-clean lock test.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt wiring, if the range is built-in and requires cabinet removal to access the back panel, or if you have replaced both the latch assembly and door switch yet the F5 code still appears (the control board may need replacement or the wiring harness inside the range chassis may be shorted). A technician has the service manual, wiring diagrams, and meters to trace the latch circuit end-to-end and can order the correct Whirlpool parts by your serial number. Also call a pro if the latch mechanism is entangled with the door hinge system in a way that requires special disassembly, or if you see any signs of arcing or burned insulation that suggest a larger electrical fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
