## Whirlpool Range F5 Error Code — What It Means

The F5 error code on a Whirlpool range indicates a problem with the oven door latch assembly, the door latch switches, or the wiring that connects them to the control board. Some models display F5 E1 with the same underlying meaning. This is a door-lock circuit fault, not a temperature sensor issue. The control has detected that the latch mechanism or its associated switch contacts are not operating correctly, or that the wiring between them has failed or become disconnected.

When the code appears, the oven may refuse to start a self-clean cycle or may lock the door unexpectedly. If the code returns immediately after a power reset, the failure is usually in the latch assembly itself, the door switch, or a damaged wire connection rather than a temporary electronic glitch.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the fault is actually a broken wire or loose connector at the door latch switch. Inspect the latch assembly and its wiring first before ordering any electronic control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or sticking door latch assembly (~50%)** The motor-driven latch mechanism seizes, wears out, or the latch hook does not extend or retract fully, causing the control to flag a circuit fault.
- **Defective door latch switch (~25%)** The microswitch that signals door-lock position fails to close or open, sending an incorrect state to the control board.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors (~15%)** Vibration, heat, or age breaks a wire or loosens a plug in the latch circuit, creating an intermittent or open connection.
- **Corroded or bent switch terminals (~10%)** Moisture or arcing oxidizes the contacts inside the door switch, preventing reliable continuity even when the latch moves correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** for the range and wait one full minute to allow the control board to clear any stored faults.
2. **Restore power** and observe the display for one minute to see whether the F5 code reappears immediately.
3. **Test oven operation** by starting a bake cycle at any temperature. If the oven heats normally and the code does not return, monitor the range over the next few uses.
4. **Remove the oven door** (if the code persists) by opening it fully and lifting it off the hinge slots to gain access to the latch assembly mounted in the front frame.
5. **Inspect the door latch assembly** for physical damage, a stuck latch hook, or broken mounting tabs. Manually move the latch hook to confirm smooth travel.
6. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the latch assembly and check each terminal and pin for corrosion, looseness, or burnt insulation. Reseat the connector firmly.
7. **Replace the door latch assembly** (or the door switch alone, if your model uses a separate switch) with a genuine Whirlpool part that matches your range model number, then reassemble the door and test a self-clean cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt wiring, if the latch assembly is difficult to reach behind insulation or sheet-metal panels, or if the code returns after you have replaced both the latch assembly and verified all wiring continuity. A technician has the service manual and multimeter skills to trace the circuit from the control board to the latch and identify intermittent faults that are hard to reproduce. Professional diagnosis is also wise if your range is still under warranty, since self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–150 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
