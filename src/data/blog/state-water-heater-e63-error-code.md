## State Water Heater E63 Error Code — What It Means

The E63 error code does not appear in State Water Heaters' published support documentation or official troubleshooting guides. State directs technicians to scan the QR code on the rating plate to access model-specific manuals and fault-code definitions. Without verified manufacturer documentation for E63, the code's meaning depends entirely on your exact model number and product type (gas tank, gas tankless, or heat-pump electric). Each platform uses different fault codes and diagnostics.

For gas models experiencing flame dropout or ignition problems, State's troubleshooting videos point to the thermocouple or flame sensor as the most common culprit. Heat-pump models include built-in diagnostics that require a multimeter to interpret. Always confirm your model type and pull the correct manual before attempting any repair.

## Common Causes

- **Undocumented fault code** E63 does not appear in State's official manuals, so the definition varies by model and platform.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor** On gas units, carbon buildup or sensor failure causes flame dropout and may trigger fault codes.
- **Faulty gas control valve** A failing gas valve can shut down the burner prematurely or prevent ignition altogether.
- **Heat-pump sensor or component fault** Electric heat-pump models use different diagnostics and may report sensor or compressor faults.
- **Missing or incorrect model manual** Without the QR-code manual for your exact model, you cannot decode the fault accurately.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the rating plate** on your water heater and note the full model number and serial number.
2. **Scan the QR code** on the rating plate to access the official State manual and fault-code table for your model.
3. **Confirm the product type** (gas tank, gas tankless, or heat-pump electric) because diagnostics differ by platform.
4. **Check for flame dropout** if you have a gas model: watch the burner through the viewing window to see if it ignites then shuts off.
5. **Inspect and clean the thermocouple or flame sensor** on gas units by removing it, wiping it with fine sandpaper or a cloth, and reinstalling it snugly.
6. **Test with a multimeter** if you have a heat-pump model, following the built-in diagnostic steps in your manual.
7. **Replace the gas control valve or gas sensor valve** if cleaning the flame sensor does not resolve repeated shutdowns on gas models.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water-heater technician if you cannot locate your model's manual via the QR code, if you are unfamiliar with gas appliance safety, or if the fault persists after cleaning the flame sensor. Heat-pump models require refrigerant and electrical diagnostics best handled by a qualified tech. Any work on gas lines, pressure regulators, or control boards should be performed by a professional to avoid safety hazards and warranty issues.
