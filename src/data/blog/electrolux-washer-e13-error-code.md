## What this code means
The E13 error on an Electrolux washer signals that the control board has detected an unexpected drop in water level during the cycle. The machine expects the tub to hold a certain amount of water, but the pressure sensor reports that the level is falling when it should remain stable. This can be triggered by an actual leak somewhere in the water path or by a fault in the pressure-sensing circuit that monitors tub water level.

The code does not always mean water is visibly leaking onto your floor. A drain hose installed too low or positioned below the tub water line can create a siphon effect that slowly pulls water out during the fill or wash phase, tricking the control into thinking there is a leak. Blocked or disconnected pressure hoses, a faulty pressure sensor, or problems with the water inlet valve can also cause the control to see a false or real water-level loss.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, assuming the error is electronic. Check for visible leaks, verify drain-hose height, and inspect the pressure hose and sensor before spending money on a new PCB.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose installed too low or creating siphon (~35%)** A drain hose positioned below the tub water line or routed incorrectly can pull water out of the tub during fill or wash, triggering the water-level loss alarm.
- **Pressure hose blocked, kinked, or disconnected (~25%)** The small air hose that runs from the tub to the pressure sensor can become clogged with debris, develop a leak, or pop off its fitting, preventing accurate water-level sensing.
- **Faulty pressure sensor or switch (~20%)** The pressure sensor that reads tub water level can drift out of calibration or fail internally, causing the control to see a false drop in water level.
- **Actual water leak in hoses, boot, or tub connections (~15%)** Cracks or loose clamps in fill hoses, the tub-to-pump hose, or the door boot can allow water to escape, triggering the E13 leak-detection logic.
- **Water inlet valve stuck or not filling properly (~5%)** A valve that does not open fully or that leaks when closed can prevent the tub from reaching the correct level, confusing the control's water-loss detection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves** to make inspection safe.
2. **Check for visible water leaks** under the machine, around the door boot, at fill-hose connections, and along the tub-to-pump hose. Tighten or replace any leaking hose or clamp.
3. **Verify drain-hose installation** so the outlet end is positioned higher than the top of the tub drum and secured in a standpipe or laundry sink. A hose that dips below the tub water line will siphon water out during fill.
4. **Inspect the pressure-sensing hose** (a small air tube running from the tub air bell to the pressure sensor or switch). Look for kinks, blockage, disconnection, or cracks. Clean or replace the hose if damaged.
5. **Test or replace the pressure sensor** if the hose is intact but the code persists. The sensor is typically mounted on the control housing or near the tub. Consult your model's wiring diagram for location and connector pinout.
6. **Check the water inlet valve** for proper operation. Confirm both fill taps are fully open and hoses are not kinked. Swap the valve if it does not open reliably or leaks when closed.
7. **Clear the error code** (unplug for one minute or use the service menu if your model provides one), then run a short wash cycle to verify the tub fills and holds water without triggering E13 again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified the drain hose is correctly positioned, found no visible leaks, cleaned or replaced the pressure hose, and swapped the pressure sensor but the E13 code returns. At that point the fault may lie in the main control board, in hidden wiring, or in the tub air-bell chamber itself. A pro can run diagnostic mode to read live pressure values and compare them to factory specifications, and has access to model-specific service bulletins that cover unusual E13 triggers. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V wiring or if your washer is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–80 in parts (hose clamp, pressure sensor, or inlet valve), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call.
