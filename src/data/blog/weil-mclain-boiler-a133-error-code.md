## Weil-McLain A133 Error Code — What It Means

A133 is not a universal factory error code verified across all Weil-McLain boiler families. The exact meaning depends on your specific model and control system. Weil-McLain boilers use manufacturer-specific fault codes that must be decoded using the correct model manual or the service menu on the control itself. The code typically indicates a lockout or control fault condition that has triggered a safety shutdown.

Because the code definition is model-specific, your first step is to locate the technical manual for your exact boiler model and check the fault code table. Common underlying causes for lockout conditions of this type include failed sensors, ignition or flame-proving problems, low system pressure, circulation faults, or controller issues. The control will often store a fault history that shows whether the issue is related to ignition, temperature sensing, or pressure, and reading that history before resetting is the correct diagnostic starting point.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many technicians replace control boards or gas valves without first testing sensors and checking system pressure. Use a multimeter to verify thermistor resistance against the manufacturer curve and confirm proper boiler pressure on the gauge before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or out-of-range temperature sensor (~30%)** A thermistor or temperature probe reading outside its normal range will trigger a lockout, and this is one of the most common causes of fault codes on modern Weil-McLain controls.
- **Ignition or flame-proving failure (~25%)** A dirty or misaligned flame sensor, weak ignitor, fouled burner, or insufficient gas delivery prevents the control from proving flame and results in a lockout.
- **Low system pressure or loss of water (~20%)** System pressure below the minimum setting will cause the boiler to shut down as a safety measure and can generate fault codes.
- **Circulation problem (~15%)** A failed circulator pump, closed valve, or air trapped in the system can cause abnormal temperature behavior that the control interprets as a fault.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~10%)** If all components test good but the fault persists, the main controller or a wiring harness connection may have an internal or intermittent fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the boiler at the service switch and turn off the manual gas shutoff valve if you will be inspecting ignition components.
2. **Locate the model number** on the boiler rating plate and download or retrieve the correct technical manual to find the A133 code definition and fault history procedure.
3. **Read the fault history** from the control display or installer menu before clearing the code, noting whether the log shows ignition failures, sensor faults, or pressure events.
4. **Check system pressure** on the boiler gauge and add water through the fill valve if pressure is below the minimum mark shown in the manual.
5. **Inspect ignition components** by removing the burner access cover and checking the ignitor for cracks or carbon buildup, the flame sensor for corrosion or misalignment, and the burner for dust or debris.
6. **Test temperature sensors** with a multimeter by disconnecting each thermistor or probe and measuring resistance, then comparing the reading to the manufacturer resistance curve in the manual.
7. **Verify circulator operation** by listening for pump noise when the boiler calls for heat and checking that hot water is flowing through the system, purging air from the boiler and piping if needed.
8. **Replace the failed component** identified by testing, then restore power and gas, clear the fault code, and monitor the boiler through a full heating cycle to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician for any A133 fault. Gas-fired boilers require specialized tools, combustion testing, and knowledge of local codes. Incorrect diagnosis or repair can result in carbon monoxide hazards, gas leaks, or property damage. A technician will retrieve the exact code definition from the control, test ignition and flame-proving circuits with a microamp meter, verify sensor readings against factory specifications, perform combustion analysis, and make sure safe operation after the repair. If the fault involves the control board, gas valve, or venting system, professional service is required.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-500.
