## Samsung Refrigerator Not Making Ice — What's Happening

When your Samsung refrigerator stops producing ice, you are dealing with a symptom rather than a specific fault code. Ice production depends on several conditions working together: the ice maker must be enabled, the freezer must reach the correct temperature, water must flow freely into the unit, and the mechanical or electronic components must cycle properly.

Samsung states that the freezer needs to be at 0°C (32°F) for the ice maker to function. If the unit is too warm, the water supply is restricted, or the ice maker is switched off or frozen over, you will get no ice. This condition can stem from something as simple as a settings menu toggle or as involved as a failed ice maker assembly.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Ice maker turned off or disabled** The most common cause is the ice maker being switched off through the control panel, smart screen, or a physical lever on the unit.
- **Freezer temperature too warm** If the freezer is running above 0°C (32°F), the ice maker will not produce ice even if everything else is working.
- **Clogged or overdue water filter** A restricted filter reduces water flow to the ice maker and is one of the first things Samsung recommends replacing.
- **Frozen or blocked ice maker fill tube** Ice buildup inside the ice maker compartment or around the fill tube will prevent new water from entering or ice from dropping.
- **Kinked, crushed, or frozen water supply line** If the supply line to the refrigerator is damaged, pinched, or frozen, water cannot reach the inlet valve.
- **Low household water pressure** Samsung requires a minimum of 30 psi and the ability to dispense at least 150 mL in 10 seconds for proper ice production.
- **Failed ice maker assembly or inlet valve** If the ice maker does not respond to a test cycle or the inlet valve does not open, the component itself has likely failed.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Check the control panel, smart screen, or physical lever to confirm the ice maker is enabled and not in off or child-lock mode.
2. Place an accurate thermometer in the freezer and verify the temperature is at or below 0°C (32°F).
3. Inspect the ice maker compartment for frost or ice buildup and thaw any blockage with warm air or a cloth, not sharp tools.
4. Confirm the household water shutoff valve is fully open and inspect the supply line for kinks, crushing, or frozen sections.
5. Replace the water filter if it is older than six months or if water flow from the dispenser is weak.
6. Run the ice maker test cycle by holding the test button or ice maker button for 3 seconds, or use the fridge manager menu on smart-screen models.
7. Check household water pressure by dispensing water into a measuring cup and confirming at least 150 mL flows in 10 seconds.
8. If the test cycle does not complete and water supply and temperature are correct, plan to replace the ice maker assembly or water inlet valve.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the ice maker does not respond to the test cycle after you have confirmed the freezer is cold, the water supply is open, and the filter is new. If you see water leaking inside the freezer or around the ice maker, or if the control board is not responding to inputs, those conditions require diagnosis with a multimeter and part-level troubleshooting. Replacing the ice maker assembly or inlet valve is straightforward, but tracing electrical faults or refrigerant issues that cause warm freezer temperatures is best left to a pro.
