## Kenmore Oven F031 Error — What It Means

The F031 error code on a Kenmore oven typically indicates a stuck relay condition on the electronic oven control (EOC) board. The control detects that a relay on the board is welded closed or failing to open when commanded, which can cause the oven to remain powered or unsafe conditions. Some models may interpret this code differently, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram if available.

Because the relay is part of the control board assembly and not separately serviceable, this code usually points to a failed electronic control board. Less commonly, a wiring short or connector fault can mimic the same symptom.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the oven temperature sensor or door lock assembly first. Check the wiring harness connectors at the control board and look for any signs of arcing or melted pins before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck relay on electronic control board (~70%)** A relay on the EOC board has welded closed or will not open, triggering the fault.
- **Failed electronic oven control board (~15%)** The control board logic circuit has failed and falsely detects a stuck relay condition.
- **Wiring harness short or damaged connector (~10%)** A wire short or corroded pin at the control board connector mimics a stuck relay signal.
- **Power surge or voltage spike damage (~5%)** A recent electrical event damaged components on the control board, causing relay circuit failure.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect all power** to the oven by unplugging it or turning off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the back panel or control panel** by taking out the screws around the perimeter of the rear or top access panel.
3. **Locate the electronic oven control board**, usually mounted behind the control panel or on the rear wall of the oven cavity.
4. **Photograph all wire connections** before disconnecting any harnesses so you have a reference for reassembly.
5. **Unplug each wiring harness** from the control board by pressing any locking tabs and pulling straight out.
6. **Remove the mounting screws** securing the control board to the chassis and carefully pull the board free.
7. **Install the new control board** in the same orientation, secure it with the original screws, and reconnect all harnesses using your photographs as a guide.
8. **Reinstall the access panel**, restore power, and test the oven by running a bake cycle to verify the error code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with household voltage (typically 240 V for electric ovens) or if removing the control panel requires disassembly of gas lines or complex cabinetry. A technician can also confirm the diagnosis with a multimeter and check for any secondary faults in the wiring or heating elements before replacing the board. If your oven is still under warranty or part of a built-in range, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150–$250 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $250–$400.
