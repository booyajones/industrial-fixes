## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E07 Error — What It Means

The E07 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition. The unit may be failing to sense a flame after ignition or unable to establish a stable flame at all. This can shut down the heater as a safety precaution.

Because error code meanings can vary slightly across different Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, always confirm the exact definition in your owner's manual or the label inside the unit's service panel. The underlying issue usually involves gas flow, ignition components, or flame sensing.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is simply a fouled flame sensor. Clean the sensor rod with fine sandpaper or emery cloth before buying parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty flame sensor (~50%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame sensor rod prevent it from detecting the flame, triggering a false fault.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~20%)** Closed gas valve, low pressure, or air in the line can prevent the burner from lighting or maintaining flame.
- **Faulty igniter (~15%)** A cracked or weak igniter may fail to spark reliably or produce a weak spark that cannot ignite the gas.
- **Failed flame sensor (~10%)** The sensor rod itself can fail due to corrosion or internal breakage, even when clean.
- **Gas valve issue (~5%)** A malfunctioning gas valve may not open fully or may supply inconsistent pressure to the burner.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve to make sure safe working conditions.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the heater according to your model's service instructions to access the burner chamber and ignition components.
3. **Locate the flame sensor rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner, and disconnect its wiring connector.
4. **Remove the sensor** by unscrewing its mounting bracket or fastener, then gently pull it out of the burner assembly.
5. **Clean the sensor rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth, rubbing along its length to remove carbon, rust, or white mineral deposits until the metal is shiny.
6. **Reinstall the sensor** in its original position, reconnect the wiring, and make sure it sits close to the burner flame path without touching the burner itself.
7. **Restore gas and power**, then open a hot water tap to call for heat and observe whether the unit ignites and stays lit without throwing the E07 code again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around gas or if cleaning the flame sensor and checking gas supply do not resolve the E07 code. Technicians have specialized meters to test gas pressure, flame current, and igniter output. If the gas valve or control board is suspected, a pro can diagnose and replace those components safely and make sure proper combustion and venting. Any time you smell gas or see visible flame rollout, shut off the gas immediately and call for service.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
