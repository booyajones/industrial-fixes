## Weil-McLain Boiler A122 Error — What It Means

A122 is not a universal manufacturer fault code in Weil-McLain documentation. It typically appears as a stored alarm or history identifier on certain control platforms, so the exact meaning depends on your boiler model and control type. The alphanumeric display alone is not enough to name the failed component. Use your boiler's diagnostics or fault-history function to retrieve the actual lockout or alarm name, then work from that recorded fault rather than guessing from A122 alone.

Because A122 itself is not defined in the provided Weil-McLain manuals, the underlying cause can be any of the usual lockout categories: ignition or flame-proving issues, low water or pressure, circulation problems, temperature-sensor faults, or condensate and venting issues on condensing models. Identifying your exact model and accessing the control's diagnostics menu is the first step a technician takes to pinpoint the real fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame sensor or ignition board without first checking system pressure, water level, or retrieving the actual fault from the control's diagnostics menu. Confirm the recorded fault name and check basic safeties before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Ignition or flame-proving fault (~30%)** Dirty flame sensor, weak flame signal, gas-supply problem, or ignition failure prevents the control from proving a stable flame and triggers a lockout.
- **Low water or system pressure (~25%)** System pressure below 12 psi, leaks requiring makeup water, or a tripped low-water cutoff shuts down the boiler to prevent damage.
- **Circulation or air-bound system (~20%)** Failed or weak circulator, air trapped in piping, closed valves, or blocked flow stops heat delivery and can cause a temperature-limit trip.
- **Temperature sensor or high-limit fault (~15%)** Faulty thermistor or temperature probe, false overheat reading, or high-limit switch activation stops the boiler for safety.
- **Condensate or venting issue (~10%)** Blocked condensate drain, vent obstruction, freeze-up, or related control shutdown on condensing models triggers a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your boiler model and control type** by noting the nameplate and control-panel label, then consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the fault-code table.
2. **Retrieve the current fault and alarm history** from the boiler's diagnostics menu by following the control's button sequence to see the recorded fault name, not just the A122 display.
3. **Check basic safeties first:** verify system pressure is above 12 psi, the gas supply shutoff is fully open, the low-water cutoff is not tripped, and the condensate drain and vent terminations are clear on condensing models.
4. **Inspect the flame-proving circuit** if the fault points to ignition or flame: remove and clean the flame sensor, check ignition-electrode alignment, and observe the ignition sequence and gas-valve operation during a call for heat.
5. **Test circulation and purge air** by confirming the circulator runs, checking for closed valves or blocked piping, and bleeding radiators or zones to restore flow if the system is air-bound.
6. **Test suspect sensors with a multimeter** when the control indicates a temperature or sensor fault, measuring resistance or voltage against the manufacturer's specification and replacing the probe if readings are abnormal.
7. **Correct the root cause before resetting:** repair leaks and restore proper pressure, clear condensate restrictions, replace failed sensors or switches after confirming the fault path, then reset the control and verify normal operation without recurring faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional whenever the boiler locks out repeatedly, when you cannot retrieve the actual fault name from the control's diagnostics menu, or when the repair involves gas supply, ignition components, sealed combustion venting, or pressure-testing the hydronic system. A qualified technician has the tools to measure flame signal strength, test sensor circuits with precision, diagnose control-board faults, and safely restore gas and water flow. If you see low system pressure or suspect a leak, do not simply add water and reset. A pro will find the leak, repair it, refill the system to around 15 psi, purge air, and verify that the boiler runs without further alarms. Proper diagnostics prevent misdiagnosis and the cost of unnecessary parts.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–400.
