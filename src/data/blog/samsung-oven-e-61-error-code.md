## Samsung Oven E-61 Error Code — What It Means

The E-61 error code on a Samsung oven typically indicates a problem with the temperature sensing or control circuit. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for your specific unit. In general, this code appears when the control board detects an abnormal signal from the oven temperature sensor or when the sensor circuit is open, shorted, or reading outside expected parameters. The oven will usually stop heating and may display the code continuously until the fault is corrected.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, which can cost over $200. Instead, measure the resistance of the oven temperature sensor at room temperature and compare it to your model's specification chart to confirm whether the sensor is the actual problem.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~55%)** The resistance element inside the sensor drifts out of range or fails open, sending incorrect signals to the control board.
- **Damaged or loose sensor wiring (~20%)** Wires to the sensor become frayed, pinched, or disconnected, breaking the circuit or causing intermittent readings.
- **Corroded or loose connector (~12%)** The plug at the sensor or control board develops oxidation or works loose, creating high resistance or an open circuit.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main oven control board has a failed input circuit or solder joint that misreads the sensor signal.
- **Shorted sensor probe (~3%)** The sensor probe touches bare metal inside the oven cavity, creating a short to ground.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven to eliminate shock hazard.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** or the side panel (depending on your model) to expose the oven temperature sensor and wiring harness.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor**, a metal probe threaded into the rear or side wall of the oven cavity, with a two-wire harness running to the control board.
4. **Disconnect the sensor harness** at the plug and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance across the two sensor terminals.
5. **Compare your reading** to the resistance table in your service manual (commonly around 1,000-1,100 ohms at room temperature for many models, but this varies).
6. **Inspect the wiring and connector** for burns, cracks, or corrosion, and check that the sensor probe is not touching grounded metal inside the oven.
7. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if resistance is out of range or the probe shows visible damage, then reconnect all wiring, restore power, and test the oven.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if you have tested the sensor and wiring and both are within specification. A technician can verify the control board input circuit, check for voltage at the sensor connector, and diagnose less common faults such as a failed relay or a grounding issue inside the control assembly. Also call a pro if the error persists after you have replaced the sensor and checked all wiring, since that points to a main control board failure that requires careful handling of live circuits.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
