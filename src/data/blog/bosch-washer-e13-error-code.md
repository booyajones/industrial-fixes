## Bosch Washer E13 Error Code — What It Means

The E13 error code on Bosch washers typically indicates a draining problem, though the exact meaning can vary by model. In most cases, the machine detects that water is not draining properly within the expected time frame, triggering a safety shutdown to prevent overflow or damage. The code may appear during the wash or spin cycle when the washer attempts to empty the tub.

Because Bosch uses different diagnostics across its product line, consult your specific model's owner's manual or wiring diagram to confirm the exact meaning. However, the code generally points to a blockage in the drain path, a faulty drain pump, or a pressure sensor issue that prevents the control board from recognizing that water has left the drum.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump when the real problem is a simple blockage in the drain filter, hose, or standpipe. Always inspect and clear the drain path before purchasing a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain filter or coin trap (~45%)** Hair, lint, coins, and small objects accumulate in the filter housing and prevent water from exiting the pump.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~20%)** The hose may be pinched behind the washer, clogged with debris, or installed too high above the pump outlet.
- **Failed drain pump (~18%)** The pump motor or impeller may be jammed by debris or electrically burned out, stopping water evacuation.
- **Faulty pressure sensor or air trap (~10%)** A dirty or blocked pressure chamber or a bad pressure switch can tell the control board that water remains even when the tub is empty.
- **Blocked standpipe or household drain (~5%)** A slow or clogged home drain prevents water from leaving the machine, causing backpressure and triggering the error.
- **Wiring or control board glitch (~2%)** Loose connectors or a software fault in the main control can falsely report a drain failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves to make sure safe working conditions.
2. **Open the drain filter access panel** at the lower front of the machine, place towels on the floor, and slowly unscrew the filter counterclockwise to release trapped water into a shallow pan.
3. **Remove and rinse the filter** under running water, clearing away lint, hair, coins, and debris, then inspect the filter housing and pump inlet for obstructions.
4. **Check the drain hose** by disconnecting it from the standpipe or drain connection, flushing it with water, and confirming it is not kinked or installed above the maximum height listed in your manual.
5. **Test the drain pump** by removing the lower front or rear panel, inspecting the impeller for jammed objects, and using a multimeter to check continuity across the pump terminals (consult your model's wiring diagram for resistance values).
6. **Inspect the pressure sensor system** by locating the small air-trap chamber near the sump and the pressure switch on the control panel, cleaning any residue from the chamber, and ensuring the connecting tube is clear and firmly attached.
7. **Reassemble all components**, restore power and water, and run a drain or spin cycle to verify the error is cleared and water evacuates completely.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared all blockages and the error persists, if the drain pump shows no continuity and you are uncomfortable removing panels or testing electrical components, or if the pressure sensor and control board diagnostics require specialized testing equipment. A technician can also diagnose intermittent wiring faults and perform software resets that are not accessible through the user interface. If the machine is under warranty, contact Bosch service before opening the cabinet to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts if a pump or pressure switch is needed, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
