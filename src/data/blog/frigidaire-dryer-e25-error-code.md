## Frigidaire Dryer E25 Error Code — What It Means

The E25 error code on Frigidaire dryers can indicate different issues depending on the model. It often relates to communication problems between the control board and other components, or faults with temperature or moisture sensors. Because Frigidaire uses this code across various models with different meanings, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer cabinet for the exact definition for your unit.

Common themes include loose or corroded wiring harnesses, failed thermistors, moisture sensor bar problems, or a faulty main control board. The dryer may stop mid-cycle, fail to heat, or refuse to start when this code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Before buying one, check every wiring connector for corrosion or looseness and test sensors with a multimeter for continuity.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring harness (~40%)** Vibration over time can work connectors loose, and moisture can corrode terminals, breaking communication between the control board and sensors.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** The temperature sensor may drift out of range or fail open, causing the control board to throw an error when it cannot read a valid signal.
- **Faulty moisture sensor strips (~15%)** The metal sensor bars inside the drum can become coated with fabric softener residue or corrode, preventing accurate moisture readings.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Rubbing, pinching, or heat can fray or break the wires between components, creating intermittent or permanent communication faults.
- **Defective main control board (~10%)** The electronic control board itself can fail due to power surges, component wear, or solder cracks, misinterpreting sensor signals or generating false errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and turn off the gas supply if applicable.
2. **Remove the top or front panel** by unscrewing the fasteners (location varies by model) to access the control board and wiring.
3. **Inspect every wiring harness connector** for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks. Disconnect and reconnect each one firmly.
4. **Clean corroded terminals** with electrical contact cleaner and a small brush. Replace any visibly damaged connectors or wires.
5. **Test the thermistor** (usually mounted on the blower housing or near the heating element) with a multimeter. Consult your model's service chart for the correct resistance at room temperature.
6. **Check the moisture sensor bars** inside the drum. Sand off any residue with fine-grit sandpaper and wipe clean with rubbing alcohol.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, plug it in, and run a test cycle or enter diagnostics mode to confirm the error is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if multimeter testing is unfamiliar, or if you have checked all connectors and sensors without finding a fault. A technician has the service manual with exact resistance tables and can quickly isolate whether the control board or a less obvious harness fault is to blame. Also call a pro if you see signs of arcing or burned components, as these suggest electrical faults that require careful inspection and may indicate other hidden damage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
