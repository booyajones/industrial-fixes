## What this code means
Frigidaire's F31 error code stands for "Shorted Probe." The electronic control board has detected a problem with the oven temperature sensing circuit. The sensor, called an RTD (resistance temperature detector) probe, monitors oven temperature and sends a resistance signal to the control. When the circuit is out of range, shorted to ground, or the probe itself has failed, the control throws F31 and disables heating to protect the appliance.

Frigidaire recommends a power reset as the first step. If the fault returns after the reset, the problem is typically the sensor probe itself, damaged wiring between the sensor and control board, or in rare cases the electronic control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the electronic control board first without testing the sensor or wiring. Always measure the sensor's resistance at room temperature and inspect the harness connectors for corrosion or loose pins before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed RTD temperature sensor probe (~65%)** The sensor inside the oven has electrically drifted out of range, shorted internally, or been damaged by heat cycling over time.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~20%)** The connector at the sensor or at the control board has worked loose, developed corrosion, or suffered heat damage at the terminals.
- **Short to cabinet ground in sensor wiring (~10%)** A wire in the sensor harness is pinched or chafed and making contact with the metal frame, creating a short to ground.
- **Electronic control board fault (~5%)** The control board's sensor-input circuitry has failed, even though the sensor and wiring test within specification.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Reset the control** by leaving power off for 30 seconds, then restore power. If the F31 does not reappear immediately, test the oven. If the code returns, continue diagnosis.
3. **Access the sensor** by removing the rear panel of the range or pulling the unit out and removing screws that secure the back cover.
4. **Inspect the sensor connector and wiring harness** for loose pins, heat discoloration, corrosion, or damaged insulation. Wiggle the connector and look for intermittent contact.
5. **Measure sensor resistance** with a multimeter at room temperature. Disconnect the sensor from the harness, set your meter to ohms, and probe across the two sensor leads. Compare the reading to your model's specification (typical values range from around 190 ohms to 1,080 ohms depending on the model).
6. **Check for a short to ground** by measuring continuity between each sensor wire and the metal oven frame or cabinet. There should be no continuity. If you find continuity, trace the harness for a pinch point or damaged wire.
7. **Replace the defective component**. If the sensor resistance is out of range or shorted to ground, install a new RTD sensor probe. If the sensor and wiring are good but the code persists, replace the electronic control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt appliance wiring, if your model requires removing the cooktop or disassembling sheet metal you cannot safely handle, or if you have replaced the sensor and inspected the wiring but the F31 persists. A technician can perform advanced circuit diagnostics, measure signal continuity at the control-board connector, and confirm whether the board itself has failed. Professional service is also recommended if the range is under warranty or if you need a service record for future parts claims.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
