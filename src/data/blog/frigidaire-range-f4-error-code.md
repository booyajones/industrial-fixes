## Frigidaire Range F4 Error Code — What It Means

On most Frigidaire electric ranges and built-in ovens, the F4 error code indicates a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit. The control board is seeing an open circuit, a short, or a reading that falls outside the expected range for normal oven temperature. This prevents the oven from regulating heat properly. Because Frigidaire uses model-specific fault codes, F4 may point to related control-board or door-lock issues on certain platforms, so always verify your exact model number against the technical sheet or wiring diagram before ordering parts.

The code typically appears when the sensor probe itself has deteriorated, when connector pins have corroded or backed out, or when harness wiring has been pinched or burnt. In a smaller number of cases the control board misreads a good sensor and needs replacement. If the fault appeared after a self-clean cycle or involves door-lock behavior, consult your model's service manual to confirm whether F4 maps to the sensor circuit or to a different subsystem on your unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first when the real problem is a $20 sensor or a loose connector. Always measure sensor resistance and inspect the harness plug before buying a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor or RTD probe (~50%)** The sensor element drifts out of spec or develops an internal open that the control reads as a fault.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged sensor harness connector (~25%)** Vibration, heat, or moisture causes pins to back out or oxidize, creating an intermittent or open circuit.
- **Pinched, burnt, or broken sensor wiring (~15%)** Wires routed near the oven cavity or hinge can be crushed or melted, opening the circuit between sensor and control.
- **Faulty electronic control board or relay board (~8%)** When the sensor and wiring test good, the control itself may be misreading the circuit or have failed solder joints.
- **Model-specific door-lock or UI fault mapping to F4 (~2%)** On certain Frigidaire platforms the F4 code can indicate a different subsystem, so always confirm against your model's tech sheet.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the range if possible to prevent shock during disassembly.
2. **Write down your exact model number** from the serial plate and look up the technical sheet or wiring diagram to confirm that F4 maps to the oven sensor circuit on your unit.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor probe** at the upper rear wall of the oven cavity and trace its wire to the connector behind the control panel or on the rear frame.
4. **Inspect the connector and harness** for loose pins, green corrosion, burnt insulation, or pinched wires; clean terminals with contact cleaner and reseat the plug firmly.
5. **Disconnect the sensor plug and measure resistance** across the sensor terminals with a multimeter; compare the reading to your model's resistance table or look for an open or dead-short reading that indicates failure.
6. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if it tests open, shorted, or far outside spec; if the sensor reads normal, verify continuity in the wiring harness from the sensor plug all the way to the control board connector.
7. **If wiring and sensor both check good**, replace the electronic control board or relay board as the remaining cause, then restore power and run a bake cycle to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt connections, if the range is hardwired and you cannot safely isolate power, or if you have replaced both the sensor and verified wiring but the F4 code persists. A technician has the model-specific resistance charts, can perform live-voltage checks at the control board, and will warranty both parts and labor. Also call if your model's tech sheet shows F4 maps to a door-lock or control fault rather than the sensor circuit, because those repairs often require specialized disassembly and recalibration.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250 including service call and sensor.
