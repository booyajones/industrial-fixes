## Frigidaire Oven F90 Error Code — What It Means

The F90 code is a door-lock or latch-system fault. The electronic oven control (EOC) commanded the door lock assembly to move, but the lock motor or switch feedback did not complete normally within the expected time. You will see this code most often during or after a self-clean cycle when the control is trying to lock the door before heating or trying to unlock the door after cooldown.

In practice, F90 is a lock system timeout or failure. The control sends a signal to the door lock motor to engage or disengage the latch, and the switch that confirms the position does not change state in time. This can be caused by a failed lock motor, a stuck or faulty lock switch, damaged or loose wiring between the lock assembly and the control board, or less commonly a failed output on the EOC itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control board first. Test the door lock motor resistance (should be near 2,000 ohms) and inspect the lock switch and wiring before buying a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock motor or assembly (~50%)** The lock motor wears out or burns out and can no longer drive the latch arm to engage or release the door strike.
- **Faulty door lock switch (~25%)** The micro-switch that signals the locked or unlocked position does not change state, so the control never sees confirmation and times out.
- **Damaged or loose wiring and connectors (~15%)** Wires between the lock assembly and the control board can break, pinch, corrode, or work loose, interrupting the command or feedback signal.
- **Failed electronic oven control board (EOC) (~10%)** The relay or triac output stage that drives the lock motor circuit can fail, preventing the motor from receiving power.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range, wait one minute, then restore power to see if the F90 code clears and the lock operates normally.
2. **Open the oven door and inspect** the latch area for any debris, foil, or oven racks blocking the strike, and clear any obstructions.
3. **Access the door lock assembly** by removing the rear panel (for slide-in models) or the front/top panel (for freestanding models) to expose the lock motor, switch, and wiring harness.
4. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** between the door lock assembly and the electronic oven control board for damage, corrosion, loose pins, or pinched insulation, and repair or replace any damaged sections.
5. **Test the door lock motor** by disconnecting power, removing the motor connector, and measuring resistance across the motor terminals with a multimeter (should read near 2,000 ohms).
6. **Test the door lock switch** for continuity changes as the latch arm moves between locked and unlocked positions, and replace the switch if it does not toggle properly.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor resistance is out of range or the motor does not move, or replace the electronic oven control board if all lock components and wiring test good but the fault returns.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if you cannot safely access the rear or top panels of your range, or if you have tested the door lock motor, switch, and wiring and all components check good but the F90 code persists. A technician has the tools and experience to isolate a failed control board output stage and can source the correct model-specific lock assembly or EOC without trial and error. Also call a pro if the lock mechanism is physically jammed inside the door frame and requires disassembly of the door itself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–150 in parts, 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
