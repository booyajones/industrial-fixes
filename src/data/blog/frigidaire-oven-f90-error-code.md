## What this code means
F90 on a Frigidaire electric range means the electronic oven control detected a door lock problem. The control sent a lock or unlock command but the door did not complete the movement within the allowed time. Frigidaire groups F90, F91, F92, F93, and F94 together as door lock issue codes. The oven may appear stuck in a locked state or refuse to start a self-clean cycle.

Because the code can be a false trip, Frigidaire recommends a full power reset first. If the code returns after power is restored, the lock assembly, wiring, or control board needs inspection.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the lock assembly is actually bad. Always test the lock motor resistance (should be around 2,000 ohms) and watch the cam and rod for binding before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Door lock motor or lock assembly failure (~50%)** The motor or mechanism cannot rotate the cam fully during lock or unlock, so the control times out and throws F90.
- **Mechanical binding or obstruction (~20%)** The cam, rod, latch, or related parts are jammed, worn, or physically obstructed by debris or hardened grease.
- **Failed door lock switch (~10%)** The switch that tells the control the lock reached position does not change state correctly, so the control does not see the lock complete.
- **Wiring problems (~10%)** Loose connectors, broken wires, or damaged insulation between the electronic control and lock assembly prevent proper signals.
- **Transient power or reset issue (~7%)** A voltage spike or brief power interruption can cause a false F90 that clears after a full power reset.
- **Electronic oven control board failure (~3%)** If the mechanism and wiring test good, the board may not be issuing or reading the lock or unlock signal correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or turning off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Perform a reset** by leaving the power disconnected for at least 30 seconds, then restore power and check if the code clears.
3. **Open the oven door** and locate the door lock assembly on the oven frame (usually near the top hinge area or front edge).
4. **Inspect the lock cam, rod, and latch** for visible binding, debris, grease buildup, or mechanical damage.
5. **Remove the back panel or top cover** (model-specific) to access the lock motor wiring and connectors.
6. **Check all connectors** between the electronic oven control and the lock assembly for looseness, corrosion, or damage.
7. **Test the lock motor resistance** by disconnecting its wires and measuring across the terminals with a multimeter; it should read about 2,000 ohms.
8. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor resistance is far off, the mechanism binds, or the switch does not change state during a lock cycle.
9. **Reassemble the range**, restore power, and run a short self-clean cycle to verify the lock operates correctly and the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt wiring or removing the range back panel. Also call if you have replaced the door lock assembly and checked all wiring but the F90 code returns, because that points to a control board issue that requires diagnostic equipment to confirm. If the oven is still under warranty, contact Frigidaire or an authorized service center before opening the cabinet yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
