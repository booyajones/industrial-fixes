## GE Oven Surface Burner Element Replacement — What This Part Does

The surface burner element (also called a Calrod or coil element) is the replaceable electric heating component on your GE range or cooktop. When you turn the burner control knob, current flows through the element to generate heat for cooking. GE plug-in Calrod burners like the 8-inch WB30M2 use a connector terminal that plugs into a terminal block under the cooktop. The element fails when internal coils burn out, when physical damage breaks the heating circuit, or when terminal connections wear and overheat from repeated expansion and contraction cycles.

Most failures show up as a burner that won't heat at all or heats only in patches. Blistered or separated coils mean the element is open and must be replaced. Damaged or burned terminals at the connection point also need replacement, since a poor connection causes arcing and uneven heating. The element itself is a consumable part rated at 2600 watts for typical 8-inch models, with 5 coils spanning the diameter and a universal plug style for easy swap.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **Burner does not heat at all** You turn the control knob and the element stays cold, which means the coil is open or the terminal connection has failed.
- **Burner heats unevenly or only in spots** Part of the element glows red while other sections stay dark, indicating internal coil damage or a poor connection.
- **Visible blistering, separation, or physical breaks in the coil** The element surface shows obvious damage like bubbled metal or a gap in the coil, confirming the element is burned out.
- **Burned, discolored, or loose terminal connections** The plug-in terminal or wire connectors show heat damage, corrosion, or wiggle freely, which prevents reliable power transfer.
- **Element does not sit flat or wobbles in its mounting** The coil tilts or moves when you touch it, often because worn terminals or broken support clips prevent proper seating.
- **Circuit breaker trips when you turn on the burner** A shorted element or damaged wiring at the terminal causes an overload, tripping the breaker each time you use that burner.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Disconnect power to the range at the circuit breaker and let the cooktop cool completely before starting any work.
2. Lift the cooktop if your model uses a hinged top (remove lower screws at the front and prop the top open), or lift the element straight up if it's a plug-in style to access the terminal connections underneath.
3. Take a photo of all wire positions and terminal connections so you can reconnect them exactly as removed.
4. Inspect the element for visible damage such as blistering, separation, or cracks, and inspect the terminals for heat damage, looseness, or corrosion.
5. Disconnect the wire terminals or unplug the element from the terminal block (you may need to release mounting clips or brackets that secure the element in place).
6. If your element has a hot-cooktop indicator wire or thermostat attachment, disconnect it carefully and note its position for reinstallation on the new element.
7. Replace any burned or worn terminal connectors on the wiring harness or terminal block before installing the new element.
8. Install the new surface element by reconnecting all wires to the correct terminals, seating the element firmly in its mounting clips, and securing any brackets or screws.
9. Lower the cooktop back into position (if raised), restore power at the breaker, and test the burner at each heat setting to confirm even heating and stable mounting.

## When to Call a Pro

If you see arcing, heavy smoke, or damage to the cooktop wiring harness or terminal block beyond the element connection itself, call a pro to inspect and repair safely. If you're not comfortable working with 240-volt appliance wiring or if your cooktop requires disassembly beyond lifting the top (such as removing control panels or built-in glass surfaces), a technician can handle the replacement and verify all connections are secure. Also call a pro if replacing the element and terminals does not restore normal heating, since the fault may be in the infinite switch, control board, or internal wiring that requires diagnostic testing and parts access. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
