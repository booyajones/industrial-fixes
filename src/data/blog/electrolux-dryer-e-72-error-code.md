## What this code means
E72 on an Electrolux dryer is an outlet thermistor (NTC sensor) out-of-range fault. Electrolux IQ-Touch error references group E71 and E72 together as "outlet thermistor is out of range." The control board is reading a temperature signal from the drying-air outlet sensor that is either too high, too low, or missing entirely.

This code points to a problem in the outlet temperature sensing circuit. Either the thermistor probe itself has failed, the wiring harness between the sensor and the control is damaged or disconnected, or a connector has backed out or corroded. The sensor is an NTC (negative temperature coefficient) resistor that changes resistance with temperature. When the control sees a resistance value that does not match any plausible temperature, it throws E72 and stops the cycle.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first, assuming an electronics fault. Always test the outlet thermistor resistance with a multimeter at room temperature (should read about 5,000 ohms at 70°F) and inspect the harness for breaks or loose connectors before spending money on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed outlet thermistor (~55%)** The NTC probe itself has drifted out of range, shorted, or opened internally so the control no longer reads a valid temperature signal.
- **Loose or disconnected wiring harness (~25%)** Wires to the thermistor have backed out of the connector, broken at a crimp, or been pulled loose by vibration.
- **Poor connector fit or corrosion (~15%)** The plug at the thermistor or at the harness terminal has oxidized pins or does not seat fully, causing intermittent contact.
- **Damaged wiring (~5%)** The harness itself is chafed, pinched, or cut, creating an open or short in the sensor circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dryer before opening any panels or touching any wiring.
2. **Remove the dryer's rear or top panel** to access the outlet air path and locate the outlet thermistor (a small probe mounted near the outlet duct or on the blower housing).
3. **Inspect the thermistor and its harness** for loose connectors, broken wires, chafing, or corrosion. Reseat any loose plugs and repair damaged wiring.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** and use a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) to measure across the thermistor terminals at room temperature (approximately 70°F).
5. **Compare the reading to about 5,000 ohms**. If the reading is significantly different, open, or shorted (zero ohms), replace the outlet thermistor.
6. **Reconnect all wiring**, reassemble the panels, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify that the E72 code does not return.
7. **If the code persists** after replacing the sensor and verifying good wiring, suspect a control board fault and consult a qualified technician for further diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with a multimeter, if you cannot safely access the rear or top of the dryer, or if the E72 code returns after you have replaced the outlet thermistor and verified all wiring is intact. A technician can perform board-level diagnostics and check for faults in the control circuitry that are beyond typical DIY scope. Also call a pro if the dryer shares a complex venting or stacking configuration that makes disassembly difficult.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220.
