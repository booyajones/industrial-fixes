## Weil-McLain A27 Error — What It Means

The A27 code is not explicitly documented in widely available Weil-McLain literature. However, the closely related E27 fault is reported as 'Outlet Water Too Hot / Short Cycling,' triggered when the outlet sensor reads over 200°F. This indicates the boiler believes the outlet water temperature is dangerously high, often due to restricted flow or a faulty sensor. If your display shows A27, consult your model's manual or wiring diagram to confirm whether it maps to the same outlet overheat condition.

The boiler shuts down to prevent damage from overheating. Common triggers include a blocked strainer, a failed circulator pump that cannot move water through the heat exchanger, restricted piping, or a bad outlet temperature sensor sending false high readings.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many techs replace the outlet sensor first, but most A27/E27 faults stem from flow restrictions. Clean the strainer and verify the circulator is running before ordering a sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked strainer (~35%)** Debris in the strainer restricts water flow through the heat exchanger, causing rapid temperature rise and overheat shutdowns.
- **Circulator pump not running or failing (~30%)** If the pump does not move water, the boiler outlet overheats within seconds of firing.
- **Faulty outlet temperature sensor (~20%)** A bad sensor sends falsely high readings to the control board even when actual water temperature is safe.
- **Restricted or blocked piping (~10%)** Scale buildup, closed valves, or kinked flex connectors downstream reduce flow and trap heat at the outlet.
- **Air lock in the system (~5%)** Trapped air prevents proper circulation, leading to localized overheating at the outlet sensor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut down the boiler** at the master switch or thermostat and allow it to cool for at least 15 minutes.
2. **Locate and remove the strainer** on the inlet or return line (consult your model's manual for exact location) and inspect for sediment, rust, or debris.
3. **Clean the strainer screen** under running water or with a brush, then reinstall it with new gaskets if supplied.
4. **Check the circulator pump** by listening for its motor hum and feeling for vibration when the boiler calls for heat; if silent, verify wiring and power at the pump terminals.
5. **Measure the outlet pipe temperature** with an infrared thermometer or by touch (if safe) immediately after the boiler fires to confirm whether the sensor reading is accurate.
6. **Test the outlet sensor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance with a multimeter; compare the reading to your model's temperature-resistance table (consult the manual if no table is in the research).
7. **Reset the boiler** at the control panel, restore power, and observe the next firing cycle to confirm the fault does not return; if it does, replace the sensor or call a licensed technician for further diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician if you cannot locate the strainer, if the circulator pump shows power but does not run, or if cleaning the strainer and verifying flow does not clear the fault. Gas-fired boilers require a qualified pro for any work involving gas piping, pressure switches, or combustion diagnostics. A technician can also measure sensor resistance against factory specs, test the control board, and diagnose blockages deep in the heat exchanger that are not accessible to a homeowner. If the boiler is under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
