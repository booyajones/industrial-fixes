## State Water Heater E118 Error Code — What It Means

The E118 error code most commonly indicates low water pressure in the heating system. This fault appears when the internal pressure drops below the minimum operating threshold required for safe ignition and heat transfer. The controller locks out the burner to prevent damage from dry firing or overheating.

Because State Water Heaters route diagnostic information by model and serial number via the rating plate QR code, confirm the exact meaning of E118 for your specific unit by consulting your installation manual or the manufacturer's tech support line. The causes and repair steps below reflect the most widely reported definition of this code across similar heating appliances.

## Common Causes

- **System leak** Slow leaks at pipe joints, valves, or the tank itself allow water to escape and pressure to drop over time.
- **Recent radiator bleeding** Releasing air from radiators without refilling the system removes water volume and lowers overall pressure.
- **Faulty pressure relief valve** A stuck-open or weeping relief valve continuously vents water and prevents the system from maintaining target pressure.
- **Defective expansion vessel** A ruptured diaphragm or loss of air charge in the expansion tank causes pressure swings and repeated faults.
- **Failed pressure sensor** The transducer or switch that monitors system pressure may read incorrectly even when actual pressure is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify current system pressure** by reading the gauge on the front of the unit (usually a dial or digital display) and note whether it sits below the cold fill range marked by the manufacturer.
2. **Inspect visible piping and fittings** for drips, corrosion stains, or pooled water that indicate an active leak requiring repair before refilling.
3. **Locate the filling loop** (typically a flexible hose with one or two isolation valves beneath or beside the unit) and make sure both ends are firmly connected.
4. **Repressurize the system slowly** by opening the filling-loop valve(s) while watching the gauge until pressure reaches the cold target specified on the rating plate or in your manual, then close the valve immediately.
5. **Reset the error** by pressing the reset button or cycling power, then observe whether the code clears and the unit fires normally.
6. **Monitor pressure over 24 hours** to confirm it holds steady, repeat the visual leak check if pressure drops again, and test the relief valve by lifting the test lever briefly to make sure it reseats cleanly.
7. **Contact State technical support** with your model and serial number if the code persists after repressurization or if you cannot locate a filling loop on your specific water heater model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or heating technician if you cannot find a filling loop on your unit, if pressure will not rise despite opening the fill valve, if you discover an active leak you cannot isolate, or if the E118 fault returns immediately after repressurization. A pro will pressure-test the system, replace faulty sensors or relief valves with the correct OEM parts, and verify that your expansion vessel is properly charged. Because State Water Heaters tie diagnostics to specific model documentation, professional access to the manufacturer's service literature ensures you get the right repair the first time.
