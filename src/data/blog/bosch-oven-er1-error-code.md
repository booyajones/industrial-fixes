## Bosch Oven Er1 Error Code — What It Means

The Er1 code on a Bosch oven signals a temperature sensor failure. The electronic control is not receiving a correct signal from the oven temperature sensor (thermistor). This sensor monitors cavity temperature and tells the control board when to cycle the heating elements. When the sensor or its wiring fails, the oven cannot regulate heat and the Er1 fault appears.

Bosch's official U.S. error-code documentation does not list Er1 specifically, so the meaning comes from third-party Bosch repair references. Technicians report that Er1 consistently points to the oven temperature sensor, its harness, or the control board's sensor input circuit. The oven will not heat normally until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Always test the sensor resistance (should be around 1,080 Ω at room temperature) and inspect the wiring harness before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~55%)** The thermistor probe in the oven cavity drifts out of range, reads open, or shorts internally so the control board cannot measure temperature.
- **Loose, damaged, or pinched sensor wiring (~25%)** The harness connector at the sensor or control board is loose, a wire is pinched behind the oven, or the insulation is damaged causing an open or intermittent connection.
- **Faulty control board sensor input (~15%)** The power board or electronic control has a failed input circuit, cold solder joint at the sensor connector header, or burned component in the sensor signal path.
- **Corroded or loose terminal-block connection (~5%)** A poor connection at the main terminal block or internal connector can introduce resistance that affects sensor readings or control-board behavior.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and verify the oven is de-energized with a non-contact voltage tester.
2. **Access the rear service area** by removing the back panel or lower kick plate to locate the oven temperature sensor and its wiring harness.
3. **Inspect the sensor wiring** for loose connectors, pinched conductors, damaged insulation, or signs of arcing at the plug and control-board header.
4. **Unplug the sensor connector** and measure resistance across the sensor terminals with a multimeter (should read approximately 1,080 Ω at room temperature).
5. **Replace the temperature sensor** if resistance is out of range, reads open, or fluctuates unstably.
6. **Inspect the control board** if sensor and wiring test good: look for burned components, cold solder joints at the sensor connector (especially the P24 header on the power board), or damaged traces.
7. **Replace the power or control board** if the sensor circuit is confirmed good but the Er1 code persists, then restore power and verify the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working around 240-volt appliance wiring, if the oven requires disassembly beyond removing the back panel, or if you have replaced the sensor and inspected the wiring but the Er1 code returns. A pro has the tools to perform board-level diagnosis, check control-board solder joints under magnification, and verify supply voltage at the terminal block. Professional service is also recommended if the oven shares a junction box with other hard-wired appliances or if local codes require a licensed electrician for any appliance repair work.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
