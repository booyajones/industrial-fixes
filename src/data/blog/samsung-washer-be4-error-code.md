## Samsung Washer bE4 Error Code — What It Means

Samsung washers do not use a single universal bE4 code across all models. On Samsung's published error-code tables, E4 corresponds to an unbalance fault (the load is distributed unevenly in the drum during spin) on some models, while 4E or 4C indicates a water-supply problem (the machine cannot fill properly) on other models. Because Samsung explicitly warns that codes can vary by model, you must check your exact model's manual or service documentation to confirm whether the displayed code points to a balance issue or a water-inlet issue before you begin troubleshooting.

For water-supply faults (4E/4C family), Samsung identifies blocked or kinked inlet hoses and closed or partially open taps as the most common causes. For unbalance faults (UE/E4/UB family), the typical cause is an uneven distribution of heavy absorbent items like towels or blankets that shift to one side of the tub during the spin cycle.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the water inlet valve before checking the mesh inlet filters or confirming the supply taps are fully open. Clean the filters and verify full tap flow before ordering a new valve.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or blocked inlet hose (~35%)** A bend or obstruction in the cold- or hot-water hose restricts flow and triggers a 4E/4C water-supply fault on many models.
- **Closed or partially open tap (~25%)** If the household shut-off valve at the wall is not turned fully open, inlet pressure falls below the required 0.5–8 bar range.
- **Clogged inlet mesh filters (~20%)** Fine screens inside the threaded hose fittings at the washer trap sediment and rust, reducing flow to the inlet valve.
- **Uneven load distribution (~10%)** Heavy towels or a single bulky item shifting to one side of the drum during spin causes an unbalance (UE/E4/UB) code on models where E4 maps to balance faults.
- **Blocked detergent drawer or dispenser path (~5%)** Hardened detergent or mineral buildup in the dispenser housing can interrupt water flow into the tub and set a 4E/4C code.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~5%)** If hoses, filters, and taps all test good, the inlet valve solenoids or seals may be defective and require replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the exact model and code family.** Look up your model number in the Samsung owner's manual or service documentation to confirm whether the displayed code is the unbalance family (UE/E4/UB) or the water-supply family (4E/4C).
2. **Power off and unplug the washer.** Turn off both hot and cold water taps at the wall for safety before working on hoses or filters.
3. **Inspect the inlet hoses.** Trace each hose from the tap to the washer and look for kinks, sharp bends, or pinched sections. Straighten any bends and make sure hoses hang in smooth loops.
4. **Turn the supply taps fully open.** Confirm both valves are rotated counterclockwise all the way. Samsung requires inlet pressure between 0.5 bar and 8 bar for reliable filling.
5. **Remove and clean the inlet mesh filters.** Unscrew both hoses from the back of the washer. Use needle-nose pliers to gently pull the small screen filters from each inlet port and rinse them under running water to remove sediment.
6. **Check and clean the detergent drawer.** Pull the dispenser drawer out and inspect the housing and siphon caps for hardened detergent or mineral deposits. Rinse all parts and reassemble.
7. **Reconnect hoses, restore power, and test.** Tighten the hose fittings by hand (do not overtighten), turn the taps back on, plug in the washer, and run a rinse-and-spin cycle. If the code persists, test the inlet valve with a multimeter or call a technician to replace the valve and inspect control-board signals.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have confirmed the taps are fully open, the hoses are straight and undamaged, and the mesh filters are clean, yet the code still appears. At that point the inlet valve itself or the wiring and control-board signals to the valve are likely faulty, and a technician will need a multimeter and service documentation to isolate the defective component. Also call a pro if your model's documentation shows the code is related to a mechanical balance issue but redistributing the load and reducing bulky items does not clear the fault, since that may point to suspension, shock absorber, or tub-bearing problems that require disassembly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $100–200.
