## Samsung Washer bE4 Error Code — What It Means

The bE4 code is not consistently documented as a standalone error across Samsung's public support pages, but it falls within the water-supply fault family commonly labeled 4E or 4C. These codes mean the washer is not receiving water at the expected rate or pressure. On some Samsung models, E4 or UE codes can indicate an unbalance issue instead, so you should verify the exact meaning against your model's service manual or on-board code list.

In most cases, bE4 points to a blockage, closed tap, low household water pressure, or a clogged inlet filter preventing the machine from filling correctly. Samsung specifies that inlet water pressure must fall between 0.5 bar and 8 bar for proper operation. The washer will not advance through the cycle until the fill requirement is satisfied.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or partially closed water supply tap** One or both supply valves at the wall are not fully open, restricting flow to the washer.
- **Kinked, bent, or crushed inlet hose** The supply hose is pinched behind the machine or damaged, blocking water flow.
- **Clogged mesh inlet filter screens** The small mesh filters at the hose connection points on the back of the washer are blocked with sediment or debris.
- **Low household water pressure** Incoming supply pressure is below Samsung's required 0.5 bar minimum, often due to plumbing issues or shared supply lines.
- **Blocked or dirty detergent dispenser drawer** Old detergent residue or foreign objects in the dispenser can restrict the water path and trigger a fill fault on some models.
- **Faulty water inlet valve or loose wiring** The valve coils are open or the electrical harness is disconnected, preventing the valve from opening even when commanded by the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code meaning** by checking the model-specific manual or on-board code list, since Samsung notes that error codes can vary by model.
2. **Confirm both water supply taps** at the wall are turned fully counterclockwise to the open position and that household pressure is within 0.5 to 8 bar.
3. **Inspect the inlet hoses** for kinks, bends, or crush damage along their entire length, and straighten or replace any damaged sections.
4. **Clean the mesh inlet filters** by shutting off the water, disconnecting the hoses at the back of the washer, using pliers to pull the small screens from each inlet port, rinsing them under running water, reinstalling them, and reconnecting the hoses with a leak check.
5. **Remove and clean the dispenser drawer** to clear any detergent buildup or blockages that may restrict water flow into the drum.
6. **Power cycle the washer** by unplugging it for 60 seconds, then run a rinse or short cycle to test whether the error clears.
7. **Test the water inlet valve** electrically if the error persists, checking for continuity across each coil and verifying that the control board delivers the correct voltage signal when a fill is commanded.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the error returns after you have verified full supply pressure, cleaned all inlet filters, inspected hoses, and cleared the dispenser drawer. Persistent bE4 or 4E codes usually mean a failed inlet valve, a broken harness connector, or a control-board output fault that requires live voltage measurement and part-level diagnosis. Because Samsung's code definitions can vary by model, a technician with access to the service manual and schematic for your exact model number will save time and prevent unnecessary part replacement.
