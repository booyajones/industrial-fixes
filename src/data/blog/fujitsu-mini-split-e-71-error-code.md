## Fujitsu E:71 Error Code — What It Means

The E:71 error on a Fujitsu mini-split indicates an outdoor unit discharge temperature thermistor fault. The outdoor unit's control board is not receiving a valid temperature signal from the sensor mounted on the compressor discharge pipe. Without this signal, the board cannot properly protect the compressor or control the refrigeration cycle, so it shuts down and displays the fault.

In practical terms, the thermistor itself has failed, the wiring harness is damaged, the connector is loose or corroded, or the outdoor PCB input circuit is defective. The discharge temperature sensor is a small resistance-based probe that changes resistance as temperature changes. When the circuit is open, shorted, or reads outside normal range, the board flags E:71.

## Common Causes

- **Failed discharge temperature thermistor** The sensor itself has burned out, gone open-circuit, or drifted out of specification due to age or heat exposure.
- **Loose or corroded connector** The plug between the sensor lead and the outdoor board has backed out, corroded, or has damaged pins that break continuity.
- **Damaged sensor wiring harness** The wire between the thermistor and the outdoor PCB has been cut, pinched, rubbed through, or chewed by pests.
- **Contaminated or oxidized terminals** Moisture, dust, or oil inside the connector housing causes intermittent or high-resistance contact that the board reads as a fault.
- **Outdoor main PCB input fault** The thermistor and wiring test good but the control board's thermistor input circuit is damaged and cannot read the sensor correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker or outdoor disconnect and wait two minutes to make sure capacitors discharge and the board resets.
2. **Confirm the displayed code** is exactly E:71 and write down the full alphanumeric string, because Fujitsu uses subcodes and model families can show similar codes with different meanings.
3. **Inspect the outdoor unit wiring harness and thermistor connector** for looseness, corrosion, bent pins, rubbing against sheet metal, or pest damage, then reseat the connector firmly.
4. **Measure the discharge thermistor resistance** at the sensor leads and again at the board-side harness plug to determine whether the sensor or wiring is open or shorted, then compare your readings to the temperature-resistance table in your model's service manual.
5. **Check continuity from sensor to PCB** if the thermistor resistance is in range, to rule out a break in the harness or connector.
6. **Replace the discharge temperature thermistor** if its resistance is out of specification, the sensor body is cracked, or the leads are broken.
7. **Replace the outdoor main control board** only after you have confirmed the sensor and wiring both test good and the board input still reads incorrectly or shows no continuity to ground.
8. **Restore power, run the system, and monitor** for several minutes to confirm the E:71 code does not return and the outdoor fan and compressor operate normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside energized equipment, do not own a multimeter, or cannot locate the discharge thermistor on the outdoor unit. Refrigerant-side components and live voltage are present. A qualified technician will have the exact service manual, resistance tables, and replacement thermistors for your model, and can rule out compressor or refrigerant-circuit issues that may mimic sensor faults. If the error returns after you replace the sensor or harness, the outdoor board likely needs professional diagnosis and replacement.
