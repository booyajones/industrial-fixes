## Navien E321 Error Code — What It Means

The E321 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a water flow issue that prevents the unit from operating correctly. The heater has detected insufficient flow through the heat exchanger or problems with components that measure or control water movement. This code is the system's way of protecting itself from running dry or overheating when water flow conditions are not met.

The exact definition of E321 can vary slightly between Navien model series, so consult your owner's manual for precise details. In most cases the code relates to flow sensor readings, inlet screen blockages, or valve position problems that interrupt the flow path during a call for hot water.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor when the real problem is debris blocking the inlet filters. Clean both hot and cold side inlet screens before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screens (~50%)** Sediment and mineral deposits collect in the fine mesh filters installed at the cold and hot water service valves, restricting flow below the minimum threshold.
- **Flow sensor failure (~20%)** The turbine-style flow sensor can become jammed with debris or its magnet and Hall-effect sensor can fail, giving false flow readings to the control board.
- **Partially closed isolation valves (~15%)** The service valves on the water inlet and outlet lines may not be fully open, reducing flow rate and triggering the error.
- **Low incoming water pressure (~10%)** Municipal supply pressure below the minimum required or a failing pressure-reducing valve upstream can starve the heater of adequate flow.
- **Clogged heat exchanger (~5%)** Hard water scale buildup inside the heat exchanger passages restricts flow and triggers protection modes even when filters are clean.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you see debris or discoloration on the inlet filter screens after removing them?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Clean or replace the screens, reassemble, and test the unit. Most E321 codes clear after this step.<br><strong>No:</strong> Proceed to check that both isolation valves are fully open and that incoming water pressure is adequate.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error appear only during low-flow demand, like a single faucet, but not during high-flow use?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The flow sensor may be reading correctly but the demand is below the heater's minimum activation flow rate. This is normal and not a fault.<br><strong>No:</strong> The flow sensor or heat exchanger may be faulty. Continue diagnostics or call a technician for flow sensor testing.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Did the error start shortly after installing a new water softener or filter upstream?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The new equipment may be restricting flow. Check its bypass valve and verify it is sized correctly for the heater's flow requirements.<br><strong>No:</strong> Focus on the heater's own filters, valves, and flow sensor as the likely cause.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the cold water isolation valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit according to your model's instructions, typically by loosening screws at the bottom edge.
3. **Locate the inlet filter screens** on the cold and hot water service valves, usually at the bottom of the unit where water lines connect.
4. **Unscrew the filter caps** using an adjustable wrench and pull out the small cylindrical mesh screens inside each valve body.
5. **Rinse the screens** under running tap water and use a soft brush to remove sediment, scale, or debris, then inspect for tears or damage.
6. **Reinstall the clean screens** into the valve bodies, replace the caps, and tighten them snugly but do not overtighten.
7. **Open the cold water isolation valve** slowly, check for leaks at the filter caps, then restore power and test the heater by opening a hot water tap.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien service technician if cleaning the inlet filters and confirming valve positions does not clear the E321 code. A professional can test the flow sensor with a multimeter, measure incoming water pressure accurately, and inspect the heat exchanger for internal scale that requires a chemical flush or descaling service. If the flow sensor has failed, a technician can replace it and verify correct operation with diagnostic software. Persistent E321 errors despite clean filters often point to heat exchanger fouling, which needs professional descaling equipment and experience to avoid damaging the copper fins.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
