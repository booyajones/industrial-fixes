## Weil-McLain Boiler A156 Error Code — What It Means

The A156 code does not appear in widely available Weil-McLain service documentation for Aqua Balance or standard control modules. Error codes on Weil-McLain boilers vary by control version and model generation. Without your specific model number and control manual, the exact meaning of A156 cannot be confirmed. Many A-series codes on Aqua Balance controls relate to ignition lockout, flame sensing, or gas-valve faults. For example, A01 indicates no burner ignition, often caused by lack of gas supply, a dirty or misaligned ignition electrode, a failed gas valve, or a blocked condensate trap.

Until you match A156 to your exact model's documentation or wiring diagram, treat it as a potential ignition or safety-lockout fault. The most reliable next step is to record the model and serial number from the boiler nameplate, consult the control module's technical manual, and check the fault-history log if your display supports it. Common root causes across Weil-McLain ignition codes include gas not purged on new installations, ignition or flame-sensing electrodes fouled or cracked, low gas supply pressure (Aqua Balance units specify 3.5 to 11 inches water column), condensate trap blockage or double-trap piping, and dirty heat-exchanger surfaces.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or ignition module before verifying that gas is turned on, the line is purged, and the condensate trap is clear. Always confirm gas supply pressure and inspect electrodes for carbon or cracks before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply not present or not purged (~30%)** On new installations or after service the gas line may still contain air, or the manual shutoff may be closed or only partially open.
- **Ignition or flame-sensing electrode dirty or misaligned (~25%)** Carbon buildup, cracks, or incorrect gap between the electrode and burner prevent spark or flame detection.
- **Gas valve failure (~20%)** The valve may not open even when the control signals it, blocking gas flow to the burner.
- **Blocked or double-trapped condensate drain (~15%)** A clogged trap or improper looped piping causes back-pressure that trips a safety switch and prevents ignition.
- **Low gas supply pressure (~10%)** Inlet pressure below 3.5 inches water column starves the burner and prevents stable ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the full model and serial number** from the boiler nameplate and locate the control module's technical manual to confirm what A156 means for your exact unit.
2. **Press the reset button** on the control module for 1.5 seconds and initiate a call for heat by raising the thermostat.
3. **Watch and listen for igniter spark** at the burner during the ignition sequence. If you see spark but no flame, gas supply or the gas valve is the issue.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve is fully open and, if the boiler is new or was recently serviced, purge air from the gas line at the union upstream of the valve.
5. **Measure inlet gas pressure** with a manometer at the test port on the gas valve. Aqua Balance controls require 3.5 to 11 inches water column.
6. **Remove and inspect the ignition and flame-sensing electrodes** for carbon deposits, cracks, or incorrect gap. Clean with fine emery cloth and verify the gap matches the installation manual.
7. **Remove the condensate trap** and check for standing water or debris. Rinse it, confirm the drain line has no loops or double traps, and reinstall.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician whenever you smell gas, cannot locate gas supply pressure test ports, or lack a manometer and combustion-analysis tools. Gas-fired boilers require precise combustion tuning, and incorrect electrode gaps or gas-valve adjustments create carbon monoxide risk. A technician will verify supply and manifold pressure, inspect the heat exchanger for fouling or cracks, download the control's fault history, test ignition transformer output voltage, and confirm proper condensate drainage and venting. Professional diagnosis is also necessary if you have replaced electrodes and verified gas supply but the code persists, since the fault may lie in the control module, a failed pressure switch, or blocked venting.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–400.
