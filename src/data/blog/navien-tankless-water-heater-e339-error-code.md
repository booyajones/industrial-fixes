## Navien Tankless Water Heater E339 Error — What It Means

The E339 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a system fault detected by the control board. The exact meaning can vary by model and firmware version, so consult your owner's manual or the service label inside the unit for the specific definition. In general, this code points to an internal communication error, a sensor issue, or a control board malfunction that prevents normal operation. The unit will typically shut down or limit heating until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board immediately, but many E339 faults are resolved by checking wiring harness connections, resetting the unit, or replacing a single sensor. Verify all connectors are seated and check for voltage at sensors before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Temporary control board glitch (~35%)** A power surge or brief voltage drop can cause the board to log a false fault that clears with a reset.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~25%)** Vibration or moisture can unseat connectors between the control board and sensors, triggering a communication fault.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~20%)** An inlet or outlet thermistor may send erratic signals or fail open, causing the board to halt operation.
- **Control board failure (~15%)** Internal component failure on the main PCB can produce persistent E339 errors that do not clear with resets.
- **Low voltage or power supply issue (~5%)** Inadequate line voltage or a weak transformer can cause intermittent faults as the board resets or misbehaves.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait 60 seconds to fully reset the control board and clear any temporary fault.
2. **Turn power back on** and observe whether the E339 error reappears immediately or after a heating cycle.
3. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to access the control board and wiring harnesses.
4. **Inspect all connectors** at the control board, checking that each plug is fully seated and pins show no corrosion or moisture.
5. **Check the service menu** (consult your model's manual for button sequence) to view error code history and note any additional codes.
6. **Test thermistor resistance** at the inlet and outlet sensors using a multimeter. Compare readings to the table in your service manual.
7. **Replace the faulty sensor** if resistance is out of range, or replace the control board if all sensors test good and the error persists after resets and wiring checks.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with 120V wiring, if multiple error codes appear together suggesting a complex fault, or if the unit still throws E339 after you have verified all connectors and tested sensors. A technician can use factory diagnostic software to read detailed fault logs and check line voltage, gas valve operation, and grounding that are difficult to test without specialized equipment. Also call a pro if the unit is still under warranty, since self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts if a sensor or wiring is needed, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-400.
