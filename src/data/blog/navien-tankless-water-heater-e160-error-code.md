## Navien E160 Error Code — What It Means

The E160 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a combustion or air supply fault. The unit has detected an issue preventing proper burner operation, typically related to insufficient airflow, venting blockage, or a fan problem. The heater will shut down to prevent unsafe operation until the fault is corrected.

Because tankless heaters rely on precise air-fuel ratios and continuous venting, any restriction in the intake or exhaust path can trigger this code. The control board monitors combustion performance and will fault out if it detects abnormal conditions. The exact meaning and troubleshooting steps can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual for model-specific guidance.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the combustion fan motor before checking the intake filter and vent pipes. Clean the filter and inspect all venting for blockages first, which solves most E160 faults at no cost.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or clogged air intake filter (~45%)** The intake filter restricts combustion air when it accumulates dust and lint, starving the burner and triggering the fault.
- **Blocked or restricted vent pipes (~25%)** Exhaust or intake vent pipes can be blocked by debris, ice, bird nests, or improper installation, preventing proper airflow.
- **Failed or weak combustion fan (~15%)** The fan may run slowly or fail to reach correct speed due to bearing wear, winding faults, or control board signal loss.
- **Air pressure switch stuck or faulty (~10%)** The pressure switch confirms proper fan operation and venting, and can stick closed or fail to close when airflow is present.
- **Condensate drain blockage (~5%)** A clogged condensate drain can cause back pressure that affects combustion chamber airflow and triggers fault codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the service switch or circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Locate and remove the air intake filter** from the front or bottom of the unit, following your model's service instructions.
3. **Wash the filter** under running water to remove dust and lint, then shake off excess water and let it air-dry completely before reinstalling.
4. **Inspect the vent terminations** outside your home for both intake and exhaust pipes, and clear any debris, leaves, ice, or obstructions.
5. **Check the condensate drain line** for standing water or blockage, and flush it with clean water if accessible.
6. **Reinstall the clean, dry filter** and make sure it seats properly in its housing.
7. **Restore power and gas** to the unit, then run a hot water tap to call for heat and observe whether the error clears and the burner fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if the error persists after cleaning the filter and clearing the vents, if you hear unusual noises from the combustion fan, or if you lack confidence working with gas appliances. A pro can test the fan motor speed, check the air pressure switch with a meter, inspect internal venting and the heat exchanger for blockages, and verify correct combustion readings with a flue gas analyzer. Because tankless heaters involve both gas and electrical components, professional diagnosis is often the safest and most efficient path when simple cleaning does not resolve the fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
