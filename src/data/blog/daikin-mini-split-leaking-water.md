## Daikin Mini Split Leaking Water — What's Happening

A Daikin mini split leaking water is a symptom of a drainage or condensate problem, not a fault code. Daikin recommends stopping use immediately, collecting the water, and checking whether water is discharging from the outdoor drain hose termination point. If water is not draining outside, the problem is usually an obstruction or bend in the drain hose or drainage path. If water is draining but the indoor unit still leaks, Daikin points to dirty air filters and restricted airflow that increase condensate formation and can cause overflow or icing at the evaporator coil.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged condensate drain line** Dirt, mold, algae, or debris blocks the drain hose and causes water to back up and overflow from the indoor unit.
- **Dirty air filters or restricted airflow** Blocked filters starve the evaporator of airflow, increasing condensate production and contributing to coil icing and leakage.
- **Obstructions, bends, or kinks in the drain hose** Daikin identifies bends and obstructions in the drain hose as a common cause of drainage malfunction and water backup.
- **Improper installation or unit not level** If the indoor unit is tilted incorrectly, condensate may not flow to the drain outlet and can spill inside or out the front.
- **Frozen evaporator coil** Low refrigerant charge or poor airflow can freeze the coil, and when it thaws, excess water can overwhelm the drain pan.
- **Cracked or disconnected drain pan or hose** Physical damage or loose connections allow water to leak directly from the indoor unit instead of draining away.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Shut off the mini split system immediately to prevent further water damage and stop any active icing.
2. Verify whether water is actually exiting the outdoor drain hose termination point outside the building.
3. If no water is draining, inspect the drain hose from indoor unit to outlet for obstructions, bends, kinks, or disconnections.
4. Remove and clean or replace the indoor air filters, then check for any blocked return grilles or supply obstructions nearby.
5. Vacuum the condensate drain line from the outdoor outlet side to remove sludge, algae, or debris, then flush the line and confirm free flow.
6. Inspect the indoor drain pan for cracks, overflow marks, or standing water, and confirm the unit is pitched correctly toward the drain outlet.
7. If the evaporator coil is frozen or shows signs of recent thaw, clean filters and verify airflow first, then call a technician to check refrigerant charge if icing persists.
8. Test the system in cooling mode and confirm steady condensate drainage with no leakage at the indoor unit and no backflow at the termination point.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the leak continues after cleaning filters and clearing the drain line, if the evaporator coil is frozen and does not resolve with airflow correction, or if you suspect low refrigerant charge or installation pitch problems. A technician should handle refrigerant diagnostics, drain pan replacement inside a sealed indoor unit, and any re-leveling or remounting of the wall-hung assembly. If the condensate pump has failed or the drain system requires disassembly beyond the external hose, professional service is recommended.
