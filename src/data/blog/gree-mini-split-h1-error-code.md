## Gree H1 Error Code — What It Means

H1 is a status code indicating your Gree mini-split is in defrost mode during heating operation. The outdoor unit is removing frost or ice buildup from the heat exchanger. In most cases this is completely normal. The code should clear automatically after a few minutes once the defrost cycle completes and the unit resumes heating.

If H1 stays on indefinitely or appears frequently, the unit may have a problem with the outdoor coil, the defrost control sensor, or restricted airflow. Extended or repeated defrost behavior points to an issue that prevents normal operation and requires troubleshooting.

## Common Causes

- **Normal defrost cycle during heating** The outdoor coil has accumulated frost or ice and the system is performing its routine defrost operation, which is expected behavior in heating mode.
- **Dirty or blocked outdoor heat exchanger** Contamination, leaves, or debris on the outdoor coil reduce airflow and cause excessive frost buildup, triggering more frequent or prolonged defrost cycles.
- **Faulty defrost or outdoor temperature sensor** The control sensor or temperature sensor that monitors the outdoor coil may be out of range, disconnected, or damaged, preventing proper defrost timing.
- **Restricted airflow around the outdoor unit** Blocked fan intake, obstructed discharge, or a failing outdoor fan motor reduce heat exchange and lead to ice accumulation on the coil.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring** Poor contact at the sensor connector or corroded terminals can disrupt the signal to the control board and cause abnormal defrost behavior.
- **Control board defrost logic failure** If the main board's defrost circuit is defective, it may hold the unit in H1 mode even when conditions do not require defrosting.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Wait 5–10 minutes** and observe whether H1 clears on its own. The defrost cycle is usually automatic and temporary, so a disappearing code means normal operation.
2. **Inspect the outdoor unit** for frost, ice, or visible debris on the heat exchanger fins. Look for blocked fan grilles, leaves, or dirt that restrict airflow.
3. **Clean the outdoor coil** with a soft brush or coil cleaner if you see contamination. Turn off power at the disconnect first, then rinse gently from the inside out to avoid bending fins.
4. **Check the outdoor fan** to confirm it spins freely and runs when the unit is powered. Listen for unusual noise or vibration that suggests a motor or bearing problem.
5. **Locate the outdoor temperature sensor** near the coil and verify the connector is seated firmly. Look for corrosion, pinched wire, or physical damage to the sensor body.
6. **Power down the system** at the breaker and disconnect, then power back up after 60 seconds. If H1 persists immediately without frost present, consult your model's service manual for sensor resistance values and test points.
7. **Replace the defrost sensor or control board** if testing shows an open circuit, short, or out-of-range reading. Use the exact Gree replacement part number for your outdoor model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if H1 does not clear after cleaning the coil and checking the sensor, if the outdoor fan does not run, or if you see refrigerant ice on the line set rather than just frost on the coil. Defrost sensor testing requires a multimeter and model-specific resistance tables. Any work involving refrigerant recovery, coil replacement, or control board diagnosis should be handled by a professional with EPA certification and Gree service training.
