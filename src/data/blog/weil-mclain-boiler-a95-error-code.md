## Weil-McLain A95 Error Code — What It Means

The A95 code is not clearly documented in the Weil-McLain manufacturer materials available for all boiler models, so its exact meaning varies by control type and model series. Weil-McLain uses A-codes to indicate lockout or fault conditions on many models, and the fault history is accessed through the control's diagnostics menu. Without your specific model documentation, you cannot reliably determine what A95 represents.

Because the code is not universally defined, your first step is to consult your boiler's owner's manual or wiring diagram to confirm what A95 means for your control board. Many Weil-McLain lockout codes relate to ignition, flame detection, gas supply, or venting problems, but guessing the cause without the manufacturer definition risks wasting time and money on the wrong repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the ignitor or control board without first checking gas supply pressure or cleaning the flame rod. Measure gas pressure and inspect the flame sensor before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Code meaning varies by model (~100%)** A95 is not defined in available Weil-McLain manufacturer documentation, so the exact fault depends on your control board and boiler series.
- **Dirty or contaminated flame rod** If A95 turns out to be an ignition or flame-detection fault, soot or corrosion on the flame sensor prevents the control from confirming ignition.
- **Insufficient gas supply pressure** Low pressure (outside the 3.5 to 11 inch w.c. range documented for some models) stops ignition even when spark is present.
- **Gas valve failure** A stuck or electrically failed gas valve will prevent gas flow to the burner despite a valid call for heat.
- **Faulty ignition electrode or control board** A cracked ignitor or failed control output stops spark generation and triggers a lockout after repeated ignition attempts.
- **Venting or combustion-air problem** Blocked intake or exhaust, reversed polarity on intake/exhaust pipes, or insufficient combustion air can cause repeated ignition failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control type** by reading the rating plate on the boiler jacket and noting the control-board part number.
2. **Access the diagnostics or fault-history menu** on the control display by pressing and holding the menu or enter button (consult your manual for the exact sequence).
3. **Cross-reference A95** in your owner's manual or service manual fault-code table to confirm what the code means for your model.
4. **Reset the boiler** only after you have identified and corrected the underlying fault condition, not before, to avoid masking a recurring problem.
5. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve is fully open, the gas line has been purged of air, and supply pressure measures between 3.5 and 11 inches water column at the boiler inlet.
6. **Inspect and clean the flame rod or ignition electrode** by removing it from the burner assembly, sanding lightly with fine-grit sandpaper to remove soot or oxidation, and reinstalling securely.
7. **Call a licensed technician** if the code persists after reset, if you cannot find the code definition in your manual, or if gas-system work is required.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician immediately if you smell gas, if the boiler repeatedly locks out after reset, or if you cannot locate the A95 definition in your model's documentation. Gas-system repairs, including measuring supply pressure, testing or replacing the gas valve, and diagnosing control-board outputs, require specialized tools and training. A technician will use the boiler's fault-history log, measure voltages at the ignitor and flame rod, and verify combustion settings to pinpoint the root cause. Because A95 is not a standardized code across all Weil-McLain models, professional diagnosis is the fastest path to a safe, accurate repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
