## Samsung Washer OE Error Code — What It Means

The OE code on a Samsung washer signals an overflow or overfill condition. The control has detected that the tub is filling abnormally, either because the washer cannot sense the correct water level or because incoming water cannot be stopped in time. This code is tied to fill control and pressure sensing, meaning the machine believes too much water is present or the level-reading circuit has failed.

Some sources also describe OE as an abnormality in the drainage process, since water that is not removed properly can confuse the fill and drain logic. The root issue is usually a mechanical or electrical fault in the fill valve, the water-level sensor, or the drain path that prevents the control from managing tub water correctly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when OE appears. First unplug the washer and watch whether water continues to enter the tub, which points directly to a stuck inlet valve rather than the board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck-open or leaking inlet valve (~40%)** The hot or cold fill valve fails to close mechanically and lets water continue entering the tub even when the control commands it off.
- **Faulty water-level sensor or pressure switch (~30%)** The sensor cannot read tub level correctly, often because the pressure hose is blocked, split, disconnected, or the switch itself has failed.
- **Blocked drain filter or pump (~15%)** Debris in the filter housing or impeller prevents water from leaving the tub, leaving residual water that confuses the fill and drain logic.
- **Kinked or improperly installed drain hose (~10%)** A crushed, clogged, or incorrectly routed drain hose restricts discharge and can cause the control to detect abnormal water level.
- **Oversudsing or external plumbing issues (~5%)** Excessive detergent foam or backflow from house plumbing can interfere with level sensing, though this is less common than valve and sensor faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves to make the machine safe before opening any service panels.
2. **Remove and clean the drain filter** at the front lower access panel, checking for coins, lint, or other debris that can block water removal.
3. **Inspect the drain pump impeller** by removing the filter housing or accessing the pump from behind, clearing any obstructions that prevent drainage.
4. **Check the drain hose** for kinks, clogs, or improper installation, ensuring it is not inserted too far into the standpipe and is routed within the recommended height range.
5. **Test the inlet valve** by leaving the washer unplugged and observing whether water continues to enter the tub; if it does, identify the hot or cold side by temperature and replace that valve assembly.
6. **Inspect the pressure hose and water-level sensor** by tracing the small tube from the tub to the sensor, looking for splits, blockage, or disconnection, and checking the sensor wiring for damage.
7. **Reassemble and run a test cycle** after any repair, confirming that the washer fills and drains normally and that the OE code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with water connections, removing panels, or testing electrical components. A technician should also handle the repair if you have cleaned the drain path, replaced the inlet valve, and checked the pressure hose yet the code persists, since that pattern may indicate a control-board fault or a wiring harness issue that requires diagnostic tools and schematics. If the washer is still under warranty, contact Samsung service before attempting repairs to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280.
