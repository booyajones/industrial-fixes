## Whirlpool Oven F5 E1 Error — What It Means

F5 E1 on a Whirlpool oven or range is a door-lock or latch-failure code. The control board expects the door latch to lock or unlock (usually during self-clean) and to report its position through switches, but the feedback does not match the command. The code appears when the latch motor or solenoid fails to move, when position switches give inconsistent readings, or when wiring between the latch and control board is open or shorted.

The fault can originate in the latch motor or solenoid itself, the latch position switches, mechanical binding in the latch rod or gear train, damaged wiring or connectors, or a failed control board that drives or reads the latch circuit. The code does not indicate a heating element problem. It is strictly a door-lock circuit issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Always test the latch motor for continuity and check the mechanical linkage for binding before condemning the board.

## Common Causes

- **Defective latch motor or solenoid (~40%)** The motor or solenoid that drives the lock rod fails electrically or mechanically and cannot move the latch when commanded.
- **Mechanical binding in latch assembly (~25%)** The latch rod, gear train, or linkage binds or misaligns so the motor cannot complete travel even if it runs.
- **Failed latch position switch (~20%)** The switch that tells the control board whether the door is locked or unlocked sticks, fails open, or gives inconsistent feedback.
- **Open, shorted, or loose wiring or connectors (~10%)** Wires between the latch assembly and control board break, short, or pull out of terminals due to heat or vibration.
- **Failed control board or sister board (~5%)** The board that sends voltage to the latch motor or reads the switch feedback has failed relay or circuit damage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range and wait three minutes for the control board to clear stored faults.
2. **Restore power** and press CLEAN or enter the service diagnostic mode (consult your model's service sheet) to command the latch and watch whether the display feedback changes.
3. **Inspect the latch area** with power off again. Remove the oven door if needed and check the latch rod, linkage, and gear train for grease, debris, or bent parts that bind the mechanism.
4. **Test the latch motor or solenoid** by disconnecting it from the harness and measuring resistance across its terminals. One field reading on a Whirlpool model showed about 57 ohms, but consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected value.
5. **Check latch position switches** by actuating them manually and testing continuity with a meter. The switches should change state cleanly when the latch moves between locked and unlocked.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness and connectors** from the latch assembly to the control board for broken wires, pushed-out pins, heat damage, or corrosion. Repair or replace damaged sections.
7. **Replace the latch motor assembly** if the motor tests open, the solenoid does not actuate, or the switches are faulty. Rerun the latch diagnostic to confirm the code clears and the latch cycles normally.
8. **Replace the control board or sister board** only if the latch assembly, switches, and wiring all test good but the code persists and the latch does not receive voltage during a commanded cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are not comfortable working inside the oven cabinet or back panel with 240-volt power present, if the wiring harness shows widespread heat damage that requires re-routing, or if you have replaced the latch motor and checked all wiring but the code still returns. A technician can use service diagnostics to isolate whether the control board latch-drive circuit has failed and can safely test high-voltage connections. Also call if the mechanical latch assembly is integrated into the oven door frame and requires special disassembly tools or alignment procedures.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
