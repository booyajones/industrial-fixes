## Maytag Range F3E0 Error Code — What It Means

The F3E0 code on a Maytag range indicates the oven control is detecting an abnormal signal from the oven temperature sensor circuit. The sensor (also called a sensor probe or RTD) may be open, shorted, or not being read correctly by the control board.

Whirlpool's published service guidance for F3 E0 identifies three potential problem areas: the oven temperature sensor itself, the electronic control board, or the wiring and connectors that link them. The fault can appear if the sensor fails, if wiring becomes damaged or corroded, or if the control board develops a poor solder joint or circuit failure in the sensor input path.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, which costs $150–350, when a simple multimeter check of the sensor resistance (should be about 1080 ohms at room temperature) or inspection of the connector terminals would identify a failed sensor or loose wire for under $50.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~55%)** The sensor probe drifts out of spec, shorts internally, or develops an open element and no longer delivers a valid resistance signal to the control.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring and connector terminals (~25%)** Heat, vibration, or moisture causes the connector between the sensor and control board to corrode, loosen, or burn, creating an intermittent or open circuit.
- **Electronic control board failure (~15%)** A poor solder joint, failed relay, or damaged trace in the sensor input circuit on the control board causes the board to misread or ignore the sensor signal.
- **Broken or chafed wiring harness (~5%)** Wires running behind the oven cavity or near sharp edges become pinched, cut, or melted, breaking continuity in the sensor circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power at the circuit breaker** for at least 1 minute, then restore power and note whether the F3E0 code returns immediately or only after the oven heats.
2. **Pull the range away from the wall** (turn off gas if applicable) and remove the rear access panel or lower kick panel to expose the oven temperature sensor and its wiring.
3. **Unplug the sensor connector** from the control board or junction and inspect both the plug and receptacle for corrosion, burned pins, or loose terminals.
4. **Measure sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature the sensor should read approximately 1080 ohms. Record the reading.
5. **Trace the sensor wiring** from the probe through the harness to the control, looking for pinched, chafed, melted, or broken wires. Repair or replace any damaged sections.
6. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if resistance is far from 1080 ohms or if the sensor shows physical damage. Mount the new probe in the same cavity location and reconnect the harness.
7. **Reassemble the range**, restore power, and run a test bake cycle. If the F3E0 code reappears and wiring plus sensor tested good, replace the electronic control board per the model's service sheet.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if the range is hard-wired or requires moving a gas connection you cannot safely disconnect, or if you have replaced both the sensor and inspected all wiring but the F3E0 code still returns. A technician can perform live voltage checks at the control board, verify sensor circuit continuity under load, and determine whether a control board has a failed input channel. Professional diagnosis typically costs $100–150 and saves the expense of replacing parts by trial and error.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25–60 in parts, 20–40 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
