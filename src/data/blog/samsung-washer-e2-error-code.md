## Samsung Washer E2 Error Code — What It Means

On Samsung washers, E2 is not a single universal fault. Samsung's U.S. support identifies E2 (along with bE2, 6E2, BE2, and 8E2) as a jammed button error on applicable models, meaning a button on the control panel is detected as stuck or continuously pressed. Samsung's Philippine support lists E2 (grouped with 5E and 5C) as a drain error on front-load washers, meaning the washer cannot drain properly.

Because of that model variation, the correct meaning depends on the exact washer model and the service documentation for that model. If your washer displays E2 alongside other symptoms like standing water, it is likely the drain version. If it appears after touching the panel or if buttons feel sticky, it is likely the jammed-button version. Check your owner's manual or the Samsung support site for your specific model to confirm which definition applies.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the control board or drain pump without first cleaning the debris filter or freeing stuck buttons. Power off, work each button back and forth, and clean the filter before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or continuously pressed panel button (~40%)** Samsung U.S. support says a button detected as stuck or continuously pressed triggers E2, often from water splashes, detergent residue, or mechanical wear on the key switch.
- **Clogged drain filter or debris trap (~35%)** Samsung Philippines support lists drain-error E2 caused by foreign material blocking the filter, preventing proper drainage.
- **Damaged drain pump impeller (~10%)** Samsung lists a damaged pump impeller as a cause of the drain-error E2, where the impeller is jammed by debris or broken blades.
- **Drain hose clogged or kinked (~8%)** Samsung lists a drain hose clogged with foreign material or improperly routed as a cause of the drain-error E2.
- **Wrong voltage supplied to parts (220 V to 110 V) (~5%)** Samsung explicitly notes that incorrect voltage can trigger the drain-error condition, though this is rare in properly installed machines.
- **Water pump terminal not connected (~2%)** Samsung identifies a loose or disconnected pump wiring terminal as a cause of the drain-error E2, interrupting pump operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the washer** at the wall outlet or breaker and unplug the machine.
2. **Test every control panel button** by individually touching and working each button back and forth to free all edges before releasing it, following Samsung's U.S. instructions for the jammed-button version.
3. **Open the debris filter access door** (usually a small panel at the lower front) and place a shallow pan underneath to catch residual water.
4. **Unscrew and remove the drain filter** (turn counterclockwise on most models) and clean out any coins, lint, hair, or foreign material, then rinse the filter under running water.
5. **Check the drain hose** at the rear of the washer for kinks, clogs, or excessive insertion into the drain pipe (Samsung specifies no more than 6 inches).
6. **Inspect the drain pump impeller** by reaching into the filter housing with a flashlight and rotating the impeller blades by hand to confirm they spin freely and are not broken.
7. **Reinstall the filter** by screwing it clockwise until snug, close the access door, plug in the washer, and run a short drain or spin cycle to verify the code is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the code persists after you have cleaned the filter, freed all buttons, and confirmed the drain hose is clear and properly routed. A pro can test the drain pump motor, verify correct voltage at the pump terminal, and replace a damaged control panel button assembly or pump impeller. Also call if you see water leaking from the filter housing after reinstalling the filter, or if the washer will not power on at all after the error appears. Samsung says service is required if any button is damaged or the code returns after restarting, and a qualified technician can diagnose control board or wiring faults that a homeowner cannot safely test.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (filter cleaning free, button assembly or drain pump if needed), 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
