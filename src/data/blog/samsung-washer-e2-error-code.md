## What this code means
Samsung's E2 code is model-dependent. On many front-load washers Samsung lists E2 (also shown as 5E or 5C) as a drain error, indicating the washer cannot empty water properly. On some other models Samsung lists E2 (also bE2, 6E2, BE2, or 8E2) as a jammed-button error, meaning a button is stuck or continuously pressed. Because Samsung uses the same code differently across models, you must check your exact model number and its error chart to confirm which variant applies to your machine.

For the drain-error variant, typical causes include a clogged debris filter, kinked or improperly installed drain hose, a damaged pump impeller, or a loose pump terminal. For the jammed-button variant, the cause is a physically stuck button, contaminated control-panel switch, or damaged button assembly. External installation issues such as incorrect hose routing or wrong power supply can also trigger either fault, so Samsung explicitly advises verifying hose and electrical installation before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the drain pump when the real problem is a clogged debris filter or a kinked hose. Clean the filter and inspect the hose routing first before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged debris filter (~35%)** Lint, coins, and small objects block the filter and prevent proper drainage, triggering the error on drain-fault models.
- **Kinked or improperly installed drain hose (~25%)** A bent, crushed, or incorrectly routed hose stops water flow, and Samsung warns against inserting the hose too far into the drain pipe.
- **Damaged or binding drain pump impeller (~15%)** A cracked impeller or debris jammed in the pump housing prevents the pump from moving water out of the tub.
- **Loose or disconnected pump terminal (~10%)** A loose wiring connector at the pump means no power reaches the motor, so the pump will not run.
- **Stuck or jammed button (jammed-button variant) (~10%)** A physically stuck key or contaminated control-panel switch is detected as continuously pressed, triggering the error on button-fault models.
- **Wrong voltage or power supply fault (~5%)** Samsung notes that incorrect supply voltage or wiring can cause component faults that register as E2.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm your exact model number** and look up the E2 code definition in your owner's manual or Samsung's support site, because E2 means different things on different Samsung washers.
2. **Clean the debris filter** by opening the small access panel at the front lower corner, unscrewing the filter, removing lint and objects, rinsing it, and reinstalling it.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** from the back of the washer to the standpipe or drain pipe. Straighten any kinks, remove clogs, and verify the hose is inserted properly and not more than 6 inches into the drain pipe.
4. **Check the drain pump and impeller** by accessing the pump housing (usually behind the debris filter or lower rear panel). Spin the impeller by hand to confirm it turns freely and is not cracked or jammed with debris.
5. **Verify the pump wiring connector** is fully seated on the pump motor terminals and not loose or corroded.
6. **Test each control-panel button** (if you have the jammed-button variant) by powering off the washer and working every button back and forth to release any stuck key. Restart the washer and see if the error clears.
7. **Run a rinse-and-spin cycle** to confirm the drain system is working. If the error returns, replace the failed component: drain pump for drain-error models, or control panel for jammed-button models.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you have verified the debris filter is clean, the drain hose is clear and properly routed, and the pump impeller spins freely, yet the error persists. A technician can test the pump motor electrically, check for correct supply voltage, and diagnose wiring or control-board faults that are not visible during basic inspection. Also call a pro if the jammed-button error returns after freeing all keys and cycling power, because replacing the control panel or user-interface board often requires disassembly of the front console and connector work that is easier with service documentation and experience.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
