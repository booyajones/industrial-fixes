## Samsung Washer E2 Error Code — What It Means

Samsung uses E2 two different ways depending on the model family, so the first diagnostic step is to confirm the exact model and check the sticker or manual for that washer. On many Samsung front-load washers, E2 is grouped with 5E and 5C as a drain error. The control board detects that the drain pump motor may be faulted, foreign material may be in the drain pump, or the main PCB may be at fault. On some Samsung US washer models, E2 (or bE2 / BE2) indicates a jammed button or continuous key input fault. The control panel is receiving constant input from one or more buttons, as if a button is physically stuck or the touch interface is damaged.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump motor before checking the filter or hose. Always clean the drain filter and inspect the hose for kinks or blockage first, since debris or installation issues cause most drain faults.

## Common Causes

- **Foreign material in the drain pump (~45%)** Lint, coins, hairpins, or other debris block the impeller or filter, preventing the pump from draining water and triggering the fault.
- **Drain hose kinked or clogged (~25%)** The hose is bent, looped, sagging, or blocked, restricting water flow and causing the drain cycle to time out.
- **Drain pump motor fault (~15%)** The pump motor has failed, the impeller is seized, or internal wiring is open, so the pump cannot run.
- **Stuck or jammed button (on models with button-fault E2) (~10%)** A physical button is stuck down or dirt and residue on the panel cause false continuous input to the control board.
- **Main PCB fault (~5%)** The main printed circuit board has failed and incorrectly reports a drain or button fault even when the pump and buttons are working.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and unplug the washer** before beginning any service work to eliminate shock hazard.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** for kinks, bends, loops, sags, or blockage, and verify the hose is connected correctly and the standpipe height is between 18 and 42 inches.
3. **Open the drain filter access door** (usually at the lower front of the washer) and place a shallow pan underneath to catch residual water.
4. **Unscrew and remove the drain filter** and clear out all lint, debris, coins, hairpins, or other foreign material from the filter and the pump housing.
5. **Check the drain pump impeller** by reaching into the filter cavity and rotating the impeller by hand to confirm it spins freely and is not seized or damaged.
6. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** to the drain pump for disconnection, corrosion, or damage, and reseat any loose connections.
7. **For jammed-button faults, gently rock each physical button** to release any that are stuck, and clean the panel surface if residue or dirt is present.
8. **Reinstall the filter, reconnect the hose, and run a test drain or spin cycle** to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared the filter and hose, confirmed the impeller spins freely, and the E2 drain fault persists. The technician will test the drain pump motor for correct resistance and verify the main PCB is sending proper voltage to the pump. Also call a pro if the jammed-button version appears, you have cleaned the panel, and the code returns, since the control panel or user interface board will need bench testing and replacement. Finally, if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or accessing the pump and wiring harness, a qualified appliance technician can complete the diagnosis and repair safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
