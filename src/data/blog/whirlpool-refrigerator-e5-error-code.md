## Whirlpool Refrigerator E5 Error — What It Means

E5 is not a universal code across all Whirlpool refrigerator models. On Whirlpool and KitchenAid French-door platforms with dedicated ice-maker compartments, E5 indicates a temperature discrepancy between the ice maker thermistor (inside the ice maker module) and the ice box or ice room thermistor. The control is seeing one sensor read cold while the other does not match, or one sensor is reporting an out-of-spec value. The exact meaning depends on your model's tech sheet, so confirm the code definition for your platform before replacing parts.

This is not a simple "thermistor is bad" code. It reflects a mismatch or sensing fault in the ice-making system, which can be caused by a failed sensor, wiring issue, or an actual airflow or frost problem that makes the two temperatures disagree. Always check your model documentation to verify the code applies to the ice system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire ice maker assembly or the control board first. Check wiring continuity and measure actual compartment temperature with an external thermometer before replacing expensive components.

## Common Causes

- **Failed ice box or ice room thermistor (~40%)** The temperature sensor in the ice storage compartment has drifted out of spec or failed open, so the control sees a mismatch between it and the ice maker thermistor.
- **Failed ice maker thermistor (~30%)** The thermistor inside the ice maker module itself is reading incorrectly or has an open circuit, causing the control to flag a temperature disagreement.
- **Wiring harness or connector fault (~15%)** A broken wire, corroded pin, or loose connector between the thermistors and the control board creates an open circuit or intermittent signal that triggers the mismatch code.
- **Frost buildup or blocked airflow (~10%)** Ice or frost in the ice compartment or around the evaporator restricts air circulation, creating a real temperature difference that the sensors correctly report as a mismatch.
- **Defrost-related icing (~5%)** Incomplete defrost cycles allow hidden frost to form around sensors or air passages, interfering with accurate temperature sensing and airflow.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** and consult your model's tech sheet or service manual to confirm that E5 on your platform refers to an ice-maker temperature mismatch.
2. **Inspect the ice compartment** for frost, ice buildup, or blocked air vents that could cause a real temperature disagreement between the ice room and ice maker sensors.
3. **Manually defrost** the refrigerator if you see any frost (unplug, remove food, leave doors open for 24 hours) and check whether the code returns after restart.
4. **Locate the ice room thermistor** (usually clipped or mounted inside the ice storage bin or air duct) and the ice maker thermistor (typically inside or attached to the ice maker module).
5. **Disconnect each thermistor connector** and use a multimeter to check resistance; compare readings to your model's specification table (the research here does not provide a universal value, so consult your tech sheet).
6. **Inspect wiring and connectors** for corrosion, breaks, or loose pins between each thermistor and the control board; repair or replace any damaged harness.
7. **Replace the failed thermistor** (ice room thermistor first if uncertain, since it is the most common cause) and reconnect; plug in and monitor for 24 hours to confirm the code does not return.
8. **If the code persists** after replacing both thermistors and verifying wiring, test the control board for correct sensor input reading or consult a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are uncomfortable working behind interior panels, if you cannot locate the thermistors on your model, or if the code returns after you have replaced both sensors and verified all wiring. A technician has model-specific diagnostic software and tech sheets that decode E5 precisely for your platform, and can test control-board sensor inputs directly. Also call if your refrigerator is still under warranty, since self-repair may void coverage on ice-maker components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280.
