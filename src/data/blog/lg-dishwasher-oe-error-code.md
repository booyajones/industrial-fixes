## LG Dishwasher OE Error Code — What It Means

An LG dishwasher OE error means the unit has detected a drainage failure. Specifically, the dishwasher has not fully drained within the expected time and is sensing that water is still remaining in the tub. LG describes this as a drain issue where the machine could not complete its draining cycle properly.

The code itself does not indicate a component failure. Instead, it alerts you to a restriction or blockage preventing water from leaving the tub. This can be anywhere in the drain path, from debris inside the dishwasher to problems in the external plumbing or installation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump assuming a component fault, when most OE errors are solved by simply cleaning the filters and inspecting the drain hose for kinks or clogs. Always clear the drain path before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filters or food debris in the tub (~50%)** Food scraps, glass, or paper labels block the drain path and prevent the pump from evacuating water.
- **Kinked, pinched, or clogged drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose from the dishwasher to the sink or disposal may be bent sharply, crushed under the unit, frozen (in cold climates), or blocked internally.
- **Blocked or clogged air gap (~10%)** If your installation uses a countertop air gap, food debris or mineral buildup can restrict the passage and trap water in the dishwasher.
- **Garbage disposal knockout plug not removed (~10%)** When a dishwasher is connected to a new or replaced disposal, the factory knockout plug must be punched out or water cannot drain into the disposal.
- **Drain hose exceeds maximum length or installation restrictions (~5%)** LG specifies the drain hose should not exceed 12 feet, and improper routing can cause slow or incomplete drainage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dishwasher** at the breaker or by unplugging it before beginning any inspection or repair work.
2. **Open the dishwasher door and inspect the tub** for loose food scraps, broken glass, labels, or any items that could obstruct drainage, and remove them by hand.
3. **Remove and clean the filters** by pulling out the lower spray arm (it usually twists or lifts free), then lifting the cylindrical filter assembly and rinsing it under hot running water to clear all food debris.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** under the sink where it connects to the disposal or drain standpipe, looking for kinks, pinches, ice blockage in cold climates, or internal clogs, and straighten or clear any restrictions you find.
5. **Check the air gap** (if your installation has one on the countertop) by removing the chrome cap and inner cover, then flushing out any debris or buildup in the passage.
6. **Verify the garbage disposal knockout plug** was removed if the dishwasher drain connects to a disposal, especially if the disposal was recently installed or replaced.
7. **Run a drain test cycle** by selecting Cancel & Drain if your model offers it, or start a Heavy Duty or Normal cycle with no dishes or detergent and let the machine run for the first 45 seconds (which is drain-only), then check whether the tub is empty and the OE code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

If you have cleaned the filters, inspected and cleared the drain hose, checked the air gap, confirmed the disposal knockout is removed, and the OE error still returns after a drain test, the problem is likely internal. A technician will need to inspect the drain pump impeller for obstructions, test the pump motor and wiring, and check the discharge tube assembly and internal plumbing for blockages or component failure. Attempting to disassemble the pump or sump without proper tools and diagrams risks water leaks and further damage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-20 in cleaning supplies, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call if pump or internal plumbing must be replaced.
