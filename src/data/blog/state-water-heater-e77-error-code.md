## State Water Heater E77 Error Code — What It Means

State does not publish a universal E77 error code across all water heater models. Error codes vary by model family and platform, so the same number can indicate different faults depending on your specific unit. To decode E77 accurately, you must locate the model and serial number on the rating plate, pull the matching troubleshooting guide from State or A. O. Smith (State's parent company), and confirm whether the fault relates to a sensor, ignition, venting, flow, or control board.

Without the exact model, attempting repairs based on generic E7 or E77 definitions from other brands can lead to misdiagnosis and unnecessary part swaps. State publishes model-specific error-code documentation, and your manual will list the precise checks, target values, and replacement parts for that code on your unit.

## Common Causes

- **Model-specific sensor failure** Temperature, pressure, or flow sensors tied to E77 in your model's literature may drift out of range or fail electrically.
- **Ignition or flame-sensing fault** Some tankless platforms use E7x codes to flag ignition lockouts or flame-rod contamination.
- **Venting or combustion-air issue** Blocked exhaust, insufficient intake air, or a faulty air-pressure switch can trigger error codes in the E7x family on certain models.
- **Control-board software or wiring** Loose harness connections, corroded terminals, or a board glitch can log undefined or spurious codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the rating plate** on the side or front panel of the water heater and write down the complete model number and serial number.
2. **Download the matching manual** from State's support site or scan the QR code on the rating plate to pull the exact troubleshooting guide for your unit.
3. **Look up error code E77** in the fault-code table of that manual to confirm whether it refers to a sensor, ignition component, vent condition, or board fault.
4. **Perform the diagnostic checks** listed in the manual for E77, including resistance or voltage tests on sensors, continuity checks on wiring, and visual inspection of venting or flame components.
5. **Replace only the component** the manual identifies as the root cause, using the part number and procedure in that document.
6. **Clear the error** by cycling power or following the reset sequence in the manual, then monitor the heater through a full heat cycle to verify the fault does not return.
7. **Document the fix** by noting the installed part number and any calibration or adjustment settings in case the code reappears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot find the model-specific manual, if the fault table in your manual requires gas-pressure or combustion-analysis tools you do not own, or if the code persists after replacing the component the manual identifies. Tankless and high-efficiency tank models often need precise venting measurements, gas-valve adjustments, or control-board programming that fall outside typical DIY scope. A pro with State factory training can cross-reference your serial number to pull the correct literature, perform the manufacturer's diagnostic sequence, and source OEM parts quickly.
