## Gree Mini Split F2 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 code on a Gree mini split indicates a temperature sensor malfunction, but the exact sensor varies by model and platform. Gree's general F-code documentation lists F2 as an indoor evaporator coil sensor fault, while some model-specific service manuals map F2 to the condenser or outdoor coil sensor. Because the fault assignment is not universal across all Gree platforms, always confirm which sensor your model assigns to F2 by checking the service manual or the label inside the indoor unit before ordering parts.

When a thermistor fails or its wiring is damaged, the control board cannot read coil temperature and throws F2 to protect the compressor and refrigerant circuit. The system may stop cooling or heating, or cycle on and off erratically. Most F2 faults trace to a shorted, open, or corroded sensor or connector rather than the control board itself.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor** The evaporator or condenser temperature sensor has shorted, opened, or drifted out of specification and no longer delivers a valid resistance signal to the board.
- **Loose or corroded connector** The sensor plug or pin contacts are corroded, backed out, or not fully seated, breaking the electrical path between the sensor and the control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** The thermistor harness is pinched, cut, chafed against sheet metal, or has water intrusion that shorts or opens the circuit.
- **Sensor displaced from coil** The sensor clip or foam securing the thermistor bulb to the coil has come loose, causing the sensor to read ambient air instead of refrigerant temperature.
- **Faulty control board sensor input** If the sensor and wiring both test correctly but F2 persists, the board's sensor-input circuit may be defective and require board replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or disconnect before opening the indoor unit cover or touching any sensor connectors.
2. **Check your model's F2 assignment** by reviewing the service manual or the error-code table printed inside the indoor unit to confirm whether F2 points to the indoor evaporator sensor or the outdoor condenser sensor.
3. **Inspect the sensor and harness** for loose plugs, corroded pins, pinched wires, or physical damage, then reseat all connectors firmly and look for signs of water intrusion or rubbed insulation.
4. **Verify sensor placement** on the coil by confirming that the thermistor bulb is clipped snugly into the coil fins or tube and not hanging free in the airstream.
5. **Measure sensor resistance** with a digital multimeter after unplugging the sensor leads from the board, if resistance reads zero (short) or infinite (open), replace the sensor.
6. **Compare resistance to the thermistor chart** in your model's service documentation (many Gree sensors are NTC 5 kΩ or 10 kΩ at 25°C, but this varies), and replace the sensor if readings fall outside the published range.
7. **Test or replace the control board** if the sensor checks good, wiring is intact, and F2 returns immediately after clearing the code, the board's sensor-input circuit is likely at fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live line voltage, if you cannot locate the sensor or the service manual for your model, or if the fault returns after you have tested the sensor and wiring. A technician has the correct thermistor resistance charts, refrigerant gauges to verify system performance after repair, and the tools to diagnose control-board faults without unnecessary part swaps. If your unit is under warranty, professional documentation of the repair may be required to preserve coverage on the compressor and sealed system.
