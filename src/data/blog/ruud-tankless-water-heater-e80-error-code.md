## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E80 Error — What It Means

The E80 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a flame detection or ignition issue. The unit is either failing to ignite the burner or the flame sensor cannot confirm that a stable flame is present. This safety feature prevents gas from flowing when proper combustion is not detected.

Because error codes can vary between models and firmware versions, consult your specific owner's manual or the diagnostic chart on the inside of the service panel to confirm the exact meaning for your unit. Common triggers include gas supply problems, dirty flame rods, ignition component failure, or venting issues that affect combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or entire circuit board when the real problem is a dirty flame rod that just needs cleaning with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~40%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame sensor prevent it from detecting the flame even when ignition occurs.
- **Insufficient gas supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a closed or partly closed valve, or air in the gas line stops the burner from lighting.
- **Failed igniter or ignition transformer (~15%)** The spark igniter or transformer may be cracked, worn out, or not producing a strong enough spark to light the gas.
- **Flame rod wiring or connector issue (~10%)** Loose, corroded, or broken wiring to the flame sensor prevents the control board from reading the flame signal.
- **Venting or combustion air restriction (~7%)** Blocked exhaust or intake vents can cause incomplete combustion or flame rollout, triggering the sensor to fault.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The main circuit board may have a failed flame-detection circuit, though this is less common than sensor or ignition issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and turn the gas supply valve to the off position.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners to access the burner chamber and flame sensor.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe near the burner, and disconnect the wire connector at its base.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-abrasive cloth to remove carbon deposits, then wipe it dry.
5. **Inspect the igniter** for cracks or heavy carbon buildup and clean it carefully if needed, or replace if damaged.
6. **Check all wiring connections** to the flame rod, igniter, and control board for tightness and corrosion, and reconnect securely.
7. **Reassemble the cover**, turn the gas valve back on, restore power, and run a hot-water tap to test for normal ignition and flame.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if you suspect low gas pressure at the meter, or if cleaning and basic checks do not resolve the error. Also call a pro if the control board appears damaged, if you smell gas at any point, or if the unit has recurring ignition faults that suggest a deeper combustion or venting problem. Professionals have manometers to test gas pressure and combustion analyzers to verify safe operation.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
