## LG Oven F10 Error Code — What It Means

On LG ranges, the F10 error code indicates a Door Locking System Failure in the upper oven. The control board cannot successfully lock or unlock the oven door, or it cannot verify the correct position of the latch. This code typically appears when you attempt to start a self-clean cycle or any function that requires the door to lock, though it can also trigger if the lock mechanism is stuck or the feedback circuit is broken.

Because LG uses different fault definitions across product families, you should confirm the exact meaning for your specific model by checking the wiring diagram or service manual. On most LG double-wall ovens and ranges with self-clean, F10 points to the upper-oven door lock circuit, but always verify your model number before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when F10 appears, but a simple continuity check of the lock motor and switch will show whether the lock mechanism itself has failed and save the cost of an unnecessary board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock motor or actuator (~50%)** The lock motor burns out or the plastic gears inside the actuator strip, preventing the latch from moving through its full travel.
- **Faulty door lock switch or latch position feedback (~25%)** The micro-switch that tells the control whether the door is latched or unlatched breaks or loses contact, so the board cannot confirm lock state.
- **Jammed or binding lock mechanism (~10%)** Grease hardens, debris enters the linkage, or a spring breaks, physically blocking the latch from moving even when the motor tries to drive it.
- **Wiring or connector problems in the lock circuit (~10%)** Loose, corroded, or broken wires between the door lock assembly and the main control interrupt the motor drive or feedback signals.
- **Main control board fault (~5%)** The relay or circuit that drives the lock motor on the PCB fails, so the control cannot energize the lock even when the mechanism is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press CLEAR/OFF** and try to cancel the function. Turn off power at the circuit breaker for two minutes, then restore power and see if the code returns.
2. **Remove the upper oven door** (if your model allows) or the inner door panel to access the lock assembly mounted at the top or side of the door frame.
3. **Inspect the lock mechanism** for mechanical binding, broken linkage, hardened grease, or a motor that does not turn freely. Move the latch by hand to confirm it travels smoothly.
4. **Disconnect the lock assembly harness** and use a multimeter to check continuity across the motor terminals and the lock switch contacts. Compare readings to your model's wiring diagram.
5. **Test the wiring from the lock to the control board** for continuity and inspect all connectors for corrosion, bent pins, or loose contacts.
6. **Replace the door lock motor or actuator assembly** if the motor is open, the switch is faulty, or the mechanism is damaged beyond cleaning.
7. **Reinstall the door panel and door**, restore power, and run a short self-clean test to verify the lock cycles properly and the F10 code does not reappear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable removing the oven door or inner door panel, if you cannot safely disconnect power at the breaker, or if you have tested the lock assembly and wiring and both appear good but the F10 code still appears. A pro can quickly verify whether the control board relay is faulty and can source the correct lock assembly for your model. Also call if the lock is stuck in the locked position and you cannot safely open the door, because forcing it may break the latch or hinge mounts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $60–120 for lock assembly, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call plus parts.
