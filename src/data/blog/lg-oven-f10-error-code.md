## LG Oven F10 Error Code — What It Means

On LG range and oven models, F10 indicates a Door Locking System Failure in the upper oven. The control board is detecting that the upper-oven locking mechanism did not reach or report the expected state during a lock-related operation. The latch may be stuck, the lock motor may have failed, or the position feedback circuit may not be confirming lock or unlock status to the control.

Some third-party appliance articles use F10 to mean a runaway temperature fault, but that definition reflects other brands or model families. Always verify your exact LG model number and consult model-specific service literature before assuming a temperature-related problem. LG's published code list defines F10 as a lock-system failure, not a thermal issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first, but a failed door lock motor or damaged wiring is more common. Check continuity from the lock motor switch to the control board and verify the latch moves freely before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Door lock motor/latch assembly failure (~45%)** The lock motor or latch mechanism is mechanically stuck, stripped, or electrically failed and cannot move or report position to the control board.
- **Lock switch or position feedback fault (~25%)** The switch inside the locking system that tells the control board the latch is locked or unlocked has failed or is out of adjustment.
- **Wiring, connector, or harness damage (~20%)** A loose, corroded, or broken wire in the lock circuit interrupts the motor feed or position signal between the lock assembly and the control board.
- **Main control board fault (~10%)** The electronic control board misreads the lock circuit or fails to drive the lock motor properly, even when the motor and wiring are intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model and code meaning** in your LG owner's manual or service sheet to confirm F10 is a lock-system fault for your exact appliance.
2. **Press CLEAR/OFF** and attempt the original function again, as LG instructs in its published code list.
3. **Turn off power at the breaker** for 2-3 minutes to clear any transient control state, then restore power and test.
4. **Inspect the lock mechanism physically** by opening the oven door and checking that the latch moves freely without binding or obstruction.
5. **Check the lock motor and switch circuit** with a multimeter for continuity from the door-lock motor switch to the control board, and test the motor for proper resistance and operation.
6. **Inspect the harness and connectors** for looseness, corrosion, burned terminals, or broken wires in the lock circuit.
7. **Replace the lock motor/latch assembly** if the motor does not operate or the latch is damaged. If the circuit is intact but the control does not drive or read it correctly, replace the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt circuits or removing the control panel and door panels to access the lock assembly. A technician can safely test voltage at the lock motor terminals, verify position-switch feedback signals, and determine whether the fault is in the motor, wiring, or control board. Professional diagnosis is also recommended if the door remains locked after the error appears and you cannot open it manually, or if you have already replaced the lock assembly and the code persists.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
