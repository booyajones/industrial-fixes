## Whirlpool Dryer No Spin — What It Means

On Whirlpool dryers, a "no spin" fault means the drum does not rotate when you start a cycle, even though the machine may power on and appear otherwise normal. Whirlpool does not present this as one single standardized error code across all dryer models. Instead, the fault is diagnosed by identifying the specific component that prevents drum rotation. Some models may display F03 for intermittent or no drum rotation, but many machines simply fail to spin without showing a code.

The symptom is tied to failures in the door switch, thermal fuse, drive belt, motor, idler pulley, drum rollers, control board, or power supply. Because the dryer's safety interlocks and drive system must all work together, a fault in any one of these parts will stop the drum from turning.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the motor or control board first when the real problem is a broken belt or failed door switch. Always inspect the belt visually and test the door switch with a meter for continuity before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Broken or slipped drive belt (~40%)** The belt wraps around the drum and motor pulley, and a broken or dislodged belt is a frequent no-spin failure on Whirlpool dryers.
- **Door not latched or failed door switch (~25%)** A door that is not fully closed or a door switch with no continuity will prevent the dryer from running at all.
- **Seized drum, bad rollers, or idler pulley issue (~15%)** If the drum is hard to turn by hand, suspect worn drum rollers, a seized bearing, a binding idler pulley, or blower interference.
- **Failed motor (~10%)** A broken motor will not turn the drum even when power and the belt are intact.
- **Blown thermal fuse or cutout (~5%)** An open thermal fuse can interrupt power to the motor circuit on some models, though it is more often associated with no-heat complaints.
- **Control Lock enabled or control board fault (~5%)** Control Lock can prevent the dryer from starting, and a failed electronic control board or timer can stop the motor signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and turn off the circuit breaker before any inspection or repair work.
2. **Check the door latch** by closing the door firmly and listening for the latch click, then open the cabinet and test the door switch for continuity with a multimeter while pressing the switch actuator.
3. **Verify Control Lock is off** by consulting the owner's manual and disabling the lock if the control panel shows the icon or the dryer will not respond to buttons.
4. **Spin the drum by hand** with the dryer unplugged to check for binding, excessive drag, or a seized bearing, and inspect drum rollers and idler pulley for smooth rotation.
5. **Open the cabinet and inspect the drive belt** by removing the front panel or top, looking for a broken, cracked, or off-track belt, and verifying correct routing around the drum, idler pulley, and motor pulley.
6. **Test the thermal fuse for continuity** by disconnecting one terminal and using a multimeter, replacing the fuse if it is open and inspecting the vent system for blockages that caused overheating.
7. **Check for proper 240-volt power** at the outlet with a voltmeter if the dryer will not power on at all, and confirm both hot legs are live and the cord is wired correctly.
8. **Replace the failed part** such as the belt, door switch, thermal fuse, idler pulley, drum rollers, or motor, following the service manual routing and installation diagrams for your model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the drum is severely seized and you cannot identify the binding point, if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt power or testing electrical components with a meter, or if you have replaced the belt, door switch, and thermal fuse and the dryer still will not spin. A pro can safely diagnose motor winding failures, control board output signals, and wiring faults that require schematic tracing. Also call for help if the cabinet disassembly is beyond your comfort level or if the dryer is still under warranty and you want to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
