## Amana Washer F7 E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F7 E1 code on an Amana front-load washer signals a motor speed sensing error. The control board is not receiving a valid speed signal from the basket, or the basket is not reaching or maintaining the speed the cycle expects. This is a drive-system and speed-sensing problem, not a water-fill issue.

Amana states this code can be triggered by shipping bolts that were never removed after delivery or a move. The manufacturer also recommends a simple power reset as a first step, since the fault can sometimes be cleared by a control reboot.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Check and reset all wiring connectors between the control and drive system, and test the shift actuator before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed shift actuator or tachometer assembly (~40%)** The speed-sensing component on Whirlpool-built platforms commonly fails and cannot send a valid speed signal to the control board.
- **Shipping bolts or spacers still installed (~25%)** Four shipping bolts at the rear of a new or recently moved washer prevent the basket from spinning freely and trigger the speed fault.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wiring and connectors (~20%)** Pins in the harness between the main control and drive components can corrode or back out, breaking the speed-signal circuit.
- **Mechanical drag or failure in the gearcase or basket drive (~10%)** Bearing failure, a jammed foreign object, or internal gearcase damage can prevent the basket from reaching commanded speed.
- **Control board interpretation issue or power interruption (~5%)** A transient power spike or board glitch can set the fault even when the drive system is healthy, and a reset often clears it.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait at least one minute to reset the control board, then restore power and retry the cycle.
2. **Check the rear panel** for shipping bolts and plastic spacers. If you find four large bolts, remove all of them and the spacers, then test the washer.
3. **Enter service diagnostics** using the button sequence in your model's tech sheet (typically hidden behind the control panel or in the drain-pump access) and confirm the F7 E1 fault is logged.
4. **Inspect all wiring connectors** between the main control board and the drive motor, shift actuator, and tachometer. Look for loose pins, corrosion, or damaged insulation. Reseat every connector firmly.
5. **Locate and test the shift actuator or tachometer assembly** (a small motor or sensor mounted on the drive system). Check for physical damage, loose mounting screws, or disconnected wires. Replace if faulty.
6. **Rotate the drum by hand** through several full turns. If you feel binding, grinding, or heavy resistance, inspect the basket bearings and gearcase for mechanical failure.
7. **Replace the failed component** (actuator, harness, or gearcase), then run any calibration or initialization cycle specified in the service manual so the control relearns motor operation. Clear stored fault codes and perform a full wash-and-spin test cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable unplugging the washer, removing panels, or handling connectors near the control board. Also call if the basket will not turn by hand and you suspect bearing or gearcase failure, since those repairs require specialized pullers and presses. A technician can run the full service-diagnostic suite, verify the exact failure with live meter readings, and perform the calibration cycle that many actuator and drive-component replacements require. If you have already replaced the shift actuator and all wiring is secure but the code persists, the issue may be a failed main control board or motor, and a pro with the correct test equipment can isolate the fault without guessing.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-180 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
