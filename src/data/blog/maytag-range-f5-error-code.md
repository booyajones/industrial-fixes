## Maytag Range F5 Error — What It Means

The F5 fault on a Maytag range is a door-latch or door-lock detection problem. The control board is not seeing the expected door-lock state from the latch and switch circuit. In plain terms, the oven cannot confirm that the door is properly locked or unlocked when it expects to. This is not a heating code. The fault points to the oven door latch assembly, door latch switches, or the wiring connecting them to the control.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Check the latch assembly and switches for binding, misalignment, or electrical failure before ordering the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven door latch assembly or latch motor (~45%)** The latch mechanism binds, the motor stalls, or the assembly does not move the switches to the correct position.
- **Defective door latch switches (~25%)** The switches that report latch position fail or give intermittent signals to the control board.
- **Wiring damage or loose connections (~15%)** Conductors between the latch assembly and the control are pinched, broken, or have poor contact.
- **Control board fault (~15%)** The board cannot interpret the latch circuit correctly even when the switches and wiring test good.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault was a transient glitch. Monitor the oven and call a technician if it returns.<br><strong>No:</strong> The latch assembly, switches, or wiring has a persistent fault. Continue diagnosis.</div>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the door latch move freely by hand and seat fully when closed?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The mechanical side is probably fine. Test the latch switches and wiring next.<br><strong>No:</strong> The latch assembly is binding or misaligned. Replace or realign the latch assembly.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you hear or see the latch motor attempt to move when you start a self-clean cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor is getting power. Inspect the switches and wiring for the fault.<br><strong>No:</strong> The latch motor is likely failed or the control board is not sending voltage. Replace the latch assembly or test the board.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker for one minute, then restore power and observe the oven for one minute to see if the F5 code returns.
2. **Inspect the door latch** for binding, bent rods, or foreign objects that prevent the latch from moving freely or seating correctly.
3. **Check the wiring** from the latch assembly to the control board for pinched, broken, or loose connectors.
4. **Test the latch switches** electrically by disconnecting power, removing the latch assembly, and using a multimeter to confirm continuity changes as you manually move the latch through its travel.
5. **Replace the door latch assembly** if it binds, the motor does not run, or the switches do not change state correctly.
6. **Evaluate the control board** if the latch assembly and wiring test good but the F5 code persists.
7. **Restore power** and run a test cycle to verify the fault is cleared and the door locks and unlocks as expected.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with live voltage or removing the oven control panel and back panel to access the latch assembly and wiring. A technician can test the latch circuit with a meter, verify that the control board is sending the correct signals, and replace the assembly or board safely. If the fault returns after you replace the latch assembly, the control board is the next likely suspect and a pro can confirm that diagnosis before you invest in the more expensive part.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
