## Maytag Range F5 Error — What It Means

The F5 error code on a Maytag range or wall oven signals that the electronic control has detected a problem with the oven door latch or lock system. The control cannot reliably determine whether the door is locked or unlocked because the latch assembly, latch switches, or associated wiring is not behaving as expected.

This code is specific to the mechanical and electrical components that lock the oven door during a self-clean cycle. When any part of that latch feedback loop fails, the control throws F5 and typically disables oven operation until the fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners immediately replace the main control board when they see F5. Instead, inspect and test the door latch assembly, latch switches, and wiring harness first, which fixes the code in the majority of cases at a fraction of the cost.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door latch assembly (~50%)** The motor, gears, or mechanism inside the latch unit wear out or bind, preventing the latch from moving fully or at all.
- **Stuck or open door latch switches (~25%)** Micro-switches that report latch position to the control become stuck, misaligned, or fail open, breaking the feedback signal.
- **Loose or damaged wiring connector (~15%)** The harness plug between the control and the latch assembly vibrates loose or corrodes, interrupting communication.
- **Control board latch-circuit fault (~10%)** Less commonly, the control board's latch driver or sense circuit fails, even when the latch hardware is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker for the range and wait one full minute to reset the control board.
2. **Restore power** and watch the display for one minute. If F5 does not reappear immediately, monitor the oven over the next day to confirm the code is gone.
3. **If F5 returns**, unplug the range or shut off the breaker again and pull the unit away from the wall to access the rear panel or top.
4. **Locate the oven door latch assembly** (usually mounted on the oven front frame near the door hinge or top edge) and trace its wiring harness back to the control board.
5. **Inspect the wiring connectors** at both the latch assembly and the control board for looseness, corrosion, or burned pins. Reseat or clean any suspect connectors.
6. **Check the latch assembly** for binding by manually pressing the latch actuator arm (if accessible). It should move smoothly and the micro-switches should click when the arm reaches each limit.
7. **Replace the door latch assembly** if it binds, does not move, or the switches do not click. If the latch moves freely and switches click properly but F5 persists, suspect the control board and call a technician for further diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you have replaced the door latch assembly and all wiring connectors are clean and tight, yet F5 still appears. At that point the control board latch circuit is the remaining suspect and requires board-level diagnostics or replacement. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet or tracing live control wiring, or if your model requires disassembly of the oven door or front panel that you do not feel confident performing.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 service call and parts.
