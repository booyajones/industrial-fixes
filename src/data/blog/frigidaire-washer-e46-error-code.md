## What this code means
E46 on a Frigidaire washer indicates a problem with the controller or electronic control board. According to Frigidaire's published error-code documentation, this fault points to a failure in the main control circuit that manages the washer's cycles, motor commands, and sensor inputs. The code is distinct from E4, which signals an out-of-balance condition during spin on top-loading models. E46 is strictly an electronic control fault, not a mechanical or load-distribution issue.

In some cases, E46 may appear alongside E47 (a door-lock open-circuit fault during spin), suggesting that control-board failures can also affect door-lock communication or related circuits. The manufacturer's remedy for persistent E46 faults is replacement of the control board after basic reset attempts have failed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Do not confuse E46 with E4. E4 is an imbalance error fixed by redistributing the load or checking suspension parts. E46 is a controller fault that almost always requires a new control board, not shocks or load adjustments.

## Common Causes

- **Failed electronic control board (~70%)** The main control board has failed due to power surges, component wear, heat, or moisture intrusion, and the Frigidaire error-code chart lists board replacement as the documented remedy for E46.
- **Loose or corroded control-board connector (~15%)** A loose wire harness plug at the control board or corrosion on the connector pins can interrupt communication and trigger a controller-fault code.
- **Moisture or heat damage to the control-board circuit (~10%)** Water spray, steam, or heat near the control-board enclosure can damage solder joints or short traces, causing intermittent or permanent E46 faults.
- **Coexisting door-lock circuit fault (E47) (~5%)** In some reported cases E46 has appeared together with E47, indicating the control board's door-lock driver circuit or the lock assembly itself may also be involved.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait two minutes to allow capacitors to discharge and the control board to reset.
2. **Plug the washer back in** and enter diagnostic mode (consult your model's service manual for the key sequence) to confirm the fault code is E46, not E4 or another error.
3. **Remove the top or rear panel** to access the electronic control board, typically mounted near the top of the cabinet or behind the control console.
4. **Inspect all wire-harness connectors** at the control board for looseness, corrosion, or bent pins, and reseat each connector firmly.
5. **Examine the control board** for visible heat damage, burned components, or moisture intrusion; if any are present, the board must be replaced.
6. **Replace the electronic control board** with a manufacturer-approved part for your model number, transferring connectors one at a time to avoid wiring errors.
7. **Reassemble the cabinet**, restore power, and run a complete wash cycle to verify that E46 does not reappear and all functions operate correctly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V AC wiring, if your washer is still under warranty (DIY board replacement may void coverage), or if E46 persists after you have replaced the control board and verified all harness connections. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the motor-control circuit, door-lock driver, and power-supply rails to rule out secondary faults that may have damaged the new board. Also call a pro if E46 appears together with multiple other error codes, since that pattern can indicate a deeper electrical fault in the main harness or grounding system.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $100–250 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $200–400.
