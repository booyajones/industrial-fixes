## Mitsubishi Mini Split P9 Error — What It Means

P9 on Mitsubishi Electric Mr. Slim and mini-split systems signals a TH5 pipe thermistor error. The TH5 sensor monitors pipe temperature (evaporator, gas line, or condenser pipe depending on your model). When the control board sees the sensor as open, shorted, disconnected, or reading outside its valid range, it throws P9 and may stop the system to protect components.

This code is specific to Mitsubishi Electric. Always check your model's service manual rather than generic mini-split guides because P9 can mean different things on other brands. The fault almost always points to the sensor circuit itself, not a refrigerant or compressor problem, though some models may also flag P9 if refrigerant flow issues prevent the sensor from seeing normal pipe temperatures.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or disconnected sensor connector** The most common cause is a TH5 connector that has vibrated loose, corroded, or never seated properly at the indoor or outdoor board.
- **Failed TH5 thermistor** The sensor itself can fail open or shorted, putting its resistance far outside the normal range for the current temperature.
- **Damaged wiring or harness** Pinched, cut, or rodent-damaged wires between the sensor and control board break the circuit and trigger the fault.
- **Faulty control board** If the thermistor tests correct but the board still reports P9, the indoor or outdoor PCB may have a bad sensor input circuit.
- **Refrigerant flow or stop-valve issue** On some models, low refrigerant or closed service valves can cause abnormal pipe temps that push the sensor reading out of range, indirectly triggering P9.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker and wait 30 seconds to reset the board and make sure safe work on sensor connectors.
2. **Locate the TH5 pipe sensor** on your model (check the service manual for exact placement on the indoor coil, outdoor coil, or refrigerant line) and inspect its connector for looseness, corrosion, or bent pins.
3. **Unplug the sensor connector** and measure resistance across the thermistor leads with a multimeter, then compare the reading to the resistance-versus-temperature chart in your model's service manual.
4. **If the sensor reads open (infinite resistance), shorted (near zero), or far outside the chart**, replace the TH5 thermistor with the exact Mitsubishi part number for your unit.
5. **If the sensor resistance is normal**, reconnect it firmly, restore power, and observe whether P9 returns. If the code persists with a good sensor, the control board input circuit is suspect.
6. **Check service valves and refrigerant charge** if your model's troubleshooting tree links P9 to refrigerant flow (consult the manual's fault-code flowchart).
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to confirm the error clears and the system operates normally. Document the reading and repair for future reference.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live 240 V power, if you lack a multimeter and the model-specific service manual, or if the sensor tests normal but the code will not clear. Refrigerant work (checking charge, recovering, and recharging) requires EPA 608 certification and manifold gauges. Mitsubishi systems often have model-specific fault trees and board configurations, so an experienced tech with Mitsubishi training will diagnose faster and avoid misdiagnosis across similar-looking error codes.
