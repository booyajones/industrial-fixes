## Kenmore Dryer F30 Error Code — What It Means

On Whirlpool-built Kenmore dryers, F30 indicates restricted airflow. The dryer senses that exhaust air is not moving freely through the lint screen, vent hose, or house ductwork. The code can appear while the dryer continues to run, and it can be cleared by touching any key. The most frequent culprits are a clogged lint screen, packed lint in the lint-screen housing, or a crushed, kinked, or blocked exhaust hose between the dryer and the wall.

Because some Kenmore appliances are built by different manufacturers, the exact meaning of F30 depends on the dryer's OEM platform and model family. Electrolux-built Kenmore appliances use F30 for a temperature-sensor fault, so always confirm your dryer's model prefix before diagnosing. For Whirlpool-platform dryers, F30 is an airflow restriction warning, not a failure of heating or electrical parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the heating element or thermostats when F30 appears, thinking the dryer will not heat. F30 is an airflow code, not a heating fault. Inspect and clean the entire vent path before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged lint screen or housing (~45%)** Lint accumulates on the screen and inside the lint-screen chute, reducing airflow below the threshold that triggers F30.
- **Crushed or kinked exhaust hose (~25%)** The flexible transition duct between the dryer and the wall is pinched, kinked, or crushed, blocking air flow.
- **Blocked house vent ducting (~15%)** Lint buildup inside the rigid vent run or at elbows restricts exhaust and causes the airflow fault.
- **Obstructed exterior vent hood (~10%)** The outside termination flap is stuck closed or packed with lint, preventing air from escaping.
- **Vent run exceeds recommended length or elbow count (~5%)** An overly long or complex duct installation with too many turns reduces airflow below acceptable limits.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the code** by pressing any button on the control panel. The dryer may continue running while F30 is displayed.
2. **Remove and clean the lint screen.** Wash it with warm soapy water if fabric-softener residue has clogged the mesh, then dry it completely.
3. **Inspect the lint-screen housing.** Use a vacuum or dryer-vent brush to remove packed lint from the chute behind the screen.
4. **Check the transition hose** at the rear of the dryer. Look for kinks, crushing, or disconnection, and straighten or replace the hose if damaged.
5. **Inspect the full vent run** from the dryer to the outside termination. Remove the duct at each joint and clean out lint buildup.
6. **Check the exterior vent hood.** Confirm the flap opens freely and is not clogged with lint or blocked by debris.
7. **Run a heated timed-dry test** after correcting airflow issues. If F30 returns, verify that the vent length and number of elbows stay within the manufacturer's installation guidelines, and consider calling a vent-cleaning specialist if the code persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the lint screen, inspected the full vent path, and replaced any kinked hoses but F30 still appears. A technician can disassemble the dryer cabinet to check for internal lint buildup around the blower wheel and confirm that the blower is spinning freely. If your vent run is longer than 25 feet or has multiple elbows, a vent-cleaning specialist with rotary brushes and high-volume vacuums can clear packed lint that hand tools miss. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable disconnecting rigid metal ducting or accessing the exterior hood from a ladder or roof.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10–40 in parts (replacement lint screen or vent hose), 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $100–200 for a service call to clean venting and inspect airflow.
