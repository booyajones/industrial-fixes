## State Water Heater E20 Error Code — What It Means

The E20 error code on State water heaters is not universally defined across all models, and the exact meaning depends on your specific unit. State is manufactured by A. O. Smith, and on many tankless or high-efficiency models, codes in this range typically indicate problems with airflow, ignition, or inverter-driven components. The most common real-world cause across similar platforms is a blocked or restricted air intake, followed by exhaust obstruction, low water flow, gas supply issues, or fan motor faults.

Because State offers multiple product lines with different control systems, the E20 fault may point to an inverter module issue on some models or a combustion-related fault on others. Always consult your model's service manual or the fault history display on the control board to confirm the exact definition before starting repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or dirty air intake** Lint, dust, or debris blocking the combustion air inlet prevents proper airflow and triggers airflow or ignition faults.
- **Obstructed exhaust vent** Crushed, incorrectly terminated, or blocked exhaust ductwork restricts flue gases and causes the unit to fault out for safety.
- **Fan motor or inverter module failure** On inverter-driven models, a failed fan motor or inverter control module stops airflow and registers as a system fault.
- **Low water flow or inlet filter clog** Restricted water flow from a dirty inlet filter or closed valve prevents the unit from firing or maintaining stable operation.
- **Gas supply issues** Low gas pressure, closed manual shutoff, or undersized line prevents ignition and can trigger combustion or ignition-related codes.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor or igniter** Carbon buildup or sensor wear prevents the control board from detecting flame, leading to lockout or ignition faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the unit** at the circuit breaker or by unplugging the power cord, then turn off the gas supply and wait three minutes before starting any inspection.
2. **Check the air intake screen** for lint, dust, animal nesting, or other blockage, and clean or clear it completely before reassembly.
3. **Inspect the exhaust vent** from the unit to the termination outside, looking for crushed pipe, bird nests, ice buildup, or recirculation from nearby windows or returns.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** at the cold water connection, flushing any sediment or debris that could restrict flow.
5. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff is fully open, checking other gas appliances in the home, and observing inlet pressure if you have a manometer.
6. **Access the combustion chamber** (if your model allows) and inspect the flame sensor, igniter, and burner assembly for carbon buildup or corrosion, cleaning gently with fine abrasive or compressed air.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then monitor the display for fault codes and listen for fan operation during a call for hot water, noting any unusual sounds or failure to ignite.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you have cleared all visible blockages and filters but the E20 code persists, if you smell gas at any point, if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, or if the fault requires component replacement you cannot safely perform. Inverter module and gas valve diagnostics often require specialized tools and training. A qualified technician can read the full fault history from the control board, measure gas inlet and manifold pressures, verify combustion air requirements, and replace failed electronic or gas components under warranty if applicable.
