## Navien E220 Error Code — What It Means

The E220 error code on a Navien tankless water heater generally indicates a flame sensing issue or ignition failure during operation. The unit detected a problem establishing or maintaining a stable flame. This can stem from fuel supply problems, ignition system faults, or sensor contamination. Because the exact meaning can vary slightly between Navien models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for the specific definition tied to your serial number. The heater will lock out to prevent unsafe operation until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame rod when the real problem is simply low gas pressure or a partially closed gas valve upstream. Check gas supply to other appliances and confirm the main shutoff and unit isolation valve are fully open before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low gas supply pressure (~40%)** Insufficient gas delivery from the meter, a partially closed valve, or high demand from other appliances starves the burner and prevents stable ignition.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~30%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensing rod insulates it from the flame and the control board cannot confirm ignition.
- **Faulty igniter (~15%)** A weak or failed ignition electrode does not generate a strong enough spark to light the gas, or the gap is out of spec.
- **Blocked or restricted burner (~10%)** Dust, debris, or insect nests obstruct the burner ports or air intake, disrupting the flame pattern and sensor feedback.
- **Gas valve malfunction (~5%)** The gas valve itself may stick, fail to open fully, or have an internal fault that prevents proper fuel delivery.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the heater at the main switch or breaker and close the gas isolation valve to the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the access panel fasteners and lifting it away to expose the burner and ignition assembly.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon deposits or white corrosion. Use fine-grit sandpaper or a soft abrasive pad to gently clean the rod until the metal is shiny, then wipe with a clean cloth.
4. **Check the igniter electrode** for cracks, heavy carbon, or an incorrect gap to the burner. Clean carefully with a soft brush and verify the gap matches your model's specification in the installation manual.
5. **Examine the burner ports** for dust, spider webs, or blockages. Vacuum gently or use compressed air to clear debris without damaging the flame rod or igniter.
6. **Confirm gas pressure** by verifying the isolation valve is fully open and checking that the meter is delivering gas to other appliances. If you have a manometer, compare inlet pressure to the spec sheet.
7. **Reassemble and power on** the unit. Open a hot-water tap and observe the ignition sequence. If the error persists after these checks, call a licensed technician to test gas valve operation and measure dynamic gas pressure at the heater.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if the error returns after cleaning the flame rod and igniter, or if you lack the tools to measure gas pressure. Professionals can perform a combustion analysis, test the gas valve with a multimeter, verify manifold and inlet pressures with a manometer, and safely diagnose control board faults. Because tankless heaters involve both gas and electrical systems, incorrect repairs can create carbon monoxide hazards or void your warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
