## Whirlpool Range FC E5 Error — What It Means

The FC E5 code on Whirlpool ranges indicates a problem with the Wi-Fi antenna or its associated wiring. This is a communications fault, not a temperature sensor or door issue. The control board cannot communicate with the Wi-Fi module, either because the antenna has failed, a connector has come loose, or the wiring harness is damaged.

This code does not affect basic cooking functions on most models, but it will prevent remote monitoring and control through the app. Whirlpool's official guidance focuses on power cycling, inspecting connections, and replacing the antenna assembly if wiring checks do not resolve the fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real fault is a $15 antenna connector that simply worked loose during shipping or cleaning. Always inspect the antenna harness and connectors before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded antenna connector (~45%)** Vibration, cleaning, or installation can unseat the plug where the Wi-Fi antenna attaches to the control board or harness.
- **Failed Wi-Fi antenna module (~30%)** The antenna itself can fail from power surges, moisture intrusion, or component fatigue over time.
- **Damaged or pinched wiring harness (~15%)** The antenna lead may be pinched behind the range during installation or routed too close to a hot surface, breaking the conductors.
- **Control board communication fault (~10%)** A failed communication circuit on the main control board can prevent it from detecting the antenna, even when the antenna is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker for the range and wait a full minute to allow the control board to fully discharge.
2. **Restore power** and observe the display for one minute to see if the FC E5 code reappears immediately or stays clear.
3. **Pull the range forward** from the wall (disconnect power again first) and remove the rear access panel to expose the Wi-Fi antenna and wiring.
4. **Locate the Wi-Fi antenna module**, typically a small rectangular box with a white or black coaxial lead and a multi-pin connector running to the main control board.
5. **Inspect the connector** where the antenna harness plugs into the board, looking for loose seating, corrosion, or burned pins, and reseat it firmly with a click.
6. **Check the antenna lead** for kinks, burns, or pinched insulation along its entire length, especially near the oven cavity or burner boxes.
7. **Replace the Wi-Fi antenna assembly** if the connector and wiring appear intact but the code returns after a power cycle, then test the range for 24 hours to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the code persists after you have reseated all connectors and replaced the Wi-Fi antenna, or if you are uncomfortable working behind the range with 240-volt wiring nearby. A technician has factory diagnostic software to verify communication between the control board and antenna, and can determine whether the main control board itself has a failed circuit. Also call if you notice other error codes appearing alongside FC E5, since multiple faults suggest a broader electrical issue that requires systematic troubleshooting.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call and antenna replacement.
