## Frigidaire Range F6 Error Code — What It Means

The F6 error code on a Frigidaire range typically indicates a problem with the touch-control panel or the communication between the keypad and the main electronic control board. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for precise information. In most cases, the code appears when the control board detects a stuck button, a failed keypad membrane, or a loose ribbon cable connection between the keypad and the main board. The fault may be intermittent or constant, and the range may stop responding to touch inputs.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the entire electronic control board when the real problem is a loose ribbon cable or a failed keypad overlay. Before buying the expensive board, disconnect and reconnect all ribbon cables and inspect the keypad for stuck buttons or wear.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded ribbon cable (~40%)** The flat ribbon cable connecting the touch panel to the main control board can work loose, collect moisture, or corrode at the connector.
- **Failed keypad overlay (~30%)** The touch-sensitive membrane wears out or develops a short, causing false button signals or stuck keys.
- **Stuck or shorted button (~15%)** Spills, grease, or wear can cause a button to remain closed or intermittently short, triggering the error.
- **Faulty main control board (~10%)** The electronic control board itself can develop component-level faults that misread keypad inputs.
- **Moisture or cleaning solution intrusion (~5%)** Liquid seeping behind the control panel can short circuits or corrode connections.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range to eliminate shock hazard.
2. **Remove the control panel** by taking out screws along the front edge or top of the panel, then carefully pulling it forward.
3. **Inspect the ribbon cables** connecting the keypad to the main control board; look for kinks, tears, or corrosion at the connectors.
4. **Disconnect and reconnect** each ribbon cable, pressing firmly to make sure a complete seat; clean any corrosion with contact cleaner if present.
5. **Check the keypad overlay** for stuck buttons, cracks, or signs of liquid damage; press each button to verify smooth, even travel.
6. **Reassemble the control panel** and restore power, then test the range by pressing several buttons to confirm normal operation.
7. **Monitor the appliance** for several heating cycles; if the F6 code returns, replace the keypad overlay or main control board as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the control panel, if the error persists after replacing both the keypad and control board, or if you suspect internal wiring damage within the range cabinet. A technician can perform voltage and continuity tests on the control circuits and trace intermittent faults that are difficult to diagnose without specialized meters. If your range is still under warranty, contact the manufacturer or an authorized service center before opening the control panel, as DIY work may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
