## Daikin C4 Error Code — What It Means

The C4 error code on a Daikin heat pump signals a faulty heat exchanger temperature sensor on the indoor unit. This sensor, often called the indoor coil thermistor or liquid pipe thermistor, monitors the temperature of the refrigerant coil inside your air handler. When the control board detects an abnormal signal from this sensor (open circuit, short circuit, or out-of-range reading), it shuts down the system and displays C4. The fault can stem from the sensor itself, its wiring, the connector, or occasionally the indoor control board input circuit.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor** The sensor resistance falls outside the expected range, or the element develops an open or short circuit.
- **Loose or corroded connector** Poor contact at the sensor plug or PCB terminal prevents a clean signal.
- **Damaged wiring or harness** Rubbed insulation, pinched wires, or moisture intrusion interrupt the sensor circuit.
- **Sensor not seated properly** The thermistor has slipped out of its coil pocket or pipe clip, causing a false temperature reading.
- **Indoor PCB input fault** If the sensor and wiring test normal but the code persists, the control board input circuit may be defective.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the C4 code** by using the Daikin remote self-diagnosis function (hold the cancel/check sequence until the code appears and a long beep sounds).
2. **Cycle power** at the breaker or disconnect, wait two minutes, then restore power and observe whether C4 returns (this rules out transient faults).
3. **Inspect the sensor physically** by locating the thermistor clipped to the indoor coil or liquid pipe, checking that it is fully seated in its pocket, and looking for looseness, corrosion, or moisture at the connector.
4. **Examine the sensor harness** for cuts, pinches, heat damage, or rubbed insulation between the sensor and the indoor PCB.
5. **Measure thermistor resistance** with a multimeter after disconnecting the sensor, then compare the reading to your model's service manual resistance table (readings vary by model and temperature).
6. **Substitute a known-good thermistor** if the resistance is out of spec or intermittent, and verify that the code clears (if the fault remains with a good sensor, suspect the PCB input circuit).
7. **Run a final operational check** in both heating and cooling modes to confirm normal coil temperature sensing and verify that C4 does not reappear.

## When to Call a Pro

C4 diagnosis requires safe access to live refrigerant lines, low-voltage wiring, and control boards inside the air handler. If you are not comfortable working with multimeters, interpreting resistance curves, or handling indoor-unit covers and electrical connectors, call a licensed HVAC technician. A professional can cross-reference your exact model's service manual, measure thermistor values accurately, and replace the PCB if needed. Always call a pro if you see refrigerant leaks, burnt wiring, or if the code returns after you have replaced the sensor and checked the harness.
