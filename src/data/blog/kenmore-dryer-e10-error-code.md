## Kenmore Dryer E10 Error Code — What It Means

The E10 error code on Kenmore dryers can indicate different faults depending on the model series. Some models use E10 to report a control board communication issue, while others may flag a thermistor or temperature sensor problem. Because Kenmore sources dryers from different manufacturers over the years, the exact meaning varies. Always consult your model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm what E10 specifically indicates for your unit.

In general, E10 points to an electrical or sensor problem rather than a mechanical failure. The dryer typically will not start or will stop mid-cycle when this code appears. The error may be intermittent if caused by a loose connection, or persistent if a component has failed outright.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but a loose wire harness or corroded connector is cheaper to fix. Inspect all connectors at the control board and thermistor before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** Vibration and heat can work connectors loose over time, breaking the signal path between the control board and sensors.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~30%)** A thermistor that drifts out of range or fails open will send invalid readings to the control board.
- **Control board communication fault (~20%)** Internal board errors or corrupted firmware can trigger E10 even when all sensors and wiring are intact.
- **Damaged wire harness (~10%)** Wires that chafe against sharp edges or get pinched during service can short or break, interrupting signals.
- **Failed main control board (~5%)** A control board with failed solder joints or burned traces will misread sensor inputs and throw error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and wait two minutes to reset the control board and discharge residual power.
2. **Locate your model number** on the door frame or rear panel and download the service manual or wiring diagram from the manufacturer's website to confirm what E10 means for your unit.
3. **Remove the top or front panel** using a screwdriver to access the control board and sensor harnesses; consult your manual for panel removal steps.
4. **Inspect every wire connector** at the control board, thermistor, and any other sensors for corrosion, loose pins, or cracks; clean contacts with electrical contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
5. **Test the thermistor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance with a multimeter; compare the reading to your model's specification table (typically a few thousand ohms at room temperature).
6. **Check the wire harness** along its entire length for pinched, chafed, or broken wires; repair or replace any damaged sections with heat-shrink connectors or a new harness.
7. **Reconnect power and run a test cycle**; if E10 persists and all wiring and sensors test good, replace the main control board following the removal and installation steps in your service manual.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet or if you lack a multimeter to test sensors and continuity. A pro should also handle the repair if the control board replacement requires desoldering components or reprogramming firmware. If the dryer shares a 240-volt circuit with other appliances and you are unsure how to safely disconnect power at the breaker, professional service is the safer choice. Finally, if you have replaced wiring and sensors but E10 continues to appear, a technician with factory diagnostic software can pinpoint intermittent faults that basic testing misses.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
