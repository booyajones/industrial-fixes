## MRCOOL F4 Error Code — What It Means

The F4 error code on an MRCOOL mini split indicates an outdoor unit EEPROM parameter error. This means the outdoor control board's stored configuration data is corrupted, unreadable, or not being interpreted correctly by the control system. The fault is centered on the outdoor electronic control board's memory circuit, not a refrigerant or airflow problem.

Because F4 points to a board-level memory failure, the repair typically involves inspecting connections and replacing the outdoor PCB if the error persists. Power events, moisture intrusion, or component failure on the outdoor board can all contribute to EEPROM faults.

## Common Causes

- **Outdoor PCB failure or corrupted EEPROM data** The primary direct cause is memory circuit failure on the outdoor control board itself.
- **Loose, damaged, or incorrect wiring and connectors** Poor connections to the outdoor board can produce false errors or prevent the board from reading stored data correctly.
- **Power supply irregularities or surge damage** Unstable voltage, surges, or electrical events can damage control board circuits and corrupt stored parameters.
- **Moisture intrusion or corrosion on the outdoor PCB** Water exposure or corrosion can degrade board components and cause memory read failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power** to the entire mini split system at the breaker or disconnect before opening the outdoor unit.
2. **Remove the outdoor unit cover** and visually inspect the control board area for burned components, water stains, corrosion, or physical damage.
3. **Check all connectors and wiring** to the outdoor PCB for secure seating, corrosion, or damaged pins and reseat any loose plugs.
4. **Verify incoming power** at the outdoor unit is correct and stable using a multimeter before condemning the board.
5. **Restore power and cycle the system** to see if the F4 error clears or returns after a fresh reset.
6. **Replace the outdoor control board** if the error persists after inspection and power cycle, as F4 indicates EEPROM-level fault that cannot be field-repaired.
7. **Test full operation** after board replacement to confirm the error clears and the unit runs normally through a complete cooling or heating cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical components or opening the outdoor unit. Board replacement requires matching the correct part to your model, handling refrigerant lines safely, and verifying proper system operation after repair. If you find evidence of surge damage or widespread corrosion, a technician can assess whether other components were also affected and whether the electrical supply needs correction before installing a new board.
