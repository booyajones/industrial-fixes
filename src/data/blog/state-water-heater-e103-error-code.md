## State Water Heater E103 Error — What It Means

The E103 error code on State (A. O. Smith) tankless water heaters indicates an ignition failure or ignition lockout condition. The unit attempted to start but could not establish or maintain flame, so it locked out for safety. This type of fault typically points to a problem with combustion air supply, gas delivery, or the ignition and flame-sensing system. The heater will not operate until the underlying issue is corrected and the unit is successfully reset.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens** Lint, dust, or debris accumulation on the combustion air intake prevents adequate airflow for ignition and is the most common cause of ignition-type errors on State tankless units.
- **Restricted venting or exhaust** Obstructed, improperly terminated, or undersized exhaust venting restricts exhaust gases and disrupts the air-fuel mixture needed for reliable ignition.
- **Insufficient gas supply or gas valve fault** A closed gas shutoff, low supply pressure, or failed gas valve prevents the burner from receiving fuel during the ignition sequence.
- **Low water flow or dirty inlet filter** Tankless heaters require minimum flow to initiate ignition, and a clogged inlet screen or insufficient demand can prevent startup.
- **Dirty or failed flame rod or igniter** Carbon buildup or component failure on the flame sensor or ignition electrode prevents the control board from detecting flame or generating spark.
- **Control board or wiring issue** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a faulty control board can interrupt the ignition sequence or flame signal even when other components are sound.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the code and model number** on the unit's display and rating plate to make sure E103 applies to your specific State tankless platform.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** first, removing any lint, dust, or debris that may block combustion air supply to the burner chamber.
3. **Check the exhaust vent termination and piping** for obstructions, proper clearance, and correct installation per the manual's venting requirements.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve is fully open and checking that other gas appliances are working normally to rule out supply issues.
5. **Inspect the inlet water filter** and remove any sediment or debris, then confirm that water flow meets the unit's minimum activation threshold.
6. **Examine the igniter and flame rod** for carbon deposits, corrosion, or damage, and clean or replace components as needed following manufacturer guidance.
7. **Reset the unit** by cycling power or pressing the reset button, then initiate a hot-water call to verify ignition and monitor for code recurrence.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if the E103 code returns after you have cleaned the air intake and inlet filter, verified gas supply, and reset the unit. Persistent ignition lockouts often require component-level testing of the flame rod, igniter, gas valve, and control board with specialized tools and knowledge of safe combustion procedures. Any work involving gas connections, venting modifications, or internal ignition parts should be performed by a licensed professional to protect your warranty and make sure code-compliant, safe operation.
