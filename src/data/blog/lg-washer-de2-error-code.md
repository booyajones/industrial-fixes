## LG Washer dE2 Error Code — What It Means

The dE2 code on an LG washer indicates that the control board detects the door or drawer as closed but not successfully locked. The door lock assembly must engage and confirm a secure latch before the wash cycle can start. When the latch hook on the door does not fully seat into the lock striker, or when the lock mechanism itself fails to extend and capture the hook, the washer throws dE2 and will not run.

LG support documentation lists overloading, items caught at the door or gasket, and a bent or broken latch as the primary triggers. In practice, the code almost always stems from something mechanical preventing the lock from completing its cycle rather than an electronic failure. Laundry pinched between the door and the gasket, a lightly pushed door that feels closed but is not firmly latched, or a bent door hook are the usual culprits. When these physical checks come up clean, the door lock assembly or its wiring is the next focus.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often order a new door lock assembly when laundry is simply wedged under the gasket or the door was not pushed hard enough to engage the latch. Open the door, inspect the entire gasket perimeter for trapped fabric, and press the door firmly until you hear or feel the latch click before assuming the lock is broken.

## Common Causes

- **Door not firmly latched (~35%)** The door feels closed but was pushed only lightly, so the latch hook does not fully engage the striker and the lock cannot capture it.
- **Laundry pinched in the gasket (~30%)** A sock, shirt tail, or elastic band is caught between the door seal and the drum opening, holding the door slightly ajar and preventing lock engagement.
- **Excessive load size (~15%)** Overloading the drum past about two-thirds full pushes laundry against the door from the inside, creating outward pressure that prevents the latch from seating or the lock from extending.
- **Bent or broken door latch hook (~10%)** The metal or plastic hook on the door itself is bent, cracked, or loose, so it does not align with the lock striker when the door closes.
- **Failed door lock assembly (~8%)** The electromechanical lock on the cabinet side no longer extends the bolt or plunger to capture the latch hook, or its internal switch does not signal lock confirmation to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~2%)** The connector at the door lock assembly or a splice in the harness has worked loose or corroded, interrupting the lock signal to the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the wall outlet or circuit breaker before any inspection or repair work.
2. **Open the door** and run your fingers completely around the rubber gasket, feeling inside the fold for any clothing, elastic, or debris wedged between the seal and the drum opening. Pull out anything you find.
3. **Close the door firmly** until you hear or feel a distinct click, confirming the latch hook has engaged the striker. Start a test cycle. If dE2 clears, the problem was incomplete latching.
4. **Inspect the door latch hook** on the door itself for bends, cracks, or looseness. If the hook is damaged, replace the door assembly or the latch hardware depending on your model's design.
5. **Remove laundry** until the drum is no more than about two-thirds full, then retry the cycle. Overloading can push fabric against the door and prevent the lock from seating.
6. **Reset the washer** by unplugging it, pressing and holding the START or PAUSE button for five seconds, then restoring power and running a test cycle.
7. **Access the door lock assembly** by removing the front lower panel or top access panel, depending on your washer model. Unplug the lock connector, inspect the terminals for corrosion or looseness, clean if needed, and reconnect. If the lock does not click or hum when you start a cycle, replace the door lock assembly and retest. If the new lock still throws dE2, check the wiring harness and control board output.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have confirmed no laundry is pinched, the door closes firmly with an audible click, the latch hook is straight, the load is reasonable, and you have already replaced the door lock assembly but dE2 returns immediately. At that point the fault lies in the wiring harness between the lock and the control board, or in the board's lock-detection circuit. Harness continuity tests and board-level diagnostics require a multimeter, the wiring diagram for your model, and experience interpreting control-board signals. LG advises that persistent dE2 after mechanical checks requires service. A professional visit typically costs between one hundred fifty and two hundred fifty dollars and includes harness tracing, connector repair, or control board replacement if needed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–90 for a door lock assembly, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250 service call including part and labor.
