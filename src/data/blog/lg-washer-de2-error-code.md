## What this code means
The LG dE2 error tells you the washer detects that the door is closed but not locked, or that the door-lock signal is dropping in and out during the cycle. LG's documentation describes it as the drawer being closed but not locking, typically because laundry is blocking the door, items are caught between the door and gasket, or the latch itself is bent or broken. In practical service terms, the control board is not seeing a stable locked-door signal from the lock assembly.

This is different from a simple door-open fault. The washer recognizes the door is physically shut, yet the latch or lock mechanism cannot confirm a secure lock. The error points to a problem somewhere in the door lock circuit, whether mechanical (latch alignment, debris) or electrical (failing lock switch contacts, damaged wiring, or a loose connector).

## Before You Replace Anything

Some homeowners replace the main control board when the lock assembly or a simple wiring connector is the real culprit. Always inspect the door lock connector for loose pins and test the lock assembly with a multimeter for continuity before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Laundry blocking the door or gasket** Overloaded drum or clothing caught between the door and gasket prevents the latch from seating fully so the lock cannot engage.
- **Worn or faulty door lock assembly** Internal switch contacts in the lock become pitted or worn, creating an intermittent lock signal that the control board reads as not locked.
- **Bent, broken, or worn door latch or hook** A damaged latch on the door or strike area cannot align properly with the lock assembly, so the lock never clicks into the locked position.
- **Damaged wiring harness or loose connector** Broken wires, backed-out pins, corrosion, or burn marks at the door lock connector interrupt the lock signal to the control board.
- **Control board failure** Less commonly the main control board itself fails to read a valid lock signal even when the lock and wiring are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** or turn off the circuit breaker so you can safely inspect the door area.
2. **Open the door and check the gasket and latch area** for clothing, debris, or any obstruction that could prevent the latch from seating fully. Remove anything you find.
3. **Perform a power reset** by keeping the washer unplugged, pressing and holding the START/PAUSE button for 5 seconds, then restoring power and attempting a cycle.
4. **Inspect the door latch and strike** for bends, cracks, or excessive play. If the latch or hook is damaged, order the correct replacement part for your model and swap it out.
5. **Access the door lock assembly** (typically behind the front panel or through the door-seal opening, depending on your LG model) and unplug the lock connector. Look for loose pins, corrosion, oxidation, or burn marks.
6. **Test the lock assembly with a multimeter** by checking continuity across the lock switch terminals while manually engaging the latch. Intermittent or no continuity indicates a failing lock assembly.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if it tests bad, or repair or replace the wiring harness if you found damaged wires or connectors. Reassemble the washer, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify the dE2 error is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable removing the front panel or door-seal boot to reach the lock assembly, if you lack a multimeter and the experience to test continuity, or if you have already replaced the lock and latch yet the dE2 error persists. A technician can perform systematic circuit testing, check for control-board-side faults, and handle any model-specific disassembly safely. Also call if you find evidence of water damage or arcing at the connectors, since that can indicate a larger electrical problem that needs expert diagnosis.
