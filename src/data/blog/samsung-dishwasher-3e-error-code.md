## Samsung Dishwasher 3E Error Code — What It Means

The 3E error code on Samsung dishwashers signals a high temperature water supply error. The dishwasher has detected that the incoming hot water is hotter than the allowed threshold. Samsung specifies that the hot water supply must be below 176°F (80°C). If the water entering the dishwasher exceeds this temperature, the machine throws the 3E code and stops the cycle to protect internal components and dishes from damage.

This code is specific to Samsung dishwashers. Some third-party repair sites confuse it with motor-related codes on Samsung washing machines or other appliances, so always confirm your model number and refer to Samsung dishwasher documentation. The fix usually involves adjusting your household water heater or mixing valve. If the error persists after confirming the supply water is within spec, Samsung directs you to contact service for possible sensor or control issues.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or temperature sensor without first checking the actual inlet water temperature. Measure the hot water at the dishwasher supply line with a thermometer before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Water heater set too high** The household water heater is configured above 176°F, sending water hotter than the dishwasher can accept.
- **Malfunctioning mixing valve** A tempering or mixing valve at the water heater or under the sink is stuck or bypassed, allowing scalding water to reach the dishwasher.
- **Recent plumbing work** A plumber or homeowner adjusted hot-water settings or reconfigured supply lines, raising the inlet temperature.
- **Faulty temperature sensor** The dishwasher's thermistor or temperature sensor reads incorrectly and reports overtemperature even when the supply is within spec.
- **Control board misreading** The main control board interprets sensor data incorrectly or has a firmware glitch, throwing a false 3E code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the dishwasher at the circuit breaker or unplug the unit.
2. **Measure the hot water temperature** at the kitchen sink or dishwasher supply valve using a cooking or infrared thermometer after letting the water run for 30 seconds.
3. **Lower the water heater thermostat** if the reading is above 176°F (80°C). Most residential water heaters should be set between 120-140°F for safety and efficiency.
4. **Wait 30 minutes** for the water heater to stabilize at the new temperature, then measure again to confirm the supply is below the limit.
5. **Power-cycle the dishwasher** by turning the breaker back on or plugging it in, then press and hold the Start or Power button for 3 seconds to reset the error code.
6. **Run a short test cycle** (rinse or express wash) and monitor the display. If 3E does not return, the repair is complete.
7. **Contact Samsung service** if the error persists with confirmed compliant inlet water temperature. The sensor or control board may need professional diagnosis or replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have confirmed the incoming hot water is below 176°F (80°C) and the 3E code continues to appear. Persistent errors after correcting the supply temperature indicate a sensor, wiring, or control board problem that requires diagnostic tools and model-specific knowledge. Also call service if you are uncomfortable working with your home water heater or if local codes require a licensed plumber to adjust water-heater settings. Samsung directs you to contact their support or an authorized service center when the code returns despite compliant inlet temperature.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 if you only need to adjust the water heater, 15-30 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for a service call to diagnose and adjust or repair sensor/control issues.
