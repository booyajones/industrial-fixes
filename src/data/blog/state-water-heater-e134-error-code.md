## State Water Heater E134 Error Code — What It Means

E134 does not appear in available State Water Heaters or A. O. Smith service documentation, so its exact meaning cannot be confirmed without your unit's model number and service manual. Many display codes on State tankless water heaters relate to ignition failure, flame detection loss, blocked combustion air, low gas pressure, or insufficient water flow. Without manufacturer confirmation, treating E134 as a valid diagnostic code may lead to incorrect part replacement.

If your display shows E134, verify the exact model number on the data plate and consult the installation and service manual that shipped with the unit or download it from State's support site. Do not assume E134 matches codes from other brands or non-State heater families.

## Common Causes

- **Lint or debris blocking combustion air intake screens** This is the most common cause of ignition and flame faults on State tankless heaters and restricts airflow to the burner.
- **Low or interrupted water flow through the heat exchanger** Clogged inlet filter screens or a partially closed isolation valve can prevent the unit from reaching minimum flow to ignite.
- **Low gas supply pressure or closed manual gas valve** Insufficient gas delivery prevents ignition or causes flame dropout shortly after startup.
- **Loose or corroded wiring at the control board or sensors** Poor electrical connections can generate spurious fault codes or prevent the controller from reading sensor values correctly.
- **Blocked or improperly terminated exhaust vent** Vent restrictions cause pressure-switch lockout or flame-rollout detection on many State models.
- **Failed sensor or control board displaying an undefined code** A malfunctioning controller may show non-standard codes that do not appear in service literature.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the data plate** on the water heater and write down the complete model number and serial number.
2. **Download or retrieve the service manual** for your exact model from the State Water Heaters support website or the envelope taped to the unit.
3. **Look up code E134** in the fault-code table in your manual. If the code does not appear, contact State technical support with your model and serial number before proceeding.
4. **Inspect and clean the combustion air intake screens.** Remove any lint, dust, or debris blocking airflow, since this is the most frequent cause of ignition faults on State tankless heaters.
5. **Check the inlet water filter** by closing the cold-water isolation valve, relieving pressure, and removing the filter screen. Rinse it under running water and reinstall.
6. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve upstream of the heater is fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
7. **Examine all wiring harness connectors** at the control board, igniter, flame sensor, and pressure switches for loose pins, corrosion, or damaged insulation. Reseat any suspect connectors.
8. **Reset the unit** by turning off power at the circuit breaker for thirty seconds, then restore power and attempt a hot-water draw. If E134 reappears and is not listed in your manual, schedule a service call.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if E134 does not appear in your service manual, if the code persists after you have cleaned the air intake and inlet filter, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections and electrical components. A qualified technician can obtain the correct fault definition from State, perform resistance and voltage tests on sensors and igniters, measure gas pressure at the valve, and confirm vent installation meets code. Professional diagnosis prevents unnecessary part replacement and protects your warranty coverage.
