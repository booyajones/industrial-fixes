## Ruud E13 Error Code — What It Means

Error code E13 on a Ruud tankless water heater indicates a combustion system failure. The unit is unable to ignite or maintain a flame due to problems with the gas supply, venting, or flame detection components. This code is part of the Ruud/Rheem tankless platform and specifically points to issues preventing proper burner operation rather than water-side faults.

The heater detects that combustion cannot proceed safely and shuts down to protect the system. Common triggers include insufficient gas pressure, a closed or restricted gas valve, improper or blocked venting, contaminated flame sensors, or restricted airflow to the combustion chamber. The unit will not provide hot water until the underlying combustion problem is resolved.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply off or restricted** The gas shutoff valve may be closed, partially open, or the supply line may have low pressure or restrictions preventing adequate fuel delivery to the burner.
- **Gas valve malfunction** The internal gas valve may be failing to open correctly or deliver the proper fuel flow even when the control board calls for heat.
- **Improper or blocked venting** Exhaust vent pipes may be installed with incorrect materials, improper slope, blocked by debris or ice, or terminated too close to air intakes, preventing combustion gases from exiting.
- **Dirty flame rod or combustion sensor** Carbon buildup, corrosion, or contamination on the flame rod or combustion sensor prevents the unit from detecting that a flame is present, causing shutdown even if ignition occurs.
- **Burner contamination or carbon buildup** Dust, lint, or carbon deposits on the burner assembly or burner tubes interfere with ignition and flame stability.
- **Restricted combustion airflow** Clogged air inlet filters, a failing combustion fan, or fouled heat exchanger passages reduce the oxygen supply needed for proper combustion.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the gas supply** by confirming that the gas shutoff valve serving the water heater is fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
2. **Measure gas supply pressure** at the unit inlet with a manometer and compare the reading to your model's specification (one common range is 5.0 to 13.0 inches of water column, but consult your model's installation manual for the exact requirement).
3. **Inspect the venting system** for blockages, improper slope, incorrect materials, or obstructed termination points, and clear any debris or ice from exhaust and air intake pipes.
4. **Remove and clean the flame rod and combustion sensor** using fine-grit sandpaper or a clean cloth to remove carbon deposits, corrosion, or contamination, then check wiring connections for looseness or damage.
5. **Inspect and clean the burner assembly** by removing the burner cover and carefully vacuuming or brushing away dust, lint, and carbon buildup from the burner tubes and combustion chamber.
6. **Check and clean the air inlet filter** and inspect the combustion fan for obstructions or failure, then verify that the heat exchanger is not fouled with scale (perform a descaling service if mineral buildup is suspected).
7. **Reset the unit** by turning off power for 30 seconds, then restore power and test for hot water demand. If the E13 code returns after verified gas supply, clear venting, and clean sensors, the gas valve or another internal component likely requires replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you do not have a manometer to measure gas pressure, or if the error persists after you have verified the gas supply and cleaned accessible components. Gas valve replacement, venting modifications, and combustion system diagnostics require specialized tools and training. Any work involving gas connections or control boards should be performed by a qualified professional to maintain safety and warranty coverage.
