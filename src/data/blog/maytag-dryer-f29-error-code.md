## Maytag Dryer F29 Error Code — What It Means

The F29 code on a Maytag dryer signals a door-unlock fault. The control has detected that the door latch mechanism is not releasing after a cycle completes. This code appears on supported Maytag and Whirlpool front-load dryer platforms and prevents the door from opening normally. It is not related to airflow, thermistors, or motor issues.

Maytag's own product help directs users to perform a complete power reset and retest before replacing parts. The fault may be a temporary electronic glitch in the control board, a mechanical problem with the door lock assembly itself, or a wiring issue between the lock and the control. If the door remains locked after you restore power, you will need to follow the manual door-release procedure in your owner's manual and then diagnose the lock hardware.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real fault is a worn or misaligned door lock assembly. Check the lock mechanism for binding and test continuity at the lock connector before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Temporary control glitch (~40%)** A brief electronic fault in the control board that clears after a full power reset and does not return.
- **Failed door lock assembly (~35%)** The solenoid or latch mechanism inside the door lock has worn out or broken and no longer releases the strike.
- **Door not fully seated (~10%)** The door was not pressed firmly into the lock area at the start of the cycle, leaving the latch only partially engaged.
- **Loose or corroded lock connector (~10%)** The wiring harness or spade terminals at the door lock have loosened or oxidized, interrupting the unlock signal.
- **Damaged door strike or hook (~5%)** The metal striker on the door or the hook in the cabinet is bent or cracked, preventing proper latch alignment.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** and wait five full minutes to reset the control board completely.
2. **Restore power** and start a timed dry cycle with an empty drum.
3. **Monitor for one minute** to see if F29 reappears or if the door unlocks normally at the end of the test cycle.
4. **If the code returns**, unplug the dryer and open the service panel to access the door lock assembly on the inside of the front panel.
5. **Inspect the lock connector** for loose or corroded terminals and check the door striker for damage or misalignment.
6. **Test the lock solenoid** by disconnecting the harness and checking continuity across the lock terminals. Consult your model's wiring diagram for expected values.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if it shows no continuity, if the mechanism binds, or if the solenoid does not click when powered, then reassemble and retest.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you cannot access the door lock assembly after removing the service panel, or if the fault persists after you have replaced the lock and verified all connections. A pro can also check control-board signals with a multimeter and safely perform the manual door-release procedure if the door remains locked. If the wiring harness shows signs of heat damage or multiple connectors are corroded, a technician can trace the full circuit and replace damaged sections rather than guessing at individual parts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
