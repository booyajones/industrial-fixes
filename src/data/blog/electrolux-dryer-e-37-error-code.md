## Electrolux Dryer E37 Error Code — What It Means

The E37 error code on Electrolux dryers typically indicates a communication fault or sensor problem within the dryer's control system. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer for your specific unit. In general, this code signals that the main control board is not receiving proper signals from one or more sensors, or that there is a wiring issue interrupting communication between components. The dryer may not start or may stop mid-cycle when this code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but most E37 codes are caused by loose connectors or damaged wiring. Inspect all wiring harnesses and connectors before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~45%)** A connector between the control board and a sensor or motor has worked loose or developed corrosion, interrupting the signal.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~25%)** Wires pinched during a previous repair or rubbed through by drum movement can create intermittent faults that trigger the code.
- **Failed temperature sensor or thermistor (~15%)** The dryer's thermistor or temperature sensor may have failed or drifted out of specification, sending no signal or a signal the board cannot interpret.
- **Faulty main control board (~10%)** The control board itself may have a failed component or corrupted firmware that prevents it from reading sensor signals correctly.
- **Moisture or environmental damage (~5%)** Water intrusion or high humidity can cause corrosion on connectors and circuit traces, leading to intermittent communication errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the top and front panels** by unscrewing the fasteners (consult your service manual for panel removal steps specific to your model).
3. **Inspect all wiring connectors** on the main control board, the motor, and any sensors visible in the cabinet, looking for loose plugs, corrosion, or burn marks.
4. **Reseat each connector** by unplugging it and firmly pushing it back in, checking that the locking tabs engage properly.
5. **Examine the wiring harness** for pinched or frayed wires, especially where the harness passes through metal edges or near moving parts like the drum.
6. **Test the thermistor or temperature sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode (consult your model's table for the expected ohm reading at room temperature).
7. **Replace any damaged wiring, connectors, or sensors** as needed, then reassemble the panels, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the error is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if the wiring inspection reveals extensive damage that requires rewiring large sections of the harness, or if all connectors and sensors test normal but the error persists. A technician can perform board-level diagnostics and has access to service software and schematics that pinpoint obscure faults. Also call a pro if the dryer shares a high-voltage dedicated circuit and you are unsure how to safely disconnect power.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts if a connector or sensor needs replacement, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
