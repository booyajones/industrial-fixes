## Maytag Washer DRN Error Code — What It Means

DRN on a Maytag washer is a long-drain or drainage fault. The machine is taking too long to remove water or cannot drain normally during the cycle. On supported top-load models Maytag ties DRN to the F9 E1 fault, which means a drain pump system problem with long drain times.

The code does not always mean the pump itself has failed. Maytag emphasizes that incorrect drain hose installation, including inserting the hose too far into the standpipe or routing the end too low, will trigger the same long-drain condition. A kinked or clogged hose, debris in the drain path, or a failing pump can also cause the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump first without checking the hose. Always verify hose routing, insertion depth, and height before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose inserted too far into the standpipe (~30%)** Maytag specifies the hose must not extend more than 4.5 inches (114 mm) into the standpipe or the washer will drain slowly.
- **Drain hose end too low or not secured correctly (~25%)** The hose end must be higher than 36 inches from the floor to prevent siphoning, and it must be attached with the drain hose form rather than taped over.
- **Kinked or crushed drain hose (~20%)** A hose pinched behind the washer or bent sharply will restrict flow and trigger the long-drain fault.
- **Clogged pump filter or debris in the drain path (~15%)** Lint, coins, and small objects can block the pump filter or trap on models that have one, preventing water from leaving the tub.
- **Faulty drain pump (~10%)** If the pump motor is weak, the impeller is damaged, or the pump will not run, water will not evacuate and the code will appear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves.
2. **Pull the washer forward** and inspect the drain hose for kinks, crushing, or tight bends behind the machine.
3. **Check the standpipe connection** and measure how far the hose enters the pipe. Pull it back so no more than 4.5 inches (114 mm) goes into the standpipe.
4. **Verify the hose end height** by measuring from the floor to the top of the standpipe or laundry sink. The drain opening must be above 36 inches and the hose must be secured with the drain hose form, not taped over.
5. **Remove and flush the drain hose** if you see or suspect a clog. Disconnect the hose from the back of the washer and the standpipe, then run water through it in a sink or outdoors.
6. **Inspect the pump filter or trap** if your model has an accessible one. Remove the lower front panel or access cover, unscrew the filter, and clean out lint, coins, and debris.
7. **Run a drain or spin cycle** to test. If the code returns and the hose is correct, remove the cabinet or rear panel, check the drain pump for debris on the impeller, and replace the pump if it will not run or move water.
8. **Reassemble and test** with a full wash cycle to confirm normal drain operation and verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have corrected the hose installation, cleared all visible blockages, and the DRN code still appears after a test cycle. A pro can verify pump operation with voltage and resistance tests, confirm that the control board is sending the correct signals, and rule out internal plumbing restrictions or a faulty pressure sensor. Also call if you are not comfortable tilting the washer, removing panels, or handling the drain pump assembly. Professional service typically costs between 150 and 280 dollars depending on the part needed and your location.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (hose or pump), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 for a service call and pump replacement.
