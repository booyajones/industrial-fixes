## Bosch Dishwasher E23 Error — What It Means

The E23 error code on a Bosch dishwasher typically indicates a drain issue. The appliance has detected that water is not leaving the tub as expected, which triggers a safety shutdown to prevent flooding or further problems. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the machine for confirmation.

Most often this code appears when something is blocking the drain path or when the drain pump cannot move water out efficiently. The dishwasher's control board monitors drain time and water level, and when those readings fall outside normal parameters the E23 fault is logged. Resolving the code usually means clearing obstructions or replacing a failed component in the drain system.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump when the real culprit is a clogged filter or kinked hose. Always remove and clean the filter and inspect the drain hose for kinks or blockages before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filter or sump (~45%)** Food particles, grease, and debris accumulate in the filter basket and sump area, restricting water flow and triggering the drain fault.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose can become pinched behind the machine or clogged internally, preventing water from exiting the tub.
- **Failed drain pump (~20%)** The pump motor or impeller can fail or seize, leaving the pump unable to move water out of the dishwasher.
- **Disposal knockout plug not removed (~5%)** If the dishwasher drains into a garbage disposal and the knockout plug was not removed during installation, water cannot exit.
- **Faulty pressure switch or sensor (~5%)** A stuck or defective water-level sensor may incorrectly report that water remains in the tub even after a successful drain cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the lower dish rack** and locate the cylindrical filter assembly in the floor of the tub.
3. **Twist and lift out the filter** and sump screen, then rinse both under running water to remove all food particles and grease.
4. **Inspect the sump cavity** below the filter for any debris, broken glass, or foreign objects and wipe it clean.
5. **Pull the dishwasher forward** to access the drain hose connection under the sink or at the disposal.
6. **Check the drain hose** for kinks, loops higher than the dishwasher outlet, or internal clogs by disconnecting it and flushing with water.
7. **Verify the disposal knockout** plug has been removed if the hose connects to a garbage disposal, and clear any blockage in the disposal inlet.
8. **Test the drain pump** by locating it at the base of the machine, disconnecting the pump wiring, and checking for continuity across the motor terminals with a multimeter.
9. **Replace the drain pump** if it shows no continuity or if the impeller is seized and cannot be freed.
10. **Reassemble the filter and hose**, restore power, and run a rinse cycle to confirm the E23 code is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the filter, inspected the hose, and tested the pump but the E23 code persists. A technician can diagnose pressure-switch faults, wiring issues at the control board, or problems with the recirculation system that require disassembly and specialized tools. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if the machine shows signs of a wiring short or water intrusion into the control area.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
