## Navien Tankless Water Heater E218 Error — What It Means

The E218 error on a Navien tankless water heater indicates the unit is detecting a problem with water flow or pressure sensing. This code typically appears when the system cannot confirm adequate water movement through the heat exchanger, either because flow is actually restricted or because a sensor is giving faulty readings.

The heater uses flow sensors and pressure switches to verify safe operating conditions before firing the burner. When these readings fall outside expected ranges, the unit halts operation and displays E218 to prevent damage. Common triggers include clogged inlet filters, low incoming water pressure, a failing flow sensor, or debris in the heat exchanger itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor or circuit board when the real culprit is simply debris in the inlet filters or heat exchanger. Always clean filters and flush the system first before ordering electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screens (~45%)** Sediment and mineral deposits accumulate on the small mesh filters at the cold-water inlet, restricting flow enough to trigger the sensor.
- **Low incoming water pressure (~25%)** Supply pressure below the minimum required (check your model's installation manual for the threshold) prevents the unit from detecting adequate flow.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~15%)** The turbine or hall-effect flow sensor inside the unit wears out or becomes fouled, sending inaccurate signals to the control board.
- **Scaled or clogged heat exchanger (~10%)** Hard-water mineral buildup inside the heat exchanger restricts passages and reduces flow rate below the sensor threshold.
- **Defective pressure switch (~5%)** The differential pressure switch that monitors flow may fail to close its contacts even when flow is present.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the cold-water isolation valve feeding the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the bottom panel fasteners and lifting the cover away from the unit.
3. **Locate and remove the inlet filter screens** at the cold-water service valve; these are typically inside threaded housings at the bottom of the unit.
4. **Clean the filters** thoroughly with a soft brush under running water, removing all sediment and scale; replace if the mesh is torn or corroded.
5. **Reinstall the filters** with new gaskets or O-rings if provided, tighten by hand plus one-quarter turn with a wrench to avoid over-torquing.
6. **Open the isolation valve** slowly and check for leaks around the filter housings; tighten gently if needed.
7. **Restore power** at the breaker, open a hot-water tap fully, and observe whether the unit fires without displaying E218; if the code persists, proceed to flush the heat exchanger or test the flow sensor per the service manual.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you have cleaned the filters and verified adequate inlet pressure but the E218 code returns. Flow-sensor replacement requires disassembly of internal plumbing and careful handling of electronic connectors. If the heat exchanger needs descaling or flushing with a pump and chemical solution, a professional has the equipment and experience to avoid damaging the stainless-steel coils. Also call a pro if you suspect gas-valve or combustion issues, or if the unit is still under warranty and you want to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
