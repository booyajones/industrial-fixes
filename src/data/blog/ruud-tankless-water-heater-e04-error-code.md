## Ruud E04 Error Code — What It Means

The E04 (or E4) error code on Ruud tankless water heaters most commonly indicates a water supply issue, such as low incoming pressure or insufficient flow through the unit. This fault prevents the heater from operating because it cannot detect adequate water movement to safely fire the burner. The exact definition can vary by model, so always verify the code against your specific Ruud installation manual. Until the water supply problem is corrected, the unit will remain in fault mode and will not produce hot water.

## Common Causes

- **Low inlet water pressure** Inadequate supply pressure from the municipal line or well system prevents the heater from sensing sufficient flow to operate.
- **Closed or partially closed shutoff valve** An isolation valve that is not fully open restricts flow and triggers the fault.
- **Clogged inlet screen or filter** Sediment, scale, or debris trapped in the inlet screen reduces flow below the minimum threshold.
- **Kinked or restricted supply line** A bent hose, crimped pipe, or obstruction in the cold-water line chokes flow to the heater.
- **Leak in the water supply system** A leak upstream or downstream reduces effective flow through the unit.
- **Faulty flow sensor or harness connection** A damaged flow-sensor harness or failed sensor can prevent the controller from recognizing adequate flow even when water supply is correct.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code definition** in your exact Ruud model's installation or service manual, because error-code meanings can differ across product lines.
2. **Confirm all water shutoff valves** (inlet isolation and any service valves) are fully open by turning them counterclockwise to the stop.
3. **Check incoming water pressure** at a nearby fixture or hose bib to confirm you have adequate supply pressure, and inspect for any system-wide low-pressure conditions.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter or screen** by shutting off the cold-water supply, relieving pressure, unscrewing the filter housing, and rinsing debris from the mesh.
5. **Inspect supply piping** for kinks, crimps, or obstructions, and walk the line from the meter or well to the heater looking for visible leaks.
6. **Inspect the flow sensor harness and connectors** for corrosion, loose pins, or damage, and reseat any multi-pin connectors firmly.
7. **Reset the heater** by powering it off for thirty seconds, then power back on and open a hot tap to test flow detection and verify the fault clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or tankless specialist if you have confirmed adequate supply pressure and a clean inlet screen but the E04 fault persists. Flow-sensor testing requires a multimeter and familiarity with the specific sensor circuit for your model, and misdiagnosis can lead to unnecessary parts replacement. A pro can also verify that your home's supply pressure and flow rate meet Ruud's minimum specifications, perform electronic diagnostics, and safely handle gas or electrical connections during sensor or controller replacement.
