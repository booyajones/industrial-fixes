## Bosch Oven F125 Error Code — What It Means

The F125 error code on a Bosch oven or range indicates that the control believes the CANCEL button is being pressed continuously or is electrically stuck. This is a keypad or control panel fault. The oven's electronic control is receiving a constant signal from the cancel key input, either because the membrane switch is physically stuck, contaminated with moisture or residue, or because the wiring between the keypad and control board has failed. Bosch does not publish a model-specific definition for F125 in their general error-code documentation, but technician field reports consistently identify this code as a stuck cancel key or membrane keypad fault.

Because the control interprets the cancel button as always pressed, the oven may refuse to start a cook cycle, cancel programs immediately, or lock out other functions. The fault usually lies in the touch panel assembly itself rather than the main control board, though wiring and connector issues between the two can also trigger the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but F125 is almost always a keypad or wiring fault. Inspect and clean the control panel and check all ribbon connectors before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or shorted membrane keypad (~55%)** Moisture, grease, or physical pressure on the touch panel causes the cancel button contact to close or short, sending a continuous signal to the control.
- **Moisture or residue on the control panel (~20%)** Condensation, spills, or cleaning spray trapped under the membrane creates a conductive path that mimics a stuck key.
- **Loose or corroded keypad ribbon connector (~15%)** The ribbon cable or harness plug between the keypad and control board is loose, corroded, or has damaged pins that short the cancel input.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~7%)** Heat, pinching, or vibration has broken or frayed the wires in the keypad harness, causing an intermittent or permanent short.
- **Faulty control board misreading input (~3%)** The main electronic control board interprets the cancel signal incorrectly due to a failed input circuit, though this is less common than keypad faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker for at least five minutes to reset the control.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether F125 reappears immediately or after a few seconds. If the code does not return, monitor the oven over the next few uses.
3. **Inspect the control panel** for visible moisture, grease, food residue, or a cancel button that appears depressed or sticky. Wipe the panel with a clean, dry cloth and check again.
4. **Remove the control panel** by taking out the screws that hold the top or front panel (consult your model's service manual for exact disassembly). Locate the ribbon cable or harness connector that links the keypad membrane to the control board.
5. **Check the ribbon connector and pins** for corrosion, bent pins, heat discoloration, or looseness. Disconnect and reconnect the plug to make sure a clean contact. Inspect the wiring harness for cuts, pinches, or frayed insulation.
6. **Replace the membrane keypad assembly** if the connector and wiring are intact but the code persists. Order the correct part number for your Bosch oven model and swap the assembly, routing the new ribbon cable carefully.
7. **Test the oven** by restoring power and starting a bake cycle. If F125 still appears after keypad replacement and wiring checks, test or replace the main control board according to your model's diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable disassembling the control panel or working near the oven's high-voltage wiring. A technician can quickly isolate whether the fault is in the keypad, harness, or control board using multimeter continuity and resistance tests. If the code persists after you have replaced the membrane keypad and verified all connections, a pro can test the control board's input circuits and replace the board if necessary. Because F125 typically requires only mechanical disassembly and connector work, many homeowners with basic tools can complete the repair, but a service call ensures correct diagnosis and avoids ordering unnecessary parts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–200 for membrane keypad, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350 including service call and keypad replacement.
