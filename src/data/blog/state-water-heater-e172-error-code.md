## State Water Heater E172 Error Code — What It Means

The E172 error code on State water heaters is not clearly documented in publicly available manufacturer materials, and its exact meaning appears to be model-specific. State directs users to locate the model and serial number via the rating plate QR code and consult the matching manual for precise troubleshooting. Based on verified guidance for State and A. O. Smith tankless units, codes related to ignition or combustion faults most commonly trace back to air intake blockages, low water flow, gas supply issues, or buildup on the heat exchanger and sensors.

If your State unit is displaying E172, the first step is to identify your exact model from the rating plate and obtain the official manual. Until you have model-specific documentation, the most common real-world culprits for ignition and combustion lockouts on these units are blocked air intakes, restricted exhaust venting, inadequate gas pressure, clogged inlet filters, and scale fouling on internal components.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screen** Lint, dust, or debris restricting combustion air supply is the most frequent cause of ignition failure on State tankless heaters.
- **Obstructed exhaust or vent termination** Ice, nests, or improper vent installation can prevent proper exhaust flow and trigger safety lockouts.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Closed fuel valve, undersized gas line, or low delivery pressure prevent the burner from lighting or sustaining flame.
- **Insufficient water flow** Clogged inlet filter, closed fixture valves, or low system pressure can prevent the unit from initiating heating cycle.
- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger or sensors** Hard water deposits on the heat exchanger, flame rod, or temperature sensor interfere with combustion detection and control.
- **Faulty fan or blower motor** A failing combustion air fan cannot deliver the airflow required to satisfy pre-ignition safety checks.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your model and serial number** on the rating plate and download the official State troubleshooting manual for your unit using the QR code or State's online support.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screen** by removing any lint, dust, or debris blocking the combustion air opening on the front or side of the unit.
3. **Check the exhaust vent termination** outdoors for blockages such as ice, bird nests, or debris and verify the vent is installed according to clearance and termination specifications in your manual.
4. **Confirm the gas supply valve** is fully open and verify that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally. If gas delivery is weak or absent, call a licensed technician to test gas line pressure and sizing.
5. **Inspect the inlet water filter** and clean or replace it if clogged. Open a hot water fixture and confirm adequate flow is reaching the heater.
6. **Perform a power reset** by turning off the unit at the service switch or breaker for thirty seconds, then restore power and attempt to run a hot water tap.
7. **Descale the heat exchanger and sensors** if your water is hard and the unit has not been serviced in over a year. Follow the descaling procedure in your model's service manual or schedule professional maintenance.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the E172 code persists after cleaning the air intake, checking the vent, and confirming adequate gas and water flow. Gas pressure testing, combustion analysis, control board diagnostics, and internal component replacement require specialized tools and training. Any work involving gas connections, venting modifications, or electrical controls should be performed by a qualified professional to maintain safety and warranty coverage. If you cannot locate the official troubleshooting guide for your specific State model, professional diagnosis is the safest path forward.
