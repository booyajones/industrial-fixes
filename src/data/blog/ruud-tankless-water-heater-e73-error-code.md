## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E73 Error — What It Means

The E73 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters indicates a fault detected by the unit's control board, often related to temperature sensing or internal wiring. Because error codes can vary by model and firmware version, the exact meaning of E73 is not universally documented across all Ruud tankless models. Some units use this code to flag a thermistor reading outside normal range, a wiring short, or a communication error between sensors and the board.

Before assuming a failed part, check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover to confirm what E73 means for your specific model. The most common pattern for E7x codes on tankless heaters involves temperature sensors or their wiring, so start diagnostics there.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a loose or corroded wire terminal at the thermistor. Inspect and clean all sensor connectors and test thermistor resistance before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~35%)** Vibration and heat cycles can work connectors loose or create corrosion on thermistor terminals, causing intermittent or out-of-range readings that trigger the error.
- **Failed thermistor (~30%)** A thermistor that has drifted out of specification or failed open or short will send incorrect temperature data to the control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring (~20%)** Wires can be pinched during service, rubbed through by vibration, or damaged by heat near the burner or heat exchanger.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main control board may have a failed input circuit or corrupted firmware that incorrectly interprets sensor signals.
- **Transient power surge or electrical noise (~5%)** A voltage spike or ground fault can cause the board to log a false fault code that clears after a power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and set it aside.
3. **Locate the thermistors** (typically two or three temperature sensors with small two-wire connectors near the heat exchanger inlet, outlet, and sometimes the burner).
4. **Inspect each connector** for corrosion, bent pins, or loose fit. Clean terminals with electrical contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
5. **Test each thermistor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance across its two terminals with a multimeter. Consult your model's service chart for acceptable resistance at ambient temperature.
6. **Replace any thermistor** that reads open (infinite resistance), shorted (near zero), or far outside the specified range.
7. **Check wiring along its length** for pinches, rubs, or heat damage, especially near metal edges and the burner assembly.
8. **Reassemble the cover**, restore power and gas, and run a hot water tap to test. If the error returns and all sensors and wiring are good, the control board may need replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water heater technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas lines or high-voltage wiring, if the error persists after replacing sensors and inspecting all wiring, or if you need to replace the control board and want the repair warrantied. A technician can also retrieve detailed fault logs from the board, perform gas-combustion analysis, and confirm that the unit's venting and gas pressure are correct, since some sensor errors can be secondary to combustion or airflow problems.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
