## Whirlpool Oven F7 Error Code — What It Means

F7 is not a single universal code across all Whirlpool ovens. On Whirlpool ranges, F7 E0 signals a problem with the oven door latch assembly or its wiring. The latch system cannot confirm proper door closure or lock state. On Whirlpool over-the-range microwaves, F7 means the appliance control board could not execute the requested function, pointing to a board or wiring fault. The exact meaning depends on whether you have a range or a microwave, so check your owner's manual if the display shows only F7 without a second digit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or latch assembly without first inspecting connectors and harnesses. A loose or burned terminal causes most F7 errors and costs nothing to reseat.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or damaged wiring (~45%)** Intermittent connections, burned terminals, or moisture in the door latch harness (ranges) or control board connector (microwaves) prevent proper signal feedback.
- **Failed door latch assembly (~30%)** On ranges showing F7 E0, a mechanically binding latch motor, failed micro-switch, or open coil inside the latch prevents the control from confirming door position.
- **Failed appliance control board (~15%)** On over-the-range microwaves, the main control board can lose the ability to execute commands due to component failure or corrupted firmware.
- **Inverter board or magnetron fault (~10%)** In some inverter-driven Whirlpool microwaves, a shorted magnetron or failed inverter board triggers F7 when the control board detects an abnormal high-voltage condition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait one full minute to clear temporary faults in the control memory.
2. **Restore power** and attempt a normal cook cycle to see whether the F7 code returns immediately or stays away.
3. **Access the control area** by removing the back panel (microwave) or the rear access panel and top trim (range) and locate the door latch harness or control board connector.
4. **Inspect every connector and wire** for loose pins, burned terminals, green corrosion, moisture, or cracked insulation and reseat all connectors firmly.
5. **Test the door latch assembly** (ranges only) by manually moving the latch bolt and checking continuity across the micro-switch terminals with the door open and closed.
6. **Replace the door latch assembly** (ranges) if it binds, shows open continuity, or fails to change state, or **replace the appliance control board** (microwaves) if wiring is intact and the fault persists.
7. **Check the inverter board and magnetron** (inverter microwaves only) if the fault returns after board replacement, looking for burned components, arcing marks, or shorted magnetron filament resistance.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside a powered microwave chassis (the inverter and magnetron retain lethal high voltage even when unplugged), if you lack a multimeter to test continuity and resistance, or if the fault returns after you have replaced the latch assembly or control board and confirmed all wiring is intact. Also call if your range uses a mechanical door lock tied to the self-clean interlock and you cannot determine which switch in the latch assembly has failed. A technician will bring the correct Whirlpool part number cross-reference, can safely discharge high-voltage capacitors in a microwave, and will test every component in the latch or control circuit before ordering parts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
