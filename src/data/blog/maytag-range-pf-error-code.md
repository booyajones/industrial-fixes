## Maytag Range PF Error Code — What It Means

PF stands for Power Failure on Maytag ranges and ovens. The control board detected that power to the appliance was interrupted, lost, or seen as invalid during operation or power-up. This code can appear after an actual blackout, a brief voltage drop, or even during startup if the control senses abnormal supply behavior. Maytag also notes that unexpected line noise or incorrect voltage during a power event can trigger PF, so the code is not limited to a complete loss of power.

The display may show PF after a household outage, a breaker trip, or if you pressed keys before the 10-second delay after power-up on some models. In the field, a recurring PF should also raise suspicion of supply-side issues such as loose connections at the terminal block, a faulty power cord, or unstable household voltage, although the manufacturer ties the code primarily to power interruption or incorrect voltage sensing at the control.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main electronic control board without first verifying the incoming supply voltage and terminal-block connections. Clear the code and reset the breaker for one minute before ordering a control.

## Common Causes

- **Actual power outage or momentary voltage loss (~50%)** A blackout, brownout, or brief interruption in household power causes the control to log PF when it restarts.
- **Circuit breaker trip or reset event (~25%)** The range breaker opened or was manually cycled, cutting power and triggering the fault code.
- **Line noise or incorrect voltage during power restoration (~15%)** Voltage spikes or sags on the supply line during an outage can be seen as invalid by the control, even if power never fully dropped.
- **Keys pressed before the startup delay completes (~5%)** On some Maytag models, pressing buttons within the first 10 seconds after power-up can cause the control to display PF.
- **Loose or corroded terminal-block connection (~3%)** A poor connection at the appliance terminal block can cause intermittent voltage drops that the control reads as a power failure.
- **Faulty or intermittent power cord (~2%)** A damaged cord can momentarily interrupt supply and trigger PF, especially if the appliance is moved or vibrations loosen the plug.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off all burners and the oven.** Make sure no cooking cycle is active before you reset the control.
2. **Press Cancel** to clear the PF display. On some models you may also need to reset the time of day using the clock or settings buttons.
3. **Locate your home electrical panel** and identify the double-pole breaker(s) feeding the range. Maytag ranges typically use a 240 V circuit.
4. **Turn the breaker(s) off** and leave them off for at least one minute. This power cycle allows the control to reset fully and clear any latched fault.
5. **Turn the breaker(s) back on** and return to the range. Wait at least 10 seconds before pressing any buttons to allow the control to complete its startup sequence.
6. **Test the range** by setting a burner or the oven to confirm normal operation. If PF does not reappear and all functions work, the issue was a one-time power event.
7. **Inspect the terminal block and power cord** if PF returns within a few days. Turn off the breaker, pull the range forward, and remove the rear access panel. Look for loose screws, burned or discolored wires, or corrosion at the terminal block. Tighten any loose connections and replace the power cord if the insulation is cracked or conductors are frayed.
8. **Check incoming voltage at the terminal block** with a multimeter if you have one. You should see approximately 240 V across the two hot legs and approximately 120 V from each hot leg to neutral. Consult your model's service manual for exact specifications.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the PF code returns repeatedly after you have cleared it and reset the breaker, or if you are uncomfortable working with 240 V wiring. A technician will verify the incoming supply voltage at the terminal block, inspect all connections for heat damage or corrosion, and test the electronic control board for proper power sensing. If your household voltage is unstable or you see flickering lights elsewhere in the home, contact an electrician to inspect the service panel and branch circuit feeding the range. Do not attempt to remove the range back panel or work inside the appliance while the breaker is on.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts (power cord if damaged), 10-20 min to clear and reset. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for service call and wiring inspection, $250-450 if control board replacement is confirmed necessary.
