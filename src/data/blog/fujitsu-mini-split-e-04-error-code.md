## Fujitsu E:04 Error Code — What It Means

The E:04 error on Fujitsu mini-split systems indicates an indoor unit pipe sensor open fault. The system detects that the pipe temperature thermistor circuit is open, disconnected, or missing entirely. This sensor monitors the temperature of the refrigerant pipe inside the air handler, and when the control board cannot read a valid resistance signal from it, the E:04 code appears.

This is an electrical or component failure, not a refrigerant issue. The thermistor itself may have failed open, the connector may be loose or corroded, or the wiring between the sensor and the indoor PCB may be broken. Less commonly, the sensor input circuit on the indoor controller board has failed.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or unplugged sensor connector** The pipe thermistor connector at the indoor PCB has backed out, become loose, or was never fully seated during installation or prior service.
- **Failed pipe thermistor** The thermistor itself has failed open-circuit and no longer provides a valid resistance reading to the control board.
- **Broken sensor lead or wiring** The thermistor lead wires or the harness between the sensor and the indoor PCB have been cut, pinched, or broken, creating an open circuit.
- **Corroded or damaged connector pins** Moisture or age has corroded the connector pins at the PCB header, preventing a good electrical connection to the sensor.
- **Failed indoor controller PCB** The sensor input circuit on the indoor unit's main control board has failed, even though the sensor and wiring are intact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off all power** to the mini-split system at the breaker or disconnect switch before opening the indoor unit.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** and locate the pipe thermistor, which is a small bead or capsule sensor attached to the refrigerant pipe with a metal clip or tape.
3. **Trace the sensor wire** back to the indoor PCB and inspect the connector for backing out, corrosion, bent pins, or obvious disconnection, then press the connector firmly into place if loose.
4. **Disconnect the sensor connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure the resistance directly at the thermistor leads, checking for an open reading (infinite resistance) that confirms a failed sensor.
5. **Compare the resistance reading** to the thermistor characteristics table in your model's service manual to verify whether the sensor is within specification at room temperature.
6. **Replace the pipe thermistor** if it reads open or out of spec, securing the new sensor to the pipe in the same location and orientation as the original.
7. **Restore power and run** the system, then verify that the E:04 code clears and the unit operates normally. If the code persists after sensor replacement and wiring is intact, suspect the indoor controller PCB and consult a technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical components, if you cannot locate the pipe sensor or PCB connectors, or if the fault persists after you have verified the sensor connector is seated and the thermistor tests within specification. If the indoor controller board is suspected, replacement requires matching the exact board revision and often involves transferring configuration settings or pairing with the outdoor unit. Technicians also carry the thermistor resistance tables and diagnostic tools needed to pinpoint PCB failures quickly.
