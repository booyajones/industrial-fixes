## LG Mini Split CH33 Error Code — What It Means

CH33 on an LG mini split indicates that the discharge temperature of compressor number 2 has exceeded safe limits. This is a protection alarm that shuts down the compressor to prevent damage from overheating. The fault is tied to a sensor on the compressor discharge line in the outdoor unit, not the indoor room temperature sensor. LG's troubleshooting documentation treats this as a discharge-temperature protection event in Multi V and inverter systems.

## Common Causes

- **Low refrigerant charge or leakage** Reduced refrigerant mass causes discharge temperatures to climb, and LG lists this as the primary cause of CH33 faults.
- **Piping leaks or improper piping condition** Brazed joints, flare connections, and deformed refrigerant piping can all allow refrigerant loss and trigger the high-temperature alarm.
- **Discharge temperature sensor problems** An open, shorted, or incorrectly installed sensor on the compressor discharge line will report false temperature readings and trip the fault.
- **Sensor wiring or connection issues** Damaged wires, loose connectors at the outdoor PCB, or corrosion can interrupt the sensor signal and cause the error.
- **Electronic expansion valve (EEV) malfunction** A stuck or non-responsive EEV prevents proper refrigerant flow regulation, which can lead to elevated discharge temperatures.
- **Outdoor PCB or control board fault** If the sensor and refrigerant system check out normal, a problem in the outdoor unit's control circuitry may be generating false alarms.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault and identify compressor 2.** Confirm the CH33 code appears on the display or remote and note that it refers specifically to compressor number 2 in the outdoor unit.
2. **Inspect for refrigerant leaks first.** Check all brazed joints, flare connections, and piping for oil stains, bubbles when applying leak detector solution, or any visible deformation.
3. **Locate and inspect the discharge temperature sensor** on compressor 2's discharge line to confirm it is properly seated and secured in the correct position.
4. **Measure sensor resistance at the outdoor PCB connector.** Using a multimeter, check that resistance is between 2 kΩ and 5 MΩ. The fault triggers if resistance is greater than 5 MΩ or less than 2 kΩ according to LG's troubleshooting guide.
5. **Check sensor supply voltage at the external PCB.** Measure DC voltage at the discharge temperature sensor connector and verify it reads 5 V, which is the normal supply per LG's service documentation.
6. **Test the electronic expansion valve (EEV) after a power reset.** Turn off power to the outdoor unit, wait 30 seconds, then restore power and listen or feel for the EEV to open and close during the reset cycle.
7. **Replace the discharge temperature sensor** if resistance or voltage readings are out of spec, or replace the outdoor PCB if all sensor and refrigerant checks pass but the fault persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not certified to handle refrigerant, because diagnosing CH33 requires pressure testing, leak detection, and potentially recovering and recharging refrigerant. If you have already verified that the discharge temperature sensor readings and supply voltage are normal but the error persists, a technician with LG service documentation and specialized tools will be needed to troubleshoot the outdoor PCB and EEV operation. Refrigerant work and electrical diagnostics on inverter-driven compressor systems carry safety risks and typically require EPA certification and manufacturer training.
