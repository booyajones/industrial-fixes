## What this code means
On most Kenmore dishwashers built on Whirlpool or Maytag platforms, F8E2 is a dispenser/diverter system fault. The control board is not seeing the dispenser or diverter move to the expected position, or it is receiving an abnormal signal from that assembly. In plain terms, the machine thinks the dispenser/diverter mechanism is stuck, has failed, or is not being reported correctly.

Because Kenmore is a brand label and not the original equipment manufacturer, the exact wording can vary by model family. On Whirlpool-built platforms, the practical meaning is a dispenser/diverter position or feedback fault. The code is cleared by cycling power to the control, which places it in the family of control-detected component faults rather than a simple user reset issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Check the dispenser/diverter motor for binding, inspect all connectors for corrosion or looseness, and test the position-sense circuit before spending on a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed dispenser/diverter motor or actuator (~40%)** The motor cannot index to the correct position, causing the control to time out and flag the fault.
- **Sticking or mechanically obstructed dispenser/diverter assembly (~25%)** Detergent buildup, stripped gears, or a jammed cam cause the motor to hunt or stall.
- **Bad position-sense switch, tracer circuit, or wiring harness issue (~20%)** A broken tracer circuit or damaged wiring between the dispenser assembly and the main control interrupts feedback signals.
- **Corroded, loose, or contaminated connectors (~10%)** Moisture or corrosion at the control, user interface, or dispenser assembly connectors can interrupt position signals.
- **Main control board failure (~5%)** The board itself fails to read or process dispenser position signals correctly after all mechanical and wiring checks pass.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **De-energize the dishwasher** at the circuit breaker or unplug it completely before opening any panels or touching wiring.
2. **Cycle power by removing power for 30 seconds** as Maytag instructs for a control reset, then restore power and check if the code returns.
3. **Access the dispenser/diverter assembly** (typically on the inner door or under the lower rack) and inspect the mechanism for mechanical binding, stripped gears, detergent buildup, or a jammed cam.
4. **Check all wiring and connectors** from the dispenser/diverter assembly to the main control board for corrosion, looseness, damage, or pushed-out terminals, and clean or reseat as needed.
5. **Verify the position-sense circuit** in the dispenser/diverter assembly and check continuity of the tracer wiring path back to the control board, looking for opens or shorts.
6. **If the assembly and wiring test good, replace the dispenser/diverter motor or actuator** as the most common failed component for this code.
7. **Reassemble, restore power, and run a short cycle** to confirm the dispenser/diverter indexes correctly and the fault does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with electrical connections or disassembling the dishwasher door to access the dispenser mechanism. Also call if you have replaced the dispenser motor and cleaned all connectors but the fault returns, since diagnosis of the tracer circuit and main control board often requires a multimeter and wiring diagram specific to your model. A technician can also confirm whether your Kenmore model uses a combined diverter/dispenser assembly or separate components, which varies by platform.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $60-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
