## What this code means
On Whirlpool and KitchenAid dishwashers, F7E1 indicates a wash motor failure or wash motor circuit problem. The control board expects to see normal circulation from the wash motor during the cycle, but it does not detect proper motor operation. The machine may fill with water but will not complete the wash because the circulation pump is not running as expected.

This is a motor-circuit fault, not a water-temperature issue. The control has lost communication with the wash motor or cannot drive it, so the cycle halts and posts F7E1. The fault points to the motor itself, the wiring harness that feeds it, or a connector problem in that circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first when the real problem is a loose or corroded connector at the wash motor. Always inspect the motor harness and connectors for damage or looseness before ordering any control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed wash motor (~50%)** The circulation motor has burned out or seized internally and no longer spins when powered.
- **Loose or damaged motor connector (~25%)** The plug at the motor or sump has backed out, corroded pins, or a broken locking tab that interrupts the circuit.
- **Broken or pinched motor wiring (~15%)** Wires in the harness running to the wash motor are cut, pinched by the sump, or have frayed insulation causing an open circuit.
- **Obstruction jamming the pump impeller (~7%)** A piece of broken glass, bone, or hard debris has locked the impeller so the motor cannot turn and draws excess current or stalls.
- **Control board motor-relay failure (~3%)** The relay or triac on the board that energizes the wash motor has failed and cannot close the circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or by unplugging the dishwasher and wait five minutes to reset the control.
2. **Restore power** and run a short cycle to see if the fault clears. If F7E1 returns immediately, proceed with diagnosis.
3. **Access the sump area** by removing the lower dish rack, spray arm, and filter assembly so you can see the wash-motor housing.
4. **Inspect all connectors** at the motor and pump for looseness, corrosion, or damaged pins. Unplug and re-seat each connector firmly.
5. **Check for obstructions** by rotating the impeller by hand (with power off). It should spin freely. Remove any debris blocking the pump.
6. **Enter service diagnostics** per your model's tech sheet (usually taped inside the door) and run the motor test to observe whether the motor energizes.
7. **Test motor continuity** with a multimeter if accessible. Consult your model's wiring diagram for the correct terminal points and expected resistance range. Replace the motor if open or shorted.
8. **Clear stored faults** after repair by cycling power or following the diagnostic-mode exit procedure, then run a full test cycle to confirm the fix.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 120-volt wiring inside the dishwasher, if the sump assembly requires removal of the tub liner or special tools, or if diagnostic mode and motor testing are unfamiliar. A technician has the wiring diagram, correct test values for your model, and can quickly isolate whether the fault is in the motor, harness, or control board. If you have already replaced the motor and connectors and F7E1 persists, the control board relay may have failed and a pro can confirm that with direct circuit testing before you buy another expensive part.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $100-200 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
