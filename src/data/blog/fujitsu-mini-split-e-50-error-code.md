## Fujitsu E:50 Error Code — What It Means

The E:50 error code on Fujitsu mini-split systems indicates that the indoor unit is not receiving a valid temperature signal from the indoor coil thermistor (evaporator sensor). The system has detected an abnormally low resistance or otherwise invalid reading from the sensor circuit, which usually means the thermistor is shorted. Because the unit cannot accurately measure the indoor heat exchanger temperature, it will stop or behave erratically to protect itself.

This fault is typically caused by a shorted sensor, damaged wiring, moisture contamination at the connector, or less commonly a failed indoor control board. The system needs a believable coil temperature reading to operate safely, so the fault must be resolved before normal operation can resume.

## Common Causes

- **Indoor coil thermistor shorted** The evaporator temperature sensor has failed internally and is reading an abnormally low resistance, sending invalid data to the control board.
- **Harness or connector damage** A loose, corroded, or damaged connection at the sensor plug or wiring creates an erroneous circuit reading that triggers the fault.
- **Moisture at the sensor connector** Water intrusion or condensation around the thermistor plug creates a short or contamination that affects the sensor reading.
- **Indoor control board fault** If the sensor and wiring test correctly but the fault persists, the controller PCB may not be interpreting the sensor input properly.
- **Airflow restriction or icing** A blocked filter or iced coil can cause abnormal temperature readings or physical damage to the sensor over time.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the system** by turning off the unit at the thermostat and the circuit breaker for 30 seconds, then restore power and check if the fault clears, as electronic faults sometimes resolve with a reset.
2. **Inspect airflow and coil condition** by removing and cleaning the indoor air filter, checking that the head unit is not blocked, and looking for ice buildup or water intrusion around the evaporator coil and sensor area.
3. **Locate and inspect the indoor coil thermistor** by accessing the indoor unit's evaporator compartment, finding the small sensor clipped or strapped to the coil surface, and checking the connector and wiring for damage, corrosion, loose pins, or moisture.
4. **Test the thermistor resistance** by unplugging the sensor, measuring resistance across its leads with a multimeter, and comparing the reading to the manufacturer's thermistor characteristics table in your service manual (resistance should match the current coil temperature).
5. **Dry and reseat connections** if you find moisture or contamination at the sensor plug, then clear any water sources and verify the connector clicks firmly into place.
6. **Replace the indoor coil thermistor** if resistance is out of spec, the sensor is visibly damaged, or it reads zero ohms (shorted), using the correct replacement part for your Fujitsu model.
7. **Test the indoor control board** if the sensor and wiring test correctly but the fault remains, as the PCB may have a failed sensor input circuit, and consult your service manual or a technician before replacing the board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside the indoor unit, if you do not have a multimeter and the service manual with thermistor resistance tables, or if the fault persists after replacing the sensor and inspecting all wiring. Refrigerant-circuit work and board-level diagnosis require specialized tools and EPA certification. If the unit is under warranty, contact Fujitsu or your installer before opening the unit to avoid voiding coverage.
