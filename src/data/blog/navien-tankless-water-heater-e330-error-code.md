## Navien E330 Error Code — What It Means

The E330 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically signals an exhaust overheat condition or a problem with the flame sensing system. The unit detects either excessive temperature in the exhaust path or the flame rod is not properly sensing combustion. This is a safety feature that shuts down the heater to prevent damage or unsafe operation.

Because the exact meaning can vary slightly across Navien models, consult your owner's manual or the diagnostic chart inside the front cover for your specific unit. The code generally points to combustion issues, whether from restricted venting, inadequate airflow, or a flame sensor that cannot confirm proper ignition.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame rod or igniter without first cleaning the existing rod. A flame rod coated in soot or mineral deposits cannot sense the flame properly. Remove the burner assembly and wipe the rod with fine sandpaper or a cloth, then test the heater before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Soot, mineral scale, or oxidation on the flame rod prevents it from sensing the flame, triggering a false overheat or flame-detection fault.
- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~20%)** Ice, debris, or a bird nest in the vent terminal restricts airflow, causing exhaust temperatures to rise and triggering the overheat sensor.
- **Faulty flame rod (~15%)** A cracked or degraded flame rod fails to conduct current properly and cannot confirm ignition even when clean.
- **Inadequate combustion air supply (~10%)** Restricted air intake or a clogged air filter starves the burner of oxygen, leading to incomplete combustion and higher exhaust temperatures.
- **Defective exhaust temperature sensor (~5%)** The sensor itself may be failing or reading incorrectly, reporting an overheat condition when exhaust temperatures are normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the mounting screws, then locate the burner assembly inside the combustion chamber.
3. **Disconnect the flame rod wire** and remove the screws holding the burner assembly in place, then carefully slide the burner out.
4. **Inspect the flame rod** for soot, white mineral deposits, or corrosion, then clean it gently with fine-grit sandpaper or an emery cloth until shiny.
5. **Check the exhaust vent** terminal outside for blockages, ice, or debris and clear any obstructions you find.
6. **Reinstall the burner assembly**, reconnect the flame rod wire, and replace the front cover, making sure all screws are snug.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then press the reset button on the control panel and open a hot-water tap to test for normal operation and verify the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error returns immediately after cleaning the flame rod and clearing the vent, or if you see physical damage to the burner assembly or heat exchanger. A technician can test the flame rod current with a microamp meter, verify proper gas pressure and combustion air ratios, and inspect the exhaust temperature sensor and control board connections. If the unit is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts if cleaning does not work, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for service call and part.
