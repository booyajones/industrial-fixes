## Weil-McLain A83 Error — What It Means

The A83 code on a Weil-McLain boiler indicates an ignition-related lockout. The control attempted to ignite the burner but did not detect flame within the allowed trial period, so it stopped the sequence and locked out for safety. This means the boiler either never lit or the flame sensor could not prove the flame was present.

The exact meaning and corrective steps can vary slightly by model family (Ultra, AquaBalance, etc.), so always consult your specific boiler's service manual for the authoritative A83 definition and troubleshooting flow. Common underlying causes include no gas reaching the burner, a dirty or oxidized flame rod that cannot sense the flame, or a weak or damaged ignitor that cannot light the gas.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the gas valve or control board first. Before ordering expensive parts, clean the flame rod with fine emery cloth and verify gas is flowing to the valve.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or oxidized flame rod (~35%)** Soot, moisture, or oxidation on the flame sensor prevents the control from proving flame even when the burner lights.
- **Gas supply issue (~30%)** Shutoff closed, low gas pressure, failed gas valve, or utility service interruption prevents gas from reaching the burner.
- **Weak or damaged ignitor (~20%)** Cracked, worn, or misaligned hot-surface igniter cannot produce enough heat to light the gas.
- **Loose wiring or poor ground (~10%)** Corroded connectors, loose neutral, or bad ground connection interrupt flame-sense current or ignitor power.
- **Blocked venting or combustion air (~5%)** Obstructed flue pipe or insufficient combustion air prevents stable ignition and flame proving.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the boiler switch or breaker and close the manual gas shutoff valve before touching any components.
2. **Retrieve your boiler's service manual** by model number to confirm A83 applies to ignition lockout and to find model-specific wiring diagrams and specifications.
3. **Check the fault history** on the control display (if available) to see whether A83 is a new event or a repeat ignition fault, then note the code before resetting.
4. **Inspect the flame rod** for soot, corrosion, or misalignment. Remove it (typically one screw or bracket) and clean the ceramic insulator and metal rod tip with fine emery cloth, then reinstall.
5. **Inspect the ignitor** for cracks, heavy deposits, or poor positioning relative to the burner ports. Replace the ignitor if it is damaged or does not glow bright orange during a call.
6. **Verify gas supply and valve operation** by confirming the shutoff is open, the gas valve is being energized (listen for a click), and that gas pressure at the inlet is within the range listed in your model's manual.
7. **Check all electrical connections** to the ignitor, flame rod, and gas valve for corrosion, looseness, or damage. Verify the neutral and ground wires are secure at the boiler and main panel.
8. **Restore power and gas, then reset the lockout** once you have corrected the likely cause. Observe a full ignition cycle to confirm the burner lights and the control proves flame without locking out again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod and checking the gas shutoff does not clear the A83, or if you see a cracked ignitor or suspect a failed gas valve. Gas work and combustion diagnostics require proper tools (manometer, multimeter, and combustion analyzer) and training to make sure safe operation. A technician will measure gas pressure, test flame-sense current, verify venting and combustion air, and replace any failed components according to the manufacturer's procedures. Repeated resets without finding the root cause can damage the control or create a safety hazard.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
