## Fujitsu E:72 Error Code — What It Means

Error code E:72 on a Fujitsu mini-split indicates a compressor thermistor error. The outdoor unit's compressor temperature sensor is reading open, short-circuited, or otherwise out of range while the compressor is powered or running. This is an electrical sensor fault, not a refrigerant or mechanical issue. The system detects that the thermistor circuit monitoring compressor temperature is not sending valid data to the control board, so it shuts down to protect the compressor from potential overheating.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or disconnected thermistor connector** The plug at the compressor thermistor or at the outdoor PCB has backed out, vibrated loose, or was not fully seated during installation or prior service.
- **Broken or open thermistor wiring** A wire in the thermistor harness has broken due to vibration, rodent damage, or wear, creating an open circuit that triggers the fault.
- **Shorted or failed compressor thermistor** The temperature sensor itself has failed internally, either showing a dead short or resistance values far outside the normal range for any temperature.
- **Corroded terminals or connector pins** Moisture intrusion or age has corroded the thermistor plug or PCB terminals, causing intermittent or permanent loss of signal continuity.
- **Defective outdoor controller PCB** The main board misreads a good thermistor signal or has failed in the sensor input circuit, reporting E:72 even when the sensor and wiring test normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker for at least 10 minutes, then restore power and observe whether the E:72 code clears or returns after a short run (this resets transient faults).
2. **Inspect the outdoor unit** and locate the compressor thermistor (a small sensor mounted on or near the compressor body with a two-wire connector).
3. **Check all connectors** along the thermistor circuit for looseness, corrosion, or pushed-back pins, and reseat each connector firmly at both the sensor and the outdoor PCB.
4. **Measure thermistor resistance** with a multimeter by unplugging the sensor and reading across its terminals, then compare the value to the resistance-versus-temperature chart in your model's service manual.
5. **Test continuity** from the thermistor connector back to the outdoor PCB to confirm no open wiring, and inspect the harness for breaks, pinches, or damage.
6. **Replace the compressor thermistor** if resistance is out of spec (infinite, near zero, or far from the chart value) and retest the system after reconnecting.
7. **Replace the outdoor controller PCB** if the thermistor and all wiring test good but the fault persists, then follow any setup or address-configuration steps required by your model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live 240V power, do not own a multimeter, or cannot locate the compressor thermistor in the outdoor unit. This repair requires interpreting resistance charts, tracing low-voltage sensor wiring, and potentially replacing a control board with refrigerant-circuit access. If the fault returns immediately after a power reset or you see any refrigerant oil around the compressor, professional diagnosis is the safest route to avoid misdiagnosis or compressor damage.
