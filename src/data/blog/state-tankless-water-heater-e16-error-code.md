## State Tankless Water Heater E16 Error Code — What It Means

E16 (or EC16) is not a widely documented State/A. O. Smith tankless code in available manufacturer literature. The closest verified match is Noritz, where error 16 means the outlet water temperature has risen abnormally high and the unit has shut down to protect itself. If your State tankless shows E16, treat it as an over-temperature fault until you confirm the exact meaning in your model's service manual. The heater detects that water leaving the unit is hotter than safe operating limits and stops firing to prevent damage or scalding.

This code typically appears when the unit cannot transfer heat away from the burner fast enough. That happens when water flow is too low, the heat exchanger is restricted by scale, or a temperature sensor is reading falsely high. Check your model's rating plate and service documentation to confirm the code definition before starting repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Low water flow through the heater** Insufficient flow keeps water in contact with the burner longer, so outlet temperature climbs too high and trips the limit.
- **Clogged inlet screen or filter** Sediment blocks the water path at the inlet, starving the heat exchanger and letting temperature spike.
- **Scale buildup in the heat exchanger** Hard-water mineral deposits insulate the exchanger tubes, reducing heat transfer and raising outlet temperature.
- **Faulty outlet temperature sensor or thermistor** A drifted or failing sensor reports false high readings to the control board, triggering the error even when water is at normal temperature.
- **Partially closed isolation valve or downstream restriction** A valve that is not fully open or a kinked line downstream reduces system flow and causes the same overheat condition.
- **Control board misreading temperature input** Corroded connectors or damaged wiring between the sensor and board can send erratic signals that look like an over-temperature event.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number and code definition** by checking the service label on the heater and downloading the manual from the State/A. O. Smith website, because E16 is not uniformly documented across all State tankless platforms.
2. **Turn off power and water supply** at the breaker and isolation valves, then relieve pressure by opening a hot tap downstream.
3. **Inspect and clean the inlet screen** by removing the cold-water service valve or inlet union and flushing any sediment from the mesh filter.
4. **Measure inlet and outlet water temperatures** using a contact thermometer or infrared gun while the unit attempts to fire, to confirm whether the heater is genuinely overheating or the sensor is faulty.
5. **Flush and descale the heat exchanger** by connecting a recirculation pump and running manufacturer-recommended descaling solution through the unit for 45 minutes if you see low flow or suspect scale.
6. **Test the outlet temperature sensor** by unplugging its connector and measuring resistance across the thermistor leads with a multimeter, then comparing the reading to the service manual's temperature-resistance chart for your model.
7. **Check all wiring and connectors** at the temperature sensor and control board for corrosion, loose pins, or damaged insulation, and clean or replace any suspect connections before reassembling and testing the unit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate E16 in your State model's documentation, if descaling and sensor tests do not clear the code, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections and electrical diagnostics. A pro will have factory service manuals, correct resistance tables, and the tools to verify gas pressure and combustion settings. Also call if the heat exchanger shows heavy scale or corrosion that cannot be cleaned, because replacement requires disconnecting gas lines and venting and must meet local code.
