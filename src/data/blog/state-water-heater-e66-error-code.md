## State Water Heater E66 Error — What It Means

The E66 error code indicates a bypass water servo abnormality. This fault points to a problem with the bypass servo itself, its wire harness connection, or the harness connection to the control board. The bypass servo regulates water flow through the heat exchanger, and when the control board cannot communicate with it properly, the unit throws this code and may shut down to protect the system.

Because State uses multiple platform designs, the exact definition of E66 can vary by model family. The most commonly documented meaning is a servo connection or circuit fault. Always verify your specific model's fault legend in the owner's manual or service literature before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or disconnected bypass servo plug** The electrical connector at the servo can vibrate loose during operation or become unseated during maintenance.
- **Corroded or moisture-damaged connector pins** Water intrusion or condensation in the connector can corrode terminals and interrupt the signal between the servo and board.
- **Damaged wire harness** The harness between the servo and control board can develop breaks, abrasion, or heat damage that interrupts continuity.
- **Poor circuit board connection** The harness connector at the control board can become loose, corroded, or improperly seated, breaking the communication path.
- **Failed bypass water servo** Internal servo motor or position sensor failure prevents the unit from modulating water flow correctly.
- **Defective control board** The control board may have a failed output driver or input circuit that cannot send or receive servo signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the water heater** at the circuit breaker or gas valve and wait two minutes for capacitors to discharge.
2. **Remove the front access panel** and locate the bypass water servo, typically mounted near the heat exchanger or water valve assembly.
3. **Inspect the servo connector** for full seating, push it firmly into place, and look for corrosion, moisture, or damaged pins.
4. **Trace the wire harness** from the servo to the control board and check for abrasion, pinching, breaks, or loose terminals at both ends.
5. **Verify the harness connector at the control board** is fully seated and the retaining clip (if present) is engaged.
6. **Restore power and monitor** for the E66 code. If it clears, run a hot water tap for five minutes to confirm normal operation under demand.
7. **If the fault persists**, isolate the failed component by substituting the servo or harness if you have spares, or contact a technician with the diagnostic tools to test the board and servo independently.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working around electrical connections or gas lines, if the fault returns after reconnecting all plugs, or if you do not have the service manual for your specific State model. A technician can perform voltage and resistance tests on the servo and board to isolate the failed component without guessing, and they will have access to manufacturer test procedures and correct part numbers for your unit. If your water heater is still under warranty, professional diagnosis and repair may be required to preserve coverage.
