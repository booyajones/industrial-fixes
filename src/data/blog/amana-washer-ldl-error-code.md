## Amana Ld Error Code — What It Means

The Ld error code on Amana top-load and high-efficiency washers stands for "long drain" and indicates the machine is taking too long to drain water. Amana's official product help confirms this is a drain pump system problem where the washer cannot empty within the expected time limit.

The code typically appears during or after the drain cycle when water remains in the tub. The washer may pause mid-cycle or stop completely. This fault points to a restriction or failure somewhere in the drain path, from the hose routing all the way to the pump itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the drain pump immediately, but the majority of Ld codes are caused by incorrect hose installation. Check the hose depth, height, and routing before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose inserted too deep (~35%)** Amana specifies the hose must not extend more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe or it creates a siphon block that prevents proper draining.
- **Drain hose end too low (~20%)** The hose end must be higher than 36 inches from the floor to prevent siphoning and allow the washer to drain at the correct rate.
- **Kinked drain hose (~20%)** A sharp bend or kink anywhere along the hose restricts water flow and causes the long-drain fault.
- **Clogged drain hose (~15%)** Lint, detergent buildup, or debris inside the hose narrows the passage and slows drainage below the acceptable threshold.
- **Failed drain pump (~10%)** If the hose setup and drain path are correct, the pump itself may be weak or seized and unable to expel water quickly enough.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and pull it away from the wall to access the rear drain hose.
2. **Measure the standpipe insertion depth** using a ruler or tape measure. The hose should extend no more than 4.5 inches into the pipe.
3. **Check the hose end height** from the floor. It must be higher than 36 inches (Amana recommends a minimum standpipe height of 39 inches for proper installation).
4. **Inspect the hose for kinks** along its entire length. Straighten any bends and make sure the hose follows a smooth path without sharp turns.
5. **Disconnect the hose** from the standpipe and the washer. Pour a bucket of water through it to check for clogs. If water flows slowly or backs up, flush with hot water or replace the hose.
6. **Reconnect the hose** using the U-shape drain hose form. Secure it to the standpipe without taping over the drain opening, which can create back-pressure.
7. **Test the washer** by running a drain and spin cycle. If the Ld code returns and all hose checks passed, replace the drain pump assembly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified the drain hose insertion depth is 4.5 inches or less, the hose end is above 36 inches, the hose is free of kinks and clogs, and the Ld code still appears after testing. The drain pump will need replacement, and a pro can also check for a blocked internal drain path or a control-board fault that incorrectly triggers the code. If you are uncomfortable working with the pump or the washer's internal plumbing, schedule service rather than risk a water leak or electrical short.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts if pump replacement needed, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 for service call and pump replacement.
