## Navien Tankless Water Heater E230 Error — What It Means

The E230 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a problem with the exhaust system. This typically means the unit is detecting abnormal exhaust flow, blocked venting, or a failure in the exhaust fan or related sensors. The heater will shut down to prevent unsafe operation until the issue is resolved.

The exhaust system is monitored to make sure combustion gases leave the unit safely. When airflow is restricted or the fan cannot maintain proper pressure, the control board throws this code. Because exhaust problems can involve combustion and gas safety, accurate diagnosis is important.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust fan motor when the real problem is a blocked vent termination or collapsed inner liner in concentric pipe. Inspect the full vent run outdoors and indoors for obstructions, ice, or damage before ordering a fan.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~45%)** Ice buildup, debris, animal nests, or a collapsed inner pipe liner can obstruct the exhaust path and trigger the code.
- **Failed exhaust fan motor (~25%)** The fan may stop spinning or run too slowly, preventing proper draft and causing the pressure switch to fault.
- **Faulty exhaust air pressure switch (~15%)** The switch that monitors exhaust flow can fail or drift out of calibration, reporting a false blockage.
- **Loose or disconnected exhaust piping (~10%)** Joints that have separated or corroded through allow exhaust gases to leak, disrupting the pressure signal.
- **Control board or wiring issue (~5%)** Damaged wiring or a faulty board can misread sensor signals and generate the error even when the exhaust system is clear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and shut the gas supply valve to the heater before any inspection or repair.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vent termination** for ice, snow, bird nests, or other debris and clear any blockage you find.
3. **Check the entire exhaust pipe run** indoors for loose joints, corrosion, or collapsed sections, especially the inner pipe of concentric venting.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the heater and locate the exhaust fan assembly, then listen for unusual noise or verify that the fan spins freely by hand when power is off.
5. **Test the exhaust fan motor** for continuity with a multimeter and inspect the wiring harness for loose or corroded connectors.
6. **Examine the air pressure switch** (usually a small round switch with rubber tubing attached) for cracks, clogs in the sensing tube, or signs of water damage.
7. **Reassemble the unit** and restore power, then monitor the heater through a call for hot water to confirm the error is cleared and the fan runs smoothly.
8. **If the code persists** after clearing blockages and verifying fan operation, call a qualified technician to test the pressure switch and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot safely access the outdoor vent termination, if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, or if the error returns after you have cleared obvious blockages. Exhaust system faults involve combustion safety, so accurate diagnosis with a manometer and combustion analyzer is often required. A technician can measure actual exhaust pressure, test the fan motor under load, and verify that the venting is sized and installed correctly. Any work involving gas connections, control board replacement, or modifications to the venting system should be left to a licensed plumber or HVAC technician.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
