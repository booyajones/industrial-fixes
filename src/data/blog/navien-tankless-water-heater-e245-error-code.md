## Navien E245 Error Code — What It Means

The E245 error code on Navien tankless water heaters signals a problem with combustion air pressure or the air pressure sensor system. The unit has detected that proper airflow for safe combustion is not being maintained, and it shuts down to prevent unsafe operation.

This code typically points to either a physical blockage preventing fresh air from entering or exhaust gases from leaving, or a failure in the pressure sensor that monitors the combustion chamber. The heater will not operate until the issue is resolved and the error is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the air pressure sensor switch first, but most E245 codes are caused by simple vent blockages. Always inspect and clear all intake and exhaust pipes before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent (~50%)** Leaves, debris, snow, ice, or animal nests can obstruct the PVC vent pipes, preventing proper airflow and triggering the pressure sensor.
- **Condensation freeze in vent pipes (~20%)** In freezing weather, water vapor can freeze inside the exhaust or intake termination, blocking airflow and causing the pressure switch to fault.
- **Failed air pressure sensor or switch (~15%)** The pressure switch that monitors combustion air can fail electrically or mechanically, sending a false signal even when airflow is normal.
- **Improper vent installation or length (~10%)** Vent pipes that are too long, have too many elbows, or slope incorrectly can cause pressure issues that trip the sensor.
- **Disconnected or cracked pressure sensor hose (~5%)** The small rubber hose connecting the combustion chamber to the pressure switch can crack, disconnect, or become clogged with debris.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the electrical panel or disconnect switch to make sure safe working conditions.
2. **Inspect outdoor vent terminations** for the air intake and exhaust, removing any visible debris, leaves, snow, ice, or nests that may block airflow.
3. **Check indoor vent piping** by following the PVC intake and exhaust runs from the unit to the exterior, looking for kinks, sags, disconnections, or condensation pooling.
4. **Examine the pressure sensor hose** (a small rubber or silicone tube running from the combustion chamber to the pressure switch) for cracks, disconnections, or blockage.
5. **Clean or replace the pressure sensor hose** if it appears damaged or clogged, ensuring secure connections at both ends.
6. **Test the air pressure switch** with a multimeter if you have one, checking for continuity when air pressure is applied, or replace the switch if all other checks are clear and the code persists.
7. **Restore power and reset** the unit according to your model's instructions, then monitor for normal ignition and hot water delivery without the error returning.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you cannot locate any blockages but the error persists, if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, or if you suspect the vent system was installed incorrectly. A professional can perform a combustion analysis, measure actual air pressure readings, and verify vent pipe sizing and slope meet manufacturer specifications. Also call a pro if the error returns repeatedly after clearing blockages, as this may indicate a more complex issue with the combustion fan, heat exchanger, or control board that requires diagnostic tools and training to resolve safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts if sensor replacement needed, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
