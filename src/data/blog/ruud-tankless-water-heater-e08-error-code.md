## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E08 Error — What It Means

The E08 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater generally indicates a flame failure or ignition problem. The unit has attempted to ignite the burner but either failed to establish a flame or detected a flame that went out unexpectedly. This code protects the system by shutting down operation until the problem is resolved.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary slightly between Ruud models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the label inside the unit's service panel for model-specific diagnostics. The most common causes involve gas delivery, ignition components, or venting issues that prevent proper combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame sensor when the real issue is a closed gas valve, kinked gas line, or venting blockage. Before ordering parts, verify that the gas valve is fully open and that the unit receives adequate gas pressure.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply interrupted or restricted (~40%)** A partially closed valve, low pressure from the meter, or a kinked flex line can starve the burner and prevent ignition.
- **Faulty flame sensor (~25%)** A dirty or failing flame sensor may not detect the flame even when present, causing the control board to shut down and display the error.
- **Igniter failure (~15%)** A cracked or worn igniter will not produce a spark strong enough to light the gas, leading to repeated ignition attempts and the error code.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructions in the intake or exhaust vent pipes can prevent proper air-fuel mixture and cause flame failure shortly after ignition.
- **Control board or wiring issue (~10%)** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a failing control board may interrupt the ignition sequence or misread flame signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off** the power to the water heater at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve upstream of the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the heater by unscrewing the panel fasteners and setting the cover aside.
3. **Inspect the gas supply line** for kinks or damage and confirm the manual shutoff valve is fully open (handle parallel to the pipe).
4. **Locate the flame sensor rod** near the burner assembly and gently clean it with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits.
5. **Check the igniter electrode** for cracks or heavy corrosion and verify the spark gap matches the specification in your model's service manual.
6. **Examine the intake and exhaust vent pipes** outside the home for blockages such as bird nests, leaves, or ice buildup and clear any obstructions.
7. **Restore gas and power** to the unit and initiate a hot-water draw to observe the ignition sequence through the viewing window or listen for the spark and burner ignition.
8. **If the error persists**, use a multimeter to test for proper voltage at the igniter and flame sensor connections, or call a qualified gas technician for further diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you lack experience working with natural gas or propane systems, if the error persists after cleaning the flame sensor and clearing vents, or if you need to test gas pressure and valve operation. Professional diagnostics include measuring inlet gas pressure, checking for proper voltage and grounding at ignition components, and verifying that the control board is sending correct signals. Any work involving gas connections, pressure regulators, or control-board replacement should be performed by a qualified technician to maintain safety and warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
