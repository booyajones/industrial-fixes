## State Water Heater E164 Error Code — What It Means

The E164 error code is not confirmed in published State or A. O. Smith documentation available for review. State directs users to scan the QR code on the rating plate to access model-specific manuals, fault tables, and technical support, because diagnostic codes vary by product family and control platform. Without the exact model number and its matching fault chart, you cannot determine whether E164 relates to ignition, airflow, sensors, or another system. Do not attempt repairs based on guesswork or generic advice for other brands.

For State tankless and hybrid models, common fault categories include ignition failure, flame loss, blocked air intake or exhaust, scale buildup in the heat exchanger, low gas or water flow, and sensor or wiring issues. The correct repair path begins with identifying your exact model and consulting its published diagnostic table.

## Common Causes

- **Model-specific fault definition missing** E164 does not appear in the provided State documentation, so the exact meaning depends on your model and control board version.
- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Air intake blockage is a common ignition-failure cause on State gas units, restricting combustion airflow.
- **Flame sensor contamination or failure** A dirty or failed flame rod prevents the control from detecting flame, triggering fault codes on tankless models.
- **Scale or sediment buildup** Mineral deposits in the heat exchanger or inlet filter restrict water flow and can trigger flow or temperature faults.
- **Low gas supply or faulty gas valve** Insufficient gas pressure or a sticking valve prevents proper ignition and burner operation.
- **Sensor or wiring harness fault** A broken thermistor, flow sensor, or corroded connector can send incorrect signals to the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the model and serial number** on the rating plate and scan the QR code or visit the State support page to download the exact fault table and manual for your unit.
2. **Check the fault table** in your model's manual to determine what E164 means, then follow the manufacturer's diagnostic sequence for that specific code.
3. **Inspect the air intake and exhaust** for blockages, debris, bird nests, or vent termination issues if the fault relates to ignition or combustion.
4. **Verify gas and water supply** by confirming inlet gas pressure, checking that isolation valves are fully open, and inspecting the inlet water screen for sediment.
5. **Examine the flame sensor and burner assembly** for soot, corrosion, or misalignment if the code is ignition-related, and clean or replace as needed.
6. **Test sensors and wiring harnesses** with a multimeter for continuity and correct resistance values listed in your model's service manual.
7. **Reset the unit** only after correcting the root cause, then monitor for code recurrence and contact State technical support if the fault persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot locate the model-specific fault table, if the code persists after basic intake and supply checks, or if diagnostics require gas-system testing, sensor resistance measurement, or control-board replacement. State provides contractor support and technical assistance through the product support page. Do not bypass safety interlocks or perform trial-and-error part swaps on gas-fired equipment.
