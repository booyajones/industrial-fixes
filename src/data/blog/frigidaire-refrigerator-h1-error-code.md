## What this code means
The H1 or HI code on a Frigidaire refrigerator is a high-temperature alert. The control has detected that one section of the unit is warmer than expected. When the alert triggers, the high-temp indicator lights up, an alarm sounds every 60 seconds, and the display shows the highest temperature reached. Frigidaire states the most common triggers are a door left open or not fully closed, incorrect temperature settings, or a poor door seal allowing warm air in.

Third-party service references commonly cite thresholds around 55°F in the refrigerator compartment or 26°F in the freezer compartment on some models, though Frigidaire does not publish a universal threshold and the exact temperature that trips the alarm is model-dependent. The code itself does not mean a specific part has failed. It means the cabinet is too warm and you need to find out why.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board or thermistor first. Instead, verify door closure, inspect the gasket for tears or gaps, and clear any blocked vents or frost buildup before replacing sensors or electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Door left open or not fully closed (~35%)** Extended open time or a misaligned door allows warm air to flood the cabinet and trip the alert.
- **Poor door seal or torn gasket (~25%)** A damaged or dirty gasket lets warm air infiltrate continuously even when the door appears closed.
- **Blocked airflow or frost buildup (~15%)** Ice or debris in the airflow passages or evaporator area restricts cold-air circulation and causes warm spots.
- **Dirty condenser coils (~10%)** Dust and lint on the condenser reduce heat rejection and raise overall cabinet temperature.
- **Failed air damper assembly (~10%)** On side-by-side models the damper may stick closed or open, starving the fresh-food section of cold air.
- **Thermistor or temperature sensor drift (~5%)** A sensor that reads incorrectly can report a false high temperature or fail to trigger cooling properly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the complaint** by confirming which compartment is warm, whether the alert is active, and if the unit has been left open or unplugged recently.
2. **Check temperature settings** and set the refrigerator to 37°F and the freezer to 0°F per Frigidaire recommendations.
3. **Inspect the door and gasket** for proper closure, alignment, torn rubber, and gaps that allow warm air infiltration.
4. **Clear blocked vents and check for frost** in the airflow passages or on the evaporator; defrost the unit if ice is blocking air movement.
5. **Clean the condenser coils** at the rear or beneath the unit to restore proper heat rejection.
6. **Reset the control** by pressing Clear Alerts if available, or unplug the refrigerator for 30 seconds and restore power.
7. **If the alert returns**, test the air damper for proper operation, check thermistor resistance against the model's specification, and inspect the defrost system for evaporator frost; replace the failed component once isolated.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the alert returns after you have confirmed door closure, replaced the gasket, cleared frost, and cleaned the coils. A pro can test thermistor resistance values, verify air damper operation with meter readings, and diagnose control board logic faults. If the defrost system is not cycling properly or the sealed system is losing capacity, refrigerant work or compressor diagnostics require EPA-certified tools and licensing. A service call typically costs $150–300 including diagnosis and a straightforward part like a damper or sensor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10–80 in parts (gasket or thermistor), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
