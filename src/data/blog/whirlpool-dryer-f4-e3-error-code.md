## Whirlpool Dryer F4 E3 Error — What It Means

The F4 E3 error code on a Whirlpool dryer typically indicates a problem with the temperature sensing system. The control board is reporting that it cannot read the thermistor or temperature sensor correctly, or the sensor readings are outside the expected range. This prevents the dryer from regulating heat properly and the unit will usually stop mid-cycle to protect itself from overheating or running too cold.

The exact definition can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cabinet for confirmation. In most cases the fault points to the thermistor circuit, which includes the sensor itself, the wiring harness, and the control board connections.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a simple resistance check with a multimeter across the thermistor terminals will confirm whether the sensor is reading correctly or is open.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~60%)** The temperature sensor has drifted out of range, shorted, or opened internally so the control board can no longer read a valid temperature.
- **Loose or corroded connector (~20%)** The wire harness plug at the thermistor or control board has backed out or corroded, breaking the signal circuit.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Wires between the thermistor and control board are pinched, cut, or have insulation worn through, creating an open or short.
- **Failed control board (~8%)** The electronic control board itself has a fault in the thermistor input circuit, though this is less common than a bad sensor.
- **Wrong or incompatible thermistor (~2%)** A previous repair installed a thermistor with the wrong resistance curve for the model, causing out-of-range readings.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the cabinet panels** as needed to access the blower housing or exhaust duct where the thermistor is mounted; consult your service manual for panel removal steps for your model.
3. **Locate the thermistor**, a small cylindrical sensor with two wire leads, typically clipped to the blower housing or ductwork near the heating element.
4. **Disconnect the wire harness** from the thermistor and inspect the connector for corrosion, moisture, or loose pins; clean with contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Test the thermistor** by measuring resistance across its two terminals with a multimeter at room temperature; compare the reading to the specification on your wiring diagram or service sheet.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the resistance is out of range, open, or shorted; remove the old sensor from its mounting clip, install the new one in the same position, and reconnect the harness.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify the F4 E3 code does not return and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if resistance testing and wiring inspection are beyond your skill level, or if replacing the thermistor and checking all connections does not clear the code. A pro can perform a complete circuit test, verify control board function, and source the correct OEM parts quickly. Also call if you suspect the heating element or gas valve assembly is involved, as those repairs require additional safety checks.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
