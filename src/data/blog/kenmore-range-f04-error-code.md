## Kenmore Range F04 Error Code — What It Means

The F04 error code on a Kenmore range most commonly indicates the electronic oven control has detected a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit. The control sees the sensor signal as out of range, either shorted, open, or reading an incorrect resistance. On some Kenmore range platforms (depending on the OEM manufacturer), F04 can instead signal a touch panel or user-interface fault. Because Kenmore rebrands ranges from multiple manufacturers, the exact meaning is model-dependent, so always verify your model number and consult the service sheet before ordering parts.

When F04 appears, the range typically locks out oven operation to prevent unsafe heating. The fault can be intermittent if caused by loose wiring or a failing sensor, or persistent if the sensor probe or control board has fully failed. A simple power reset clears the code temporarily, but the underlying hardware problem must be repaired to prevent the code from returning.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor probe** The sensor element inside the oven cavity has failed open, shorted, or drifted out of the acceptable resistance range, causing the control to flag the circuit.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged sensor wiring** The harness between the sensor and the control board has a broken wire, corroded connector pin, or intermittent contact that breaks the circuit signal.
- **Failed electronic oven control board** The control board itself has a defective sensor input circuit or processor fault and misreads or cannot process the sensor signal correctly.
- **Touch panel or user-interface board failure** On ranges where F04 indicates a keypad fault, the membrane switch or UI board has failed or a key is stuck by moisture or physical pressure.
- **Poor connector pin fit at the control** The plug or spade terminal at the control board is loose, burned, or oxidized, creating an intermittent or high-resistance connection that triggers the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** for at least one full minute, then restore power and check whether the F04 code returns after the control reboots.
2. **Identify your exact Kenmore model number** from the rating plate (usually inside the oven door or on the frame) and verify the F04 definition for your OEM platform in the service manual or online parts diagram.
3. **Inspect the oven temperature sensor** in the rear wall of the oven cavity and trace its wiring harness to the control board, looking for loose connectors, corrosion, burn marks, or physical damage to the probe or wires.
4. **Measure the sensor resistance with a multimeter** by disconnecting the sensor plug and testing across the two sensor terminals at room temperature (around 1,100 ohms is typical, but consult your model's service sheet for the exact specification and acceptable tolerance).
5. **Test harness continuity** from the sensor plug all the way to the control board connector if the sensor reads correct resistance at the probe but the fault persists, and repair or replace any section of harness with an open or intermittent connection.
6. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if the resistance is out of range, infinite (open), or near zero (shorted), using the correct OEM or aftermarket part number for your range model.
7. **Replace the electronic oven control board** if the sensor and wiring both test good, or replace the touch panel/user-interface board if your model's F04 definition points to a keypad fault and the code returns after power reset.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with live 240-volt circuits, if you cannot locate or access the oven temperature sensor, or if the error code persists after you have verified sensor resistance and inspected all wiring. Control board diagnosis and replacement require safe handling of the board connectors and proper grounding to avoid static damage. If your range uses gas, any work near the burner assembly or gas valve also requires care to avoid creating a leak or ignition hazard. A professional has the model-specific service literature, correct replacement boards, and test equipment to identify whether the fault lies in the sensor circuit or the control electronics, and can complete the repair safely in one visit.
