## Weil-McLain A146 Error Code — What It Means

The A146 code on Weil-McLain boilers is a lockout or ignition-failure fault. The boiler called for heat, attempted to light the burner, and did not prove flame. The exact code format and meaning can vary by model and control platform, so confirm the code against your specific boiler manual or control history screen. This lockout stops the boiler from running until the fault is cleared and the underlying problem is fixed.

The code typically appears when the burner does not light successfully during the ignition sequence. Common real-world triggers include gas supply problems, dirty or failed flame-sensing components, a failed ignitor, or a control or fuel delivery issue that prevents the flame from establishing. Do not repeatedly reset the boiler without correcting the cause, as that can mask a dangerous condition.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the gas valve when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor or bad ignitor. Test the sensor and ignitor with a multimeter and inspect them for contamination before ordering expensive gas-valve parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~40%)** Carbon buildup or a weak sensor signal prevents the control from proving flame even when the burner lights briefly.
- **No gas supply or closed valve (~25%)** An upstream shutoff left closed, an empty propane tank, or a utility supply interruption stops fuel from reaching the burner.
- **Failed ignitor (~20%)** A cracked, broken, or weak ignitor cannot produce the spark or heat needed to light the gas.
- **Gas valve problem (~10%)** A stuck, failed, or miswired gas valve does not open on demand or opens too slowly for the ignition window.
- **Wiring or grounding fault (~5%)** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or poor grounding disrupt the ignition or flame-sense signal path.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the boiler's main switch and at the circuit breaker to prevent accidental startup.
2. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the upstream shutoff is open and that other gas appliances in the building are working.
3. **Identify your exact boiler model and control** from the nameplate and write down the model number and serial number.
4. **Access the fault history** on the control panel (if your model has a digital display) and note any additional codes or event logs.
5. **Remove the burner cover or access panel** and inspect the ignitor and flame sensor for cracks, contamination, or corrosion.
6. **Test the ignitor and flame sensor** with a multimeter against the service data in your model's manual (or call a technician if you do not have the specs).
7. **Replace the failed component** (ignitor, flame sensor, or gas valve) with the exact part number from the boiler's parts list, then reassemble and restore power.
8. **Run a full ignition cycle** and watch the control confirm flame, then let the boiler complete at least two heating cycles to verify stable operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician immediately if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you do not have a multimeter and the manufacturer service data for your model, or if the boiler locks out again after you clear the fault. Gas-fired boiler repair involves combustion safety, proper venting, and flame-proving diagnostics that require training and specialized tools. A technician will pull the fault history from the control, test the ignition train with calibrated meters, measure gas pressure at the valve, and verify safe combustion before clearing the lockout. If you suspect a gas leak (smell of rotten eggs or hissing sound), shut off the gas immediately, leave the building, and call your gas utility or fire department before calling a repair service.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
