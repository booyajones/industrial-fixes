## Weil-McLain A181 Error — What It Means

The exact meaning of A181 is not documented in available Weil-McLain manufacturer literature, and the definition depends on your specific boiler model and control. Weil-McLain uses alphanumeric fault codes that vary across product lines, so you must match the code to your model's service manual or diagnostics menu to determine the precise fault condition. The CP/serial number on your boiler label is needed to retrieve the correct manual and parts information.

Because the code is unverified, approach it as you would any Weil-McLain fault: enter the contractor diagnostics menu, review both the current fault and the fault history, and follow the troubleshooting sequence in your model-specific manual. Common fault families on these boilers include temperature-sensor drift, low-water or low-pressure conditions, circulator or flow problems, and ignition or flame-sensing issues on gas-fired models.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or gas valve without first testing the thermistor or temperature sensor with a multimeter. Measure sensor resistance and compare it to the resistance table in your manual before replacing more expensive components.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or drifting thermistor/temperature sensor (~30%)** A sensor that reads out of range or has incorrect resistance will trigger temperature-related faults and may cause the boiler to lock out or refuse to fire.
- **Low system pressure or air in the system (~25%)** Pressure below the minimum threshold (often around 12 psi) prevents proper circulation and can cause low-water or flow faults.
- **Circulator pump not running or failed (~20%)** A stuck, seized, or electrically dead circulator stops flow and causes the boiler to overheat or fail to detect proper heat transfer.
- **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor (gas models) (~15%)** Soot buildup, flame-rod offset, or weak microamp signal will cause ignition faults or intermittent lockouts on gas-fired units.
- **Blocked vent, intake, or condensate drain (~10%)** Obstructions in the combustion-air path or a clogged condensate line can trigger safety lockouts and prevent firing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control.** Write down the model number and CP/serial number from the boiler label so you can retrieve the correct service manual and fault-code table.
2. **Enter the contractor diagnostics menu** by following the key sequence in your manual (often a series of button presses on the front panel). Record the current fault code and review the fault history for patterns.
3. **Match the A181 code to your model's fault table** in the service manual. If you do not have the manual, download it from Weil-McLain using your model number or contact technical support with your CP number.
4. **Check system fundamentals:** verify system pressure is in range (typically 12-15 psi cold), confirm the circulator runs on a call for heat, make sure zone valves are fully open, and inspect venting and intake for obstructions.
5. **Test the suspected component** identified by the fault table. For a temperature sensor, disconnect the thermistor and measure its resistance with a multimeter, then compare to the resistance-versus-temperature chart in the manual. For ignition faults, inspect the flame sensor for soot, check burner flame quality, and verify gas pressure.
6. **Replace the failed part** using the exact Weil-McLain replacement (reference your CP number for correct part compatibility). Follow all wiring diagrams and torque specs in the manual.
7. **Reset the boiler** and run a complete firing cycle, monitoring the display and diagnostics menu to confirm the fault does not reappear. Check for proper ignition, stable flame, and normal operating temperature.
8. **Document the repair** in the fault history and verify that all safeties (high-limit, low-water cutoff, pressure relief) are functioning before returning the system to normal service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician for any Weil-McLain fault code that you cannot match to a specific table in your manual, or whenever the repair involves gas piping, combustion tuning, sealed-system pressure testing, or control-board replacement. Weil-McLain directs all diagnostics beyond basic resets and visual checks to qualified professionals who can use calibrated meters, combustion analyzers, and the manufacturer's service bulletins. If you have added system pressure and the fault returns, or if the diagnostics menu shows multiple unrelated faults, a technician should inspect for wiring harness damage, control failure, hidden leaks, or air-binding in the system. Warranty service and part verification also require a professional to submit the CP number and service history to Weil-McLain.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-400.
