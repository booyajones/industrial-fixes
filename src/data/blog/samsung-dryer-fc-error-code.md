## Samsung Dryer FC Error Code — What It Means

The Samsung dryer FC code (sometimes displayed as FE or 1FC) indicates a power-frequency or power-source fault. The dryer's control board is detecting an invalid or unstable incoming electrical frequency rather than a normal operating condition. This is not a drum, heater, or airflow error. The control is interpreting the AC supply as abnormal or disrupted.

The code does not point to a mechanical dryer component. Instead, it signals a problem with the incoming power supply, the connections delivering that power, or the control board's ability to read the supply correctly. Most cases trace back to installation issues, unstable outlet supply, partially tripped breakers, loose power connections, or incorrect installation on a non-dedicated circuit or extension cord.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first. Before spending on a new board, verify the power source, reset the breaker, eliminate extension cords, and inspect the terminal block and cord connections.

## Common Causes

- **Partially tripped or weak circuit breaker** A breaker that has not fully tripped but is no longer delivering stable power will cause the control to flag a frequency fault.
- **Loose power cord or terminal block connection** Intermittent contact at the dryer's power input or wall outlet creates an unstable supply that the control reads as abnormal frequency.
- **Extension cord or non-dedicated circuit** Connecting the dryer through an extension cord, adapter, or shared circuit can introduce voltage sag and frequency instability.
- **Faulty wall outlet or receptacle** A worn or damaged outlet may deliver inconsistent voltage or frequency, especially under the heavy load of a dryer.
- **Main control board misreading supply** If the power source is verified good, the control board's sensing circuit may be faulty and incorrectly flagging the incoming frequency.
- **Damaged power cord or harness** Heat damage, pinched wires, or corrosion in the cord or internal harness can create intermittent faults the control interprets as power disruption.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and turn off the circuit breaker to make sure safe access to the power connections.
2. **Inspect the power cord and wall outlet** visually for scorch marks, melted plastic, loose prongs, or any signs of heat damage or wear.
3. **Remove the rear access panel** (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations) and examine the terminal block where the power cord connects to the dryer's internal harness for loose screws, burned terminals, or corroded wires.
4. **Reset the circuit breaker** by flipping it fully off, then firmly back on, and verify it is a dedicated 240 V circuit (for electric models in North America) or the correct supply for your country and dryer type.
5. **Restore power and run a short test cycle** without any extension cords or adapters, observing whether the FC code clears or returns immediately.
6. **If the code persists after verified-good connections and breaker reset**, use a multimeter to measure the incoming line voltage at the terminal block under no load and again when the dryer attempts to start, looking for sag or instability (consult your model's wiring diagram for nominal voltage).
7. **Replace the main control board** only after confirming stable supply voltage and sound connections, as a faulty board sensing circuit is the remaining cause when the power source is proven good.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240 V connections, if visual inspection reveals burned or corroded terminals you cannot safely clean or tighten, or if the code returns after you have verified the breaker, eliminated extension cords, and inspected the cord and terminal block. A technician will measure line voltage under load, test for frequency stability, and isolate whether the fault lies in the building's electrical supply or the dryer's control board. If the outlet or circuit itself is faulty, call a licensed electrician to inspect the dedicated branch circuit and replace the receptacle or breaker as needed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (cord or outlet), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
