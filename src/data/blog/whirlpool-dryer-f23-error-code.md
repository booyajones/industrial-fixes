## Whirlpool Dryer F23 Error Code — What It Means

The F23 code on Whirlpool Duet and HE-style dryers indicates a fault in the outlet thermistor circuit. The control board is not receiving the expected temperature signal from the exhaust thermistor, which monitors air temperature as it leaves the drum and enters the blower housing. This sensor is located near the thermal fuse on the blower housing.

The code can be triggered by a failed thermistor, loose or corroded wiring at the sensor connector, harness damage from heat or vibration, or a fault in the control board's input circuit. Restricted airflow from a clogged vent or kinked exhaust hose can contribute to overheating conditions that damage the sensor or its wiring over time.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, but checking thermistor resistance with a multimeter and inspecting the connector for corrosion or damage usually identifies the real problem and saves hundreds of dollars.

## Common Causes

- **Failed outlet thermistor (~50%)** The exhaust thermistor itself has failed open, shorted, or is reading out of range, so the control board cannot monitor exhaust temperature.
- **Loose or corroded connector at the thermistor (~25%)** The plug or terminal at the thermistor has worked loose, corroded from heat and humidity, or developed a high-resistance connection that interrupts the signal.
- **Damaged harness between thermistor and control (~15%)** Wiring insulation has cracked from heat or vibration, creating an intermittent open or short to ground in the thermistor circuit.
- **Control board thermistor input circuit fault (~5%)** The electronic control board's thermistor input has failed even though the sensor and wiring test correctly.
- **Restricted exhaust vent or airflow problem (~5%)** Heavy lint buildup in the vent, a kinked exhaust hose, or blockage in the blower housing creates overheating that damages the thermistor or nearby wiring.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the breaker** for a full 5 minutes, then restore power and start a timed-dry cycle. Watch for 1 minute to see if F23 returns. This is Whirlpool's official first troubleshooting step and clears transient faults.
2. **Unplug the dryer** and pull it away from the wall. Remove the lower front access panel or top panel as needed to reach the blower housing at the rear of the drum.
3. **Locate the outlet thermistor** on the blower housing near the thermal fuse. It is a small sensor with two wires and a push-on or plug connector. Inspect the connector for corrosion, melted plastic, or loose pins.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor** and use a multimeter set to resistance mode. Measure across the two terminals at room temperature. Field observations show many good units reading around 10 kΩ, but verify against your model's service data if available. An open (infinite resistance) or dead-short (zero resistance) confirms a failed sensor.
5. **Check harness continuity** from the thermistor connector back to the control board. With the thermistor unplugged, measure resistance of each wire to ground. You should see infinite resistance. Look for cracked insulation, burned areas, or pinched wires where the harness passes through sheet-metal edges.
6. **Inspect and clean the vent system** while you have access. Remove the exhaust hose and use a vent brush or vacuum to clear lint from the blower housing outlet, the hose, and the wall vent. A restricted vent can cause overheating that damages the thermistor over time.
7. **Replace the outlet thermistor** if it tested bad or the connector shows heat damage. Reconnect the harness, reassemble the panels, restore power, and run a full timed-dry cycle to verify the code is gone. If F23 persists and the sensor and harness test good, the control board input circuit is the next suspect.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with a multimeter to test resistance and continuity, if you cannot access the blower housing area safely, or if the thermistor and harness both test good but the F23 code continues. A pro has the service manual with exact resistance specifications for your model, can trace intermittent harness faults with specialized test equipment, and can confirm whether the control board input circuit has failed. Also call if you find heavy lint buildup inside the cabinet or blower housing that you cannot clean safely yourself, since that poses a fire risk and may indicate other mechanical problems.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25–50 for thermistor, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250 service call plus parts.
