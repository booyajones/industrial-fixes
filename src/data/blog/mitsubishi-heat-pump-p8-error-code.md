## Mitsubishi P8 Error Code — What It Means

The P8 error code on Mitsubishi ductless and mini-split heat pumps signals an outdoor unit fault related to abnormal compressor discharge temperature or insufficient temperature change after startup. The system has detected that it cannot produce the required heating or cooling effect within the expected timeframe, triggering a protective lockout. Because Mitsubishi uses model-specific fault definitions, you must verify the exact meaning against your installed unit's service manual rather than assuming P8 means the same thing across all series.

In field troubleshooting, this code typically appears when the outdoor unit fails to achieve proper heat transfer due to refrigerant circuit problems, airflow restrictions, or component failures that prevent the compressor from operating normally. The fault protects the compressor and inverter from damage by shutting down the system before temperatures reach destructive levels.

## Common Causes

- **Low or lost refrigerant charge** A leak or undercharge prevents proper heat transfer and causes abnormal pressure and temperature behavior that triggers the protection circuit.
- **Blocked outdoor coil or fan failure** Debris, dirt buildup, or a non-rotating fan restricts airflow over the condenser and causes the outdoor unit to overheat.
- **Restriction in refrigerant flow** A closed service valve, clogged filter-drier, or malfunctioning electronic expansion valve blocks refrigerant circulation and starves the evaporator or condenser.
- **Failed discharge or coil thermistor** An open, shorted, or incorrectly reading temperature sensor gives false feedback to the control board and can trigger a false fault or prevent proper compressor control.
- **Compressor or outdoor PCB fault** A weak compressor that cannot build pressure or a failing inverter board can prevent the system from reaching target temperatures within the allowed window.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not EPA 608 certified to handle refrigerants, if you lack refrigerant gauges and recovery equipment, or if you are uncomfortable working with live 208-240V outdoor unit wiring. Professional diagnosis is also recommended when multiple components test normally but the fault persists, when the service manual diagnostic tree points to inverter or compressor replacement, or when you need to confirm refrigerant charge and superheat/subcooling measurements that require specialized tools and model-specific target values. Compressor and inverter board replacement on mini-splits often approaches the cost of a new outdoor unit, so get a detailed estimate and system age assessment before authorizing major repairs.
