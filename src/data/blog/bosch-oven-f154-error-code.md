## Bosch Oven F154 Error Code — What It Means

F154 is a control-board overtemperature fault. The electronic control module is getting too hot, usually because cooling airflow is restricted around the console or the board itself is failing. This is an electronics thermal fault rather than a heating-element problem.

Bosch's general error-code guidance recommends resetting the oven first to clear transient faults. If the code returns after a power cycle, the issue is typically blocked vent louvers, damaged wiring at the control board, or a failing control module that needs service support.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the entire electronic control board without first cleaning the console vent louvers and checking for obstructions. Clear any blocked vents and verify wiring connections before condemning the board.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked vent louvers or poor airflow (~40%)** Dirt, debris, or obstructions around the control console prevent heat from escaping the board area, causing the electronics to overheat.
- **Damaged, shorted, or loose wiring harness (~25%)** Connectors at the electronic control board are loose, corroded, or damaged, creating high-resistance connections that generate excess heat.
- **Failing electronic control board (~20%)** Internal components on the control module are degrading and generating excessive heat even when airflow and wiring are normal.
- **Heat-soaking from nearby components (~10%)** Abnormal airflow patterns or hot components near the control board area raise the ambient temperature beyond the board's thermal limits.
- **Transient electronics fault (~5%)** A temporary voltage spike or software glitch triggers the overtemperature sensor without any physical damage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait two minutes to allow the control board to fully reset and clear any transient fault.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether the F154 code returns immediately or after a cook cycle. If the code does not return, monitor the oven over the next few uses.
3. **Inspect the console vent louvers** on the top or sides of the control panel for dust, grease, or obstructions. Clean any blocked vents with compressed air or a soft brush.
4. **Remove the control panel cover** (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations) and visually check the wiring harness and connectors at the electronic control board for looseness, burn marks, or corrosion.
5. **Reseat all harness connectors** at the control board by unplugging and firmly reconnecting each one. Look for signs of arcing or melted plastic that indicate a high-resistance connection.
6. **Verify airflow** around the control board area is not blocked by insulation, packaging material, or heat-soaking from nearby components. Correct any airflow restrictions.
7. **Replace the electronic control board** if vents are clear, wiring is intact, and the F154 code returns after power-cycling. Transfer any software settings or perform a factory reset per the service manual.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the control panel area or if the fault returns after you have cleaned vents and checked wiring. Technicians have model-specific wiring diagrams and can verify that the control board is the true cause before replacement. Also call for service if the oven shows other intermittent faults or if you suspect a broader electrical issue, such as incorrect supply voltage or a damaged main harness behind the wall cavity.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $200-400 in parts (control board), 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $250-500.
