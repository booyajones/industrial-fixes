## What this code means
There is no recorded F154 error code in official Bosch oven documentation or authorized service manuals. The code F154 does not align with Bosch's standardized fault code structure for ovens, which typically uses F40-F49 for door latch, temperature sensor, and control board issues. You are most likely seeing F41, F42, or F43, which indicate the motorized self-cleaning latch has failed to lock or unlock properly during a self-clean or bake cycle. The control board detects the door is not in the correct state within the expected timeframe.

If you are certain the display shows F154, consult your model's wiring diagram and owner's manual, as it may be a model-specific code not widely documented. For most Bosch ovens, door latch faults (F41-F43) are the most common F-code errors and typically result from mechanical obstructions, worn latch motors, or faulty door switches.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the control board first, but the real cause is usually a dirty or jammed latch assembly or a failed latch motor. Clean the latch tracks and test the motor for continuity before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Grease or food debris on latch tracks (~40%)** Burnt food or spatter builds up on the latch mechanism or door edges, preventing the latch from moving freely during the self-clean cycle.
- **Worn or failed latch motor (~30%)** The small motor that drives the latch assembly has worn out after years of use and no longer generates enough torque to lock or unlock the door.
- **Defective door latch switches (~15%)** Micro-switches that signal the door is closed or open are corroded, misaligned, or broken, so the control board cannot verify door position.
- **Pinched or corroded wiring at latch motor connectors (~10%)** Wires connecting the latch motor to the power board (often at headers P24 or P10) are damaged, pinched behind the control panel, or corroded at the terminals.
- **Failed control board (~5%)** The main power board is not sending the correct voltage to the latch motor or is not reading feedback from the latch switches.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or unplug the oven to prevent electrical shock during inspection and repair.
2. **Inspect the latch mechanism and door edges** visually for grease buildup, food spatter, or debris that could block the latch from moving freely.
3. **Clean the latch assembly and tracks** using a kitchen degreaser and a soft cloth, then dry completely and test the latch by hand to confirm it moves smoothly.
4. **Access the power board** behind the control panel (remove screws and lift the panel up) and inspect wiring connections at headers P24 (latch motor) and P10 (door switches) for loose, corroded, or pinched wires.
5. **Test the latch motor for continuity** using a multimeter across the motor terminals (disconnect the motor connector first). If you get no reading, replace the door latch assembly.
6. **Check the door switches** by closing the door and testing continuity across each switch in both open and closed positions. Replace the switch assembly if any switch fails to show continuity when actuated.
7. **Reassemble and restore power**, then run a short self-clean cycle to verify the latch locks and unlocks without error. If the code returns, replace the control board only after confirming all wiring and switches are functional.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with oven wiring or removing the control panel. If cleaning the latch and testing the motor do not resolve the issue, and you have verified wiring and switch continuity, a technician can perform voltage measurements at the control board and diagnose whether the board itself has failed. Pros also have access to model-specific schematics and can quickly identify intermittent faults that are hard to reproduce during home testing.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
