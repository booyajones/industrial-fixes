## Bosch Oven Er7 Error Code — What It Means

The Er7 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a communication or circuit fault within the oven's control system. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for your specific unit. In general, this code points to a problem with the electronic control board, a temperature sensor, or the wiring between components. The oven may not heat properly or may refuse to start until the fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, spending several hundred dollars, when the real problem is often a failed temperature sensor or a corroded wire connector that costs far less. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty oven temperature sensor (~40%)** The sensor that monitors cavity temperature can drift out of specification or fail open, triggering the error.
- **Loose or corroded wire connector (~25%)** High heat and vibration can cause harness connectors to oxidize or work loose, breaking the signal path.
- **Failed electronic control board (~20%)** The main control board can develop solder cracks, bad relays, or failed circuitry that generates false fault codes.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Wires near the oven cavity or hinges can fray, melt, or short against metal, interrupting sensor signals.
- **Software glitch or transient voltage spike (~5%)** Power surges or static discharge can lock the control in an error state that clears with a full power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the oven if possible.
2. **Remove the back panel or side access cover** to expose the control board and temperature sensor.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** (a metal probe extending into the oven cavity with a two-wire connector).
4. **Disconnect the sensor connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure sensor resistance at room temperature, comparing the reading to your model's specification table.
5. **Inspect all wire connectors** on the sensor harness and control board for corrosion, melting, or loose fit; clean with contact cleaner or replace if damaged.
6. **If the sensor is out of range**, replace it with a new unit and reconnect the harness securely.
7. **If wiring and sensor are good**, test the control board by reseating all connectors and checking for visible solder cracks or burn marks; replace the board if defects are found.
8. **Reassemble the oven**, restore power, and run a test bake cycle to verify the error has cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside the oven's electrical cabinet, if the wiring harness shows extensive heat damage requiring tracing through walls or insulation, or if replacing the sensor and reseating connectors does not clear the fault. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board and check for intermittent shorts that are difficult to reproduce. Also call a pro if the oven uses a high-voltage European circuit (240V hardwired) and you lack experience with appliance wiring.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
