## Weil-McLain A12 Error Code — What It Means

The A12 error code is not explicitly defined in official Weil-McLain fault code lists for AquaBalance and CGa/CGi series boilers. However, based on the pattern of other "A" series codes in these models (which typically relate to temperature sensor faults or high outlet water temperature conditions), A12 most likely indicates a problem with the outlet water temperature sensor, its wiring, or an actual high-temperature condition. Codes like A44 in the same series are confirmed as "bad/disconnected temperature sensor" faults, suggesting A12 follows a similar pattern.

Without the specific fault code definition from your model's technical manual, the exact meaning can vary. The code may appear when the sensor reports an unrealistic reading (too high or too low), when wiring is damaged or disconnected, or when the boiler outlet temperature exceeds safe limits (above 210°F). Always consult your model's service manual or wiring diagram for the precise definition of A12 before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the temperature sensor itself when the real problem is a loose or corroded wire terminal at the sensor connection. Inspect and reseat all sensor plug connections before ordering a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Disconnected or Damaged Sensor Wiring (~40%)** Chafed insulation, loose terminal connections, or intermittent shorts in the harness connecting the temperature sensor to the control board cause false readings and trigger the fault.
- **Failed Temperature Sensor (Thermistor) (~30%)** The outlet water temperature sensor itself has failed internally, reporting incorrect resistance values or open/short circuit conditions even when the boiler is at normal temperature.
- **Actual High Outlet Water Temperature (~15%)** The boiler is genuinely overheating (above 210°F) due to low water circulation, a stuck or failed circulator pump, or closed zone valves preventing flow.
- **Failed Low-Water Cutoff Sensor (~10%)** A defective low-water cutoff thermistor provides false temperature readings to the control board, triggering temperature-related faults even when water level is adequate.
- **Low System Pressure (~5%)** Insufficient boiler pressure (below 12 psi) causes localized overheating at the sensor location, leading the control board to read abnormally high temperatures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and fuel** to the boiler at the disconnect switch and gas valve before beginning any inspection or repair work.
2. **Access the diagnostics menu** by holding the up and down arrows on the control panel until the contractor menu appears, then navigate to Diagnostics > Errors > Lockout History 1 to confirm the A12 fault and view any additional details.
3. **Inspect all sensor wiring** at the supply temperature sensor (typically mounted in the outlet piping) and the low-water cutoff sensor for loose connectors, chafing against metal edges, corrosion, or burned terminals, and reseat any loose plugs.
4. **Check boiler system pressure** on the gauge. If below 12 psi, add water via the fill valve until pressure reaches 12 to 15 psi, then bleed air from radiators or zone valves if present.
5. **Swap the supply and return sensors** (if your model has both) to determine if the fault follows the sensor component or stays at the original location, which helps isolate a defective sensor from a wiring or board issue.
6. **Test sensor resistance** with a multimeter across the sensor terminals, comparing the measured resistance to the manufacturer's thermistor curve in the service manual for the current water temperature. Replace the sensor if resistance is infinite, zero, or far outside the expected range.
7. **Verify circulator pump operation** by listening for the pump motor running and feeling the outlet pipe for warmth during a call for heat. A stuck or failed pump causes overheating and real high-temperature faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician for A12 faults if you are uncomfortable working with boiler wiring or electrical testing. Gas-fired boilers require a professional to diagnose and repair high-temperature conditions caused by failed circulators, zone valve problems, or control board faults. A technician has the tools to measure sensor resistance accurately, access the full fault history, and make sure the boiler operates safely after repairs. If swapping sensors or reseating connectors does not clear the fault, or if the boiler is genuinely overheating (outlet pipe too hot to touch), shut down the system and call for service immediately to prevent damage or safety hazards.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
