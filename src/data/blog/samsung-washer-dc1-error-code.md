## What this code means
The dC1 (or DC1) code on a Samsung washer signals a door-latch or door-lock fault. The machine has detected that the door is not properly closed, latched, or electrically confirmed locked before a cycle can begin. The control board is not receiving the expected lock confirmation from the door lock mechanism.

This code appears on both front-load and top-load Samsung models. On front-loaders it typically points to the door lock assembly and strike alignment. On top-loaders it indicates the lid lock mechanism is not engaging or signaling properly. Until the fault is resolved, the washer will not start or advance the cycle.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many users replace the door lock assembly before checking for simple obstructions. Always inspect the door seal, latch area, and strike alignment for lint, buildup, or clothing fragments before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Door not fully closed or clothing obstructing the latch (~45%)** Garments, small items, or buildup prevent the door from seating completely against the strike, so the lock cannot engage.
- **Dirty or misaligned door seal and latch area (~25%)** Detergent residue, lint, or a shifted gasket block the strike plate or prevent the latch from sliding home.
- **Failed door lock or lid lock assembly (~20%)** The electromechanical lock mechanism no longer actuates or does not signal the closed/locked state to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring at the lock assembly (~7%)** The harness connector at the door lock or lid lock is loose, pinched, or corroded, interrupting the lock-confirmation signal.
- **Transient control-board fault (~3%)** A temporary glitch in the main control board misreads the lock status, which a power reset usually clears.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** from the wall outlet and wait 60 seconds to reset the control board, then plug back in and retry the cycle.
2. **Open the door fully** and inspect the rubber door seal (gasket) and latch strike for clothing, coins, lint, or detergent buildup, removing any obstructions.
3. **Wipe the seal and strike area** with a damp cloth to remove residue, then close the door firmly until you hear a click and verify no gap remains.
4. **If the code persists**, unplug the machine and remove the top panel (top-load) or front service panel (front-load) to access the door lock or lid lock assembly.
5. **Check the lock harness connector** for loose pins, corrosion, or damage, and reseat it firmly onto the lock assembly.
6. **Inspect the lock mechanism** for broken plastic tabs, cracks, or a latch that does not move freely when you manually operate the door or lid.
7. **Replace the door lock or lid lock assembly** if it shows physical damage or does not actuate when the door is closed, matching the part number to your washer's model and serial tag.
8. **Reassemble the panels**, plug in the washer, close the door, and run a short cycle to verify the dC1 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable removing panels or working near electrical connectors, if the lock assembly is difficult to access on your specific model, or if replacing the lock does not clear the code. A pro can test the control board's lock-signal circuit and diagnose wiring faults that are not visible during a basic inspection. Also consider professional service if the washer is still under warranty, since DIY disassembly may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $35-85 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
