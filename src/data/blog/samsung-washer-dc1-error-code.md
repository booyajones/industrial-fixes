## Samsung Washer dC1 Error Code — What It Means

The dC1 or DC1 code on a Samsung washer signals a door or door-lock fault. The machine is detecting that the door is not properly closed, the latch has not engaged, or the door lock switch assembly is not confirming a valid locked state. This code appears on both front-load and top-load models when the control board cannot verify that the door or lid is securely locked before the wash cycle begins.

The washer will not start or continue the cycle until the door lock condition is resolved. The code may trigger if laundry is blocking the latch, if the door seal area is dirty, if the latch strike is misaligned, or if the door lock mechanism itself has failed or lost its electrical connection.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the door lock assembly before checking for debris in the latch area or a loose connector. Inspect and clean the latch strike and verify the lock harness is firmly seated before ordering a new lock.

## Common Causes

- **Door not fully closed (~35%)** The door was not pushed firmly enough to seat the latch and engage the lock switch.
- **Laundry or debris in the latch area (~25%)** Clothing, lint, or buildup prevents the door from closing completely or the latch from engaging.
- **Failed door lock assembly (~20%)** The door lock mechanism is broken, worn, or electrically faulty and cannot confirm the locked state.
- **Loose or damaged lock connector (~12%)** The wiring harness to the door lock is disconnected, corroded, or damaged.
- **Misaligned door latch or strike (~8%)** The latch or strike plate is bent, shifted, or loose and will not seat properly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the washer** at the wall outlet or circuit breaker and wait 60 seconds to reset the control board.
2. **Open and firmly re-close the door**, applying steady pressure until you hear or feel the latch engage. Do not slam the door.
3. **Inspect the door seal and latch area** for any clothing, lint, detergent buildup, or foreign objects and remove them. Wipe the latch strike and seal clean with a damp cloth.
4. **Restore power** and start a short cycle to see if the code clears. If it does, the fault was a partial close or debris.
5. **If the code persists**, unplug the washer again and remove the front or top panel (consult your model's service manual for access) to reach the door lock assembly.
6. **Check the wiring connector** at the door lock for corrosion, loose pins, or damage. Disconnect and reconnect firmly to make sure a solid connection.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the connector is sound but the lock does not actuate or if the lock housing is cracked or worn. Reinstall the panel, power on, and test with a normal cycle to confirm the code is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable removing the washer panel or if the door lock replacement does not clear the code. A pro can test the lock circuit with a multimeter, verify the control board is sending the correct lock signal, and check for wiring faults inside the cabinet. Also call if the door hinge or frame is damaged and preventing proper alignment, since that may require cabinet disassembly or frame repair beyond typical DIY scope.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts (door lock assembly), 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 including service call and lock replacement.
