## Frigidaire Dryer Shuts Off Early — What's Happening

When a Frigidaire dryer shuts off early, it means the machine is ending or interrupting the cycle because it detects a safety, sensing, or control condition. This is not a unique fault code but a symptom tied to several underlying issues. In some cases the dryer may display codes like E1, AD, E5, or E68, which are associated with motor faults, overheating, vent problems, or sensor issues, but many units will simply stop mid-cycle without a code.

The most common reason is restricted airflow causing the dryer to overheat and trip its thermal protection. Moisture sensor contamination or failure can also cause premature shutoff, especially in sensor-dry modes. Less often, a timer or control board glitch, a blown thermal fuse, or motor overheating will interrupt the cycle. The exact cause depends on whether the dryer stops in sensor-dry only or also in timed-dry, and whether the unit is a mechanical-timer or electronic model.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Restricted airflow from lint or duct blockage** Lint accumulation in the screen, exhaust hose, or outdoor vent reduces airflow and causes the dryer to overheat and shut down for safety.
- **Overheating protection tripping** Poor airflow triggers the thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat, which cuts power to prevent damage or fire.
- **Moisture sensor contamination or failure** Fabric softener residue or sensor failure causes the dryer to read 'dry' prematurely and stop the cycle in sensor-dry modes.
- **Blown thermal fuse** A thermal fuse that has opened due to past overheating will cut power to the heating circuit or motor, stopping the cycle.
- **Control board or timer fault** A faulty electronic control or mechanical timer can interrupt the cycle due to a glitch or component failure.
- **Motor overheating or fault** An overheating motor can trigger a shutdown or display an E1 code on some Frigidaire models.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify whether the dryer stops in sensor-dry only or also in timed-dry to help isolate sensor issues from airflow or control problems.
2. Clean the lint screen thoroughly and inspect the exhaust hose and outdoor vent for blockages, kinks, or crushed ducting.
3. Disconnect power for 5 to 10 minutes to reset the control board, then retest the dryer on a normal or timed-dry cycle.
4. Locate the moisture sensor bars inside the drum and clean them with rubbing alcohol and a soft cloth to remove fabric softener residue.
5. Access the thermal fuse (usually on the blower housing or heating element housing) and test it for continuity with a multimeter (should read 0 ohms).
6. If the thermal fuse is open, replace it and address the airflow problem that caused it to blow.
7. If the dryer still stops early after airflow and sensor cleaning, test the timer or electronic control board for faults and replace if necessary.
8. Run a full test cycle after each repair to confirm the dryer completes a normal load without stopping early.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 220-volt dryer wiring, if the thermal fuse keeps blowing after you have cleared the vent system, or if the control board or moisture sensor test procedures are beyond your skill level. A professional can also diagnose intermittent motor faults and perform a full system airflow test to rule out hidden duct restrictions.
