## Samsung Dishwasher LE Error Code — What It Means

The LE error code on a Samsung dishwasher indicates a leak or moisture-detection fault. The machine has detected water or moisture collecting in the base pan or underbody area and has stopped operation to protect itself from further damage. This is not a normal wash-cycle fault but rather a protection response to an actual or perceived leak condition.

Samsung service materials often treat LC and LE codes as the same leak-related condition, with the exact display varying by model or region. The code can be triggered by visible water leaks from hoses or seals, but it can also appear due to over-sudsing from wrong detergent, installation problems like drain backflow, or contamination around the leak sensor itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or leak sensor first. Instead, dry the base pan completely and inspect all hoses and connections for visible leaks before replacing any electronic components.

## Common Causes

- **Leaking drain or supply hose** Cracked hoses, loose hose clamps, or poorly seated connections allow water to drip into the base pan and trigger the sensor.
- **Door seal damage or misalignment** A torn, warped, or improperly seated door gasket allows water to escape from the front of the tub during the wash cycle.
- **Sump or pump area seepage** Worn seals or cracks in the sump assembly or drain pump housing let water leak into the lower compartment.
- **Over-sudsing from wrong detergent** Using non-dishwasher detergent or overdosing creates excessive foam that pushes water into areas it should not reach.
- **Improper drain installation or backflow** A kinked drain hose or missing high loop or air gap can cause water to back up and spill into the base.
- **Contaminated or stuck leak sensor** Old water, mineral buildup, or debris around the leak sensor can falsely signal a leak even when the base is dry.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power and water supply.** Disconnect the dishwasher from power at the breaker or unplug it, then close the hot water shut-off valve under the sink before inspecting for leaks.
2. **Pull the dishwasher out and check the base pan.** Remove the lower access panel or slide the unit forward to look for standing water, wet spots, or mineral deposits in the base pan and around the leak sensor area.
3. **Dry the base pan completely.** Use towels and a wet-dry vacuum to remove all moisture from the base, leak sensor area, and surrounding components, then let it air out for at least 30 minutes.
4. **Inspect all hoses and connections.** Check the water supply line, drain hose, hose clamps, and under-sink fittings for cracks, looseness, drips, or water stains, and tighten or replace any compromised parts.
5. **Examine the door seal and sump area.** Run your hand along the entire door gasket for tears, gaps, or hardening, and look underneath the tub for wetness around the sump, pump, and lower seals.
6. **Verify drain hose routing and detergent type.** Confirm the drain hose has a proper high loop or air gap where required, is not kinked, and that you are using only automatic dishwasher detergent in the recommended amount.
7. **Reset power and run a test cycle.** Reconnect power and water, start a short rinse cycle, and monitor the dishwasher and base area for any sign of leakage or code recurrence.
8. **Inspect the leak sensor and wiring if the code persists.** If the LE code returns with a dry base and no visible leaks, check the leak sensor area for contamination or trapped moisture and verify all wire harness connections are clean and seated.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you find no visible leaks but the LE code continues to appear after the base is completely dry and all hoses and seals have been inspected. A pro can test the leak sensor circuit, check for sump cracks or internal seal failures that are not visible from outside, and diagnose control board or wiring harness faults. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water supply connections, if the dishwasher is built-in and difficult to access, or if the leak source is inside the tub or pump assembly and requires disassembly of sealed hydraulic components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (hose, clamp, or seal), 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250 for diagnosis and leak repair.
