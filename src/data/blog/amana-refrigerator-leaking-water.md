## Amana Refrigerator Leaking Water — What's Happening

Water leaking from your Amana refrigerator is a symptom, not a fault code. Amana handles leak complaints by tracing the water path and checking three main systems: the drainage system, the water-supply plumbing, and the door seals. Water on the floor can come from cracked supply tubing, loose connections at the rear valve or filter head, or overflow from a blocked defrost drain inside the freezer.

If the leak appears at the front or inside the freezer compartment, the defrost drain is often frozen or clogged. Water that should run down the drain trough during the defrost cycle backs up, overflows, and drips onto the floor or pools in the cabinet. If water shows up near the dispenser or on the exterior, check the drip tray, ice chute, filter O-rings, and all tubing connections from the household supply to the dispenser inlet.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Frozen or clogged defrost drain** Ice buildup in the drain trough or tube blocks meltwater flow, causing overflow into the freezer or onto the floor.
- **Cracked or damaged water filter** A cracked filter body or missing O-rings at the filter head allows water to seep out during dispensing or normal operation.
- **Loose water-supply tubing connections** Connections at the household shutoff valve, rear refrigerator inlet, or hinge-area routing can loosen and drip.
- **Cracked or damaged supply line** The plastic or braided supply line from the wall valve to the refrigerator can crack, kink, or develop pinholes over time.
- **Full or overflowing dispenser drip tray** Meltwater and dispensed-water residue collect in the drip tray below the dispenser and spill when full.
- **Ice shavings or blockage in the dispenser chute** Ice buildup in the chute melts and drips out the front of the cabinet between dispense cycles.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Identify the leak location by checking for water at the back of the cabinet, bottom front, inside the freezer, or around the dispenser.
2. Shut off the household water-supply valve before inspecting any tubing, filter, or connection.
3. Inspect the rear plumbing by tracing the supply line from the wall valve to the refrigerator inlet and checking for wetness, cracks, kinks, or loose fittings.
4. Remove the water filter and examine the body for cracks and the O-rings for damage or missing seals, then replace the filter if damaged.
5. Check the dispenser area by emptying and cleaning the drip tray, clearing any ice shavings from the chute, and testing whether the dispenser continues to drip after you release the paddle.
6. Access the freezer evaporator drain by removing the rear freezer panel or access cover, inspect for ice buildup at the drain trough and tube, then thaw and clear the line to confirm water flows to the drain pan.
7. Repair the failed component by replacing the supply line if cracked, replacing the filter if leaking, reseating or replacing damaged tubing or fittings if loose, or clearing and installing updated drain parts if the drain repeatedly freezes.
8. Verify operation by running the dispenser and checking a defrost cycle, then confirm no dripping at the front, no seepage from the back, and no water pooling in the cabinet or on the floor.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you cannot safely access the rear freezer panel to inspect the evaporator drain, if the leak persists after you have replaced the filter and checked all visible connections, or if you find water inside sealed compartments that require disassembly of the liner or sealed-system components. Professional help is also recommended if the household supply valve is corroded or inaccessible, or if repeated drain clearing does not resolve freezer overflow.
