## Weil-McLain Boiler A55 Error — What It Means

Weil-McLain does not publish a universal A55 error code across all boiler series. The exact meaning depends on your specific model (ULTRA, CGi, Alpine, Evergreen, AquaBalance, etc.) and control platform. The same alphanumeric display can indicate different faults on different product lines. To identify the exact fault, enter the boiler's diagnostics or fault history menu (consult your model manual) and record the stored code before resetting anything.

Based on Weil-McLain's published troubleshooting guidance, most Axx-series faults on their boilers relate to temperature limits, flow issues, or sensor problems. Common root causes include loss of water circulation, air trapped in the system, low system pressure (below about 12 psi on residential hydronic systems), failed temperature sensors, or a weak circulator pump. On condensing models, blocked condensate drains or vent obstructions can also trigger high-limit or temperature-rise faults.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace temperature sensors or control boards without first checking system pressure, purging air, or testing the circulator pump. Verify gauge pressure is at least 12 psi and that the pump is moving water before ordering sensor or control parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low system pressure or air in the lines (~35%)** Water level drops below the minimum or air locks prevent circulation, causing rapid temperature rise and a high-limit or flow fault.
- **Failed or weak circulator pump (~25%)** The pump runs but does not move enough water, or it has failed entirely, starving the boiler of flow and triggering a temperature or safety limit.
- **Faulty supply or return temperature sensor (~20%)** A drifted or open sensor sends incorrect readings to the control, causing the boiler to fault on an apparent overheat or temperature-rise condition.
- **Closed or clogged valve or strainer (~15%)** A shut isolation valve, debris in a strainer, or a clogged heat exchanger passage restricts flow and raises supply temperature too quickly.
- **Blocked condensate drain or vent on condensing models (~5%)** Trapped condensate or a plugged vent/intake can cause pressure-switch faults or high-limit trips that may display as an Axx code on some control platforms.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control platform** from the nameplate or CP number (on ULTRA models this may be inside the jacket near the heat exchanger).
2. **Enter diagnostics or fault history** on the control display (follow the model manual) and write down the stored fault code before resetting.
3. **Check system pressure** at the gauge. If it reads below 12 psi, slowly open the fill valve to bring pressure to about 15 psi, then close the valve.
4. **Bleed air from the system** starting at the highest or furthest radiator or baseboard, opening the bleed valve until water flows steadily with no bubbles.
5. **Verify the circulator pump is running** by feeling for vibration and warmth on the pump body when the boiler calls for heat. If the pump is cold or silent, check for power and test the pump.
6. **Test supply and return temperature sensors** with a multimeter. Compare resistance readings to the boiler manual's table at current water temperature. Replace any sensor that reads open or far out of range.
7. **Clear any flow restrictions** by opening all zone valves, checking and cleaning strainers, and on condensing models verifying the condensate drain line and vent/intake are clear and unobstructed.
8. **Run the boiler through a full heating cycle** and confirm the fault does not recur. If the code returns, call a qualified technician with the exact model number and fault history.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating technician if you cannot identify the exact fault code from your boiler's diagnostics menu, if system pressure will not hold after refilling (indicating a hidden leak), if the circulator pump tests good but flow is still poor (suggesting a clogged heat exchanger or serious restriction), or if the fault returns after you have purged air and verified sensors. Gas boilers require proper combustion analysis and safe ignition sequencing. A technician will use the boiler's fault history, test sensors and pumps with proper meters, verify gas supply and venting on condensing models, and make sure the system meets manufacturer specifications before clearing the fault and returning the unit to service.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
