## KitchenAid CAL Error Code — What It Means

CAL on a KitchenAid oven or range is not a fault code. It is the oven calibration feature or status display. KitchenAid identifies CAL as the oven calibration function, which allows you to adjust the temperature offset so the oven heats to the temperature you set. When CAL appears, the oven has entered calibration mode, either because you or someone else accessed the settings menu or because a button sequence was pressed accidentally.

Because CAL is a feature rather than a hardware failure signal, no component is inherently broken when you see this display. If your oven is baking poorly at the same time, the root issue is usually inaccurate temperature sensing or an incorrect offset value, not the CAL display itself. KitchenAid directs users to the owner's manual for model-specific calibration instructions rather than treating CAL as an error condition that requires repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the oven temperature sensor or control board thinking CAL signals a failure. Before ordering parts, confirm the oven is simply in calibration mode and exit the menu or verify actual oven temperature with an independent thermometer to see whether calibration adjustment is all that is needed.

## Common Causes

- **Accidentally entering calibration mode (~60%)** A button sequence or menu navigation put the oven into the calibration feature, displaying CAL without any component fault.
- **Temperature offset was changed (~25%)** Someone adjusted the calibration offset previously and the display is showing the current calibration status or value.
- **Poor baking performance prompts calibration check (~10%)** The oven temperature is inaccurate, so you entered calibration mode to adjust the offset, and CAL appeared as part of that process.
- **Control panel sensitivity (~5%)** A wet cloth or accidental touch on the control panel triggered the calibration menu sequence without intent.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact model number** from the label inside the oven door or on the frame and locate the owner's manual online if you do not have the printed copy.
2. **Exit calibration mode** by pressing Cancel, Stop, or turning the oven off at the circuit breaker for 60 seconds, then powering it back on to see if CAL clears.
3. **Verify oven temperature** by placing an oven thermometer on the center rack, setting the oven to 350°F, and comparing the thermometer reading after 20 minutes of preheating.
4. **Enter calibration mode** following your manual's button sequence (often pressing and holding Bake or a combination of keys) if the actual temperature differs from the setpoint by more than 25°F.
5. **Adjust the offset** up or down in small increments (typically ±5°F steps within a ±35°F range) using the arrow or number keys as directed by your manual.
6. **Save and exit** the calibration menu by pressing Start or the key specified in your manual, then test bake again to confirm the adjustment corrected the temperature.
7. **Inspect the temperature sensor** if calibration does not fix poor baking performance. The sensor is a thin metal probe at the rear oven wall. Check for visible damage, corrosion, or loose wiring, and consult the service manual for resistance specifications if you have a multimeter.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the CAL display will not clear after exiting calibration mode and power cycling the oven, if the oven temperature remains incorrect after you adjust the calibration offset to the maximum allowable range, or if you are uncomfortable using a multimeter to test the temperature sensor resistance. A technician will verify sensor operation, check control board diagnostics, and determine whether the sensing circuit or main control needs replacement. Because CAL itself is a feature rather than a fault code, any real repair focuses on the underlying temperature control components, and a qualified service provider has the model-specific service documentation and calibration tables to diagnose accurately.
