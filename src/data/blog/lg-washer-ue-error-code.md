## LG Washer UE Error Code — What It Means

UE on an LG washer indicates the machine has detected an unbalanced load during the spin cycle. The washer attempts to redistribute the load automatically, but if it cannot achieve balance, it displays UE (or uE or Ub depending on the model) and either reduces spin speed or stops entirely. LG identifies this code on both top-load and front-load washers as a balance issue, not a drain or motor fault.

The code appears when laundry is piled to one side of the drum, the load is too small or too large, or bulky items naturally clump together during spin. It can also signal that the washer itself is not level or the floor beneath it is unstable. Less commonly, worn suspension components allow excessive tub movement and trigger repeated balance faults even with properly distributed loads.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or order shock absorbers before confirming the washer is level and testing with a balanced load. Always check that all four feet touch the floor, the cabinet does not rock, and a small evenly distributed test load completes a spin cycle without error.

## Common Causes

- **Load bunched to one side (~50%)** Wet laundry shifts during the spin ramp-up and collects on one side of the drum, creating an imbalance the machine cannot correct.
- **Washer not level or floor unstable (~25%)** One or more leveling feet do not contact the floor, lock nuts are loose, or the floor flexes under spin vibration.
- **Load too small or too large (~15%)** A single towel or very small load cannot distribute evenly, and an overloaded drum prevents items from moving freely to balance.
- **Bulky or heavy items clumping (~5%)** Comforters, sleeping bags, and rugs naturally clump and resist even distribution during spin.
- **Worn suspension or shock absorbers (~5%)** Springs, dampers, or shocks lose tension or leak fluid, allowing excessive tub motion that the control interprets as imbalance.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Stop the cycle** and unplug the washer or turn off the circuit breaker for thirty seconds to clear the error state.
2. **Open the door or lid** and manually redistribute the wet laundry so items are spread evenly around the drum, not bunched to one side.
3. **Check all four leveling feet** by pressing down on opposite corners of the cabinet and watching for rocking or gaps between a foot and the floor.
4. **Adjust the feet** by turning them clockwise to raise or counterclockwise to lower until the washer sits level and stable, then tighten the lock nuts snug against the cabinet base.
5. **Place a bubble level** on the top of the washer front-to-back and side-to-side to confirm level in both directions.
6. **Run a rinse-and-spin test** with a small balanced load (three or four towels) to verify the machine completes the spin cycle without displaying UE.
7. **Inspect suspension components** (springs, shock absorbers, dampers) by tilting the washer back and looking for broken hardware, leaking fluid, or loose mounting points if the error persists on an empty or test load.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the UE code appears on every cycle after you have confirmed the washer is level, the floor is solid, and a small evenly distributed test load still fails. A technician will inspect the suspension system for worn shocks, broken springs, or loose tub mountings and follow model-specific diagnostics if mechanical parts check out. Also call if you are uncomfortable tilting the washer to access the underside or if you lack a bubble level and basic wrenches to adjust the feet properly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in leveling adjustments or $40-120 for suspension parts if worn, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for a service call including leveling, load testing, and suspension inspection.
