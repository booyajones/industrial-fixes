## Navien E306 Error Code — What It Means

The E306 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates the unit is having trouble establishing or maintaining a flame, or the system cannot confirm proper combustion. This can stem from ignition failure, flame detection issues, or interrupted gas flow. The control board expects to see a stable flame signal during operation and will shut down the heater when it does not.

Because tankless water heaters fire on demand, consistent ignition and flame sensing are critical to safe operation. The E306 code is the controller's way of protecting the system from unsafe conditions such as incomplete combustion or gas leaks. Address this code promptly to restore hot water and prevent further fault cycles.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or igniter when the actual problem is a flame rod covered in soot or scale. Clean the flame rod first before ordering any expensive components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame sensor prevent it from detecting the flame current, causing the unit to fault even when ignition is successful.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~20%)** Insufficient gas pressure or a partially closed valve starves the burner and prevents stable ignition.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition transformer (~15%)** A worn igniter or weak transformer cannot produce a reliable spark, resulting in repeated ignition failures.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructed air intake or exhaust pipes disrupt the air-fuel mixture and cause incomplete combustion or flame rollout.
- **Failed gas valve (~5%)** A stuck or malfunctioning gas valve will not open properly, preventing gas from reaching the burner even when the igniter is sparking.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the panel fasteners, then locate the burner assembly and flame rod inside.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for carbon buildup, corrosion, or mineral deposits. The rod should be clean and shiny.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit sandpaper, a green abrasive pad, or steel wool until all deposits are removed and the metal surface is bright.
5. **Check the igniter** for cracks or heavy wear. If damaged, mark it for replacement.
6. **Verify the exhaust and intake pipes** outside are clear of obstructions such as leaves, nests, or ice.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and run a hot-water tap to test for normal ignition and flame sensing.
8. **Monitor the display** for the E306 code. If it clears, the repair is complete. If the code persists, measure gas pressure at the inlet and confirm the gas valve is opening properly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod does not resolve the error, or if you lack the tools to measure gas pressure and electrical signals. Professionals have manometers to verify inlet and manifold pressure, multimeters to test flame current, and the training to safely diagnose and replace gas valves or control boards. Because tankless water heaters use both gas and electricity under pressure, mistakes can lead to carbon monoxide hazards or equipment damage. A technician can also inspect venting integrity and make sure your unit meets local codes.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
