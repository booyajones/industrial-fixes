## Kenmore Microwave E1 Error Code — What It Means

The E1 code on a Kenmore microwave is listed in the Kenmore microwave code chart as a thermistor error. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that monitors cavity or component temperatures during operation. Because Kenmore is a private-label brand sold by Sears, the exact fault definition and diagnostic path can vary depending on which OEM manufacturer actually built your specific model. The first step in any repair is to identify the manufacturer from your full model number, since the same code can behave differently across model families.

When the control board detects an abnormal reading from the thermistor circuit (either out of range, open, or shorted), it throws the E1 code and typically halts microwave operation to prevent overheating or unsafe conditions. The fault may appear at power-up or partway through a cooking cycle.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a simple resistance check of the thermistor with a multimeter will identify a failed sensor for a fraction of the cost.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor sensor has drifted out of its normal resistance range or failed open or shorted, sending an invalid signal to the control board.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~25%)** Connectors or wires in the thermistor circuit have vibrated loose, corroded, or been pinched, breaking continuity or creating intermittent opens.
- **Control board input fault (~15%)** The control board's thermistor input circuit is damaged and cannot correctly interpret a valid sensor signal.
- **Incorrect thermistor installation (~7%)** A previous repair installed the wrong thermistor type or left the sensor poorly seated against the monitored surface, causing out-of-spec readings.
- **Wiring harness connector corrosion (~3%)** Moisture or heat has corroded the plastic connector pins at the thermistor or control board, adding resistance or creating an open circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and wait at least two minutes to allow high-voltage capacitors to discharge. Do not proceed with any testing until power is disconnected.
2. **Remove the outer cabinet** by taking out the screws along the top and sides (locations vary by model). Slide or lift the cover off to expose the control board and internal harness.
3. **Locate the thermistor** using your model's wiring diagram or service manual. It is typically a small disc or probe with two wires, mounted near the magnetron, cavity, or air duct.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** at the control board or inline harness. Inspect the pins and housing for burns, corrosion, or cracks. Clean or replace the connector if damaged.
5. **Measure the thermistor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. Consult your model's table for the correct value at room temperature. A reading of infinite ohms (open) or zero ohms (shorted) indicates a failed sensor.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if the resistance is out of range. Use the exact OEM part number for your model to make sure correct calibration and mounting.
7. **Reassemble the cabinet**, restore power, and run a short test cycle. If the E1 code returns and the thermistor tests good, the control board input circuit is likely faulty and the board will need replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside a microwave cabinet, since even unplugged units retain high-voltage charge in the capacitor and magnetron circuitry. A technician should also handle the repair if you cannot locate the thermistor using your wiring diagram, if resistance testing is unfamiliar, or if the control board requires replacement. Because Kenmore models are built by different OEMs, a tech with access to the correct service documentation can quickly identify the exact thermistor specification and diagnostic procedure for your model family, saving you from trial-and-error part swaps.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
