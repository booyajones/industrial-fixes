## GE F96 Error Code — What It Means

The F96 code appears on GE ranges and wall ovens, not water heaters. GE states that this fault indicates an internal component needs to be replaced. In practical terms, repair technicians commonly interpret F96 as a problem with the upper oven cooling fan speed or fan feedback signal. The control board is not receiving the expected speed signal from the fan sensor, which can happen when the fan motor stalls, the Hall sensor fails, or the wiring between the fan and control board is damaged or loose. GE recommends turning the appliance off at the breaker until service is performed.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or stalled upper cooling fan motor** The cooling fan blower may be binding, have broken blades, or no longer spin freely, preventing normal speed feedback to the control.
- **Bad fan speed sensor or Hall sensor** The sensor that reports fan speed to the control board can fail or lose signal, even when the fan motor itself still turns.
- **Loose or damaged wiring and connectors** Harness connections to the fan, poor grounds, or damaged wires can interrupt power or the feedback signal between the fan and control board.
- **Faulty control board or FAD board** If the fan and sensor test good but the speed signal is still not detected, the machine control or fan control board may need replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power at the circuit breaker and leave the unit de-energized before any disassembly or testing.
2. Reset the oven by restoring power briefly and check whether the F96 code returns after a short wait to confirm the fault is persistent.
3. Access the upper cooling fan by removing the back panel or interior shields and inspect the fan for obstructions, broken blades, or binding that prevents free rotation.
4. Check the fan motor power supply with a multimeter while the oven calls for cooling, confirming the fan receives 120 VAC at its harness connector when commanded on.
5. Test the fan feedback signal at the control board connector, looking for a 5 V pulse on the feedback circuit pins when the fan is running.
6. Replace the fan motor assembly or sensor if the fan has proper voltage but produces no valid speed signal at the control board.
7. Replace the machine control or FAD board if the fan and sensor both test good but the F96 fault persists, then reassemble and run a short test cycle to verify the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside a live appliance with mains voltage, if you cannot safely access the upper cooling fan and control boards, or if the fault persists after replacing the fan motor and you need to diagnose the control board feedback circuit with a schematic. Because GE does not publicly map F96 to a single part number, a qualified technician with service documentation can save you the cost of replacing multiple boards by pinpointing whether the issue is in the fan assembly, wiring, or control logic.
