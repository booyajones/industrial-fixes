## KitchenAid KFGG500ESS Problems & Error Codes — Overview

The KitchenAid KFGG500ESS is a freestanding gas range that shares the error-code architecture common across KitchenAid's cooking appliances. The most reported fault on this model is F9E0, which KitchenAid attributes to miswired electrical connections or unexpected power-line noise. Because KitchenAid does not publicly release a full service sheet for every SKU, diagnosis beyond the documented codes requires the tech sheet tucked inside the unit or field experience with the same control platform.

Reliability issues center on electrical connection faults, oven temperature sensor drift, and door-lock mechanism problems. The good news is that many error codes clear with a simple power reset, and the oven temperature sensor is a straightforward swap. Gas burner work and any live voltage testing should be handled by qualified technicians.

## Most Common Problems on This Model {#fix}

- **F9E0 error code appears on the display** Miswired electrical supply or unstable line voltage triggers this fault; verify the 240V connection at the terminal block and have a licensed electrician check the outlet wiring if the unit was recently installed.
- **PF (power failure) code after an outage** A utility interruption or tripped breaker causes this code; press Cancel to clear and reset the clock per the owner's manual.
- **Oven temperature runs too high or F2 code** A failing oven temperature sensor, shorted bake or broil element, or stuck relay on the control board causes over-temperature faults; measure the sensor for approximately 1050 ohms at room temperature and replace if it reads open or far off spec.
- **F3 code or oven won't heat accurately** An open or damaged oven temperature sensor circuit prevents the control from reading temperature; swap the oven temperature sensor and inspect the wiring harness for breaks or high resistance.
- **Display shows Celsius instead of Fahrenheit** This is a user-setting issue, not a failure; toggle the temperature display mode in the control settings per the owner's manual.
- **Door lock faults or F9 on models with self-clean** A stuck door-lock switch, misaligned latch assembly, or broken wiring in the lock circuit prevents the control from confirming lock position; inspect the door-lock motor, switches, and harness.
- **F6E0 or F6E6 communication errors** Loss of communication between the user interface and the appliance manager board points to a loose ribbon cable, a failed user-interface board, or a bad control board; reseat all harness connectors and replace the faulty board.
- **F6E1 or F6E3 oven over-temperature faults** The control detects runaway oven temperature due to a shorted heating element, bad sensor, or relay failure on the control board; check elements for shorts to ground and replace the sensor or control as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician for any live 240V electrical diagnostics, gas-supply work, or burner orifice adjustment. If you see repeated F9E0 codes after verifying the appliance cord and terminal-block connections, ask a licensed electrician to verify your household wiring and voltage stability. Door-lock disassembly during an active fault can be tricky because the latch may be energized, so let a tech handle it. Always disconnect power and shut off the gas at the supply valve before any DIY work, and never bypass thermal or lock safeties. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
