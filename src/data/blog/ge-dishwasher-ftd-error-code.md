## GE Dishwasher FTD Error Code — What It Means

FTD on a GE dishwasher stands for Failure To Drain. The control board has detected that water did not empty from the tub as expected, or the water level remained too high after a drain attempt. Some GE troubleshooting resources group FTD with FED (a high-water-level condition), treating them as two sides of the same drain-fault family.

When the dishwasher tries to drain and the pressure sensor or timer sees water still present, it throws the FTD code and stops the cycle. In many cases the machine actually did drain, but contamination in the pressure sensor or its hose makes the control think water is still there. In other cases, standing water really is present because the drain path is clogged or the pump has failed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump first when the real problem is a contaminated pressure sensor or a pinhole sensing port clogged with mineral deposits. Always clean the pressure sensor and its tiny port, and clear the entire drain path before condemning the pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filters or sump debris (~30%)** Food particles, seeds, and other small objects restrict water flow into the drain pump, preventing the tub from emptying.
- **Contaminated pressure sensor or sensing port (~25%)** Mineral deposits or grease block the tiny pinhole in the sensor assembly, causing the control to read a false high-water condition even when the tub is empty.
- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~20%)** A crushed, collapsed, or internally blocked drain hose stops water from reaching the disposal or standpipe.
- **Garbage disposal knockout plug still installed (~10%)** A new or replaced disposal connection sometimes leaves the dishwasher inlet knockout plug in place, completely blocking drain flow.
- **Debris jammed in the drain pump (~10%)** Glass shards, toothpicks, or other foreign objects lodge in the impeller and prevent it from spinning freely.
- **Failed drain pump (~5%)** The pump motor or impeller has failed electrically or mechanically and cannot move water even when the line is clear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power** at the breaker or unplug the dishwasher so it is safe to work on.
2. **Remove and clean the filters** in the bottom of the tub, then inspect the sump opening for seeds, glass, or other debris and clear it.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** by pulling it from the disposal or standpipe connection and checking for kinks, crushing, or internal clogs. Confirm the hose has a proper high loop or anti-siphon routing.
4. **Check the garbage disposal** and air gap. Make sure the disposal knockout plug was removed when the dishwasher was connected, and clean any blockage in the air gap if your installation uses one.
5. **Access and inspect the drain pump** by removing the lower access panel or kickplate. Look for foreign objects in the pump housing and try to spin the impeller by hand. If it does not turn freely, remove the obstruction.
6. **Clean the pressure sensor** by locating the sensor on its white elbow or hose assembly, pulling it out, and clearing the tiny sensing pinhole with a toothpick or compressed air. Reinstall the sensor and reconnect its hose.
7. **Restore power and test** by running a short rinse or drain cycle. If the code clears and the dishwasher drains normally, the repair is complete. If the FTD returns, test the drain pump for continuity and replace it if it fails, or replace the pressure sensor if cleaning did not resolve a false reading.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified that the filters, sump, hose, disposal connection, and air gap are all clear, the pressure sensor has been cleaned, and the FTD code still returns. A technician can perform continuity and voltage tests on the drain pump, pressure sensor, and control board to isolate an electrical failure. Also call if you are uncomfortable working around standing water and electrical connections, or if your dishwasher requires disassembly beyond the kickplate and lower panels to reach the pump or sensor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (if a pump or sensor is needed), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
