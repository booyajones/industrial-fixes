## GE Dishwasher FTD Error Code — What It Means

FTD stands for Failure to Drain on GE dishwashers. The control board has detected that water is still in the tub after the drain cycle should have finished. Either the dishwasher cannot move water out fast enough or the pressure sensor believes water remains in the tub even after draining has attempted to complete.

In practical terms, you will usually see standing water in the bottom of the tub when you open the door, and the machine will refuse to run another cycle until the problem is fixed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump motor first, but most FTD codes are caused by simple clogs. Pull and clean the filter, inspect the sump for debris, and check the drain hose for kinks before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filter or sump (~40%)** Food particles, seeds, labels, glass, or other debris block the drain inlet or pack the sump area so water cannot exit the tub.
- **Blocked drain pump or jammed impeller (~25%)** Foreign material (bones, plastic, stickers) lodges in the pump housing or wraps around the impeller, preventing it from spinning freely.
- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~15%)** The hose is crushed under the machine, routed too tightly, or filled with grease and debris, restricting water flow.
- **Air gap or garbage disposal problem (~10%)** The air gap is blocked with gunk, or the dishwasher knockout plug was never removed from a newly installed garbage disposal, blocking the drain path.
- **Dirty pressure sensor or sensor port (~7%)** Grease or mineral buildup on the pressure sensor or in the sensing tube gives a false reading that water is still present.
- **Failed drain pump motor (~3%)** The pump motor windings are open or shorted, or the motor no longer develops enough torque to move water out.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the drain pump hum or make noise during the drain cycle, but water does not leave?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The pump is trying to run but is blocked or the drain path is clogged. Check for debris in the impeller and inspect the drain hose and disposal connection.<br><strong>No:</strong> The pump is silent, which suggests it is jammed, has no power, or has failed. Spin the impeller by hand and test the pump for continuity.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After cleaning the filter and sump, does the machine drain normally when you run a rinse cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The clog was the only problem. The code should clear and normal operation should resume.<br><strong>No:</strong> The blockage is further downstream (hose, air gap, disposal) or a component (pump, sensor) has failed and needs replacement.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dishwasher** at the circuit breaker or unplug it to eliminate shock risk before opening any panels or touching components.
2. **Remove the bottom dish rack** and pull out the cylindrical fine filter and any coarse filter screens from the floor of the tub.
3. **Clean the sump area thoroughly** by scooping or vacuuming out all food debris, seeds, glass, and foreign objects from the drain inlet and surrounding well.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** under the sink for kinks, crushing, or blockage, and confirm the high loop is installed and the air gap (if present) is clear.
5. **Check the garbage disposal connection** to verify the dishwasher knockout plug was removed and the inlet is not clogged with food waste.
6. **Access and inspect the drain pump** by removing the lower access panel or the sump cover, clear any debris from the impeller, and spin it by hand to confirm it turns freely.
7. **Test the drain pump electrically** with a multimeter for continuity, and clean or replace the pressure sensor if the pump is good but the code persists after all mechanical obstructions are cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working under the dishwasher, if the drain pump or pressure sensor requires soldering or wire-harness repair, or if the code returns after you have cleaned every visible obstruction and tested the pump for continuity. A technician can also diagnose control-board faults or wiring issues that a multimeter test alone cannot pinpoint. If your dishwasher is still under warranty, contact GE or an authorized service center before opening any panels to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (if pump or sensor needed), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for a service call and parts.
