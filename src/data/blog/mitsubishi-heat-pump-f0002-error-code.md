## Mitsubishi F0002 Error Code — What It Means

F0002 does not appear in Mitsubishi Electric's documented fault-code library for mini-split and heat-pump systems. Mitsubishi uses 2-digit or 4-digit/lettered codes like E6, 1102, or 1500 in their standard format. F0002 is most likely a controller-specific display label, a stored history item, or a model-specific service code that must be cross-referenced with your exact indoor and outdoor unit model numbers. The underlying fault could be communication-related, a control-board issue, a sensor problem, or a wiring fault. Always retrieve the actual manufacturer fault code from your unit's service menu or LED blink pattern before diagnosing, because F0002 alone does not point to a single Mitsubishi-wide failure mode.

## Common Causes

- **Indoor/outdoor communication wiring fault** Loose, damaged, reversed, or open interconnect wiring between the indoor and outdoor units will trigger communication errors on Mitsubishi systems.
- **Indoor or outdoor control PCB failure** A failed or malfunctioning printed circuit board in either the air handler or the condenser will generate fault codes and prevent normal operation.
- **Thermistor or sensor circuit problem** Temperature sensor faults or open/shorted thermistor circuits can produce model-specific error codes on Mitsubishi heat pumps.
- **Power supply or polarity issue** Incorrect voltage, reversed polarity, or phase problems at the outdoor unit can cause control faults and non-standard code displays.
- **Stored fault history display** F0002 may be a previous error recorded in the controller's memory rather than an active fault, requiring a manual clear after the real issue is resolved.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker and wait two minutes before opening any panels or attempting diagnostics.
2. **Record your exact model numbers** from the nameplates on both the indoor and outdoor units, then locate the service manual or fault-code table for those models.
3. **Retrieve the real fault code** using your remote's CHECK button or fault-recall menu, or read the LED blink pattern on the indoor unit per the manual, and write down the code and count.
4. **Inspect all field wiring and terminals** between the indoor and outdoor units, checking for tight connections, correct polarity, and undamaged insulation on both high-voltage and low-voltage communication wires.
5. **Check the indoor and outdoor control boards** for visible damage, burned traces, or loose connectors, and verify that all sensor plugs and ribbon cables are fully seated.
6. **Measure power supply voltage** at the outdoor unit and confirm it matches nameplate requirements, and verify that any three-phase or reversing-valve polarity is correct per the wiring diagram.
7. **Clear the fault history** if your manual allows it and the root cause has been repaired, then restore power and monitor the system through a full heating and cooling cycle to confirm normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you cannot locate the service manual for your exact Mitsubishi model, if the fault-code retrieval procedure does not match any published instructions, or if you do not have a multimeter and refrigeration gauges to safely test control voltages and refrigerant pressures. Also call a pro if the real fault code points to inverter or compressor failures, or if you have already replaced wiring or a board and the error returns. Mitsubishi systems often require model-specific software tools and refrigerant handling that are beyond typical homeowner scope.
