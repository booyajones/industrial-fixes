## Navien E311 Error — What It Means

The E311 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates the unit is having trouble establishing or maintaining ignition. This means the burner either failed to light within the expected time window or the flame sensor cannot detect the flame. The control board halts operation as a safety measure to prevent unburned gas from accumulating. Because tankless heaters demand precise coordination between gas flow, ignition spark, and flame sensing, several factors can trigger this code. The error may appear immediately when you call for hot water or during operation if the flame drops out unexpectedly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or igniter when the real problem is a dirty flame rod that costs nothing to clean. Always inspect and clean the flame rod before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or misaligned flame rod (~40%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame rod insulate it from the flame, preventing the sensor from detecting ignition and triggering a false shutdown.
- **Insufficient gas supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an undersized gas line can starve the burner and prevent stable ignition.
- **Faulty igniter or spark wire (~15%)** A cracked ceramic igniter, damaged spark lead, or poor ground connection prevents the spark from lighting the gas.
- **Air in the gas line (~10%)** After initial installation or a gas service interruption, trapped air can disrupt the fuel-air mixture and delay or prevent ignition.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~7%)** Obstructed exhaust or air intake pipes disrupt combustion airflow, causing incomplete combustion or flame rollout that the sensor reads as no flame.
- **Failed gas valve (~3%)** A stuck or defective gas valve may not open fully or modulate correctly, preventing proper fuel delivery to the burner.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and carefully set it aside.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe extending into the burner chamber, and inspect it for carbon buildup, corrosion, or misalignment.
4. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or an emery cloth until the metal is bright and shiny, then wipe away any residue.
5. **Check the igniter electrode** for cracks or heavy carbon deposits and clean it gently if needed, taking care not to damage the ceramic insulator.
6. **Inspect the gas supply line** and confirm the shutoff valve is fully open, then look for kinks or undersized piping that might restrict flow.
7. **Examine the intake and exhaust vent pipes** for blockages such as nests, ice, or debris, and clear any obstructions you find.
8. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and initiate a hot-water call to test ignition and verify the error has cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician or gas fitter if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances or high-voltage ignition systems. Professionals have combustion analyzers to measure gas pressure, air-fuel ratios, and flame current, which pinpoint problems a visual inspection cannot catch. If cleaning the flame rod and clearing vents does not resolve the E311 error, a technician can test the gas valve operation, inspect the control board for failed relays, and make sure the venting system meets code requirements. Any work that involves adjusting gas pressure or replacing the gas valve requires proper licensing and liability coverage in most jurisdictions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
