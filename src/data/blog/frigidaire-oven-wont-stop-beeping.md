## Frigidaire Oven Won't Stop Beeping — What's Happening

A Frigidaire oven that won't stop beeping is usually not a specific fault code. It's a generic alarm behavior triggered by an active timer or alarm setting, a control panel input problem, or a control board or sensor fault. When there's no code on the display, the beeping is commonly a general alert from the electronic control board.

If the display shows F10 along with the beeping, Frigidaire says the oven is detecting higher-than-normal temperatures. That's a safety alert. The unit should be turned off and allowed to cool before you attempt any reuse or further diagnosis.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Timer or alarm setting left on** An active cook timer, alarm, or preheat chime that was accidentally triggered or never canceled will cause continuous beeping.
- **Stuck or phantom keypad input** A faulty membrane keypad or moisture behind the control panel can create phantom button presses that trigger repeated alarms.
- **Temporary control board glitch** The electronic control can latch a false alarm state after a power surge or brief interruption, causing nuisance beeping with no real fault.
- **Loose wiring or harness connector** Intermittent connections between the control board and temperature sensor or other components can destabilize the control and trigger alarms.
- **Oven temperature sensor problem** A failing RTD or sensor with incorrect resistance can send false high-temperature readings to the board, causing F10 beeping or runaway heating.
- **Faulty control board or stuck relay** A control board with a stuck relay or internal fault can allow unwanted heating, erratic behavior, or continuous alarm beeping even when no real issue exists.
- **Sabbath mode or demo mode enabled** Some models can enter special settings modes that alter display and beeping behavior, confusing normal operation.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Identify whether a fault code is displayed. If you see F10, treat it as an overheating safety condition and turn the oven off immediately to let it cool.
2. Reset power by unplugging the unit for at least 30 seconds or flipping the breaker. Frigidaire guidance says this clears many false codes and temporary control-board glitches.
3. Check all timer and alarm functions on the control panel. Cancel any active cook timer, alarm, preheat chime, or other alert setting that may have been left on.
4. Inspect the control panel keypad for stuck buttons or moisture intrusion. Press each key firmly and feel for any that don't spring back or seem sticky.
5. If F10 or overheating is present, test the oven temperature sensor circuit. Check the sensor resistance at room temperature (typically around 1,100 ohms) and inspect the harness and connectors back to the control board for looseness, corrosion, or damage.
6. Examine all wiring harnesses and connectors between the control board, sensor, and other components for scorching, pinched wires, or poor contact.
7. Evaluate the control board for stuck relays or internal faults if the sensor and wiring check out but the unit still alarms, overheats, or behaves erratically.
8. Do not keep cycling power or attempting to use the oven if it is overheating, won't shut off, or repeatedly alarms. Frigidaire treats F10 as a safety alert requiring professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the beeping persists after a power reset and timer check, if you see F10 with actual overheating or runaway temperature, or if you're not comfortable testing sensor resistance and inspecting control-board wiring. Overheating conditions and stuck relays are safety hazards that require proper diagnosis and part replacement. A pro can also verify whether the control board, sensor, or wiring harness is at fault and source the correct model-specific parts. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
