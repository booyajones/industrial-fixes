## Weil-McLain A170 Error Code — What It Means

A170 is not a universal Weil-McLain error code with one single meaning across all boiler families. The specific meaning depends on your boiler model and control platform. Weil-McLain controls store fault codes in a diagnostic or contractor menu, and A170 must be interpreted by consulting that menu or the model-specific fault table in your service manual. The code signals that the control module has detected a problem with one of its monitored circuits or sensors, but you cannot know which one without identifying your exact boiler series and reading the fault history.

Most Weil-McLain gas boilers monitor the air pressure switch, boiler limit circuit, flame sensor, gas supply, and system pressure. When the control logs a fault code, it is responding to an out-of-range input from one of these devices. A stored fault like A170 may point to ignition failure, a safety-circuit lockout, low system pressure, or a blocked condensate drain on condensing models. The correct diagnostic path is to pull the fault history from your control panel, verify the safety and demand inputs, and then test the specific component the code identifies.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace the flame sensor or igniter without first confirming gas supply, system pressure, or a blocked condensate trap. Always verify gas flow, boiler pressure (typically 12-15 psi cold), and drain-line clearance before ordering any sensor or ignition part.

## Common Causes

- **Flame proving or ignition failure (~25%)** The burner lights but cannot maintain flame, or the flame sensor does not prove flame to the control.
- **Low system pressure or air in the system (~20%)** Boiler pressure has dropped below the safe operating range (typically below 12 psi), or air pockets prevent circulation.
- **Blocked condensate drain or frozen trap (~20%)** On condensing models, a clogged or frozen condensate trap or drain line triggers a safety lockout.
- **Gas supply issue (~15%)** No gas supply, weak gas flow, or the manual shutoff valve is closed or partially closed.
- **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor (~12%)** The flame sensor rod is contaminated with carbon or oxide buildup, or it is positioned too far from the burner flame.
- **Pressure switch or limit circuit fault (~8%)** The air pressure switch, boiler high-limit, or another safety-circuit input is open or out of range for the specific model.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control platform.** Look on the rating plate inside the jacket or on the control display for the series name (e.g. CGi, Ultra, EG) and write it down.
2. **Access the diagnostic or contractor menu** on your control panel and pull the active and past fault history. Consult your owner's manual for the button sequence to enter diagnostics.
3. **Check boiler system pressure** on the gauge. If it reads below 12 psi, open the fill valve slowly to bring pressure to about 15 psi, then close the valve and reset the fault.
4. **Verify gas supply.** Confirm the manual gas shutoff valve is fully open and that gas is flowing to the boiler. On propane systems, check tank level and regulator lock-out.
5. **Inspect the condensate trap and drain line** on condensing models. Remove the trap, flush it, check for ice in the drain line, and reinstall before resetting.
6. **Test the flame sensor and ignition sequence.** If the fault history indicates ignition or flame-proving failure, remove the flame sensor rod, clean it with fine sandpaper or emery cloth, and check alignment to the burner flame.
7. **Clear the fault code and run a full heating cycle.** After correcting the identified fault, reset the control and monitor one complete call for heat to confirm the repair. If the code returns, call a qualified technician to test pressure switches, limits, and control inputs.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician if you cannot access the diagnostic menu, if the fault history shows a pressure-switch or limit-circuit fault you cannot identify, or if the boiler locks out repeatedly after you have verified system pressure, gas supply, and drain clearance. Work on gas-fired boilers requires proper combustion analysis, pressure-switch testing, and control-board diagnostics that are beyond typical homeowner tools. A qualified technician will pull the full fault log, measure gas pressure at the valve, test flame-sensor microamp signal, verify air-pressure-switch operation, and check all safety circuits before replacing any component. If your boiler is still under warranty, professional service is usually required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
