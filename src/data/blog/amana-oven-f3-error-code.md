## Amana Oven F3 Error Code — What It Means

The F3 error code on Amana ovens typically indicates a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit or the electronic control board. This code appears when the control detects an open, shorted, or out-of-range reading from the sensor that monitors oven cavity temperature. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit for details specific to your oven.

When the F3 code appears, the oven usually stops heating and beeps to alert you. The control is designed to shut down heating to prevent damage or unsafe operation when it cannot accurately read oven temperature. In most cases the fault lies with the sensor itself, its wiring, or the connections between the sensor and the control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, which is expensive. Test the oven temperature sensor with an ohmmeter and inspect the wiring and connectors for damage or looseness before replacing the control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~55%)** The sensor probe that monitors cavity temperature develops an open circuit, short, or drifts out of spec and sends invalid readings to the control board.
- **Damaged sensor wiring or connector (~25%)** Wires between the sensor and control board are broken, pinched, or corroded, or the connector is loose or has burned terminals.
- **Electronic control board failure (~15%)** The control board itself has a faulty sensor input circuit or relay that causes it to register a false error even when the sensor is good.
- **Intermittent connection or ground fault (~5%)** A poor ground or intermittent connection at the sensor or control causes erratic readings that trigger the error sporadically.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven to eliminate shock hazard.
2. **Locate the oven temperature sensor**, a metal probe that extends into the oven cavity, usually on the upper rear wall or side.
3. **Remove the sensor** by unscrewing the mounting bracket inside the oven and pulling the probe and wire through the cavity wall.
4. **Disconnect the sensor wire connector** from the harness behind the oven and inspect both plug and socket for burn marks, corrosion, or loose pins.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** using an ohmmeter; at room temperature it should read in the general range of 1000 to 1200 ohms (consult your model's wiring diagram for the exact value).
6. **Replace the sensor** if the reading is open, shorted, or far out of range, or if the probe shows physical damage.
7. **Reconnect all wiring**, secure the new or tested sensor in the mounting bracket, restore power, and run a test bake cycle to confirm the error is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical testing equipment or if the wiring harness behind the oven requires extensive repair. A technician should also handle control board replacement if you have tested the sensor and wiring and both are in good condition, since board-level diagnostics and programming may be required. If the oven is still under warranty or if you smell gas or see any evidence of arcing or melted insulation, shut off power and contact a qualified service provider immediately.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-60 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
