## Navien Tankless Water Heater E247 Error — What It Means

The E247 error code on Navien tankless water heaters signals a communication failure between the main control board and the front control panel. The controller cannot send or receive signals properly, which prevents normal operation of the unit. This is typically an electrical or wiring issue rather than a water flow or combustion problem.

The fault can stem from loose or corroded connections, damaged wiring harnesses, moisture intrusion at connector points, or a failed component on either the main board or the panel itself. Because the system relies on continuous digital communication between these components, even a marginal connection can trigger the code and shut down the heater.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but a loose or dirty connector is far more common. Disconnect, inspect, and reseat every harness between the board and panel before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~50%)** Vibration, humidity, or installer error can cause harness plugs to back out or develop oxide on the pins, interrupting the data signal.
- **Moisture intrusion at the control panel (~20%)** Condensation or a leak near the panel can short or corrode traces on the circuit board or connector contacts.
- **Damaged ribbon cable or wire harness (~15%)** Pinched, bent, or frayed wiring between the main board and panel breaks continuity or creates intermittent faults.
- **Failed main control board (~10%)** A component failure on the board itself can prevent it from communicating with the front panel, though this is less common than wiring issues.
- **Failed control panel assembly (~5%)** The display and button module can fail internally, losing the ability to send or receive signals from the main board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and wait two minutes to allow the system to reset.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the Navien unit by unscrewing the fasteners and lifting the panel away to expose the control board and wiring.
3. **Locate the ribbon cable or wire harness** running from the main control board to the front control panel assembly.
4. **Disconnect each connector** carefully by releasing the locking tab or clip, then inspect the pins and sockets for corrosion, bent pins, or debris.
5. **Clean any corrosion** with electronics contact cleaner and a soft brush, and straighten any bent pins gently with needle-nose pliers.
6. **Reconnect each plug firmly** until the locking tab clicks or the connector seats fully, then give a gentle tug to confirm it is secure.
7. **Restore power** at the breaker and observe the control panel. If the E247 code clears, run a hot water tap to verify normal operation; if the code persists, test continuity through each wire in the harness with a multimeter and replace the harness or suspect board as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working inside the water heater cabinet or if you lack a multimeter to test continuity and voltage. A technician can quickly isolate whether the fault lies in the harness, the main board, or the panel by swapping known-good components. Also call a pro if the error persists after reseating connectors and you see no obvious damage, since firmware mismatches or internal board failures require specialized diagnostics and may be covered under warranty if the unit is still within the service period.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
