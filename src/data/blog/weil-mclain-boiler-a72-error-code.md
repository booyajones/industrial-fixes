## Weil-McLain Boiler A72 Error Code — What It Means

The A72 code is not uniformly defined across all Weil-McLain boiler models, so the exact meaning depends on your control platform and model series. Consult your specific boiler manual and pull the fault history from the control display to confirm. In many Weil-McLain units, lockout codes in this range point to ignition failure or flame-proving problems, meaning the burner attempts to light but the control does not detect a stable flame within the allowed time.

Because Weil-McLain uses different control boards across product lines, the same numeric code can represent different faults on different models. Rather than guessing, verify the code definition in your installation and service manual, then use the fault-history menu to see if the error is repeating or if other codes appeared first. This approach prevents unnecessary part replacement and targets the actual interlock or sensor that stopped the sequence.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many technicians replace the gas valve or ignitor first, when a dirty flame sensor is the real problem. Pull and clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and a dry cloth before ordering hard parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~40%)** Soot, corrosion, or a cracked ceramic insulator on the flame rod prevents the control from proving flame even when the burner lights.
- **Bad ignitor or ignition electrode (~25%)** A cracked hot-surface igniter, broken spark electrode, or damaged ignition cable stops the burner from lighting at all.
- **No gas flow or closed valve (~15%)** The manual gas cock is closed, the upstream regulator has failed, or the gas valve itself is stuck shut.
- **Low gas pressure (~10%)** Insufficient inlet pressure or a partially restricted gas line prevents stable flame establishment and the control locks out.
- **Loose wiring or poor ground (~10%)** Corroded spade connectors, a missing ground jumper, or a loose flame-sensor wire cause intermittent signal loss and lockout.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Look up the exact A72 definition** in your boiler's installation and service manual, because Weil-McLain uses different control platforms and the same code number can mean different things on different models.
2. **Pull the fault history** from the control display menu to see if other codes appeared before A72 or if the error repeats every ignition attempt.
3. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas cock is fully open, the upstream shutoff has not been closed, and you can hear or smell gas flow when another appliance on the same line operates.
4. **Inspect the flame sensor and ignitor** by removing the burner cover, pulling the flame rod, and checking for soot, corrosion, cracks in the ceramic, and loose or corroded wire terminals.
5. **Clean the flame sensor** with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth until the metal is bright, wipe it dry, and reinstall with firm connections on both the rod and the ground path.
6. **Check ignition spark or glow** by watching the ignitor during a call for heat; a hot-surface igniter should glow orange-white within seconds and a spark electrode should produce a steady blue arc.
7. **Reset the control once** using the reset button or by cycling power, then observe a full ignition sequence from fan purge through flame proving; if A72 returns immediately, stop resetting and repair the failed component before continuing.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating contractor if you are not comfortable working with natural gas or propane, if your local code requires a permit for boiler service, or if you have checked gas flow, cleaned the flame sensor, and verified ignition but the A72 fault persists. A technician will measure gas inlet and manifold pressure with a manometer, test flame-current micro-amps with a multimeter, and pull diagnostic data from the control that is not visible on the home display. Because boiler lockouts can also stem from blocked venting, incorrect combustion-air supply, or failed interlocks, a pro can isolate the root cause quickly and avoid the cost of wrong-part replacement. Warranty coverage on Weil-McLain equipment is handled through the original installer and distributor, so contact your installing contractor first if the boiler is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
