## Navien E332 Error Code — What It Means

The E332 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with flame sensing or ignition. The unit has attempted to ignite the burner but either failed to detect a stable flame or lost the flame signal during operation. This code is a safety feature that shuts down the heater to prevent gas buildup or unsafe conditions.

The error can stem from issues in the ignition sequence, flame sensing circuitry, or fuel and air supply. Because the code involves both gas flow and electrical sensing, it requires careful diagnosis to pinpoint whether the problem is mechanical, electrical, or related to combustion conditions.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or gas valve when the actual fault is a dirty flame rod that can be cleaned in minutes. Remove and inspect the flame rod for corrosion or soot buildup before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents the sensor from detecting the flame signal, triggering a false no-flame condition.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~20%)** Obstructed intake or exhaust pipes starve the burner of oxygen or prevent proper exhaust flow, disrupting combustion and flame stability.
- **Gas supply interruption (~15%)** Low gas pressure, a closed valve, or air in the gas line can prevent the burner from lighting or maintaining a flame.
- **Faulty igniter (~10%)** A cracked or weak igniter may fail to generate a spark strong enough to ignite the gas consistently.
- **Failed flame rod or wiring (~7%)** A broken flame rod or loose connection between the rod and the control board interrupts the flame-sensing circuit.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** The main PCB may fail to process the flame signal correctly even when the rod and igniter are functioning.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas supply valve to the water heater before beginning any service work.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the panel fasteners, taking care not to pinch any wires when lifting it away.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe positioned near the burner assembly, and disconnect its wire connector at the control board or junction.
4. **Unscrew and remove the flame rod** from its mounting bracket, then inspect the sensing tip for black carbon deposits or white corrosion.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a Scotch-Brite pad until the metal surface is shiny and smooth, then wipe it with a dry cloth.
6. **Reinstall the flame rod** in the same position, reconnect the wire, and check that the rod is not touching any metal parts of the burner or chassis.
7. **Open the gas valve** and restore power, then initiate a hot-water call and observe the ignition sequence through any viewing window or LED indicators on the board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you smell gas at any point, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and clearing the vents, or if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances. A professional can measure gas pressure at the valve, test flame-signal continuity with a multimeter, and replace the control board if diagnostics point to a PCB fault. Tankless water heaters involve both high-voltage AC wiring and natural-gas or propane systems, so any work beyond cleaning accessible sensors or resetting the unit is best left to a qualified technician who carries combustion analyzers and proper licensing.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
