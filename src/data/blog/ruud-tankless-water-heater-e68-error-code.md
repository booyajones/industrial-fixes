## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E68 Error — What It Means

The E68 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically signals a communication fault or sensor malfunction within the unit's control system. Because Ruud uses different code definitions across model lines, the exact meaning can vary. On some models it points to a flame sensing problem, while on others it indicates a circuit board communication error or a failed temperature sensor. Always check your specific model's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front cover for the precise definition.

The error usually stops the heater from firing or causes it to shut down mid-cycle. Common triggers include loose wire connections at the control board, a dirty or corroded flame rod, or a faulty sensor that has drifted out of range. Water flow interruptions or low inlet pressure can sometimes cause the same code if the unit cannot verify proper operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board when the real problem is a loose connector or dirty flame rod. Reseat every plug on the board and clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wire connector (~35%)** Vibration or humidity can loosen plugs on the control board or at sensors, interrupting communication and triggering the error.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or mineral deposits on the flame sensor prevent it from detecting the flame correctly, causing a fault code.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~20%)** An inlet or outlet thermistor that reads out of range will stop the unit and log a sensor error.
- **Faulty control board (~15%)** A damaged processor or failed relay on the main board can generate spurious error codes even when all sensors are good.
- **Low or interrupted water flow (~5%)** Insufficient flow rate or a clogged inlet filter can prevent the unit from verifying operation, sometimes registering as a communication fault.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by lifting it off the mounting hooks or unscrewing the retaining screws.
3. **Press each wire connector** on the control board firmly to reseat it, then check every sensor plug the same way.
4. **Locate the flame rod** near the burner assembly and remove it by unscrewing the mounting bracket or retaining nut.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a Scotch-Brite pad until the metal is shiny, then wipe away any residue with a dry cloth.
6. **Inspect the inlet filter screen** at the cold-water connection and rinse it under running water if clogged.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod and front cover**, restore gas and power, then run a hot-water tap to test for normal operation and check that the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if reseating connectors and cleaning the flame rod do not clear the code, or if you need to test sensors with a multimeter and interpret resistance values against your model's specifications. A pro can also verify correct gas pressure, inspect the venting system for blockages, and replace the control board safely if diagnostics confirm it has failed. Because tankless heaters involve both high-voltage AC power and natural gas or propane, mistakes during troubleshooting can create serious safety hazards.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
