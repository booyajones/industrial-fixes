## LG Dishwasher AE Error Code — What It Means

The AE error code (sometimes displayed as E1) on LG dishwashers indicates that water has leaked into the base pan beneath the tub and triggered the float switch. This is a protective shutdown to prevent water damage to your floor and internal components. The code does not appear in LG's published washer documentation, so if you see AE on an LG washing machine rather than a dishwasher, consult your owner's manual or the model-specific service sheet for the correct definition.

For LG dishwashers, the manufacturer directs technicians to inspect for leveling issues, door gasket buildup or damage, and spray-arm blockage because these conditions allow water to escape the tub and collect in the drain pan below. Once water pools in the pan, the float switch lifts and the unit stops mid-cycle to protect the machine.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the float switch itself when the real problem is an active leak. Before ordering a new switch, dry the base pan completely and run a short cycle while watching for water escaping from the door, hoses, or spray arms.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or damaged door gasket (~40%)** Soap residue, food debris, or a torn seal around the door allows water to drip past the tub edge into the base pan during the wash cycle.
- **Unlevel dishwasher (~25%)** When the unit tilts forward or to one side, water can spill over the door threshold or internal seams and collect in the drain pan.
- **Clogged or misaligned spray arm (~15%)** Blockage or a loose spray arm causes high-pressure jets to spray outside the tub, sending water into the base area.
- **Loose or cracked water supply hose (~10%)** A fitting that has backed off or a hose with a hairline crack drips water directly into the pan beneath the machine.
- **Excess detergent or suds (~7%)** Oversudsing from too much soap or the wrong detergent type can overflow the tub and trip the float switch.
- **Failed inlet valve or drain hose seal (~3%)** A valve body crack or a worn drain hose clamp leaks slowly during fill or drain, pooling water in the base over multiple cycles.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dishwasher** and turn off the water supply valve under the sink to make the work area safe.
2. **Remove the lower kickplate** and tilt the unit back slightly to access the base pan, then use towels or a wet-dry vacuum to remove all standing water from the pan so the float switch can drop.
3. **Inspect the door gasket** by running your fingers around the entire perimeter, feeling for tears, hard spots, or gaps, and wipe away soap scum or food debris with a damp cloth.
4. **Check the spray arms** by pulling them off their mounts, clearing any blockage in the spray holes with a toothpick, and confirming the arms spin freely when reinstalled.
5. **Verify the unit is level** by placing a bubble level on the door sill front-to-back and side-to-side, then adjust the front leveling legs until the bubble centers.
6. **Examine hose connections** at the inlet valve and drain pump for loose clamps, cracks, or moisture, tightening or replacing any suspect fittings.
7. **Restore power and water** supply, then run a short rinse cycle while watching the door seal, spray arms, and base pan area with a flashlight to catch any active drip, and clear the code by pressing Start or Cancel if the leak is resolved.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have dried the base pan and confirmed the machine is level but the AE code returns immediately on every cycle, since a hidden tub crack or a failed sump seal requires disassembly and parts that are not stocked at most hardware stores. Also get professional help if you find water leaking from the circulation pump housing or the tub-to-pump boot, because those repairs involve removing the spray-arm assembly and lower pump cover. If the float switch itself is physically broken or the wiring harness to the switch is corroded, a pro can test continuity and replace the switch quickly without trial-and-error part swaps.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-40 for a door gasket or hose clamp, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220 for service call and gasket replacement.
