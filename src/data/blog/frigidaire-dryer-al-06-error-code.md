## Frigidaire Dryer AL-06 Error Code — What It Means

AL-06 is not a standard code listed in Frigidaire's published support materials, but technicians commonly see this displayed when the dryer detects a heating safety circuit fault. This is most often the same issue as an E66 code, where a thermal switch, high-limit thermostat, or thermal fuse in the heater assembly has opened or gone out of range. The dryer stops heating to prevent fire or component damage.

The thermal switch acts as an overtemperature safety device. When airflow is restricted or the heater circuit overheats, the switch opens and triggers the fault. Because model-specific code displays vary across Frigidaire and Affinity dryers, confirm your exact model number before ordering parts or interpreting the code.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermal switch or thermal fuse** The safety device in the heater circuit has opened permanently due to age, overheating, or repeated cycling and will not reset.
- **Clogged vent or lint blockage** Restricted exhaust airflow causes the dryer to overheat, tripping the thermal safety and opening the heating circuit.
- **Crushed or kinked duct** A damaged vent line reduces airflow enough to allow the heater housing to exceed safe temperature limits.
- **Open or damaged wiring at heater assembly** Heat-damaged connectors or broken wires at the thermal switch or heater create an open circuit the control reads as a fault.
- **Faulty high-limit thermostat** The high-limit device protecting the heater has failed open and needs replacement alongside or instead of the thermal switch.
- **Misread or model-specific code display** Some Frigidaire models display codes differently in diagnostic mode, so what looks like AL-06 may be a variant of E66 or another thermal fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall or switch off the circuit breaker to eliminate all power before any disassembly or testing.
2. **Check the entire venting system** by disconnecting the duct at the dryer and inspecting for lint buildup, crushed sections, or blocked exhaust. Clean or replace any restricted sections before proceeding with parts diagnosis.
3. **Locate your model number** on the data tag inside the door or on the back panel and write it down. Code meanings and part numbers vary by model, so confirm the exact thermal switch or fuse your dryer uses.
4. **Access the heater housing** by removing the front or rear panel per your model's service manual. The thermal switch and high-limit thermostat are usually mounted on or near the heater can.
5. **Test the thermal switch with an ohm meter** by removing one wire and checking continuity. A functioning switch at room temperature should show continuity (close to zero ohms). An open reading means the device has tripped and must be replaced.
6. **Inspect wiring and connectors** at the heater assembly for heat discoloration, loose terminals, or melted insulation. Repair or replace any damaged wiring before installing a new thermal device.
7. **Replace the failed thermal switch or thermal fuse** with the correct part for your model, reassemble the dryer, restore power, and run a test cycle. Monitor temperature rise and verify the error does not return. If the new part fails quickly, recheck venting and airflow.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical testing equipment or disassembling the dryer cabinet. If you replace the thermal switch and the code returns immediately or the dryer still will not heat, the issue may involve the heater element, control board, or a more complex wiring fault that requires diagnostic tools and a schematic. Persistent overheating after cleaning the vent also points to a problem beyond a simple safety-device replacement and warrants professional diagnosis.
