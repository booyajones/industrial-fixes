## Navien E132 Error Code — What It Means

The E132 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a problem with ignition or flame sensing. The unit either cannot establish a flame during startup or loses the flame signal shortly after ignition. This triggers a safety lockout to prevent unburned gas from accumulating. The code typically points to the flame rod, igniter, gas supply, or control board, though the exact definition can vary slightly across Navien models. Check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel for model-specific details.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or control board when the real problem is simply a dirty flame rod. Pull the flame rod and inspect it before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~60%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod insulates it from the flame, preventing the sensor from confirming ignition.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~20%)** Insufficient gas pressure or a partially closed valve can cause weak ignition or flame dropout.
- **Worn or cracked igniter (~10%)** A failing igniter may not produce a strong enough spark to light the gas reliably.
- **Faulty control board (~5%)** The PCB may fail to send or receive the flame signal even when combustion is normal.
- **Improper venting or air supply (~5%)** Blocked or incorrectly sized vent pipes can starve the burner of oxygen or prevent exhaust, disrupting combustion.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the burner ignite briefly and then the error appears, or does it never light at all?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> If the burner lights then shuts down, suspect a dirty flame rod or weak flame signal connection.<br><strong>No:</strong> If it never lights, check gas supply first, then inspect the igniter for cracks or carbon tracking.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you see soot, white powder, or corrosion on the flame rod when you remove the burner cover?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Clean the rod with fine steel wool or sandpaper, wipe it down, and reinstall.<br><strong>No:</strong> Move on to testing gas pressure or inspecting the igniter and wiring harness.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are all upstream gas valves fully open and is the inlet gas pressure within the range shown on the unit's rating plate?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Gas supply is likely adequate; focus on the flame rod, igniter, and control board.<br><strong>No:</strong> Open any closed valves fully and have a technician measure inlet pressure with a manometer.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve on the supply line to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws, then locate the burner assembly and flame rod inside the combustion chamber.
3. **Pull the flame rod** straight out of its ceramic insulator; note its position and wiring connections before removal.
4. **Inspect the rod** for soot, white corrosion, or pitting; if dirty, clean it gently with fine steel wool or 400-grit sandpaper until the metal is shiny.
5. **Reinstall the flame rod** in the same orientation, ensuring the ceramic insulator seats properly and the wire connection is snug.
6. **Check gas supply** by confirming all upstream valves are fully open and that no other gas appliances are starving the heater.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then initiate a hot-water call and observe whether the burner lights and holds a flame without tripping the error code again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections or high-voltage ignition systems. A professional should also measure inlet gas pressure with a manometer, inspect vent piping for code compliance, and use a multimeter to test flame-rod microamp current and igniter voltage. Any repair that involves the gas valve, burner orifice replacement, or control-board diagnosis requires training and calibrated tools to make sure safe operation and warranty compliance.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
