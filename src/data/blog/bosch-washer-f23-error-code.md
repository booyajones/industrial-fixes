## Bosch Washer F23 Error Code — What It Means

The F23 error code (also displayed as E23 on some Bosch washers) indicates that the AquaStop leak-protection system has been triggered. Bosch uses this code to signal that the machine has detected a leak or flood condition in the base pan of the washer. When water accumulates in the base tray, a sensor or float activates the AquaStop system and the washer stops operating to prevent further water damage.

Bosch instructs users not to operate the washer when E23 or F23 appears and to turn off the water supply immediately. The code means either an actual leak has occurred somewhere in the plumbing or internal components, or the leak sensor itself has malfunctioned and is falsely detecting water. Until the source of the leak is identified and repaired, or the faulty sensor replaced, the washer will remain in a locked-out state.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some people replace the control board or pump assembly first. Instead, inspect all hose connections and the detergent drawer for leaks and check the base pan for trapped water before replacing any expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Damaged or loose inlet/fill hose. (~35%)** A cracked, kinked, or improperly seated inlet hose can allow water to drip into the base pan and trip the AquaStop sensor.
- **Damaged or loose drain hose connection. (~25%)** A worn, split, or loose drain hose at the pump or rear connection point can leak during the drain cycle and trigger the flood protection.
- **Detergent drawer not seated correctly or leaking. (~15%)** If the dispenser drawer is cracked or not fully inserted, water can overflow and run down inside the cabinet into the base pan.
- **Faulty AquaStop or base leak sensor. (~15%)** The float-style leak detector in the base pan can stick or fail electrically, signaling a false leak when no water is present.
- **Internal leak from pump, tub seal, or hose fitting. (~10%)** Cracks in pump housing, worn tub seals, or loose internal hose clamps can allow water to seep into the base during fill or spin.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off water and power.** Shut the inlet valve behind the washer and unplug the machine before beginning any inspection or repair work.
2. **Check the inlet hose.** Inspect the fill hose from the wall tap to the washer inlet valve for cracks, kinks, loose fittings, or wet spots. Tighten or replace as needed.
3. **Inspect the drain hose and connections.** Follow the drain hose from the pump to the standpipe or laundry sink and examine clamps, fittings, and the hose itself for splits or looseness.
4. **Remove and inspect the detergent drawer.** Pull the dispenser drawer fully out and check for cracks, warping, or detergent buildup that prevents a tight seal. Look inside the housing for water stains.
5. **Tilt the washer to drain the base pan.** Carefully tip the machine back or onto its side over towels to pour out any trapped water from the base tray, then dry the interior thoroughly.
6. **Access the base and inspect the leak sensor.** Remove the lower access panel or tip the washer to view the base pan. Check the AquaStop float or sensor for debris, corrosion, or a stuck position.
7. **Replace the failed part.** If a hose, clamp, or dispenser is damaged, install the correct replacement. If no leak is found and the sensor remains tripped, replace the base leak sensor or AquaStop assembly and re-test the machine.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate any visible leak after inspecting all hoses and the dispenser, yet the F23 code persists. Internal leaks from the tub seal, pump housing, or pressure-switch tubing require disassembly of the cabinet and possibly the outer tub, which is beyond typical DIY scope. Also contact Bosch Customer Support or a factory-authorized technician if the AquaStop hose itself is suspect, because Bosch directs users with E23 or F23 to professional service for AquaStop-related faults. If the washer is still under warranty, do not open the machine yourself as that may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (hose or sensor), 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for a service call and part replacement.
