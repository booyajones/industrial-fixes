## EcoSmart ECO 19 Error Codes — What It Means

EcoSmart does not publish a universal 'eco19' fault code in their official troubleshooting materials. If you see an error display on your ECO 19 model unit, it is likely an E1, E2, or E3 sensor or thermistor code, or you are experiencing a no-heat or no-power condition. EcoSmart troubleshooting guides for the ECO 18–27 series walk technicians through voltage, breaker, fuse, and sensor checks in sequence rather than naming individual alphanumeric codes.

Most service calls on these units trace back to incoming power problems, blown internal fuses, failed temperature sensors, or insufficient water flow. If your display shows an E-series code or the unit will not heat, follow the diagnostic sequence below to isolate the fault before replacing parts.

## Common Causes

- **No or incorrect line voltage** The unit requires 240 V supply, and voltage below 220 V will significantly reduce heating performance or prevent startup.
- **Breaker or wiring fault** Tripped breakers, loose terminals at the panel, or damaged feeder wiring will cut power to the heater entirely.
- **Blown internal fuse** A failed fuse on the control board path interrupts the low-voltage circuit and prevents the unit from responding to flow or thermostat calls.
- **Thermostat or thermistor continuity problem** Failed temperature-sensing components or open thermostat circuits stop the control board from reading water temperature or activating heating elements.
- **Insufficient water flow** Flow rates below the activation threshold of 0.3 to 0.5 GPM prevent the unit from sensing demand and turning on the elements.
- **Scaling or mineral buildup** Hard water deposits on sensors or inside the heat exchanger can insulate temperature probes and cause false readings or E-code faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Disconnect all breakers feeding the unit and verify zero voltage at the terminal block before opening the enclosure to begin diagnostics.
2. Measure line voltage at the incoming supply legs and at the terminal block, checking each phase pair methodically. The ECO 18–27 troubleshooting guide shows 208 V across L1-L1 with one breaker energized as a reference reading.
3. Check the internal fuse with a multimeter by measuring voltage to ground on the fuse lead with breakers on. A reading of 110–120 V indicates the fuse path is live, and no reading means the fuse is blown and must be replaced.
4. Verify thermostat and sensor continuity with breakers on by following the manufacturer's test sequence to confirm power reaches and passes through the sensor circuit. If an E1, E2, or E3 code is present, try a reset by holding the round temperature knob for 3–5 seconds, then restore water flow and retest.
5. Test water flow rate at the fixture by timing how long it takes to fill a gallon container. If flow is too high or too low, adjust the shutoff valve or fixture aerator to bring it within the unit's operating range.
6. Inspect the heat exchanger and temperature sensors for white mineral scale. If buildup is visible, perform a descaling procedure with the appropriate vinegar or citric-acid flush kit, then rinse and reassemble.
7. Restore power and open a hot-water tap to verify the unit activates, heats to setpoint, and clears any error codes. Monitor for stable operation over several cycles before closing the enclosure.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed electrician or plumber if you measure line voltage below 220 V at the panel, if breakers trip repeatedly when you restore power, or if you are not comfortable working inside a 240 V appliance enclosure. Also call for help if the internal fuse blows again immediately after replacement, which indicates a short on the control board or in the wiring harness. If descaling and sensor replacement do not clear persistent E-codes, the control board itself may need factory service or replacement, and a qualified EcoSmart technician can perform that diagnosis safely.
