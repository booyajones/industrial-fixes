## Electrolux Dryer Squeaking — What's Happening

An Electrolux dryer squeaking or squealing is a mechanical noise symptom, not an error code. Electrolux groups this under general dryer noises and does not publish a specific fault code for squeaking. The sound points to friction or wear in the drum drive system, drum supports, or blower assembly.

Because the noise happens during drum rotation or motor operation, the most common sources are the drive belt, idler pulley, drum rollers or bearings, front glides, blower wheel, or a foreign object contacting moving parts. Community repair guides and service documentation consistently list these mechanical wear points as the top causes of squeal and squeak complaints.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Worn or glazed drive belt** A belt that is frayed, cracked, or glazed from age creates friction against the drum or pulleys and produces a high-pitched squeal during rotation.
- **Dry, worn, or seized idler pulley** The idler pulley keeps belt tension and will squeak or squeal when its bearing dries out, wears, or seizes.
- **Rough or worn drum rollers or rear bearing** Drum support rollers and the rear bearing develop flat spots or lose lubrication over time, causing a rumbling or squeaking sound as the drum turns.
- **Worn front glides or drum seals** The front drum supports or felt seals wear down and let the drum rub against the cabinet or bulkhead, creating a squeak or scraping noise.
- **Blower wheel debris or damage** Lint buildup, cracks, or a separated blower wheel can create noise that sounds like squeaking or rattling from the blower housing.
- **Foreign object or loose fastener** Coins, buttons, screws, or other loose items can wedge in the drum seal area or blower and create a squeal or clanking when they contact rotating parts.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Run the dryer empty to confirm the squeak is from the machine and not clothing hardware, zippers, or load imbalance.
2. Unplug the dryer and disconnect power before opening any panels or accessing internal components.
3. Check the drum and door seal area for coins, buttons, bra wires, or other foreign objects that could rub or rattle during operation.
4. Verify the dryer is level and stable on the floor or pedestal, as an unlevel machine can contribute to noise.
5. Remove the top or front panel to expose the drum, belt, and idler pulley assembly.
6. Inspect the drive belt for glazing, fraying, cracking, or misalignment, and replace if worn or damaged.
7. Spin the idler pulley by hand to check for roughness, wobble, or binding, and replace if it does not turn smoothly.
8. Rotate the drum by hand and feel each drum roller and the rear bearing for rough spots, flat areas, or looseness, then replace any defective rollers or bearings.
9. Check the front glides and rear drum seal surfaces for wear or signs the drum is rubbing the cabinet.
10. Inspect the blower wheel for lint packing, cracks, or hub separation, and clean or replace as needed.
11. Reassemble the dryer, restore power, and run an empty test cycle to verify the squeak is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable opening the dryer cabinet, handling the belt and pulley system, or diagnosing multiple wear points, call a qualified appliance technician. A pro can quickly isolate the noise source, replace all worn components in one visit, and test the machine to confirm the repair. If the squeak persists after replacing the obvious parts, or if you find unusual motor or blower damage, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent incorrect part orders.
