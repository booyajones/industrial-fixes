## State Water Heater E41 Error Code — What It Means

The E41 error code is not universally documented across all State water heater models, but based on verified manufacturer documentation for similar codes, E41 typically indicates a short circuit in the water temperature sensor or its wiring. In some cases, it can also point to water intrusion or a leak affecting the sensor circuit. The control board detects abnormal resistance or a dead short in the thermistor circuit and shuts down heating to prevent damage. This is a safety feature that protects the unit from operating with faulty temperature feedback.

## Common Causes

- **Failed water temperature sensor** The thermistor has shorted internally and sends a constant low-resistance signal to the control board.
- **Damaged or pinched sensor wiring** The harness insulation is cut, melted, or crushed, allowing bare wires to contact each other or metal housing.
- **Corroded or wet sensor connector** Moisture intrusion or condensation causes a short across the terminal pins or connector housing.
- **Internal water leak affecting the sensor area** A boiler or tank leak allows water to reach the sensor or wiring, creating a short circuit.
- **Control board misreading sensor resistance** The control board itself has failed and incorrectly interprets a healthy sensor signal as a short.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** for a full 30 seconds, then restore power and check if the E41 code clears after a restart.
2. **Locate the water temperature sensor**, usually mounted on the inlet/outlet piping or inside the control compartment near the heating elements.
3. **Inspect the sensor connector and wiring** for visible damage, melted insulation, pinched wires, corrosion, or any signs of moisture.
4. **Check for leaks** around the sensor mounting point, boiler connections, and inside the control compartment that could be shorting the circuit.
5. **Disconnect the sensor from the harness** and use a multimeter to measure resistance across the sensor terminals, then compare to your model's specification table (if no reading or zero ohms, the sensor is shorted).
6. **Replace the water temperature sensor** if it tests shorted or if the wiring harness is damaged beyond repair, making sure all connections are clean and dry before reassembly.
7. **Test the unit** by restoring power and running a heating cycle to verify the error does not return and water heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water heater technician if the power reset does not clear the code, if you find an active leak you cannot isolate, or if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components and multimeter testing. A pro can perform accurate resistance checks against factory specifications, identify hidden wiring faults inside the control cavity, and handle any tank or boiler repairs that may be causing sensor circuit shorts. If your unit is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.
