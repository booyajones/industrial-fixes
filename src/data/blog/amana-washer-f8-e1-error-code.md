## What this code means
The F8 E1 fault (also shown as Lo FL on some front-load models) tells you the washer is not detecting the correct amount of incoming water. The control board expected water to enter within a set time, but the sensed fill amount or rate did not meet the requirement. This is a no-fill or long-fill condition.

Amana lists this code on both top-load and front-load washers. It triggers when water supply or flow is restricted, when the inlet valve cannot open properly, or when water drains out as fast as it enters through a siphoning drain hose.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the water inlet valve first, but most F8 E1 faults are caused by closed supply valves, kinked hoses, clogged inlet screens, or siphoning drain hoses. Check and clear those restrictions before buying a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Hot or cold supply valves not fully open (~30%)** A partially closed shutoff valve at the wall cuts flow so the washer cannot fill within the control's time limit.
- **Kinked, crushed, or restricted inlet hoses (~25%)** Bends, internal blockage, or external crushing of the supply hoses reduce flow rate below the threshold.
- **Clogged inlet screens at the water inlet valve (~20%)** Mineral scale or debris trapped in the fine mesh screens on the back of the valve chokes off flow.
- **Drain hose siphoning or improper installation (~10%)** If the drain hose is inserted too far (more than 4.5 in.), sealed into the standpipe, or lacks an air gap, water drains out as fast as it enters.
- **Failed water inlet valve assembly (~10%)** When supply, hoses, screens, and drain are all correct, a stuck or weak inlet valve solenoid may prevent proper fill.
- **Anti-flood or flood-safe device restricting flow (~5%)** Add-on flood-protection valves or check devices reduce flow; Amana recommends connecting the washer directly to the house supply for best performance.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify house water supply.** Turn both hot and cold shutoff valves at the wall fully counterclockwise and confirm supply pressure by briefly opening a nearby faucet.
2. **Inspect inlet hose routing and condition.** Pull the washer away from the wall, trace each hose from the valve to the washer, and straighten any kinks, loops, or crushing. Confirm hot connects to hot and cold to cold.
3. **Remove anti-flood devices if present.** Disconnect any add-on flood-protection or check-valve devices and connect the washer's inlet hoses directly to the house supply for maximum flow.
4. **Clean the inlet screens.** Shut off water, disconnect both hoses from the back of the washer, and gently brush the fine mesh screens visible in each valve port with a soft brush, mild soap, and water. Do not remove the screens. Rinse and reconnect.
5. **Test hose flow separately.** With hoses disconnected from the washer, turn on the supply valves and run water through each hose into a sink or bucket. Replace any hose with weak or restricted flow.
6. **Inspect drain hose installation.** Measure drain-hose insertion into the standpipe or laundry sink; it must not extend more than 4.5 in. Remove any tape or sealant around the hose to make sure an air gap so water cannot siphon.
7. **Replace the water inlet valve if the fault persists.** With supply, hoses, screens, and drain all verified, unplug the washer, shut off water, disconnect hoses, remove the rear access panel, unplug the valve harness, unbolt the valve, and swap in a new assembly. Reconnect, restore power and water, and run a test cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared all hose restrictions, cleaned the inlet screens, corrected drain-hose installation, and verified full house supply pressure but the washer still throws F8 E1. A pro can test the inlet valve coil resistance, confirm control-board output voltage, and rule out a wiring fault or control issue. Also call if you are uncomfortable shutting off water, removing panels, or working with plumbing connections behind the washer.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts if a valve or hose is needed, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
