## Samsung Microwave E-14 Error — What It Means

The E-14 code on a Samsung microwave indicates a dry-up or no-load gas sensor error. Samsung lists this code in its official error table as a fault detected by the gas sensor circuit. The microwave's control board has seen an abnormal reading from the sensor path, which is part of the safety system that monitors internal conditions during operation.

Samsung treats E-14 as a technician-level fault. The manufacturer recommends a hard reset first (unplug or kill power at the breaker for at least 60 seconds), but if the code comes back after the reset, the issue requires professional diagnosis and repair rather than further troubleshooting by the owner.

## Common Causes

- **Gas sensor circuit fault** An open circuit, short, or out-of-range reading in the gas sensor itself is the most direct cause, because Samsung groups E-11 through E-14 as gas-sensor-related faults in its microwave code tables.
- **Control board detection problem** The main PCB interprets the sensor signal and generates the code, so a failed input circuit or faulty logic on the board side can produce E-14 even when the sensor element is intact.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector** The fault is circuit-based, so a poor connection, corroded pins, or damaged plug in the sensor harness path can break the signal and trigger the error.
- **Temporary glitch after moisture or power event** Samsung notes that moisture exposure or recent power surges can cause temporary faults, and a 60-second hard reset often clears these if no hardware damage exists.
- **Damaged wiring harness** Broken conductors or heat damage in the sensor cable between the sensor and the control board will interrupt the circuit and cause the board to log E-14.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off at the breaker or unplug the microwave** for at least 60 seconds to perform a hard reset, then restore power and test whether the code clears.
2. **Inspect for moisture or contamination.** Look for signs of steam damage, spills inside the cabinet, or any visible corrosion around vents and connectors before opening the unit.
3. **Disconnect power and remove the outer cover** to access the gas sensor circuit, following all electrical safety precautions and checking capacitors for stored voltage.
4. **Locate and inspect the gas sensor, harness, and connectors.** Check for loose plugs, burned terminals, broken wires, or any visible damage in the sensor path.
5. **Test the sensor electrically** if your service data provides resistance or voltage specifications (Samsung's public pages do not publish a numeric value for E-14, so consult your model's service manual).
6. **Replace the failed component.** Install a new gas sensor, repair the harness, or replace the main control PCB depending on which part tested out of spec.
7. **Reassemble, restore power, and run a test cycle** to confirm the code does not return and that normal operation resumes.

## When to Call a Pro

Samsung directs all persistent E-14 codes to qualified service after the 60-second reset fails. The repair requires disassembly, live voltage work around high-voltage capacitors, and access to service-level diagnostic data that is not published in the consumer support pages. If you are not trained in microwave repair, do not have a multimeter and the correct test procedures, or are uncomfortable working with stored high voltage, contact a Samsung-authorized technician or a qualified appliance repair professional. The gas sensor circuit and control board are not user-serviceable parts under Samsung's guidance for this fault.
