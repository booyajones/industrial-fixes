## Bosch Oven Er4 Error Code — What It Means

The Er4 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a fault has been detected by the control system. The exact meaning of this code depends on your specific model and its control board generation. Some models use Er4 to flag a sensor circuit problem, while others may assign it to a different component or relay issue. Without model-specific documentation, the code should be treated as a general system fault.

Because Bosch uses different control platforms across its product lines, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel to confirm what Er4 means for your oven. This prevents misdiagnosis and unnecessary part replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when Er4 appears, but a loose connector or failed sensor is often the real cause. Check all sensor plugs and measure resistance across sensor terminals before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty temperature sensor or loose sensor connector (~40%)** A temperature sensor with an open circuit, short, or corroded plug will send out-of-range readings that trigger a fault code.
- **Wiring harness damage or pinched wire (~25%)** Wires routed too close to the oven cavity or heating elements can melt insulation or break strands, causing intermittent faults.
- **Failed relay or triac on the control board (~20%)** A shorted or open relay that drives a heating element or fan can register as a fault and display an error code.
- **Control board memory or software glitch (~10%)** Voltage spikes or age can corrupt stored parameters, causing the board to throw spurious codes even when hardware is sound.
- **Ground fault or poor chassis ground connection (~5%)** A missing or corroded ground strap can let electrical noise interfere with control signals and trip error detection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait two minutes to allow the control board to reset.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether the Er4 code reappears immediately or only during a heating cycle.
3. **Remove the oven door** (if needed for access) and take off any trim panels or the back service cover to reach the control board and sensor.
4. **Locate the temperature sensor**, typically a probe mounted in the oven cavity or behind the back wall, and trace its wire to the control board connector.
5. **Inspect the connector** for bent pins, corrosion, or a loose fit; clean with electronics contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
6. **Measure sensor resistance** using a multimeter set to ohms; consult your model's service sheet for the correct range at room temperature (often 1,000 to 1,100 ohms near 70°F, but verify).
7. **Check the wiring harness** along its entire length for pinch points, burns, or frayed insulation near heating elements or sharp metal edges.
8. **Replace the sensor** if resistance is open, shorted, or far outside specification; if the sensor tests good but the code persists, the control board input circuit may be faulty and the board will need replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if the wiring diagram is not available for your model, or if you have replaced the sensor and checked all connectors but the Er4 code still appears. A pro can use a service manual and a multimeter to trace signals from the sensor input through the control board and identify whether a board-level repair or replacement is needed. Also call if you find evidence of arcing, burnt wiring, or if the oven trips the circuit breaker, since those conditions require immediate attention to prevent fire hazards.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
