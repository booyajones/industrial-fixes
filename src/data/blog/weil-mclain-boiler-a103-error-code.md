## Weil-McLain Boiler A103 Error — What It Means

The A103 code generally indicates a lower temperature sensor or thermistor circuit failure, and the boiler will shut down for safety. On some control platforms this means an open or shorted sensor circuit. An open circuit may display as approximately –40°F on the service screen, while a shorted sensor may read around +250°F. Because Weil-McLain uses different control platforms across its model families, the exact meaning of A103 can vary by boiler model. Always check your specific model's diagnostics menu and fault history to confirm the fault before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace the main control board when the real problem is a corroded sensor plug or a failed thermistor. Always measure the sensor resistance with a multimeter and compare to the manufacturer's ohm chart before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~40%)** Vibration, moisture, or age can loosen the plug at the thermistor or control board, or corrode the terminals and interrupt the circuit.
- **Failed lower temperature sensor (~35%)** The thermistor itself can fail open or short, sending an out-of-range signal that triggers the A103 fault and shuts down the boiler.
- **Damaged sensor harness (~15%)** Rodent damage, pinching during service, or heat can break wires in the harness between the sensor and the control board.
- **Control board input failure (~10%)** A failed input circuit on the main board can read the sensor incorrectly even when the sensor and wiring test good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the boiler** at the main switch or breaker and wait for all displays to go dark.
2. **Locate the lower temperature sensor** on the heat exchanger or lower tank (consult your model's service manual for the exact location).
3. **Inspect the sensor connector and harness** for loose pins, corrosion, moisture, or damaged insulation, and clean or repair as needed.
4. **Unplug the sensor from the control board** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure the sensor resistance across its two terminals.
5. **Compare the reading to the manufacturer's thermistor resistance chart** for your boiler model at the current temperature (consult the service manual or wiring diagram).
6. **Replace the sensor or harness** if the resistance is out of specification, the wiring is damaged, or the connector is badly corroded.
7. **Restore power, clear the fault code** from the diagnostics menu, and verify the boiler runs normally without the A103 code returning.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician if you are not comfortable working with the boiler's electrical system, if the sensor and wiring test good but the fault persists (suggesting a control board problem), or if your boiler model requires special tools or software to access the diagnostics menu and clear fault codes. Gas-fired boilers also require a technician to verify safe combustion and venting after any repair. Weil-McLain recommends confirming the exact fault in the boiler's diagnostics menu before replacing parts, because the same alphanumeric code format can have different meanings across model families.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
