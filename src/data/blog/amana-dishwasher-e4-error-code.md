## Amana Dishwasher E4 Error Code — What It Means

The E4 or F8E4 code on an Amana dishwasher indicates that water has entered the drip tray (base pan) underneath the machine. This activates the float switch or leak detection system and prevents normal operation. The dishwasher is protecting itself from further flooding by shutting down.

The code is not a simple drain fault. It signals that water has escaped from the internal water path and pooled in the protective drip tray below the tub. The water may have come from a loose supply connection, an over-sudsing event, or an internal leak in a hose, pump, seal, or valve assembly. Until the tray is emptied and the leak source is corrected, the dishwasher will not run.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the float switch or control board first. Instead, empty the drip tray and inspect all water supply connections for loose fittings or damaged washers before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or damaged water supply connection (~40%)** The inlet valve elbow or fill hose fitting is not fully seated, or the rubber washer or gasket inside is cracked or missing, allowing water to drip into the base pan during fill.
- **Disconnected or loose float switch connector (~20%)** The wiring harness plug at the float switch in the drip tray has backed out or vibrated loose, causing a false leak signal even when no water is present.
- **Drip tray full of water from a previous leak or overflow (~15%)** Water accumulated in the tray from an earlier event (over-sudsing, overfill, or small leak) and has not been emptied, so the float remains triggered.
- **Over-sudsing from wrong detergent or excessive detergent (~15%)** Using regular dish soap, too much high-efficiency detergent, or a rinse aid overfill creates foam that overflows the tub and spills into the drip tray, activating the float.
- **Internal hose, pump seal, or tub seal leak (~10%)** A crack in a water circulation hose, a worn pump seal, or a compromised door or tub gasket allows water to escape the closed water path and collect in the base pan during operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press Cancel once** to silence the alarm, then **press Cancel again** to clear the E4 or F8E4 code from the display.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** and carefully **tilt it back** or remove the lower access panel to inspect the drip tray underneath the tub for standing water.
3. **Remove and empty the drip tray** if water is present, taking care not to pull on or damage the float switch wire connector attached to the tray.
4. **Shut off the water supply** and disconnect the inlet fill line at the valve, then **inspect the 3/4 in. elbow fitting** and the rubber washer or gasket inside for wear, cracks, or improper seating.
5. **Reconnect the supply line** firmly by hand, then **snug with pliers** if needed, and verify the float switch wiring harness plug is fully seated and locked into place.
6. **Check for over-sudsing** by looking inside the tub for foam or residue, and if present, run an empty rinse cycle and switch to a low-sudsing HE dishwasher detergent going forward.
7. **Run a test cycle** and monitor the drip tray area for new water accumulation. If water reappears, inspect all internal hoses, pump seals, door gasket, and tub underside for cracks or active leaks, and replace any damaged components before returning the unit to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have emptied the drip tray and reseated all visible connections but the code returns immediately or water reappears in the tray during the next cycle. A technician can pressure-test the internal water path, inspect the pump housing and tub seals with the unit disassembled, and replace hard-to-reach components such as the circulation pump, sump assembly, or door gasket. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working under the dishwasher, if the float switch wiring is damaged and requires soldering or harness replacement, or if the leak source is not obvious after your initial inspection. Persistent E4 or F8E4 codes without a visible external leak often point to a hairline crack in a molded tub fitting or a failed internal seal that requires factory service documentation to locate and repair correctly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $5-25 in washers or fittings, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 for service call and leak diagnosis.
