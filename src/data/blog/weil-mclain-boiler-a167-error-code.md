## Weil-McLain A167 Error Code — What It Means

The A167 error code on a Weil-McLain boiler typically indicates an ignition or flame-proving failure during a call for heat. The boiler attempted to start its ignition sequence but could not successfully establish or prove flame. Because Weil-McLain fault codes are model-specific and vary across product lines, confirm the exact meaning of A167 by checking your boiler's service manual and rating plate. The code generally means the boiler tried to light but failed, leaving you without heat until the problem is resolved.

Common root causes include no gas flow to the burner, a closed or faulty gas valve, a weak or failed ignitor, a dirty or faulty flame sensor, poor grounding or wiring connections, or a control board issue that prevents proper ignition supervision. The boiler's safety system will lock out after repeated failures and display the fault code on the front panel or control screen.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board first. Instead, verify gas supply, test the igniter for continuity, and clean the flame sensor before replacing any electronic components.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or faulty flame sensor (~35%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame-sensing rod or pilot assembly prevents the control from detecting flame even when combustion is present.
- **Weak or failed ignitor (~25%)** The spark ignitor or hot-surface ignitor does not produce enough energy to ignite the gas-air mixture at the burner.
- **No gas supply or closed gas valve (~20%)** The manual gas shutoff is closed, the meter valve is off, or the automatic gas valve inside the boiler is stuck closed or has a failed coil.
- **Poor grounding or loose wiring (~10%)** A missing or corroded ground connection or a loose wire harness can cause false flame-failure signals or prevent proper ignition voltage.
- **Control board or module fault (~10%)** The ignition-control module fails to send the correct sequence of commands to the ignitor and gas valve, or it misreads the flame-proving signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control type** by reading the rating plate on the boiler jacket and locating the service manual for your series, because Weil-McLain fault codes are not universal across all product lines.
2. **Verify safe operating conditions** by confirming the gas shutoff is open, there is adequate gas supply pressure at the meter, there is no smell of gas, and the venting is clear of blockage or damage.
3. **Observe the ignition sequence** during a call for heat to see whether the inducer fan starts, the ignitor sparks or glows, and the gas valve clicks open in the proper order.
4. **Test the ignitor** by removing it from the burner assembly and checking continuity with a multimeter, or by verifying 120 V at the igniter circuit when the boiler is calling for heat (consult your model's wiring diagram for the exact test points).
5. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor** by removing the sensor rod or pilot assembly, wiping it with fine emery cloth or a dollar bill to remove carbon and corrosion, and reinstalling it in the proper position in the flame path.
6. **Check control outputs** by measuring 24 V at the gas-valve coil terminals when the boiler calls for heat; if voltage is present but the valve does not open, replace the gas valve; if no voltage, trace back to the control board.
7. **Inspect grounding and wiring harnesses** by confirming the boiler's metal frame is properly bonded to electrical ground and that all plug connectors at the ignitor, flame sensor, and gas valve are fully seated and free of corrosion.
8. **Replace the failed component** only after test evidence confirms which part is not performing, then reset the boiler and observe a full ignition cycle to verify the repair before returning the boiler to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician for any Weil-McLain A167 ignition fault. Gas-fired appliances require precise diagnosis of ignition timing, flame proving, gas-valve operation, and safe venting. A technician will use a combustion analyzer to verify proper air-fuel mixture, test ignition components with manufacturer-specified voltage and continuity checks, inspect the heat exchanger and burner for cracks or carbon buildup, and confirm that all safety interlocks are functioning before clearing the fault. Attempting DIY repair on a gas boiler without proper training risks gas leaks, carbon-monoxide exposure, and damage to the boiler's control system. Professional service typically costs between $150 and $300 and includes a full safety inspection to protect your home and warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
