## State Water Heater E142 Error Code — What It Means

The E142 code on State tankless water heaters is not precisely defined in widely available public documentation, but State and A. O. Smith tankless units share engineering platforms. Error codes in this range typically indicate ignition failure, flame sensing problems, or combustion airflow restrictions. The unit is locked out because it cannot establish or confirm a stable flame during startup or operation.

Because State does not publish a universal E142 definition across all models, the best first step is to locate your exact model and serial number on the rating plate and use the QR code or online lookup at State's support portal to pull the correct service manual. That manual will decode E142 for your specific unit and list the exact troubleshooting sequence. In the meantime, the most common root causes mirror those of similar lockout codes and involve blocked air intakes, dirty flame sensors, gas delivery issues, or vent restrictions.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or clogged inlet screen** Lint, dust, or debris restricts combustion air flow and prevents proper ignition or flame stability.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod** Buildup on the flame sensor prevents the control board from detecting the flame even when ignition occurs.
- **Low gas pressure or gas supply restriction** Insufficient gas volume or pressure at the valve prevents reliable ignition or sustains a weak flame that drops out.
- **Vent or exhaust blockage** Obstructed exhaust piping, too many elbows, or incorrect termination forces combustion gases back into the chamber and trips the safety lockout.
- **Scale or carbon buildup in the heat exchanger or burner** Hard water scale or combustion residue restricts flame geometry and airflow, causing poor combustion and repeated fault codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** at the service switches and gas shutoff valve before opening any panels or touching internal components.
2. **Check the air intake screens** on the exterior and interior of the unit for lint, dust, spider webs, or other debris and clean or replace any clogged filters.
3. **Inspect and clean the flame rod** by removing it from the burner chamber and gently polishing the sensing tip with fine abrasive cloth or emery paper to remove carbon or scale deposits.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming that the manual gas valve is fully open, the pressure regulator is set correctly, and no other appliances are starving the line.
5. **Examine the vent piping** from the exhaust outlet to the termination cap for blockages, excess length, improper slope, or too many elbows per the installation manual.
6. **Reset the unit** by restoring power and following the manufacturer reset procedure, then monitor startup for repeated faults or abnormal flame behavior.
7. **Consult the service manual** using your rating plate QR code or model number to confirm the exact E142 definition and any model-specific diagnostics, and call a licensed technician if the code persists after cleaning.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if the E142 code returns after you have cleaned the air intake, flame rod, and vent path. Persistent ignition faults often require combustion analysis, gas pressure measurement with a manometer, or control board diagnostics that are beyond typical DIY scope. Also call a pro immediately if you smell gas, hear unusual noises during ignition, or see soot or yellow flame, since these indicate incomplete combustion or a gas leak hazard.
