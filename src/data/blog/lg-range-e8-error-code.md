## LG Range E8 Error Code — What It Means

E8 is not a single factory-standard code across all LG range models. LG's official U.S. range error-code documentation does not list E8 as a universal entry. Based on LG's published troubleshooting for ovens and ranges, the most plausible interpretation is a temperature-sensing or heating-related fault on certain models. The code typically points to a problem in the oven's ability to monitor or control heat, which involves the thermistor (temperature sensor), its wiring, or the main control board.

Because E8 is not uniformly defined by LG, confirm your exact model number from the rating label before ordering parts. LG's verified fault families for heating and temperature issues include bad thermistor sensors, wiring or connector problems, control-board faults, low voltage, or failures in the heating circuit that prevent proper temperature control. A power reset is always the documented first step.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty thermistor or oven temperature sensor** The sensor may read out of spec, be damaged, or have failed entirely, preventing accurate temperature monitoring.
- **Loose or corroded wiring and connectors** Poor connections between the thermistor and control board interrupt the temperature signal and trigger a fault.
- **Main control board failure** The board's sensor-input or heating-output circuits may have failed, misinterpreting or failing to act on temperature data.
- **Low voltage or power-supply problem** Insufficient or unstable voltage can prevent the control system from operating the heating and sensing circuits correctly.
- **Heating element or relay fault** If the oven cannot heat normally, the control system may report a temperature-related error because target temperature is never reached.
- **Model-specific code definition** E8 may have a different meaning on your particular LG range model, so always consult the service sheet or tech-support line for your serial number.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the model and serial number** on the rating label (usually inside the oven door frame or on the back) and write them down for parts lookup and service-manual reference.
2. **Power-reset the range** by turning off the unit at the breaker or unplugging it for 30 seconds, then restore power and see if the code clears and the oven operates normally.
3. **Test whether the oven heats.** Set the oven to bake at a moderate temperature and listen for the fan and heating element, watching whether the cavity warms up within a few minutes.
4. **Inspect the thermistor and its wiring.** Disconnect power, remove the oven-cavity panels as needed to access the sensor probe, check for visible damage or corrosion, and verify that connectors are fully seated and clean.
5. **Measure the thermistor resistance** (if you have a multimeter and the service manual for your model) at room temperature and compare to the manufacturer's table. Replace the sensor if the reading is open, shorted, or far out of range.
6. **Check the main control board** for burned traces, loose connectors, or obvious component damage. If the thermistor and wiring test good but the code persists, the board's sensor-input or relay-driver section is the likely fault.
7. **Replace the failed component** (thermistor, harness, or control board) with the correct LG part number for your model, reassemble the range, restore power, and run a test bake cycle to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the power reset does not clear the code, if you are uncomfortable working inside a live 240-volt appliance, or if you lack a multimeter and the service documentation needed to test the thermistor and control signals safely. Also call for help if the thermistor and wiring both check good but the code returns, since that points to a control-board fault that requires board-level diagnosis and replacement. Licensed techs have access to LG's full service manuals, correct part numbers by serial-number range, and the test equipment to verify sensor accuracy and board output voltages without guessing.
