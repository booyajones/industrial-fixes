## Maytag Dishwasher F4E3 Error — What It Means

F4E3 on a Maytag dishwasher signals a circulation pump or wash motor communication and performance fault. The control board cannot properly drive or receive feedback from the motor that circulates water during the wash cycle. This is not an airflow issue, it is specific to the dishwasher's wash pump circuit in Whirlpool-family control platforms.

The fault may appear if the circulation pump has failed internally, if wiring between the control board and pump has loosened or corroded, or if the control board itself has lost the ability to read or command the pump. Some models use a combined sump or drain and wash pump module, so the exact assembly involved depends on your specific dishwasher design.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first, but a loose harness connector or a burned terminal at the pump is cheaper to fix. Always inspect the pump wiring and connectors with a flashlight and wiggle-test them before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed circulation pump or wash motor (~55%)** Internal windings or bearings in the pump motor have failed, preventing the control from spinning the impeller or reading normal current draw.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring harness or connector (~25%)** The plug between the control board and the pump has backed out, corroded pins have increased resistance, or heat damage has broken a conductor.
- **Faulty main control board (~12%)** The electronic control cannot properly drive the pump triac or read feedback, even though the pump itself is good.
- **Mechanical binding at the pump or sump (~8%)** A broken piece of glass, bone fragment, or foreign object jams the impeller, stalling the motor and triggering the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker, and leave it off for a full minute to clear any latched fault in the control board memory.
2. **Restore power** and press Start to run a normal wash cycle, watching for the F4E3 code to return and listening for the circulation pump to attempt startup during the wash phase.
3. **Access the circulation pump** by removing the lower front panel or the toe-kick and pulling the dishwasher forward enough to reach the sump area beneath the tub (consult your model's service manual for exact disassembly steps).
4. **Inspect the pump wiring harness and connector** for loose plugs, corroded terminals, burned pins, or damaged insulation, and wiggle each connector firmly while watching for intermittent contact.
5. **Check for mechanical binding** by rotating the pump impeller by hand (through the drain opening or directly at the motor shaft) to feel for grit, broken glass, or seized bearings.
6. **Test the circulation pump motor** with a multimeter set to resistance mode and compare the reading across the motor terminals to your model's service data (the research does not provide a universal ohms value, so consult your wiring diagram or tech sheet).
7. **Replace the circulation pump or wash motor assembly** if the resistance is out of range, the motor does not spin freely, or the wiring and control are verified good, using the part number specific to your dishwasher model and noting whether your design uses a separate pump or an integrated sump module.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V wiring, if your dishwasher is built-in and you cannot safely slide it out to access the pump, or if you have replaced the circulation pump and verified all connectors but the F4E3 code still returns. A technician has model-specific service data, diagnostic software, and the meter skills to isolate control board faults versus pump faults without guesswork. If your dishwasher is still under warranty or a service contract, contact Maytag or your dealer before opening any panels, since DIY disassembly can void coverage on some models.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–200 in parts, 1–2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200–400 including service call and pump assembly.
