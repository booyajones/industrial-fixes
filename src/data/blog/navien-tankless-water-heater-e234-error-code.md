## Navien Tankless Water Heater E234 Error — What It Means

The E234 error code on Navien tankless water heaters generally indicates a problem with the exhaust system or combustion air intake. Because exact code definitions can vary by model, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit to confirm the specific meaning for your model.

The code often points to blocked or obstructed vent pipes, improper venting installation, or a fault in the components that monitor exhaust temperature or airflow. The heater's control board expects certain airflow and temperature conditions during combustion, and when those conditions are not met, it triggers this fault and shuts down the unit to protect against unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust temperature sensor or air-pressure switch immediately. Before buying parts, inspect the entire vent run outdoors for nests, ice, lint, or debris and confirm the vent termination is clear and meets clearance requirements.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or obstructed vent pipes (~50%)** Exhaust or intake pipes clogged by ice, bird nests, lint, or other debris prevent proper airflow and trigger the fault.
- **Improper vent installation or termination clearance (~20%)** Vent pipes that are too long, have too many elbows, or terminate too close to a wall or soffit can create back-pressure or recirculation that trips the code.
- **Faulty exhaust temperature sensor (~15%)** A degraded or failed exhaust thermistor sends incorrect readings to the control board, causing a false alarm.
- **Faulty air-pressure switch (~10%)** The pressure switch may fail to close or may stick, signaling the board that airflow is insufficient even when the vent is clear.
- **Control board software glitch or connector corrosion (~5%)** Intermittent wiring issues or a temporary board fault can trigger the code without a physical blockage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve for safety.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vent termination** for blockage. Remove any ice, bird nests, lint, or debris from both the exhaust outlet and the intake opening.
3. **Trace the entire vent run** inside the home to confirm all pipes are firmly connected, properly supported, and free of sags or kinks.
4. **Check vent-pipe length and elbows** against the installation manual for your model to make sure the configuration does not exceed the maximum equivalent length.
5. **Power-cycle the heater** by restoring power at the breaker and reopening the gas valve, then wait two minutes and call for hot water to see if the error returns.
6. **Test the exhaust temperature sensor** with a multimeter for continuity and resistance if the error persists and venting is confirmed clear. Replace the sensor if readings are outside the expected range.
7. **Inspect the air-pressure switch** and its hoses for cracks, blockage, or disconnection. Test the switch for proper operation or replace it if faulty.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the vent configuration appears incorrect and you are unsure how to correct it, or if you have cleared all blockages and tested the sensors but the error code persists. A qualified technician can perform combustion analysis, verify proper draft, and diagnose intermittent control-board or wiring faults that are difficult to trace without specialized tools. Also call a pro if the heater requires vent reconfiguration or if local codes mandate licensed work on gas-fired equipment.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts if a sensor is faulty, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on diagnosis and part.
