## Frigidaire Range F2 Error Code — What It Means

On most Frigidaire electric ranges, the F2 code signals that the control detected an oven over-temperature condition or a fault in the temperature sensor circuit. The control shuts down heating to protect the oven from damage. Some Frigidaire control families use F2 to flag a touchpad or keypad problem instead, so the exact meaning depends on your model. Check your tech sheet or user manual to confirm which fault tree applies to your range.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor** The RTD probe at the rear of the oven cavity has drifted out of range or shorted, sending false readings to the control.
- **Damaged sensor wiring or connector** Loose pins, heat-damaged insulation, or broken wires between the sensor and control board interrupt the signal or create a short.
- **Stuck relay or faulty control board** A welded relay contact or failed output circuit on the electronic control keeps the heating element energized, driving true overheat.
- **Actual oven overheating from usage conditions** Blocked vents, foil covering the sensor area, or a self-clean cycle that ran too hot can trip the over-temperature limit.
- **Touchpad or ribbon cable failure** On models where F2 indicates a user-interface fault, a damaged keypad membrane or connector can trigger the code at power-up.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker, wait three minutes, then restore power to clear transient faults and confirm the code returns.
2. **Inspect the oven cavity** for foil, pans, or debris contacting the rear wall or sensor probe, and verify the oven has cooled completely after any self-clean cycle.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** at the rear of the cavity and check the probe and connector for physical damage, corrosion, or looseness.
4. **Measure sensor resistance** with a multimeter at room temperature; common RTD sensors read around 1,080 to 1,100 Ω, but consult your model's tech sheet for the exact specification.
5. **Trace the sensor harness** from the probe back to the control board, looking for burned insulation, broken wires, or loose pins at both ends.
6. **Inspect the electronic control board** for overheated traces, discolored relays, or visible damage; replace the board if a relay is welded closed or if the board outputs false heat signals.
7. **Test the touchpad and ribbon cable** if your model uses F2 for a UI fault; disconnect the ribbon, inspect for cracks or loose seats, and reconnect or replace the touchpad assembly as needed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240 V electric ranges, if resistance measurements do not match expected values and you lack a model-specific tech sheet, or if replacing the sensor does not clear the code. A pro can trace high-voltage relay circuits, test control-board outputs under load, and source the correct board or sensor for your exact model without guesswork. Because F2 definitions vary across Frigidaire control families, a technician with access to manufacturer service literature can confirm the fault tree and avoid unnecessary part swaps.
