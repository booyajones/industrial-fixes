## Frigidaire Range F2 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 code on most Frigidaire ranges signals an oven over-temperature fault. The electronic control has detected that the cavity temperature is too high or is being driven outside the safe operating range. This can happen when the sensor reports incorrect readings, when a relay on the control board sticks closed and allows uncontrolled heating, or after an actual overheat event such as a self-clean cycle that trapped heat in the cavity.

On some Frigidaire model families the F2 code can also indicate a touchpad or control board issue rather than a temperature problem. Because Frigidaire has used many different control platforms over the years, always check your model's tech sheet or wiring diagram to confirm the exact meaning for your unit. The diagnostic steps below address the most common over-temperature scenario documented across the majority of Frigidaire ranges.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, but a failed oven temperature sensor is usually the culprit. Test the sensor resistance with a multimeter at room temperature before ordering any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~50%)** The sensor probe inside the oven cavity reads out of range or opens intermittently, causing the control to see a false over-temperature condition.
- **Stuck bake or broil relay on the control board (~25%)** A relay on the electronic control board welds closed and drives the element continuously, creating actual uncontrolled heat and triggering the fault.
- **Wiring or connector damage (~15%)** Burnt, loose, or corroded terminals between the sensor and the control board cause intermittent readings that register as over-temperature.
- **Touchpad or control board fault (~10%)** On certain Frigidaire models the F2 code can indicate a keypad overlay or main control board issue rather than a true temperature problem.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> A stuck relay on the control board is feeding power to the element. Disconnect power immediately and replace the control board.<br><strong>No:</strong> The problem is likely a sensor reading or wiring fault. Proceed to test the sensor resistance.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>When you measure the oven sensor at room temperature, does it read 1,080–1,100 ohms?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The sensor is healthy. Inspect the wiring harness for burnt or loose connectors, then test the control board if wiring is intact.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor is out of specification. Replace the oven temperature sensor and retest operation.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Did the F2 code appear immediately after a self-clean cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The oven may have retained heat. Let the unit cool completely for several hours, then clear the code. </details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker before removing any panels or touching internal wiring.
2. **Note when the code appears** (during preheat, after self-clean, or at idle) and whether the oven continues to heat uncontrollably.
3. **Locate the oven sensor probe** inside the oven cavity, usually mounted on the rear wall, and follow its wire harness to the electronic control board.
4. **Inspect the sensor and harness** for visible damage, burnt insulation, or loose connector terminals at both the sensor and control board ends.
5. **Disconnect the sensor connector** and measure resistance across the sensor terminals with a multimeter at room temperature; a healthy sensor reads 1,080–1,100 ohms.
6. **Replace the sensor** if resistance is out of range, then reconnect power and test the oven through a heat cycle to verify the code clears.
7. **Inspect the control board** for burnt relays, scorch marks, or stuck contacts if the sensor tests good; replace the control board if a relay is stuck or the board shows damage.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if the control board shows signs of arcing or burnt components, or if you have replaced both the sensor and wiring harness without clearing the code. A pro can cross-reference your exact model's tech sheet to rule out a keypad fault or other platform-specific issue, and can safely diagnose stuck relays or control board failures that involve live voltage testing. If the oven continues to heat uncontrollably, disconnect power immediately and call for service to prevent fire risk.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–60 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
