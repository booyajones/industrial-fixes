## Fujitsu Mini Split E:45 Error — What It Means

The E:45 code on a Fujitsu mini split is a model-dependent fault code, meaning its exact definition varies by model family and is not universal across all Fujitsu systems. The code typically indicates a communication or control problem between the indoor and outdoor units, involving the PCBs, wiring, sensors, or DC voltage transfer. To diagnose E:45 accurately, you must reference the fault chart printed on the indoor unit's access door or consult the service manual specific to your model series, as Fujitsu fault codes are platform-specific rather than manufacturer-wide.

## Common Causes

- **Communication wiring fault** Loose connectors, open cables, miswiring, or damaged harnesses between the indoor and outdoor units prevent proper board-to-board communication.
- **Defective main PCB** A failed indoor or outdoor controller board cannot send or receive signals, triggering a persistent communication fault.
- **Sensor or thermistor failure** An open or shorted thermistor prevents the PCB from reading temperature correctly, causing the system to fault.
- **Power supply or grounding problem** Voltage drop, poor grounding, or electrical interference on the circuit disrupts DC communication voltage and creates fault conditions.
- **DC communication voltage loss** Field wiring issues or internal board faults can interrupt the low-voltage DC signal required for the units to communicate.
- **Transient electrical glitch** A temporary power surge or voltage spike can trigger the fault code even when no permanent failure exists.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Locate and consult the fault chart on your exact indoor unit model (printed on the access door or in the service manual) to confirm the specific meaning of E:45 for your system, as Fujitsu codes are model-dependent.
2. Power off the entire system at the breaker, wait 60 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the fault returns immediately or clears, isolating transient versus persistent faults.
3. Inspect all wiring and connectors between the indoor PCB, outdoor PCB, and any terminal blocks for looseness, visible damage, corrosion, or miswiring, and reseat any suspect connections.
4. Check DC communication voltage between the indoor and outdoor boards using your multimeter to determine whether the fault originates indoors, outdoors, or in the field wiring, following the voltage test procedure in your model's service guide.
5. Test thermistors and temperature sensors for open or short conditions by measuring resistance and comparing readings to the manufacturer's thermistor characteristics table for your specific model.
6. Verify power supply quality at the unit, checking for correct voltage, solid grounding, and absence of external electrical interference or voltage drop on the circuit.
7. Replace the faulty PCB (indoor or outdoor, as indicated by your diagnostic steps) if wiring and sensors check out, then reconfigure or re-address the system if required by your model's service procedure, and retest operation to confirm the code clears.

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not confident reading your model's fault chart, if you lack the tools to measure DC communication voltage between boards, or if the fault persists after power reset and wiring inspection. Communication and control faults on Fujitsu mini splits often require model-specific diagnostic procedures, board replacement with proper configuration, and refrigerant handling if mechanical access is needed.
