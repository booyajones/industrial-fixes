## Gree P5 Error Code — What It Means

The P5 error code on a Gree mini split indicates compressor overcurrent protection. The outdoor inverter system detected that the compressor is drawing more current than the allowed limit and shut the compressor down to protect the drive and compressor assembly. Gree's service manual groups P5 with other inverter-protection faults, meaning the outdoor unit's control electronics stopped operation during cooling or heating to prevent damage to the power module or compressor.

This is a protective shutdown, not a sensor fault. The system detected a real overload condition in the compressor circuit. The fault can stem from mechanical issues in the compressor itself, electrical problems in the inverter drive or power supply, or system conditions that force the compressor to work too hard.

## Common Causes

- **Compressor mechanical lock or high load** A seized, binding, or heavily loaded compressor will draw excessive current and trigger the protection immediately.
- **Inverter power module or drive board fault** The IPM (intelligent power module) or inverter control board can fail internally or deliver incorrect drive signals that cause overcurrent conditions.
- **Supply voltage problems** Incorrect line voltage, unstable power, or loose connections feeding the outdoor unit will cause abnormal compressor current draw.
- **Wiring or connection faults** Damaged, loose, or corroded compressor terminals, harness connectors, or outdoor-board wiring can create resistance or false contact that triggers the fault.
- **Refrigerant system issues** Abnormal refrigerant charge, restrictions, or poor heat exchange increase compressor load and push current above the protection threshold.
- **Blocked condenser coil or outdoor fan failure** Dirty outdoor coils or a failed condenser fan prevent proper heat rejection and force the compressor into overload conditions.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cycle power and confirm the fault.** Turn off the breaker or disconnect for 60 seconds, restore power, and see if the P5 code returns when the unit tries to run.
2. **Inspect the outdoor coil and fan.** Clean the condenser coil if it is blocked with debris or heavy dirt, and verify the outdoor fan spins freely and runs at the correct speed during operation.
3. **Check supply voltage at the outdoor unit.** Measure line voltage at the outdoor disconnect or terminal block to confirm it matches the nameplate rating and that all connections are tight and free of corrosion.
4. **Inspect compressor wiring and terminals.** Remove the outdoor unit access panel and check the compressor terminal connections, harness plugs, and wiring for loose pins, burns, or damaged insulation.
5. **Evaluate refrigerant charge and system load.** Check operating pressures and superheat/subcooling against the model's specifications to identify overcharge, undercharge, or blockage issues that may overload the compressor.
6. **Test the inverter drive and IPM.** If supply voltage and mechanical checks are normal, measure DC bus voltage at the power module (roughly 150 V DC on 110 V supply or 300 V DC on 220 V supply when energized) and perform diode checks on the IPM following the service manual procedure.
7. **Replace the failed component.** Once you isolate the root cause through testing, replace only the defective part, whether it is the compressor, IPM, outdoor control board, fan motor, or damaged wiring.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live line voltage, refrigerant systems, or inverter electronics. P5 diagnosis requires multimeter skills, refrigerant gauges, and knowledge of inverter drive circuits. Misdiagnosis can lead to expensive parts replacement or safety hazards. If the fault returns after a power cycle and you cannot identify an obvious outdoor airflow or voltage problem, professional testing of the compressor, IPM, and control board is the safest and most cost-effective next step.
