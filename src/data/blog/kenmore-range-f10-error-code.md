## Kenmore Range F10 Error Code — What It Means

The F10 error code on a Kenmore range indicates that the electronic control has detected a runaway oven temperature or a shorted temperature sensor circuit. The control interprets temperature data that is either rising too fast, reading too high, or showing an electrical short. As a safety measure, the control locks out the oven and displays F10 to prevent overheating and potential fire hazards.

The exact behavior can vary slightly by model. Some units will beep continuously when the code appears, while others simply display the code and disable the oven. The error may appear during preheating, during a bake cycle, or even when the oven is off if the sensor circuit is shorted. Always consult your model's wiring diagram and service manual for the specific resistance values and troubleshooting steps, as sensor specifications differ across Kenmore range families.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control board first, which can cost over $200. Test the oven temperature sensor with a multimeter at room temperature and compare the reading to your model's specifications before spending money on a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~60%)** The sensor resistance drifts out of specification or develops an internal short, sending incorrect temperature data to the control.
- **Shorted or damaged sensor wiring (~20%)** The wires between the sensor and the control board may short against the oven frame, insulation may melt from heat exposure, or connectors may corrode and create erratic signals.
- **Faulty electronic oven control board (~15%)** The control board's temperature input circuitry or processor can fail and misinterpret a good sensor signal as a runaway condition.
- **Actual oven overheating from stuck relay (~5%)** A relay on the control board that switches the bake or broil element can weld closed, causing the element to stay on and the oven to overheat genuinely.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the range** or shut off the circuit breaker to remove all power before beginning any work.
2. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** inside the oven cavity, typically at the upper rear wall. It is a metal probe about 1/4 inch in diameter protruding into the oven.
3. **Remove the oven racks** and unscrew the mounting bracket that holds the sensor to the oven wall, usually two screws.
4. **Disconnect the wire connector** at the back of the oven where the sensor wiring passes through. You may need to remove the back service panel of the range to access the connector.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature, compare the reading to your model's specification table. A typical sensor reads around 1,000 to 1,100 ohms at 70°F, but consult your model's service manual for the exact value.
6. **Install the new sensor** by threading the probe through the oven wall, reconnecting the wire harness, and securing the mounting bracket with screws.
7. **Restore power** and run a test bake cycle at 350°F for 15 minutes to verify the code does not return and the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with electrical components or removing panels from the range. Also call a pro if you have tested the sensor and wiring and both are within specification, as diagnosing a faulty control board or stuck relay requires advanced tools and experience. If the range uses a high-voltage 240-volt circuit and you are unfamiliar with safely de-energizing and testing that wiring, a technician should handle the repair. Finally, if the oven continues to overheat even after the sensor is replaced, a stuck relay or control board fault requires professional diagnosis to avoid further damage or safety hazards.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
