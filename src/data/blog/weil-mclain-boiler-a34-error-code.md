## Weil-McLain Boiler A34 Error Code — What It Means

A34 is not a universal fault code across all Weil-McLain boilers. The exact meaning depends on your boiler model and which control board is installed. Weil-McLain stores fault and lockout history in the control's diagnostics menu, and the manufacturer directs technicians to read the displayed fault name in that menu and then consult the model-specific installation and service manual for troubleshooting steps. Without knowing your boiler family and control platform, the code cannot be reliably interpreted.

Across Weil-McLain models, common fault and lockout conditions generally involve ignition problems, flame-sensing issues, gas supply interruptions, low water pressure or loss, and sensor failures. If your boiler has locked out or displayed A34, start by identifying your exact model number and control type, then pull the fault history from the diagnostics menu to see the full fault description stored with the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace ignitors or gas valves without first cleaning the flame sensor or checking that the gas supply valve is fully open. Use a multimeter to test sensors by resistance before ordering parts, since a failed thermistor or low-water sensor can mimic ignition or temperature faults.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~30%)** Carbon buildup or a faulty flame rod prevents the control from proving ignition, leading to lockout after the ignitor fires.
- **Gas supply issue (~25%)** A closed manual valve, empty propane tank, or utility interruption stops gas flow to the burner.
- **Failed thermistor or low-water sensor (~20%)** A bad temperature or water-level sensor sends incorrect readings, causing the control to shut down or display a fault before attempting ignition.
- **Low system pressure or water loss (~15%)** A leak or air-bound circulator drops pressure below the low-water cutoff threshold, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Faulty ignitor (~10%)** A cracked or weak hot-surface ignitor does not generate enough heat to light the gas, resulting in repeated ignition failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your boiler model and control board.** Look on the rating plate inside the jacket and on the control itself. Write down the full model number and control part number.
2. **Enter the diagnostics or contractor menu.** Follow the button sequence in your manual to access the control's fault history or lockout log. Record the exact fault name and code displayed alongside A34.
3. **Check gas supply and system pressure.** Confirm the manual gas shutoff valve upstream of the boiler is fully open. Verify the system pressure gauge reads within the recommended range when cold, typically 12–25 psi for many residential systems.
4. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor.** Shut off power and gas, remove the flame rod from the burner assembly, and polish the sensing tip with fine emery cloth until shiny. Reinstall and restore power.
5. **Test suspect sensors with a multimeter.** If the fault history indicates a temperature or low-water error, disconnect the thermistor or low-water sensor and measure resistance. Compare the reading to the resistance table in the service manual.
6. **Replace the failed component.** If a sensor, ignitor, or gas valve tests outside specification, turn off power and gas, drain the boiler if required, swap the part, refill the system, and purge air from the loops and radiators.
7. **Clear the lockout and verify operation.** Press the reset button or cycle power to clear the fault. Monitor the boiler through a complete heating cycle to confirm ignition, flame proving, and normal operation without repeat faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot locate the fault history menu, if the stored fault description is unclear, or if the boiler repeatedly locks out after you reset it. Gas boiler work requires checking combustion, adjusting gas pressure, and testing flame-proving circuits with specialized meters. A technician will pull the full diagnostic log, measure sensor resistance and gas pressure, verify ignition timing, and replace any failed components while ensuring the boiler meets code and warranty requirements. Do not attempt to bypass safety interlocks or repeatedly reset a persistent fault without diagnosing the root cause.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–400 depending on the failed component and labor.
