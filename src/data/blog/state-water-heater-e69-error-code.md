## State Water Heater E69 Error Code — What It Means

State water heater manuals are model-specific and the exact definition of E69 is not published in universal support documents. The code appears on gas units and based on field troubleshooting patterns for similar State and White-Rodgers control systems, E69 most likely indicates a flame-sensing or ignition-proving failure. This means the control board or gas valve is not detecting flame correctly after the burner attempts to light, or the ignition sequence is not completing. The heater shuts down to prevent unsafe operation. Check your unit's QR-linked manual or rating plate for the exact fault definition and wiring diagram for your model.

In the field, this type of fault is most often caused by a dirty or corroded flame sensor or thermocouple that cannot detect the burner flame. Soot, dust, or carbon buildup prevents the sensor from proving flame presence. Other causes include burner assembly fouling, loose or corroded wiring at the gas valve or ignition module, low gas pressure, or a failing gas control valve. Cleaning the flame-sensing component resolves the majority of cases on otherwise healthy heaters.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor or thermocouple** Soot, dust, or carbon deposits coat the sensor tip and prevent it from detecting flame, causing the control to shut down the burner.
- **Contaminated or misaligned burner assembly** Debris in the burner orifice or air shutter causes incomplete combustion or a weak flame that the sensor cannot prove reliably.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections** Poor contact at the gas valve, ignition module, or sensor connector interrupts the flame-proving signal and triggers the fault.
- **Low gas supply pressure** Insufficient gas pressure prevents the burner from establishing a stable flame for the sensor to detect.
- **Failing gas control valve or ignition module** Internal faults in the valve or control board prevent proper ignition sequencing or flame sensing even when other components are clean.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the manual gas shutoff valve to the water heater before opening any panels or touching components.
2. **Remove the burner access cover** and visually inspect the burner chamber for soot, rust, debris, or signs of incomplete combustion.
3. **Locate and remove the flame sensor or thermocouple** from the burner assembly, taking care not to bend or damage the probe.
4. **Clean the sensor tip** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove all carbon, soot, and corrosion, then wipe clean.
5. **Check the burner orifice and air shutter** for blockage or misalignment and clean any debris from the burner ports with a small brush or compressed air.
6. **Inspect all wiring connectors** at the gas valve, ignition module, and sensor for corrosion, looseness, or damage, and reconnect securely.
7. **Reinstall the flame sensor** in the correct position in the flame path, restore gas and power, and test the ignition cycle to verify the fault clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame sensor does not clear the fault, or if you see evidence of sooting, incomplete combustion, or corrosion inside the burner chamber. Gas pressure testing, valve replacement, and ignition control diagnostics require specialized tools and training. If your heater is still under warranty, contact State customer service or an authorized service provider before replacing major components to avoid voiding coverage.
