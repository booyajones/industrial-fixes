## Samsung Washer C7 Error Code — What It Means

The C7 error code on Samsung front-load and top-load washers typically indicates a problem with water temperature sensing or water supply configuration. The washer has detected that the water entering the drum is not at the expected temperature for the selected cycle, or the hot and cold supply connections may be reversed. Some models may also trigger this code when water temperature sensors fail to communicate properly with the main control board.

Because Samsung uses different error code systems across model lines, the exact meaning of C7 can vary. Consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram behind the service panel to confirm the specific definition for your model. In general, this code points to the water inlet system rather than drain or spin issues.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many users replace the water temperature sensor or thermistor first. Before ordering parts, swap the hot and cold inlet hoses at the back of the washer and run a test cycle to see if the error clears.

## Common Causes

- **Reversed hot and cold inlet hoses (~45%)** Hot and cold supply lines connected to the wrong inlet valves confuse the temperature sensor and trigger the error.
- **Failed water temperature sensor or thermistor (~25%)** A faulty thermistor sends incorrect temperature readings to the control board or loses continuity entirely.
- **Clogged inlet valve screens (~15%)** Sediment or debris in the inlet screens restricts water flow and can affect temperature mixing.
- **Faulty inlet water valve (~10%)** One of the dual solenoid valves sticks or fails to open, preventing proper hot or cold water flow.
- **Wiring or connector issues (~5%)** Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring between the temperature sensor and the main control board disrupts signal communication.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves at the wall.
2. **Disconnect the inlet hoses** from the back of the washer and check that hot is connected to the hot inlet and cold to the cold inlet (consult the service label on the back panel for markings).
3. **Inspect the inlet screens** inside each valve port on the washer; use needle-nose pliers to remove and rinse under water if clogged with sediment.
4. **Reconnect the hoses** in the correct configuration, turn on the supply valves, and plug in the washer.
5. **Run a rinse or test cycle** to see if the error clears; if not, pull the washer forward and remove the top or rear panel to access the temperature sensor.
6. **Locate the water temperature sensor** (a small thermistor clipped into the tub or mounted near the inlet) and disconnect its two-wire harness.
7. **Test the sensor** with a multimeter set to ohms; at room temperature it should show continuity (consult your model's tech sheet for the resistance spec); replace the sensor if it reads open or far out of range.
8. **Reassemble the panels**, restore power, and run a full cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have confirmed the inlet hoses are correctly connected and the screens are clean, yet the error persists and you are uncomfortable removing panels or testing electrical components with a multimeter. A technician can quickly diagnose whether the issue lies in the temperature sensor, the inlet valve solenoids, or the main control board. Also call a pro if you discover water leaks, damaged wiring, or corroded connectors that require soldering or board-level repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
