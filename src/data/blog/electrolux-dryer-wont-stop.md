## Electrolux Dryer Won't Stop — What's Happening

When an Electrolux dryer won't stop running, it means the control board is not ending the cycle or cutting power to the motor and heater when it should. This is a symptom, not a single fault code. Electrolux dryers may also display codes like E42 (door open) or EF1 (ventilation blockage) if those conditions are present, but many run-on complaints happen without any code at all.

The most common root cause is a defective electronic control board where a relay stays closed or the logic stops recognizing cycle completion. Door switch faults, blocked venting, dirty moisture sensors, and wiring harness problems can also prevent the dryer from stopping normally. Electrolux fault charts point to control board and wiring issues when the machine behaves abnormally or does not respond to inputs.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Defective electronic control board or stuck relay** A failed relay circuit or logic fault on the main control board keeps power supplied to the motor and heater even after the cycle should end.
- **Faulty door switch or door not fully latched** A defective or misaligned door switch can prevent the dryer from recognizing that the door is closed, causing erratic cycle behavior or the machine to run continuously.
- **Blocked vent, exhaust tube, or lint filter** Restricted airflow from a clogged lint filter, kinked duct, or blocked exterior vent can trigger an EF1 code and cause the dryer to run longer or not stop as it tries to complete drying.
- **Contaminated or failed moisture sensor bars** Lint film or residue on the moisture sensor strips inside the drum prevents accurate moisture detection, so the dryer never detects dry clothes and keeps running.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness connections** Damaged, burned, or loose wiring between the control board, motor, user interface, or inverter can cause intermittent signals that prevent the dryer from stopping.
- **Motor or drive circuit fault** A motor relay, centrifugal switch, or motor-drive module problem can prevent the control from cutting off the motor even when commanded to stop.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the dryer from the wall outlet and wait one minute before performing any diagnostics or component tests.
2. Enter the dryer's diagnostic mode according to the service manual and retrieve any stored fault codes to identify the specific fault the machine has logged.
3. Open the door and inspect the door latch and switch area for damage or misalignment, then use a multimeter to test the door switch for continuity when pressed and no continuity when released.
4. Remove and clean the lint filter, then disconnect the exhaust duct and inspect it for kinks, blockages, or buildup, and verify the exterior vent cap opens freely.
5. Locate the moisture sensor bars inside the drum near the lint filter and clean them with rubbing alcohol and a soft cloth to remove any lint film or fabric softener residue.
6. Remove the rear panel or top cover and visually inspect all wire harnesses running to the control board, motor, and user interface for loose connectors, burn marks, or broken wires.
7. If wiring and mechanical components test good, replace the electronic control board as it is the most common cause when no other fault is found.
8. Reassemble the dryer, restore power, and run a short test cycle to confirm the machine stops at the correct end point and does not continue running.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with live voltage, retrieving diagnostic codes, or removing panels to access internal wiring and the control board. A qualified appliance repair pro can safely trace wiring circuits, test control outputs with specialized meters, and replace the electronic control board or other components without risk of shock or further damage. Professional diagnosis is especially important if the dryer has intermittent faults or multiple stored codes that require systematic elimination.
