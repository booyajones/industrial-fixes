## KitchenAid F6 E4 Error Code — What It Means

The F6 E4 code has different meanings depending on your KitchenAid appliance. On dishwashers, F6 E4 indicates an overfill or float switch open condition, usually triggered when the dishwasher is not level or the overfill float circuit is stuck or open. KitchenAid's dishwasher help documentation confirms that incorrect leveling can actuate the overfill or floater switch and trigger this code.

On KitchenAid ranges and wall ovens, F6 E4 points to a different problem entirely: a possible fault in the Oven Appliance Manager, Appliance Manager Control, or Oven Control, or the associated wiring between these components. KitchenAid recommends starting with a power reset for oven and range models showing this code.

## Before You Replace Anything

On dishwashers, many people replace the main control board when the actual problem is simply an unlevel installation or a stuck float. Always verify the unit is level and check the float mechanism before ordering any control.

## Common Causes

- **Dishwasher not level (~40%)** KitchenAid documentation states that incorrect leveling can actuate the overfill or floater switch and trigger F6 E4 on dishwashers.
- **Overfill float stuck or obstructed (~30%)** The float mechanism may be stuck in the up position, obstructed by debris, or not returning to its normal position, causing the float switch to remain open.
- **Control reset needed (range/oven) (~15%)** On wall ovens and ranges, a transient fault in the Oven Appliance Manager or control may trigger F6 E4 and clear with a power cycle.
- **Faulty control board (range/oven) (~10%)** The Oven Appliance Manager, Appliance Manager Control, or Oven Control may have failed and require replacement.
- **Damaged wiring or connectors (~5%)** Loose, damaged, or intermittent wiring harness connections between the float switch and control (dishwasher) or between oven controls (range/oven) can produce an open circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your appliance type.** Confirm whether you have a KitchenAid dishwasher or a range/wall oven, because F6 E4 means different things for each.
2. **For dishwashers, check leveling first.** Use a bubble level on the top edge of the door (when open) to verify the unit is level front-to-back and side-to-side. Adjust the leveling legs as needed until the dishwasher sits flat.
3. **Inspect the overfill float.** Remove the lower kick plate or access panel to expose the base pan. Locate the small plastic float (usually white or clear, shaped like a dome or cylinder). Press it down gently and release. It should spring back up freely without binding.
4. **Clear any obstructions.** If the float is stuck, remove any debris, food particles, or mineral buildup around the float housing. Wipe the area clean and test the float motion again.
5. **For ranges and ovens, perform a power reset.** Turn off the circuit breaker(s) feeding the appliance for one full minute, then restore power. Clear the code and run a test cycle to see if it returns.
6. **Check wiring and connectors.** If the float moves freely (dishwasher) or the reset did not clear the code (range/oven), unplug or disconnect power, then inspect all visible wiring harnesses and connectors for loose pins, corrosion, or damage.
7. **Replace the identified component.** If leveling, float cleaning, reset, and wiring checks do not resolve the fault, replace the float switch assembly (dishwasher) or the Oven Appliance Manager/control board (range/oven) as indicated by your model's service documentation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working around 120-volt or 240-volt wiring, if you cannot safely access the base pan or control area of your appliance, or if you have verified leveling and float operation (dishwasher) or completed a reset and wiring inspection (range/oven) but the code persists. A technician can run the service diagnostic cycle, measure continuity in the float switch circuit or control communication lines, and replace the correct component without guesswork. If your appliance is still under warranty, contact KitchenAid or an authorized service center before attempting any repairs yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
