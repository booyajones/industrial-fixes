## Navien Tankless Water Heater E226 Error — What It Means

The E226 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a communication or sensor issue within the unit's control system. The exact definition can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for your specific unit. This code generally appears when the main control board cannot communicate properly with a sensor or when a sensor is providing readings outside expected parameters. The fault may be intermittent or persistent depending on the underlying cause.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but loose wiring or a single failed sensor is usually the culprit. Inspect all sensor and board connections and test sensor continuity before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~40%)** Vibration, moisture, or poor initial installation can cause connectors at sensors or the control board to work loose or develop corrosion, breaking communication.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~25%)** A thermistor or other temperature sensor may drift out of range or fail open, triggering a communication or out-of-range fault.
- **Failed flow sensor (~15%)** The flow sensor may fail to send accurate pulses to the board, causing the system to register a communication or data error.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** The main PCB may have a failed communication circuit or damaged input, though this is less common than wiring or sensor issues.
- **Moisture or condensation intrusion (~7%)** Water vapor or condensation inside the unit can short sensor leads or corrode connectors, especially in high-humidity environments.
- **Voltage spike or power surge (~3%)** Lightning or utility transients can damage communication circuits on the board or sensors, often accompanied by other intermittent errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners. Set the cover aside and locate the main control board and sensor wiring harnesses.
3. **Inspect each connector** at the control board and at every sensor (temperature, flow, and any other visible sensors). Look for discoloration, corrosion, moisture, or loose fit.
4. **Reseat each connector** firmly. If you find corrosion, spray the pins lightly with electrical contact cleaner and wipe dry before reconnecting.
5. **Test sensor resistance** (if equipped with a multimeter). Disconnect one sensor at a time and measure resistance across its terminals. Consult your model's service manual for expected values at room temperature. Replace any sensor that reads open (infinite resistance) or far outside the specified range.
6. **Check the wiring harness** along its length for pinched, burned, or chafed insulation. Repair or replace damaged wire sections with appropriate gauge wire and crimp or solder connections.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then run a hot-water tap to start the unit. Observe the display for error codes and listen for normal ignition and burner operation. Clear the E226 code if it persists by cycling power once more.
8. **Monitor for recurrence** over the next few days. If the error returns, suspect a failing control board or an intermittent sensor and call a technician for advanced diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 120V wiring, if sensor resistance testing is beyond your skill level, or if all wiring and sensors check out but the error persists. A technician has factory diagnostic software and can read live sensor data to pinpoint intermittent faults. Also call a pro if the unit is still under warranty, since DIY repairs may void coverage. Gas-fired appliances require proper venting and combustion analysis after any repair, so professional verification is recommended whenever internal components are replaced.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
