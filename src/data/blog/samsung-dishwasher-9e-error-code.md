## Samsung Dishwasher 9E Error Code — What It Means

The 9E error code indicates a water-level sensing or low-water-level fault. The control board is not detecting the expected water level during the wash or refill sequence. This code appears when the dishwasher cannot detect proper water level during the wash cycle, or when a low water level persists for more than 5 seconds during operation.

The underlying issue is usually that the machine is not filling properly, water is not moving as expected through the system, or the water-level sensing circuit is not reporting correctly to the control board. The error triggers when the sensor assembly cannot accurately read the water level inside the tub.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water-level sensor or control board when the real problem is a contaminated fill reservoir on the left side. Pull the unit out, remove the reservoir, and clean it in warm soapy water before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty fill reservoir (~50%)** Food particles, grease, and oil accumulate in the fill reservoir and shutoff reservoir on the left side, blocking proper water flow and sensor operation.
- **Low incoming water supply (~20%)** Insufficient water pressure or partially closed supply valve limits the fill volume below what the sensor expects.
- **Restricted inlet valve or screen (~15%)** Debris in the inlet valve screen or a failing valve prevents adequate water from entering the tub.
- **Faulty water-level sensor (~10%)** The sensor assembly itself has failed or its wiring harness is damaged, preventing accurate level reporting to the control board.
- **Filter or circulation restriction (~5%)** A clogged filter or blocked circulation path affects water movement and the machine's ability to sense level correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cut power and water supply** to the dishwasher at the breaker and shutoff valve.
2. **Remove the lower rack** and pull the dishwasher forward enough to access the left side panel.
3. **Locate the fill reservoir and water-level sensor assembly** on the left side of the unit.
4. **Disconnect hoses and wiring** from the reservoir, noting the position of each clamp and connector for reassembly.
5. **Remove the fill reservoir** and clean it thoroughly in warm soapy water to remove all food particles, grease, and oil buildup.
6. **Inspect all wiring connectors and hoses** for damage, corrosion, or looseness and reconnect firmly.
7. **Reinstall the cleaned reservoir**, reattach hoses with clamps secure, restore power and water, and run a test cycle to confirm the 9E code is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are uncomfortable pulling the dishwasher out and accessing internal plumbing, or if cleaning the reservoir and reconnecting all wiring do not clear the 9E code. A technician can perform a full diagnostic of the water-level sensor circuit, test the control board, and replace components that require model-specific part numbers and reassembly procedures. Also call if you find water leaks during disassembly or if the inlet valve or control board needs replacement and you lack the tools or experience to swap those parts safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
