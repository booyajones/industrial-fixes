## Samsung Dishwasher 9E Error Code — What It Means

The 9E code signals that the control board is not detecting enough water in the tub during operation. This is a low water level or insufficient fill fault. The dishwasher expects a certain volume of water for washing and rinsing, and when the level sensor or control logic sees less than required, it stops the cycle and displays 9E.

The fault can stem from a blocked water supply, a dirty filter at the inlet connection, a kinked hose, debris in the circulation path, or a stuck float in the water-level sensing assembly. On some Samsung models the fill reservoir contains a small float that can become caked with mineral deposits or detergent sludge and fail to signal that water has entered. Less commonly, the inlet valve solenoid itself may be faulty or the main control board may have a sensing circuit problem.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first. Before ordering any circuit board, check the inlet filter screen for debris and verify the supply hose is not kinked or crushed, since a simple flow restriction will trigger 9E every time.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted water supply or shutoff valve not fully open** If the house supply valve is only partially open or home water pressure is too low (below 20 PSI in field practice), the dishwasher cannot fill to the required level.
- **Kinked or blocked fill hose** A hose that is crushed behind the unit or has an internal blockage will starve the dishwasher of water and prevent the tub from reaching normal depth.
- **Dirty inlet filter screen** The mesh screen where the supply hose threads onto the dishwasher traps sediment and mineral scale over time, choking flow and causing a low-level fault.
- **Stuck or contaminated water-level float assembly** Some Samsung models use a fill reservoir with a small float that signals when water enters, and mineral deposits or detergent sludge can glue the float in place so it never rises to tell the control board the tub is full.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** The solenoid valve that opens to let water in can fail mechanically or electrically, or become blocked with debris, preventing adequate fill even when supply pressure is good.
- **Clogged circulation filter or sump restriction** Heavy food debris in the filter basket or sump area can block water movement and affect the apparent water level, confusing the control into thinking the tub is low.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water supply valve under the sink or at the wall stub before starting any work.
2. **Run a test cycle** to confirm the 9E code appears and note when during the cycle it stops (usually early during fill).
3. **Check home water pressure** by running a nearby faucet at full force and verifying the supply shutoff valve to the dishwasher is turned completely open.
4. **Inspect the supply hose** from the shutoff to the dishwasher for kinks, crushing, or visible damage, straightening or replacing as needed.
5. **Remove and clean the inlet filter screen** by unscrewing the fill hose from the dishwasher inlet port, pulling out the small mesh screen with needle-nose pliers, rinsing under running water, and reinstalling.
6. **Pull out and clean the dishwasher filter assembly** from the tub floor, rinsing away food particles and checking the sump area below for debris or sludge that could block water flow.
7. **Inspect the water inlet valve** for mineral buildup or debris on the valve seat, and if accessible check the fill reservoir float assembly for sticky residue or restricted movement, cleaning with warm water and vinegar if needed.
8. **Test the inlet valve solenoid** with a multimeter set to resistance mode (field videos suggest 2 to 5 ohms, though manufacturer specs may differ), and replace the valve if it reads open or shorted.
9. **Restore power and water**, run a short cycle, and verify the dishwasher fills normally and the 9E code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified full water pressure, cleaned the inlet screen and filter assembly, inspected the hose, and the 9E code still appears. At that stage the problem is likely a failed inlet valve solenoid, a stuck float in the fill reservoir that requires disassembly to reach, or a fault in the water-level sensing circuit on the main control board. A pro can test voltage at the valve during a fill cycle, measure solenoid resistance against the service manual spec, and access hidden fill-reservoir components without risk of leaks or incorrect reassembly. Also call if you are uncomfortable working around water connections or if your dishwasher is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call and parts.
