## Mitsubishi Mini Split F4 Error — What It Means

The F4 error code on Mitsubishi mini split systems indicates a fault detected by the unit's control board. The exact meaning can vary by model and series, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the indoor unit cover for the specific definition. In many cases, the code relates to a communication problem, a sensor reading outside normal range, or a protection trigger that has shut down operation to prevent damage.

Because Mitsubishi uses different control logic across its product lines, the F4 code may point to different issues depending on whether you have a single-zone or multi-zone system, and the age of the equipment. Always verify the code definition in your documentation before attempting repairs.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the indoor control board assuming it has failed, when the real problem is a loose connector or a failing outdoor sensor. Check all wiring connections and measure sensor resistance before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Communication fault between indoor and outdoor units (~30%)** Wiring issues, damaged control cable, or connector corrosion can interrupt data signals and trigger the code.
- **Temperature sensor out of range or failed (~25%)** A thermistor on the indoor coil or refrigerant line may read incorrectly or have an open circuit.
- **Outdoor unit sensor failure (~20%)** The outdoor ambient or discharge sensor can drift out of tolerance or break, sending invalid data to the control board.
- **Control board malfunction (~15%)** The indoor or outdoor PCB may have a component failure or corrupted firmware that causes false fault codes.
- **Transient power issue or surge (~10%)** A momentary voltage spike or brownout can lock the system into an error state that clears after a full power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait two minutes to allow the system to reset.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether the F4 code reappears immediately or after a short delay.
3. **Remove the indoor unit cover** to access the control board and wiring terminals.
4. **Inspect all wire connections** between the indoor and outdoor units for loose pins, corrosion, or damaged insulation.
5. **Locate the thermistor connectors** on the indoor coil and measure resistance with a multimeter. Compare readings to your model's service table.
6. **Check outdoor unit sensors** in the same manner, looking for open circuits or readings far outside the expected range.
7. **Replace any sensor** that tests out of range or shows an open circuit, noting the connector orientation before removal.
8. **If all sensors test good**, consult a licensed technician to diagnose the control boards or refrigerant system.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the free checks do not clear the code, if you lack a multimeter to test sensors, or if both indoor and outdoor sensors measure within spec but the fault persists. Refrigerant-side diagnostics, control board replacement, and system commissioning require specialized tools and EPA certification. A pro can also retrieve detailed fault logs from the unit's memory and cross-reference the F4 subcode with Mitsubishi service bulletins for your exact model.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
