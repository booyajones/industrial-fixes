## Samsung Microwave E-62 Error — What It Means

The E-62 code on a Samsung microwave indicates the control board has detected a short condition in the humidity-sensing circuit. This is not a door-switch, voltage, or demo-mode problem. Samsung specifically classifies E-62 as a humidity sensor short. The microwave will usually not operate until the fault is cleared.

The error points to one of three components: the humidity sensor itself, the wiring or connector between the sensor and the control board, or the main control board misreading the sensor circuit. Moisture intrusion, corrosion, or physical damage anywhere in this circuit can trigger the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when E-62 appears. Inspect the humidity sensor and its wiring for moisture, corrosion, or pinched wires before ordering a board, since the sensor and harness fail far more often.

## Common Causes

- **Failed humidity sensor** The sensor element has shorted internally and continuously reports a fault to the control board.
- **Moisture intrusion at the sensor or connector** Steam, spills, or condensation have created a conductive path that the board reads as a short.
- **Damaged or pinched wiring** The harness between the sensor and control board has bare wires touching each other or the chassis.
- **Corroded or loose connector terminals** Oxidation or poor contact at the plug creates intermittent or permanent short conditions.
- **Failing main control board** The board's sensor input circuitry is faulty and misreads a normal sensor as shorted.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** and unplug the microwave. Wait 60 seconds, then restore power and check whether the E-62 code returns immediately or after a cook cycle.
2. **Access the electronics compartment** by removing the outer cabinet panels according to your model's service manual. Locate the humidity sensor, usually mounted near the vent or cavity opening.
3. **Inspect the sensor and harness** for water damage, corrosion, pinched wires, or loose connector pins. Look for discoloration, condensation, or any sign of moisture intrusion.
4. **Disconnect the humidity sensor connector** from the control board. Power the unit back on and observe whether the E-62 code still appears on the display.
5. **Test the sensor** using a multimeter if you have service data for your model, or substitute a known-good sensor to confirm the fault. If the code clears with the sensor disconnected or replaced, install a new humidity sensor.
6. **Check the wiring harness** for continuity and insulation integrity between the sensor and the control board. Repair any damaged wire segments or replace the harness.
7. **Replace the main control board** only if the E-62 code persists with the sensor disconnected and the harness verified intact. Clear any error memory by cycling power after the repair, then run a test cycle to confirm normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

This repair requires safe access to live and stored high-voltage circuits inside the microwave cabinet. Microwaves store lethal charge in the high-voltage capacitor even after unplugging, and improper discharge or contact with the magnetron circuit can cause severe injury or death. A qualified appliance technician has the tools and training to safely discharge the capacitor, isolate the humidity sensor circuit, measure the board's sensor input, and replace components without risk. If you are not comfortable working with high-voltage appliances or lack a service manual with the correct test points and discharge procedure for your exact model, call a professional. Most service calls for E-62 involve sensor or harness replacement and cost between 150 and 300 dollars, far less than the risk of electrical injury.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–300.
