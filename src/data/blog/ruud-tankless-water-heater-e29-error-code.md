## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E29 Error — What It Means

The E29 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters generally indicates a problem related to combustion or flame sensing. The unit is detecting an abnormal condition that prevents reliable ignition or flame detection. Because error codes can vary slightly between models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's service panel for the exact definition on your specific model.

The most common triggers involve scale accumulation on the heat exchanger or flame sensor, a dirty or corroded flame rod, gas supply problems, or venting restrictions. The error is designed to shut down the heater before unsafe operation occurs.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the gas valve or igniter when the real problem is a flame rod coated in carbon or scale that simply needs cleaning. Inspect and clean the flame rod before replacing expensive gas components.

## Common Causes

- **Scale or mineral deposits on flame rod or heat exchanger (~40%)** Hard water leaves calcium and mineral scale that insulates the flame sensor, preventing proper flame detection and triggering the error.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensing rod disrupts the signal to the control board, causing false flame-out detection.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or flow (~15%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed supply valve, or a failing gas regulator can prevent stable combustion and trip the error.
- **Restricted air intake or exhaust vent (~10%)** Blocked venting or intake screens reduce oxygen supply and prevent complete combustion, leading to flame sensing errors.
- **Faulty flame sensor or wiring (~7%)** A failed flame rod or broken wire between the sensor and control board can send incorrect signals and trigger the code.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** Occasional firmware glitches or failed components on the main control board can generate false E29 codes even when combustion is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and the manual gas shutoff valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** and locate the burner compartment. Consult your model's service manual for the exact location of the flame rod.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for soot, carbon, or white mineral scale. Remove the rod by loosening the mounting screw or bracket.
4. **Clean the flame rod** gently with fine-grit emery cloth or steel wool until the metal surface is shiny. Wipe with a clean dry cloth.
5. **Flush and descale the heat exchanger** if your water is hard or if the unit has run for more than a year without service. Use a pump and descaling solution designed for tankless heaters.
6. **Check the intake and exhaust vents** for blockages, corrosion, or improper slope. Clear any debris and verify that the vent run matches the manufacturer's installation requirements.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod and burner assembly**, restore gas and power, and monitor the heater through several hot-water cycles to confirm the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas connections, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and descaling the heat exchanger, or if you measure low gas pressure at the inlet. Combustion diagnostics and gas-pressure testing require specialized tools and training. A pro can also verify that the venting is installed correctly and that the control board and wiring are functioning properly. Any work involving the gas valve or internal wiring should be left to a qualified service technician.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
