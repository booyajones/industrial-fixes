## LG Microwave F-5 Error Code — What It Means

The F-5 code on an LG microwave indicates a damper switch error. The control board has detected a fault in the damper switch circuit, which monitors the position or operation of an internal air damper used in convection or combination cooking modes. LG identifies this as a service-required fault.

Before assuming a part has failed, LG recommends checking for anything that prevents the door from closing fully. Packing material left inside the cavity, a mispositioned cooking rack, or foreign objects wedged in the door latch area can all trigger the code by preventing the door from latching correctly. If the door closes firmly and the code remains, the damper switch assembly, its actuator mechanism, associated wiring, or the control board's interpretation of that circuit is at fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the actual fault is a stuck damper actuator or a loose wire at the switch. Visually inspect the damper mechanism and connectors before ordering expensive boards.

## Common Causes

- **Door or latch obstruction (~35%)** Packing material, a cooking rack positioned incorrectly, or debris in the door latch prevents the door from closing completely and can mimic a damper switch fault.
- **Failed damper switch (~30%)** The switch that signals damper position has failed open, failed closed, or developed intermittent contact, sending a fault signal to the control.
- **Stuck or broken damper actuator (~20%)** The motorized or mechanical actuator that moves the damper blade is jammed, stripped, or disconnected, preventing the switch from toggling properly.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~10%)** A wire terminal at the damper switch or along the harness has pulled loose, corroded, or broken, creating an open circuit the control reads as a switch error.
- **Control board misinterpretation (~5%)** The main control board is reading the damper switch circuit incorrectly due to a failed input trace, relay, or microprocessor fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and wait 30 seconds to clear the code and reset the control board.
2. **Open the door and inspect the cavity** for any packing material, plastic wrap, foil, or cooking racks that extend beyond the turntable and could interfere with door closure.
3. **Close the door firmly** and listen for a positive latch click. If the door does not latch securely, inspect the latch striker on the door and the catches on the frame for damage or misalignment.
4. **Plug the unit back in** and attempt a short cook cycle. If the F-5 code reappears immediately, the fault is not a simple obstruction.
5. **If the code persists**, the damper switch circuit requires internal inspection. This involves removing the outer cabinet, locating the damper assembly (typically mounted near the magnetron or convection fan), checking connector integrity, and testing the switch for continuity.
6. **Test the damper actuator** by hand (with power off). The damper blade should move smoothly through its range. If it binds or the actuator gear is stripped, replace the actuator assembly.
7. **Measure continuity across the damper switch terminals** in both open and closed positions. Consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected state. Replace the switch if it does not toggle or shows infinite resistance in the closed position.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the door closes correctly, you have removed all obstructions, and the F-5 code returns after a power reset. Accessing the damper switch and actuator requires removing the microwave cabinet and working near high-voltage components, including the magnetron and capacitor. Even with the unit unplugged, the high-voltage capacitor can hold a lethal charge and must be safely discharged before any internal work. Technicians have the service manuals, wiring diagrams, and test equipment to isolate whether the fault lies in the switch, actuator, harness, or control board, and they carry the correct replacement parts for your specific model.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–$300.
