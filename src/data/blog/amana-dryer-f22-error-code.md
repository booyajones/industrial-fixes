## Amana Dryer F22 Error Code — What It Means

The F22 code on an Amana dryer signals that the machine control board has detected a fault in the outlet thermistor (exhaust temperature sensor) circuit. The control is not receiving a valid temperature reading, either because the sensor itself has failed, the circuit is open or shorted, or a wiring connection has broken down. In practice the dryer will usually run briefly and then shut down the heat or stop completely when this fault is logged.

Amana's own product help documentation lists F22 as a thermistor or outlet temperature sensor fault and recommends a five-minute power reset as the first corrective step. Because Amana dryers share the Whirlpool platform and control systems, service sources consistently tie this code to the exhaust or outlet thermistor circuit rather than door lock, airflow, or power supply issues. The fault means the board cannot trust the temperature reading it is receiving, so it stops the cycle to prevent overheating or unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some people replace the control board first when the actual problem is a loose connector at the thermistor or a broken wire. Always check the thermistor resistance with a multimeter and inspect the harness connections before swapping the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed outlet thermistor (~60%)** The sensor itself has gone open, shorted, or is reading out of range so the control rejects the signal.
- **Loose or corroded connector at thermistor or control (~20%)** A marginal pin fit, corrosion, or vibration-induced loosening breaks the circuit or creates intermittent contact.
- **Broken or damaged thermistor wiring (~10%)** A wire has been pinched, burned, or broken in the harness between the sensor and the board.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** If the sensor and all wiring test good, the board's thermistor input circuit itself has failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the dryer at the circuit breaker** and leave power disconnected for five full minutes to reset the control, then restore power and attempt a test cycle.
2. **Unplug the dryer and pull it away from the wall** to access the rear panel or top depending on your model.
3. **Locate the outlet thermistor** on the exhaust duct or blower housing and inspect the connector and wiring for burns, corrosion, or loose pins.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor connector** and use a multimeter in resistance (ohms) mode to measure across the thermistor terminals. Consult your model's service manual for the expected resistance at room temperature.
5. **If the thermistor reads open, shorted, or clearly out of range**, replace it with the correct part for your model number.
6. **If the thermistor reads normal**, check continuity in each wire from the sensor connector back to the control board connector. Repair or replace any broken or high-resistance wire.
7. **Reassemble the dryer, restore power, and run a test cycle** to verify the F22 code is gone. If the code returns with a known-good thermistor and wiring, replace the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you do not own a multimeter or are unsure how to measure resistance, or if you have replaced the thermistor and checked all wiring but the F22 code still returns. A technician has the service manual resistance tables for your exact model, the tools to trace intermittent wiring faults, and the diagnostic software to confirm whether the control board input circuit itself has failed. Professional service is also the safer choice if your dryer shares a 240-volt circuit with other appliances and you are not confident isolating power correctly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
