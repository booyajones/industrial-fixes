## Carrier E23 Error Code — What It Means

Carrier's E23 or code 23 is not a heat-pump outdoor-unit fault code in the available service literature. On Carrier furnace and air-handler diagnostics, code 23 indicates the pressure switch did not open or is stuck closed. This means the control board sees the switch as closed when it should have opened during the inducer sequence, pointing to a problem with the pressure-sensing system, the inducer draft proving, or the switch itself. If your system is a heat pump, the E23 display is most likely coming from the indoor furnace or air-handler section rather than the outdoor heat-pump unit.

The pressure switch monitors the draft from the inducer motor to confirm safe venting before allowing the burners to fire. When the code logs, the unit will lock out and not heat. Troubleshooting focuses on the pressure tubing, the pressure switch operation, the inducer motor and its airflow path, the intake and exhaust vents, and the control-board wiring that powers these components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the pressure switch first, but the switch itself fails less often than blocked tubing or a weak inducer. Inspect the tubing for kinks, water, or disconnection and confirm the inducer runs before buying a switch.

## Common Causes

- **Obstructed or kinked pressure tubing (~35%)** The small rubber hose that carries draft pressure to the switch becomes kinked, fills with condensate or debris, or pulls off its nipple, preventing the switch from sensing the pressure change when the inducer runs.
- **Stuck or failed pressure switch (~25%)** The diaphragm inside the switch sticks in the closed position or the contacts fail, so the board never sees the switch open even when draft is present.
- **Inducer motor not running or weak (~20%)** The inducer does not spin, spins too slowly, or does not develop enough draft to change the pressure switch, often because of a failed motor, capacitor, or lack of power from the board.
- **Blocked intake or exhaust vent (~10%)** The PVC or metal vent pipe is obstructed by ice, a bird nest, or installation debris, preventing the inducer from pulling enough air to prove the pressure switch.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~10%)** A failed board output, loose connector, or damaged wire prevents the inducer from receiving power or the pressure-switch signal from reaching the board correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the furnace service switch and the main breaker before opening any panels or touching components.
2. **Record the fault code** from the display or LED blink pattern and verify it matches code 23 in your model's service literature or on the unit label.
3. **Inspect the pressure tubing** that runs from the inducer housing to the pressure switch for kinks, disconnection, water, or debris, and clear or replace it if damaged.
4. **Test the inducer motor** by restoring power and calling for heat, listening for the inducer to spin up and feeling for airflow at the inducer outlet or vent termination.
5. **Check the intake and exhaust vents** outside for blockage, ice, bird nests, or installation debris, and clear any obstructions.
6. **Test the pressure switch** with a multimeter to confirm it opens when the inducer runs and closes when the inducer stops, and replace the switch if it stays closed or does not respond to draft.
7. **Inspect control-board outputs and wiring** if the inducer does not receive power or the pressure-switch signal does not reach the board, checking connectors and harnesses for damage or corrosion.
8. **Clear power for 30 seconds** to reset the lockout, then run a full heat cycle and monitor the code to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician for E23 whenever you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, high-voltage wiring, or the inducer and venting system. The repair requires accurate diagnosis of the pressure-switch circuit, testing the inducer motor and draft, inspecting the venting path, and sometimes replacing the control board. A technician has the meters, manometers, and service literature to test pressure setpoints, verify inducer performance, and safely restore the furnace to code-compliant operation. If you have cleared the tubing and vents but the code persists, or if the inducer does not run, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent misdiagnosis of the pressure switch or control board.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
