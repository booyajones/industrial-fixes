## Bosch Oven F32 Error Code — What It Means

The F32 error code on Bosch ovens can indicate different faults depending on the exact model series. On many models it points to a door-lock mechanism problem or an oven-temperature sensor circuit issue. The control board has detected that the lock relay, sensor, or wiring is out of range or not responding as expected. Because Bosch uses this code across different product lines with varying meanings, always consult your oven's wiring diagram or owner's manual to confirm what F32 specifically represents for your model number.

When F32 appears the oven typically will not start a cook cycle or will halt mid-cycle. The display may beep and the door may fail to latch or unlock. The root cause is usually a faulty door-lock assembly, a disconnected or shorted oven-temperature sensor, or a failing relay on the main control board. Inspect the door latch and sensor connections before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Test the door-lock motor continuity and the sensor resistance with a multimeter to isolate the real fault and avoid a $200–400 board replacement.

## Common Causes

- **Door-lock assembly worn or jammed (~40%)** The motor or latch mechanism inside the door-lock assembly sticks or fails to complete the lock cycle, triggering a fault.
- **Oven temperature sensor disconnected or shorted (~30%)** A loose plug or damaged wire at the sensor causes an out-of-range reading that the control interprets as F32.
- **Faulty relay on the main control board (~15%)** A relay that drives the door lock or sensor circuit fails, preventing the board from detecting proper lock or temperature feedback.
- **Wiring harness damage (~10%)** Heat or rodent chewing frays wires between the lock, sensor, and control board, causing intermittent or persistent errors.
- **Control-board software glitch (~5%)** A transient spike or memory error logs a false F32 that clears with a full power reset.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The door-lock mechanism may be working, so focus on the oven-temperature sensor and its wiring next.<br><strong>No:</strong> The door-lock assembly is likely jammed, misaligned, or failed and should be inspected or replaced.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error clear after a 2-minute breaker reset?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> A transient fault is likely, but if the code returns during the next cycle, check door-lock and sensor connections.<br><strong>No:</strong> A hardware fault in the lock, sensor, or control board is present and needs further diagnosis with a multimeter.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Do you hear the door-lock motor buzz or click when starting a cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The lock is receiving power, so check for mechanical jamming or a misaligned striker on the door.<br><strong>No:</strong> Test the lock motor's continuity and verify the control board is sending voltage to the lock circuit.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven to make sure safe work.
2. **Remove the oven door** by opening it fully, flipping the hinge locks forward, then lifting straight up off the hinges.
3. **Locate the door-lock assembly** on the front frame of the oven cavity, typically on the top edge or side, and note the wire harness plugged into it.
4. **Unplug the lock connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to test continuity across the motor terminals, looking for a reading between 50 and 200 ohms; infinite resistance means the motor is open.
5. **Inspect the latch striker** on the oven door for damage or misalignment that prevents full engagement with the lock.
6. **Check the oven temperature sensor** by locating its probe at the rear wall of the cavity, unplugging the connector, and measuring resistance at room temperature; consult your model's table for the correct value.
7. **Replace the faulty component** by unscrewing the old lock assembly or sensor, installing the new part, reconnecting the harness, and securing any mounting brackets.
8. **Reinstall the oven door**, restore power, and run a test bake cycle to confirm the F32 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with household voltage, if multimeter tests show conflicting results, or if replacing both the door lock and sensor does not clear the F32 code. A technician can perform board-level diagnostics, check relay function, and trace wiring faults inside the control panel. Also call a pro if the oven is still under warranty or if you lack the tools to safely remove the door and access internal components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–120 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280.
