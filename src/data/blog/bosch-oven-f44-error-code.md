## Bosch Oven F44 Error Code — What It Means

The F44 error code on Bosch ovens indicates a fault detected by the main control board, but the exact meaning can vary by model and generation. Some Bosch models use F44 to flag a control board communication issue, a temperature sensor problem, or a relay fault. Without documentation specific to your exact model number, the safest approach is to consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the oven cabinet for the precise definition.

Because Bosch uses proprietary error coding that changes across product lines, an F44 on one series may point to a different circuit than on another. The code generally means the control has detected an unexpected signal or loss of communication between components. Power cycling the oven and checking connector integrity are the first steps before ordering parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, which can cost over two hundred dollars. Before doing that, test the oven temperature sensor with a multimeter; a faulty sensor is much cheaper and often the real culprit.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~30%)** Vibration and heat cycles can loosen plugs between the control board and sensors, causing intermittent faults that trigger error codes.
- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~25%)** The resistance of the sensor drifts out of specification or the sensor fails open, sending incorrect readings to the control board.
- **Main control board fault (~20%)** Solder joints crack or a relay on the board fails, preventing normal operation and logging an error.
- **Power surge or brownout (~15%)** A momentary voltage fluctuation can lock the control into a fault state that clears after a full power cycle.
- **Faulty door lock assembly (~10%)** On self-cleaning models, a defective lock switch or mechanism can send conflicting signals that the control interprets as a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait two minutes, then restore power to reset the control board and see if the error clears on its own.
2. **Locate your model number** on the label inside the oven door or on the frame, and download the wiring diagram from the Bosch support site to confirm what F44 means for your specific unit.
3. **Remove the rear access panel or lower kick panel** (depending on your model) to expose the main control board and sensor wiring.
4. **Inspect every wire connector** leading to the control board, temperature sensor, and door lock assembly; look for loose pins, corrosion, or burn marks, and reseat each connector firmly.
5. **Test the oven temperature sensor** by unplugging it and measuring resistance with a multimeter; consult your model's table for the expected resistance at room temperature (often around 1,100 ohms at 70°F, but verify your specs).
6. **Replace the sensor** if the reading is far out of range or open circuit; if the sensor tests good, test the door lock switch if your model has self-clean, or proceed to control board replacement.
7. **Install a new main control board** if all other components test good and the error persists; transfer any jumpers or settings from the old board and reconnect all harnesses carefully, then restore power and run a test cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if the wiring diagram is unclear, or if you have replaced both the sensor and checked all connections but the error returns. A technician has the diagnostic software and part cross-reference tools to pinpoint faults quickly, especially on newer touchscreen models where control boards integrate multiple functions. Also call a pro if you see any evidence of arcing, burnt wiring, or a strong electrical smell, as these indicate a more complex electrical fault that requires expert troubleshooting.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-180 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
