## What this code means
The tE1 code on Samsung washing machines signals a temperature-sensor fault. The machine has detected either a heater error, a problem with the temperature sensor itself, or an incorrectly connected or disconnected sensor connector. In some cases the code appears when the washer reads an abnormally high temperature (roughly 100 to 150 °C) and shuts off input power to protect the tub and components from damage. This is an overtemperature protection event, often triggered when the heater runs without enough water in the tub or when the sensor gives a false reading.

Samsung support identifies three primary fault points for tE1: a bad heater or heater-related fault in the tub assembly, a loose or misconnected temperature-sensor connector or wiring, and an overtemperature condition that causes the control to cut power. The code is washer-specific in Samsung's own documentation and should not be confused with dryer fault codes that use similar letters.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when they see tE1, but the real culprit is usually the temperature sensor or its connector. Test sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect the harness before ordering any control board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty temperature sensor (thermistor) (~50%)** The thermistor at the tub reads out of range or open-circuit, causing the control board to flag a temperature fault and stop the cycle.
- **Loose, disconnected, or corroded sensor connector (~25%)** Samsung specifically lists connector problems as a cause, and a poor connection can create intermittent or false readings that trigger tE1.
- **Failed heater or heater circuit fault (~15%)** A shorted or open heater element can cause the control to read abnormal temperatures or detect a heating fault that sets the tE1 code.
- **Overtemperature event from low water level (~10%)** If the machine runs a heat cycle with insufficient water, the sensor may read 100 to 150 °C and the control cuts power to prevent damage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power and water.** Unplug the washer from the wall outlet and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves before any testing or disassembly.
2. **Access the temperature sensor.** Remove the lower front access panel or tilt the machine back to reach the sensor, which is typically mounted on the tub assembly near the heater.
3. **Inspect the connector and wiring.** Pull the sensor connector apart and look for corrosion, bent pins, or physical damage. Samsung lists connector faults as a common cause of tE1.
4. **Test sensor resistance.** With the sensor disconnected, measure resistance across its two terminals using a multimeter. At room temperature (68–76 °F) you should see roughly 54 to 66 kΩ. If the reading is open, shorted, or far out of range, replace the sensor.
5. **Check the heater circuit.** If the sensor and wiring test good, measure continuity across the heater element terminals and check for any short to ground. A faulty heater can trigger tE1.
6. **Replace the failed component.** Install a new sensor, heater, or connector pigtail as needed. Secure the sensor with its two Phillips screws and reconnect all harnesses.
7. **Reassemble and test.** Reinstall any access panels, restore water and power, and run a hot-water test cycle to confirm the code is cleared and heating works normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical diagnostics or if the sensor, connector, and heater all test within specification but the code persists. In that case the main control board may be reading the sensor circuit incorrectly, and board-level troubleshooting requires a service technician with a wiring diagram and replacement board on hand. Also call a pro if the machine shows signs of water damage to internal wiring or if you do not have a multimeter and the skills to safely measure resistance and continuity on live and unpowered circuits.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250.
