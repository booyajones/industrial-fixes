## What this code means
LdU on Whirlpool top-load washers indicates the lid-lock system cannot unlock the lid. The washer has detected a fault preventing the lid from releasing at the end of the cycle. On Cabrio-style and related models, this code points to a problem with the lid-lock mechanism, not a drain or water-level issue. The machine will not proceed to the next cycle or allow you to open the lid until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first when the fault persists. Before ordering the board, inspect the lid-lock assembly and wiring harness carefully. A worn lock or loose connector is much cheaper and far more common.

## Common Causes

- **Items on the lid or around the lock area (~40%)** Clothing, detergent bottles, or other objects resting on top of the lid or wedged near the lock strike physically prevent the mechanism from retracting.
- **Failed lid-lock assembly (~30%)** The lock actuator or switch mechanism inside the assembly wears out over time and can no longer release the lid.
- **Detergent buildup or misalignment (~15%)** Dried detergent residue or a slight shift in the lid alignment jams the lock strike so it cannot disengage cleanly.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~10%)** The connector to the lid-lock assembly becomes loose, corroded, or the wiring harness develops a break, preventing the control board from receiving the unlock signal.
- **Control board failure (~5%)** The main control board's lid-lock relay or circuit fails and cannot send the unlock command, even when the lock and wiring are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and remove any items from the top of the lid and the area around the lid-lock strike.
2. **Inspect the lid and lock area** for detergent buildup, foreign objects, or visible misalignment between the lid and the strike plate.
3. **Plug the washer back in** and run a short cycle to see if the code clears. If the lid unlocks normally, the obstruction was the problem.
4. **If the code persists**, unplug the machine again and locate the lid-lock assembly (usually under the top panel or behind the control console on top-load models).
5. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the lid-lock assembly and inspect the connector and wires for corrosion, damage, or looseness.
6. **Remove the lid-lock assembly** (typically two or three screws or clips) and test it by hand or replace it with a new assembly if the mechanism does not move freely.
7. **Reassemble the machine**, reconnect the harness, and test the cycle. If the fault remains after a new lock and clean wiring, the main control board is the likely cause.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable removing the top panel or control console to access the lid-lock assembly, or if you have replaced the lock and verified the wiring but the code still appears. A technician can test the control board's output signals and confirm whether the board or another component in the lid-lock circuit has failed. Also call a pro if the washer is still under warranty, since self-repair may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-100 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
