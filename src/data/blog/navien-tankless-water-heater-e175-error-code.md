## Navien E175 Error Code — What It Means

The E175 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a communication problem between the main control board and the air pressure sensor (APS). The control board is not receiving the expected signal from the sensor that monitors combustion air pressure. This prevents the unit from verifying safe operating conditions and the heater will not fire.

The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel. In most cases the system detects either no signal at all or a signal that is out of range, which can stem from bad connections, a failed sensor, or a control board issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the air pressure sensor when the real problem is a loose wire or corroded pin in the connector. Inspect and reseat all connectors to the sensor before ordering a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~45%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen the plug or corrode the pins at the air pressure sensor, breaking the signal path to the control board.
- **Failed air pressure sensor (~30%)** The sensor itself can fail internally and stop sending a valid signal even when wiring and hoses are intact.
- **Blocked or kinked sensor hose (~15%)** The small rubber hose that connects the sensor to the combustion chamber can become kinked, cracked, or clogged with debris, preventing accurate pressure readings.
- **Control board fault (~10%)** The main PCB may have a damaged input circuit or solder joint that prevents it from reading the sensor signal correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater using the front panel switch or by flipping the circuit breaker.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the mounting screws and lifting the panel away to expose the internal components.
3. **Locate the air pressure sensor** on the side of the combustion chamber. It is a small cylindrical component with a wiring plug and a rubber hose.
4. **Inspect and reseat the wiring connector** at the sensor. Pull it off and check the pins for corrosion or moisture. Spray with electrical contact cleaner if needed and push the plug back on firmly.
5. **Check the rubber sensor hose** for kinks, cracks, or blockage. Straighten any kinks and replace the hose if damaged.
6. **Power the unit back on** and monitor the display. If the E175 code clears, test hot water operation.
7. **If the code persists**, disconnect the sensor wiring and use a multimeter to check for continuity or consult the service manual for sensor resistance specifications. Replace the sensor if readings are out of range or if no signal is present.
8. **Reinstall the front cover** and secure all screws once the repair is complete and the heater operates without error.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the heater cabinet, if the error persists after replacing the air pressure sensor, or if you suspect a control board fault. Control board replacement requires disconnecting multiple wiring harnesses and ensuring correct gas and venting configuration. A technician can also perform combustion analysis to rule out venting problems that might stress the sensor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
