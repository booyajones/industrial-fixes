## Samsung Range E-01 Error Code — What It Means

The E-01 error code on Samsung ranges typically signals a communication issue between the control board and one of the sensors, most commonly the oven temperature sensor. This code may also appear when the control board detects an open circuit, short, or out-of-range reading from the temperature sensor. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the range for model-specific details.

When this code appears, the range usually stops heating or will not allow the oven to start. The display may beep and show E-01 continuously until the fault is corrected. In most cases, the problem is either a loose connector at the sensor, a damaged sensor probe, or a faulty sensor that needs replacement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real fault is a failed or loose oven temperature sensor. Always test the sensor resistance with a multimeter and inspect the connector before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Oven temperature sensor failure (~50%)** The sensor probe degrades over time or burns out, sending an open or incorrect reading to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~25%)** The plug at the sensor or control board comes loose or corrodes, breaking the signal path.
- **Damaged sensor wiring (~15%)** The sensor wire is pinched, cut, or melted by heat, causing an open or short circuit.
- **Control board communication fault (~10%)** The main control board loses the ability to read the sensor signal due to a failed input circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the range** from the wall outlet or turn off the circuit breaker to cut all power.
2. **Pull the range** away from the wall enough to access the back panel or rear of the oven cavity.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor**, a metal probe that extends into the oven cavity, usually at the upper rear wall.
4. **Disconnect the sensor's wire connector** and inspect it for corrosion, burns, or looseness. Clean with contact cleaner if needed and reseat firmly.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature, check the reading against your model's specification table (often around 1,000-1,100 ohms at 75°F). An open circuit (infinite resistance) or very low reading means the sensor is bad.
6. **Remove the mounting screw** holding the sensor bracket to the oven wall and pull the sensor probe out of the cavity.
7. **Install the new sensor** by sliding the probe into the mounting hole, securing the bracket with the screw, and plugging the connector back in.
8. **Restore power** and test the oven by setting it to bake at any temperature. Confirm the E-01 code does not return and the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working near 240-volt connections, if the sensor and wiring both test good but the error persists (suggesting a control board fault), or if the wiring harness is damaged and requires routing new wire through the range cabinet. A technician has the tools and experience to safely diagnose control board faults and can often source the correct part faster than a general supplier.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
