## Mitsubishi Mini-Split PA Error Code — What It Means

The PA error code on Mitsubishi mini-split systems does not have a single universal definition across all models. On many Mitsubishi Electric multi-zone systems, PA indicates a water protection or condensate protection fault at the indoor unit, triggered when the system detects abnormal liquid-line temperature combined with a water safety condition active for an extended period. On other Mitsubishi models, PA can represent a forced compressor error or refrigerant-flow protection state. Because Mitsubishi uses model-specific fault tables, you must verify the exact meaning using your unit's nameplate and service manual rather than assuming one definition fits all systems.

## Common Causes

- **Mode conflict between zones** In multi-zone installations, one indoor unit set to cooling and another to heating can trigger PA as the system cannot satisfy incompatible operating modes simultaneously.
- **Blocked or improper condensate drainage** A clogged drain line, incorrect slope, or blocked condensate pan can activate the water-safety float switch and hold the protection input long enough to generate the PA fault.
- **Water-safety device engaged** A stuck or correctly activated float switch or moisture sensor keeps the protection circuit closed, signaling the control board that a water overflow condition exists.
- **Abnormal liquid-line temperature** The indoor unit's liquid-line thermistor reading conditions consistent with poor refrigerant flow or charge problems can combine with other protection inputs to trip PA.
- **Refrigerant charge or flow issue** Low refrigerant, a closed service valve, or restricted filter-drier can cause compressor or refrigerant-side protection logic to activate the PA code on models where PA represents a forced compressor error.
- **Control or communication fault** Faulty wiring, corroded terminals, or a communication error between indoor and outdoor units can force the system into a persistent protection state displayed as PA.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact model number** from the nameplate on the indoor and outdoor units, then look up the PA code definition in your model-specific service manual or Mitsubishi installer fault table to confirm what PA means for your system.
2. **Check operating modes on all zones** if you have a multi-zone system and set every active indoor unit to the same compatible mode (all cooling or all heating), avoiding Auto mode to eliminate mode-conflict conditions.
3. **Inspect the condensate drain system** by verifying the drain hose has continuous downward slope, clearing any visible blockages, checking the drain pan for standing water, and confirming the float or water-safety switch moves freely and is not stuck in the activated position.
4. **Power-cycle the system** by turning off the circuit breaker for at least 30 seconds, then restoring power to reset the fault, and monitor whether PA returns during the next full operating cycle.
5. **Measure liquid-line temperature** at the indoor unit's service port using a calibrated thermometer or clamp probe, comparing the reading to room temperature to confirm the line is at least 10°F colder during normal cooling operation (this threshold is from field guidance, not factory spec).
6. **Check refrigerant charge and valve positions** by confirming all service valves are fully open, inspecting for frost or oil at fittings, and verifying superheat and subcooling values if you have gauges and the model-specific target ranges.
7. **Examine control wiring and connections** at the indoor unit terminal strip and outdoor unit control board, looking for loose terminals, corrosion, pinched wires, or any sign of water intrusion that could create a false protection signal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the PA code returns after you have confirmed all zones are in compatible modes and the drain system is clear, or if you do not have the tools to check refrigerant charge and pressures. Because PA is model-specific and can involve compressor protection, refrigerant-side diagnostics, or control-board logic, an incorrect guess can waste time and money on parts that do not fix the root cause. A qualified tech will have the Mitsubishi service manual, fault-table lookup access, and refrigerant gauges needed to diagnose the exact protection input that is active on your system and perform any refrigerant or electrical repairs safely and correctly.
