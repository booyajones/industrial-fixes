## LG CH45 Error Code — What It Means

The CH45 error on LG mini-split systems indicates a fault with the outdoor unit's mid-condenser coil temperature sensor thermistor. This sensor monitors refrigerant or coil temperature at a specific section of the outdoor condenser. The code appears when the control board detects an open circuit, short circuit, or abnormal reading from that sensor. It belongs to LG's temperature sensor error family and points to a sensor or wiring problem rather than a refrigerant or compressor issue.

## Common Causes

- **Open or shorted thermistor** The mid-condenser coil sensor itself has failed internally and reads infinite resistance, zero resistance, or an out-of-range value.
- **Loose or disconnected sensor wiring** The harness between the thermistor and the outdoor PCB has come unplugged, or a terminal has backed out of the connector.
- **Corroded or damaged sensor connector** Moisture, dirt, or physical damage at the connector pins causes intermittent contact or false readings.
- **Sensor not properly clipped to coil** The thermistor has shifted out of position or lost thermal contact with the condenser coil, producing erratic temperature signals.
- **Failed outdoor control board input circuit** If the sensor and wiring both test good, the PCB circuit that reads the thermistor may have failed.
- **Broken or pinched wire in harness** A conductor inside the sensor cable is fractured or pinched, creating an intermittent open that triggers the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Power down the entire mini-split system at the breaker and wait one minute, then power it back on to confirm the CH45 code reappears and is not a transient fault.
2. Remove the outdoor unit service panel and locate the mid-condenser coil temperature sensor, a small thermistor clipped or strapped to the condenser coil tubing.
3. Inspect the sensor connector and harness for loose fit, corrosion on the pins, broken wires, or damaged insulation, and clean or reseat the connector if needed.
4. Disconnect the thermistor from the outdoor PCB and measure its resistance with a multimeter set to the kiloohm range, noting whether the reading is zero, infinite, or a stable value.
5. Warm the sensor body gently with your hand and watch the resistance value, it should decrease smoothly if the thermistor is good.
6. Compare your reading to the manufacturer's resistance chart for your specific model if available, or replace the sensor if it reads open, shorted, or does not change with temperature.
7. If the sensor and wiring are confirmed good, inspect the outdoor control board for damaged traces or component failures at the sensor input, and replace the PCB if the code persists after sensor replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside line-voltage equipment, if you lack a multimeter or do not know how to test thermistor resistance safely, or if the code returns after you have replaced the sensor and verified all wiring. A technician has access to the exact resistance specifications for your model, refrigerant gauges to rule out related system faults, and the tools to test or replace the outdoor control board if the sensor circuit has failed. Professional diagnosis is also recommended if you see multiple temperature sensor codes at once, since that may point to a broader PCB or power supply issue.
