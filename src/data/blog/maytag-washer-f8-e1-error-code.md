## What this code means
On Maytag front-load washers, F8 E1 (also shown as Lo FL on some models) means the machine is not detecting the expected water level rise during the fill cycle. The washer tried to fill but did not sense enough incoming water in the time allowed. This is a fill problem or slow-fill condition, not a drain or pump fault. The machine may pause and display the code, sometimes running a brief drain routine before accepting further inputs.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the water inlet valve assembly first when the real problem is kinked hoses, clogged inlet screens, or a partially closed supply valve. Check water supply and clean the screens before ordering a valve.

## Common Causes

- **Hot or cold water valve not fully open (~30%)** A supply valve turned only partway open restricts flow and the washer cannot fill fast enough to satisfy the timer.
- **Clogged inlet screens (~25%)** Mineral scale, sediment, or rust collects in the fine mesh screens where hoses attach to the washer, choking water flow.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hoses (~20%)** A hose pinched behind the washer or bent sharply at a fitting blocks water flow.
- **Drain hose inserted too far or sealed into standpipe (~10%)** When the drain hose extends more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe or is taped air-tight, siphoning or pressure issues can confuse the level sensor.
- **Faulty water inlet valve assembly (~10%)** A solenoid coil that sticks or a valve body clogged with debris prevents one or both sides of the valve from opening fully, even when supply is good.
- **Low household water pressure or anti-flood device restricting flow (~5%)** Whole-house pressure below 20 psi or an inline flood-stop device cutting flow will starve the washer during fill.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are both hot and cold water supply valves behind the washer turned fully open?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Supply is correct. Move to checking the hoses and screens.<br><strong>No:</strong> Turn both valves fully counterclockwise, then run a cycle. If the code clears, the problem was restricted supply.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>When you disconnect a fill hose at the washer and turn on the supply valve, does water flow strongly into a bucket?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Supply pressure and the hose are fine. The blockage is at the washer inlet screens or inside the valve.<br><strong>No:</strong> The hose is kinked, crushed, or the household supply is weak. Replace the hose or call a plumber to check pressure.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After cleaning both inlet screens, does the washer fill normally when you start a cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The screens were clogged. The repair is complete.<br><strong>No:</strong> The water inlet valve assembly itself is likely faulty and needs replacement.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or unplug the washer, then turn off both hot and cold water supply valves.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the rear panel and the inlet hose connections.
3. **Inspect both hoses** from the wall to the washer for kinks, crushing, or sharp bends and straighten any restrictions you find.
4. **Disconnect each inlet hose** at the washer (keep a towel handy for residual water) and look into the inlet ports on the back of the machine where the hoses attach.
5. **Remove the small mesh inlet screens** with needle-nose pliers or a screwdriver and rinse them under running water, brushing gently with a soft toothbrush to clear sediment without tearing the mesh.
6. **Reinstall the clean screens**, reconnect the hoses snugly by hand plus a quarter-turn with pliers, and turn the supply valves fully open.
7. **Check the drain hose setup** at the standpipe: pull it out so it extends no more than 4.5 inches into the pipe and do not tape or seal the opening.
8. **Restore power** and run a short test cycle. If the washer fills normally and the code does not return, the repair is complete. If F8 E1 reappears after confirming good supply flow, replace the water inlet valve assembly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the inlet screens, confirmed strong supply pressure, verified correct drain hose installation, and the F8 E1 code still appears after replacing the water inlet valve assembly. At that point the fault is likely in the pressure sensor, wiring harness, or main control board and requires diagnostic tools to isolate. Also call a pro if your household water pressure is chronically low and you need a plumber to diagnose the supply side, or if you are uncomfortable working with water connections and electrical plugs at the same time.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
