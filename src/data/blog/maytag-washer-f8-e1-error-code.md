## Maytag F8 E1 Error Code — What It Means

F8 E1 on a Maytag washer means the machine is not detecting the correct amount of incoming water. You may also see LF (low fill) displayed along with F8 E1 for the same water-supply condition. The washer expects a certain fill rate and volume within a timed window, and when that target is not met the control throws this fault and stops the cycle.

Field experience shows this is almost always a water-supply problem rather than an electronic failure. Closed or partially closed hot or cold valves, kinked or restricted inlet hoses, clogged inlet screens, and improper drain hose installation that allows siphoning are the typical culprits. If the control appears unresponsive when the code displays, wait 8 to 10 minutes for the washer to finish its automatic drain routine before the buttons will accept input again.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the water inlet valve or main control board before checking the basics. Test hose flow into a bucket and inspect the inlet screens first, those fixes cost nothing and solve most F8 E1 faults.

## Common Causes

- **Closed or partially closed supply valves (~30%)** One or both hot and cold shutoff valves behind the washer are not turned fully open, starving the machine of water flow.
- **Clogged inlet screens (~25%)** Mineral buildup, sediment, or debris blocks the fine mesh screens where the hoses connect to the washer's inlet valve.
- **Kinked or restricted inlet hoses (~20%)** The supply hoses are bent, crushed against the wall, or internally blocked so water cannot flow at full rate.
- **Improper drain hose installation (~15%)** The drain hose is pushed too far into the standpipe or sealed airtight, creating a siphon that drains water out as fast as the washer fills.
- **Failed water inlet valve (~7%)** The valve's solenoid coils or mechanical ports are stuck closed or partially open even when the control energizes them.
- **Wiring or control board fault (~3%)** Loose connectors, corroded pins, or a failed fill-control circuit prevent the inlet valve from opening or the pressure sensor from reading correctly.

## Quick Diagnosis

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Wait for the drain cycle to finish.** If the control is locked and unresponsive when F8 E1 appears, the washer will automatically drain for 8 to 10 minutes before accepting any button press.
2. **Check both supply valves.** Turn the hot and cold shutoff valves behind the washer fully counterclockwise to make sure they are wide open.
3. **Inspect inlet hoses for kinks or crushing.** Pull the washer forward and straighten any bends or loops where the hoses connect at the wall or the back of the machine.
4. **Clean the inlet screens.** Turn off the water, disconnect both hoses from the washer, and use needle-nose pliers to pull the small mesh screens out of each inlet port. Rinse them under running water or soak in vinegar to dissolve mineral scale, then reinstall.
5. **Test hose flow.** With the hoses still disconnected from the washer, aim each into a bucket and turn on the supply valves briefly to confirm strong flow. Replace any hose that shows weak or restricted output.
6. **Verify drain hose installation.** Make sure the drain hose is inserted no more than a few inches into the standpipe and is not sealed airtight with tape or a rubber grommet. An air gap prevents siphoning.
7. **Run a test cycle.** Reconnect the hoses, restore power, and start a normal wash. If F8 E1 returns after confirming good water supply and correct drain routing, the inlet valve assembly, wiring harness, or main control may need professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified that both supply valves are fully open, both hoses flow strongly into a bucket, the inlet screens are clean, and the drain hose is installed correctly, yet F8 E1 still appears on every cycle. At that point the fault is inside the machine and requires electrical testing of the inlet valve coils, inspection of the wiring harness and connectors, and possibly replacement of the main control board. A service call will include proper voltage and resistance measurements that are not safe or practical for most homeowners to perform.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in hoses or screens, 20–45 min. A pro service call runs about $120–220 service call plus parts.
