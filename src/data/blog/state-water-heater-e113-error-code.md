## State Water Heater E113 Error Code — What It Means

The E113 error code does not appear in available State Water Heater manufacturer documentation, and because State is an A. O. Smith brand with model-specific fault codes, the exact meaning depends on your unit. State directs technicians to scan the rating plate QR code or reference the model-specific manual for accurate fault definitions. Without your exact model number and manual, any interpretation of E113 would be a guess.

For State and A. O. Smith tankless water heaters, ignition-related fault codes commonly point to blocked air intake screens, insufficient gas supply or pressure, or restricted water flow from a clogged inlet filter. If your unit is displaying E113 and failing to ignite or heat, these are the field checks most technicians start with. Locate your model number on the rating plate and pull the official troubleshooting guide from State's support resources to confirm the exact fault definition and component test procedures for your heater.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or dirty air intake screens** Restricted combustion air prevents ignition and is a common trigger for fault codes on State and A. O. Smith tankless models.
- **Low gas supply pressure or closed gas valve** Insufficient gas flow or an upstream shutoff valve not fully open will prevent the burner from firing.
- **Clogged water inlet filter or low flow rate** Sediment or debris in the inlet filter reduces flow below the minimum threshold required to activate the heating cycle.
- **Model-specific sensor or ignition component fault** Without the exact fault definition from your model's manual, the code may indicate a failed flame sensor, ignition transformer, or gas valve specific to your unit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your model and serial number** on the rating plate and scan the QR code or visit State's support page to download the exact troubleshooting manual for your heater.
2. **Inspect and clean the air intake screens** on the exterior vent termination and at the heater itself, removing any lint, debris, or blockage that could restrict combustion air.
3. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the upstream shutoff valve is fully open and verifying that other gas appliances in the home are operating normally.
4. **Remove and clean the cold water inlet filter** at the heater's inlet connection, flushing out sediment and checking for adequate flow when you restore water supply.
5. **Power cycle the unit** by turning off the gas valve and electrical power for two minutes, then restoring both and observing whether the code clears and the heater fires normally.
6. **Consult the model-specific fault table** in your manual for E113, noting any component test procedures, resistance or voltage specs, and reset instructions listed for that code.
7. **Call a licensed technician** if the code persists after these checks or if your manual directs you to test or replace ignition components, gas valve assemblies, or control boards that require specialized tools and gas-line work.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E113 code reappears after cleaning intake screens and filters, if your manual identifies E113 as a control board or gas valve fault, or if you lack the exact troubleshooting guide for your model. Any work involving gas line pressure testing, ignition transformer replacement, or control board diagnostics requires specialized tools and certifications. If the unit is under warranty, contact State customer support with your model and serial number before attempting component-level repairs to avoid voiding coverage.
