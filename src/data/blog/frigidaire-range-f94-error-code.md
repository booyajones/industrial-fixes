## Frigidaire F94 Error Code — What It Means

The F94 code on a Frigidaire electric range indicates a door lock issue in the oven lock system. Frigidaire groups F90 through F94 under the same door-lock fault family, so the troubleshooting approach is identical for all these codes. The control board cannot lock or unlock the oven door correctly, either because the motorized latch is stuck, the lock motor has failed, the position switch is not reporting back, or the wiring between the board and the lock assembly has a problem.

Frigidaire's first official step is to reset power for up to 30 seconds. If the code comes back after the reset, the manufacturer directs you to service because the fault is persistent and requires hands-on diagnosis of the lock mechanism, motor, switch, and related wiring.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or obstructed latch mechanism** Debris, grease, or a bent strike can physically block the lock rod or motorized latch from moving freely.
- **Failed door-lock motor** The small motor inside the lock assembly may be worn out or burned out and cannot drive the latch to the locked or unlocked position.
- **Faulty lock position switch** The control board relies on a feedback switch to confirm the door is locked or unlocked, and if that switch sticks or fails the board sets F94.
- **Wiring or connector problem** Loose, corroded, or broken wires between the control board and the lock assembly prevent the motor from running or the switch signal from reaching the board.
- **Control board output failure** The relay or driver circuit on the oven control board may be stuck, damaged, or unable to send the correct voltage to the lock motor.
- **Residual heat after self-clean** On some units a hot oven delays the unlock sequence, and if the timer or thermal interlock misbehaves the control can register a lock timeout as F94.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or unplug the range, wait 30 seconds, then restore power to reset the control board and clear any transient fault.
2. **Inspect the oven door and latch** for visible obstructions, grease buildup, bent hardware, or misalignment that would prevent the lock rod from sliding freely in and out of the strike.
3. **Access the lock motor assembly** by removing the control panel or the rear service panel (consult your model's service manual) and visually check all connectors and wiring for looseness, corrosion, or damage.
4. **Test the lock motor** by commanding a lock cycle (often by starting self-clean and immediately canceling) and listening or feeling for motor operation. If the motor does not run, use a multimeter to verify 120 VAC at the motor terminals when the lock is commanded. If voltage is present but the motor is silent, replace the motor lock assembly.
5. **Check the lock position switch** with an ohmmeter while manually moving the latch arm. The switch should change continuity state between locked and unlocked positions. If it does not toggle cleanly, replace the motor lock assembly (the switch is usually integral to the assembly).
6. **Inspect the control board** for signs of a stuck relay or burned traces near the lock-motor output. If the motor is always energized or the board shows visible damage, replace the oven relay board or main control board as applicable.
7. **Re-test the system** by restoring power, running a short lock and unlock cycle, and confirming the door transitions fully and the F94 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the power reset does not clear the code, if you are not comfortable working around 240 VAC wiring, or if you cannot safely access the lock motor assembly and control board. Diagnosing F94 requires a multimeter to test live voltage and continuity, plus the ability to disassemble the control console or back panel. A qualified appliance technician has the service manual, the correct replacement lock assembly for your specific Frigidaire model, and the experience to trace wiring faults without damaging the control board. Professional repair is also the safer choice if the oven is still under warranty or if you discover burned components that suggest a larger electrical issue.
