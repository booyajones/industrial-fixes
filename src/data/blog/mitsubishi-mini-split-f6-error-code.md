## Mitsubishi Mini Split F6 Error — What It Means

The F6 code on Mitsubishi mini split systems typically signals a communication fault or sensor problem between the indoor and outdoor units, or within the indoor unit itself. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the service cover. In general, the system has detected a data signal that is out of range, missing, or corrupted, preventing normal operation.

Because Mitsubishi uses different control protocols across its product lines, the F6 fault may relate to temperature sensor drift, a loose connector in the communication harness, or a control board that cannot read incoming signals. The system will shut down or limit capacity until the error clears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often buy a new indoor control board when the real problem is a loose or oxidized connector at the outdoor unit terminal strip. Inspect and tighten all low-voltage connections before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded communication wiring (~40%)** Vibration, moisture, or poor installation can loosen the low-voltage terminals that carry data between units.
- **Faulty temperature sensor (~25%)** The indoor or outdoor thermistor may drift out of spec or develop an open circuit, sending invalid readings to the control board.
- **Indoor control board failure (~20%)** Power surges or component aging can damage the microprocessor or communication circuit on the indoor PCB.
- **Outdoor control board failure (~10%)** The outdoor board may fail to send or receive data, especially after lightning strikes or capacitor wear.
- **Transient electrical noise (~5%)** Nearby motors or poor grounding can inject noise into the communication line, causing intermittent faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and confirm both indoor and outdoor units are de-energized.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** and locate the terminal block where the communication wires land, usually marked L1, L2, or similar low-voltage labels.
3. **Inspect and tighten** each screw terminal on the indoor block, looking for discoloration, corrosion, or strand breakage in the wire.
4. **Open the outdoor unit service panel** and locate the corresponding terminal strip, then repeat the inspection and tightening process.
5. **Measure thermistor resistance** at the indoor sensor connector (typically two-pin) using a multimeter set to ohms, and compare the reading to your model's temperature-resistance table.
6. **Restore power** and observe the display for thirty seconds. If the F6 clears, run a test cycle in cooling or heating mode.
7. **If the code returns**, consult the service manual for board-level diagnostics or contact a Mitsubishi-certified technician to test communication signals and replace any failed PCB.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring, if the error persists after you have cleaned and tightened all terminals, or if thermistor readings fall outside the published range and you lack the tools to replace the sensor safely. Mitsubishi mini splits use proprietary communication protocols, and misdiagnosing a board can waste hundreds of dollars. A technician with a dedicated service tool can read live sensor data, test each board's output, and pinpoint whether the fault lies in wiring, a sensor, or a control module.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
