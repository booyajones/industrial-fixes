## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E61 Error — What It Means

The E61 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater signals a problem with the combustion fan circuit. The control board is unable to confirm that the fan is operating correctly, which prevents the unit from firing. This can be caused by a mechanical failure of the fan itself, a faulty connection, or a problem with the fan sensor or control board communication.

The unit will not produce hot water while this code is active because safe combustion requires verified airflow. The fan must spin at the correct speed to evacuate exhaust gases and draw in fresh air for the burner. When the control board cannot detect proper fan operation, it locks out ignition as a safety measure.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board when the issue is actually a failed combustion fan motor or a loose wiring connector at the fan. Test the fan for continuity and check all connectors before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed combustion fan motor (~50%)** The fan motor windings burn out or the bearings seize, preventing the fan from spinning and signaling the control board.
- **Obstructed or blocked fan (~20%)** Dust, debris, or insect nests block the fan blades or intake, preventing proper rotation and airflow.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~15%)** Connections at the fan or control board become loose or corroded over time, interrupting the signal and power to the fan.
- **Faulty fan speed sensor or hall sensor (~10%)** The sensor that monitors fan RPM fails or sends incorrect readings, causing the board to register a fan error even when the fan spins.
- **Control board failure (~5%)** The circuit on the board that drives or monitors the fan malfunctions, triggering a false error code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the access panel to expose the combustion fan and control board.
3. **Inspect the combustion fan** for visible obstructions, debris, or damage to the blades and housing.
4. **Check all wiring connectors** at the fan motor and control board for looseness, corrosion, or burnt terminals, and reseat any suspect connections.
5. **Test the fan motor** for continuity using a multimeter across the motor terminals with power off; consult your model's wiring diagram for expected resistance.
6. **Replace the combustion fan assembly** if the motor shows no continuity or the bearings are seized, following the manufacturer's installation instructions.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then run a test cycle and monitor the unit for proper fan operation and code clearance.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working around gas appliances or if you lack a multimeter and wiring diagrams to test components safely. Combustion fans involve both electrical and gas safety, and improper diagnosis can lead to unsafe operation or carbon monoxide hazards. A pro can quickly test the fan motor, sensors, and control board, and will have access to OEM parts and the proper venting safety checks required after any combustion component replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
