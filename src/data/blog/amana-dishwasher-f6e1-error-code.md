## Amana Dishwasher F6E1 Error Code — What It Means

F6E1 on an Amana dishwasher indicates a water inlet or flow fault. The control board expected the machine to fill with water within a set time window, but it did not detect the correct fill signal or flow. This is most commonly caused by a physical restriction in the water supply path or a failed inlet valve, not a general control error.

While some Amana appliance documentation references F6 E1 as a possible appliance manager or wiring issue on ranges, field evidence for Amana dishwashers (which share architecture with Whirlpool-family machines) points clearly to a water fill problem. The machine either cannot get water in, or it cannot confirm that water has entered properly.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet valve screen** Sediment, mineral deposits, or debris block the fine mesh filter inside the valve inlet, restricting flow below the threshold the control expects.
- **Failed water inlet valve solenoid** The valve coil is open, shorted, or the valve mechanism is stuck closed, preventing water from entering even when power is applied.
- **Kinked or restricted supply hose** The fill hose is bent, crushed, or internally clogged, reducing flow so the dishwasher cannot fill in time.
- **Low household water pressure or shutoff not fully open** The supply valve at the wall is partially closed or house pressure is below the minimum needed for proper fill.
- **Control board or wiring fault** If the valve and plumbing test good, a bad connection, corroded terminal, or control board issue may prevent the fill circuit from completing correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset power** by switching off the dishwasher breaker for one full minute, then turn it back on and run a cycle to confirm whether the F6E1 code returns.
2. **Verify the water supply** is fully on at the shutoff valve under the sink and that the supply hose is not kinked, crushed, or visibly damaged.
3. **Disconnect power and water**, remove the supply hose from the inlet valve on the dishwasher, and inspect the inlet valve screen for mineral buildup or debris, cleaning it with a soft brush and water if restricted.
4. **Test the inlet valve electrically** using a multimeter on the valve solenoid terminals. Consult your model's service sheet for resistance specs or look for obvious open or shorted coil readings.
5. **Run the dishwasher diagnostic mode** (if your model supports it) to observe whether the machine calls for water and whether the valve opens when commanded.
6. **Replace the water inlet valve assembly** if the valve screen is clear, supply pressure is good, but the valve does not open or tests out of specification.
7. **Inspect wiring and control connections** to the inlet valve and, if all fill components check good, consider main control board replacement or professional diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with water supply plumbing, if you do not own a multimeter or cannot safely test electrical components with power disconnected, or if the inlet valve and supply path check out but the code keeps coming back. Control board diagnosis and replacement require model-specific knowledge and proper handling of electronic components. A pro can also run factory diagnostic routines and has access to up-to-date service bulletins for your exact Amana model.
