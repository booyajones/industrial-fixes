## Navien E225 Error Code — What It Means

The E225 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates that the unit has detected an overheat condition during operation and triggered a cascading safety shutoff. This means the water temperature inside the heat exchanger exceeded safe limits, causing the system to lock out and prevent further heating. The code typically appears when the burner continues firing but water flow is restricted or heat exchange efficiency has degraded.

This fault is designed to protect the heat exchanger from damage. The unit will not resume normal operation until the underlying cause is corrected and the error is cleared. Common triggers include scale buildup on the heat exchanger, a failed or weak circulation pump, a clogged inlet filter, air in the lines, or a faulty temperature sensor giving incorrect readings. Because the code involves both combustion and water flow systems, diagnosis requires checking multiple components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or temperature sensors when the real problem is scale coating the heat exchanger tubes. Flush the system and inspect the heat exchanger before replacing electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger (~50%)** Hard water deposits coat the internal tubes, reducing heat transfer and causing localized hot spots that trip the overheat sensor.
- **Clogged inlet filter (~20%)** Sediment or debris in the cold water filter restricts flow, reducing the volume of water passing through the exchanger and causing overheating.
- **Weak or failing circulation pump (~15%)** The internal pump cannot move water through the exchanger fast enough, allowing temperature to climb beyond safe limits.
- **Faulty temperature sensor (~10%)** An out-of-calibration or failed thermistor sends incorrect readings to the control board, triggering a false overheat alarm.
- **Air trapped in the system (~5%)** Air pockets after installation or service reduce water flow and create uneven heating that can trip the sensor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the dedicated circuit breaker and close the cold water inlet and hot water outlet isolation valves on the heater.
2. **Remove and clean the inlet filter screen** by unscrewing the cold water service valve cap, pulling the mesh filter, and rinsing under tap water to remove sediment.
3. **Flush the heat exchanger** using a descaling kit and pump. Connect hoses to the service valves, circulate manufacturer-approved descaling solution through the unit for the recommended time (consult your model's manual), then rinse with clean water.
4. **Inspect the circulation pump** by listening for unusual noise or vibration during operation. If the pump runs but flow is weak, it may need replacement.
5. **Check and test the temperature sensors** by measuring resistance across the thermistor terminals with a multimeter. Compare readings to the temperature-resistance table in the service manual.
6. **Bleed air from the system** by opening a hot water tap at the highest fixture in the home and running water until flow is steady and free of sputtering.
7. **Restore power and water supply**, then run a hot water tap and monitor the unit. If the code does not return and water heats normally, the repair is complete. If the E225 reappears, call a qualified technician for further diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if flushing and filter cleaning do not resolve the E225 code, or if you suspect a faulty pump or control board. Professionals have the tools to test gas pressure, measure flow rates, verify sensor calibration, and safely open the combustion chamber. Also call if the unit is still under warranty, as DIY repairs may void coverage. Gas-fired appliances require proper combustion analysis and venting checks that are beyond typical homeowner capability.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $180-400.
