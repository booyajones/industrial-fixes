## What this code means
On Amana top-load HE washers, the lid-related codes are LdL and LdU. LdL means the control requested the lid to lock but the lid lock did not engage. LdU means the control requested the lid to unlock but the lid lock did not release, often because something is physically blocking the lid or lock area. The lid must remain closed and locked during fill, wash, drain, rinse, and spin for safety, and the lock stays engaged while the basket is moving.

Amana specifically notes that objects on top of the washer or items directly under the lid can prevent the lock or unlock action and will trigger these codes. The washer will not proceed with a cycle until the lid can lock properly, and it will not unlock until the tub has fully stopped.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first, but most lid-lock codes are resolved by cleaning debris from the lid strike and lock area or replacing the lid lock assembly. Always clear obstructions and test the lock mechanism before swapping the board.

## Common Causes

- **Obstruction at lid or lock area (~45%)** Clothing, lint, detergent buildup, or items sitting on top of the washer prevent the lock from engaging or releasing.
- **Failed lid lock assembly (~25%)** Mechanical wear, broken latch mechanism, or electrical failure in the lock prevents proper lock or unlock operation.
- **Load interference under the lid (~20%)** Items directly beneath the lid keep the lid from closing completely or push against the lock strike.
- **Control board misreading lock state (~8%)** The main control board (such as W11116498 on some models) fails to correctly interpret feedback from the lid lock circuit.
- **Damaged wiring or connector (~2%)** Corroded pins, loose connectors, or broken wires between the control board and lid lock interrupt the lock signal or feedback.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Remove all obstructions** from the top of the washer and open the lid to inspect for clothing, lint, detergent buildup, or items directly beneath the lid that could block the lock strike.
2. **Clean the lid lock area** using a damp cloth to remove any residue or debris around the lock mechanism and strike plate on the washer cabinet.
3. **Wait for the tub to stop** if the washer has just finished a spin. The lid lock will not release while the basket is still moving.
4. **Unplug the washer** for 5 minutes to reset the control board, then reconnect power and close the lid to see if the lock engages and the cycle proceeds.
5. **Run the diagnostic mode** if the code persists. Consult your model's service manual for the button sequence to enter diagnostics and observe whether the lock command is issued and feedback is received.
6. **Inspect the lid lock assembly and wiring** by removing the top panel or cabinet access. Check the lock mechanism for mechanical damage, test the connector for corrosion or loose pins, and verify the harness is intact.
7. **Replace the failed component** if the lock does not actuate or if the lock works but the control continues to report an error. Start with the lid lock assembly, then move to the control board if the lock tests good.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable accessing the internal lid lock assembly, if diagnostic mode reveals a control-board fault you cannot interpret, or if you have replaced the lid lock and the code returns. A technician can use voltage and continuity tests to isolate whether the issue is in the lock circuit, the wiring harness, or the main control board. Professional service is also recommended if the washer cabinet is difficult to disassemble or if warranty coverage is still in effect.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts (lid lock assembly), 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
