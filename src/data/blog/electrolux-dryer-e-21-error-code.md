## Electrolux Dryer E21 Error Code — What It Means

The E21 error code on Electrolux dryers typically indicates a problem with the heating system or temperature sensing circuit. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for your specific dryer. In general, this code appears when the control board detects that the dryer is not heating properly, the temperature is out of range, or a sensor or safety device in the heating circuit has failed.

The dryer may run but produce no heat, or it may stop mid-cycle. Because the code relates to the heating and temperature system, the most common culprits are thermal fuses, thermostats, the heating element, or wiring connections in that circuit. Less often, the control board itself may be at fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a blown thermal fuse or failed thermostat on the heating element or blower housing will also trigger this code. Use a multimeter to test continuity on the thermal fuse and thermostats before spending money on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Blown thermal fuse (~40%)** A thermal fuse on the heating element housing or blower opens permanently when the dryer overheats, breaking the circuit and triggering the error.
- **Failed high-limit thermostat (~25%)** The high-limit thermostat cuts power to the heating element if temperature exceeds its setpoint, and if it sticks open or fails, the control sees an open circuit.
- **Open heating element (~15%)** A break in the heating element coil creates an open circuit that the control interprets as a heating system fault.
- **Loose or corroded wire connections (~10%)** Connectors at the heating element, thermal fuse, or thermostats can vibrate loose or corrode, causing intermittent or permanent open circuits.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or NTC thermistor (~5%)** If your model uses a thermistor to monitor exhaust or inlet air temperature, a failed sensor will send erratic readings to the control board.
- **Control board failure (~5%)** The main control board may misread signals or develop a fault in the heating-circuit relay, though this is less common than failed thermal components.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the dryer drum turn and run normally, but produce no heat at all?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor and drive system are fine; focus on the heating circuit, starting with the thermal fuse and thermostats.<br><strong>No:</strong> If the dryer stops immediately or won't start, the thermal fuse may be blown or a wire is disconnected; unplug and inspect the heating components.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>When you test the thermal fuse with a multimeter set to continuity, does it show zero resistance (a closed circuit)?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The thermal fuse is good; move on to testing the high-limit thermostat and cycling thermostat on the heating element housing.<br><strong>No:</strong> The thermal fuse is blown and must be replaced; also clean the exhaust vent thoroughly to prevent future overheating.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the exhaust vent hose kinked, crushed, or heavily clogged with lint?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Restricted airflow causes overheating and blows the thermal fuse; clear the vent, replace the fuse, and retest.<br><strong>No:</strong> Airflow is adequate; the fault is likely a failed thermostat, heating element, or wiring issue inside the dryer.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off the gas supply if you have a gas model.
2. **Remove the lower front panel or rear access panel** (depending on your model) to expose the heating element housing and blower assembly.
3. **Locate the thermal fuse** on the heating element housing or blower housing and disconnect one wire terminal.
4. **Test the thermal fuse for continuity** with a multimeter; if it shows infinite resistance (open), replace it with an exact replacement part.
5. **Inspect and test the high-limit and cycling thermostats** on the heating element housing the same way; replace any that are open.
6. **Check the heating element** for visible breaks in the coil and test it for continuity; replace if open.
7. **Reconnect all wire terminals firmly**, reassemble the panels, plug in the dryer, and run a test cycle to confirm heat and no error code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if you have tested the thermal fuse, thermostats, and heating element and all show good continuity yet the error persists. A technician can diagnose wiring faults, test the control board under load, and check any thermistor or sensor circuits that require specialized tools. Also call a pro if you have a gas dryer and suspect a problem with the gas valve or igniter, as gas appliance repairs require specific safety training.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
