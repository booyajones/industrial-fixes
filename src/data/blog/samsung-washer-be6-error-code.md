## Samsung bE6 Error Code — What It Means

The bE6 code on Samsung front-load washers is generally an unbalanced-load or spin-balance error, often displayed or misread as U6, Ub, or Ur depending on the model. Samsung describes Ur as an 'Unbalanced Load Retry' condition, where the machine attempts to add water, redistribute the load, and retry the spin cycle. The code means the washer has detected that the drum load is not balanced enough to safely complete the spin cycle.

This is a load distribution problem or a mechanical condition that prevents stable spinning, not a drain fault. The washer is protecting the drum, cabinet, and internal components from damage caused by excessive vibration during high-speed spin.

## Common Causes

- **Unevenly distributed laundry** Bulky items, single heavy pieces, or tangled loads bunch up on one side of the drum and prevent balanced spin.
- **Overloaded drum** Too much laundry exceeds the machine's ability to distribute weight evenly during the spin cycle.
- **Washer not level** An unlevel floor or improperly adjusted feet cause the cabinet to rock and trigger balance protection.
- **Worn suspension components** Failed shock absorbers, broken or stretched suspension springs, or damaged suspension rods allow excessive tub movement and spin instability.
- **Drive belt wear or damage** On belt-driven models, a worn, glazed, cracked, or stretched belt can contribute to spin instability and balance detection problems.
- **Temporary electronic glitch** A software or sensor fault can trigger the code without a mechanical cause and may clear after a power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power reset the washer** by switching it off, unplugging it for three to five minutes, then plugging it back in and restarting the cycle.
2. **Open the drum and redistribute the load** by separating tangled items, spreading laundry evenly around the drum, and removing any single heavy pieces that may have clumped together.
3. **Check that the washer is level** by placing a bubble level on top of the cabinet front-to-back and side-to-side, then adjust the leveling feet until the unit does not rock and all corners are stable.
4. **Run a spin-only test** with a small, evenly distributed load of towels or similar items to determine whether the fault follows the load or persists with the machine.
5. **Inspect suspension springs and rods** by tilting the washer back or removing the top and rear panels, looking for broken springs, disconnected mounting points, or rods that have lost tension or separated.
6. **Check the shock absorbers** for oil leakage, physical damage, or weak resistance by pushing down on the drum and watching for excessive bounce or slow return to rest.
7. **Examine the drive belt** on belt-drive models by accessing the rear panel, checking for cracks, glazing, stretching, or improper seating on the motor and drum pulleys, and replace if worn.
8. **Replace the failed component** found during inspection, whether suspension springs, shock absorbers, rods, or belt, then retest the spin cycle with a balanced load.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the code returns after you have redistributed the load, verified the washer is level, and run a balanced test load. Diagnosis and replacement of suspension components, shock absorbers, or the drive belt require disassembly, part identification by model, and reassembly with correct torque and alignment. If you have replaced mechanical parts and the code persists, a control board fault is possible but less common, and board-level diagnosis requires service tools and schematic knowledge. A qualified appliance technician can perform a complete tub-stability inspection, test all moving parts under load, and confirm whether the issue is mechanical or electronic.
