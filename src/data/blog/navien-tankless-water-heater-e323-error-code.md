## Navien E323 Error Code — What It Means

The E323 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a combustion-related fault, often tied to the flame sensor or airflow issues. The unit detects a problem with flame sensing or combustion stability and shuts down to prevent unsafe operation. This can stem from restricted ventilation, a dirty or failing flame rod, or incorrect gas pressure.

Because the exact definition of error codes can vary between Navien models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel for the precise meaning on your unit. The most common thread across models is a flame-detection or combustion anomaly that requires inspection of the burner assembly, venting, and gas supply.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame rod when the real culprit is a blocked vent termination or debris in the air intake. Clear all vents and check gas pressure before buying a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted air intake or exhaust vent (~40%)** Lint, bird nests, snow, or ice can obstruct the termination caps outside, starving the burner of combustion air or preventing exhaust gases from escaping.
- **Dirty or faulty flame rod sensor (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents it from detecting the flame reliably, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Incorrect gas pressure (~15%)** Supply pressure that is too low or too high disrupts stable combustion and causes the control board to fault.
- **Faulty igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked or misaligned igniter may produce weak ignition, leading to intermittent flame detection failures.
- **Control board or wiring issue (~10%)** Corroded connectors or a failing PCB can send false flame signals or fail to process sensor input correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the front panel switch and close the gas isolation valve below the unit.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vent terminals** for obstructions, including nests, lint, ice, or leaves, and clear any blockages you find.
3. **Check all vent pipe joints** inside and out for gaps, sagging sections, or condensate pooling that might restrict airflow.
4. **Power-cycle the unit** by switching the front panel back on and attempting to run hot water; note whether the error returns immediately or after ignition.
5. **Call a qualified gas technician** to measure inlet gas pressure, inspect the flame rod for carbon buildup, verify igniter function, and check control board connections.
6. **If the technician confirms a dirty flame rod**, they will remove the burner assembly, clean the sensor with fine sandpaper or a cloth, and reassemble the unit.
7. **Test the repair** by running multiple hot-water taps in sequence and monitoring for stable operation without fault codes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician whenever the E323 code persists after you have cleared all vent obstructions and power-cycled the unit. Diagnosing combustion faults requires a manometer to measure gas pressure, a multimeter to test flame-rod microamp current, and hands-on inspection of the burner and ignition components. Working inside a gas appliance also carries risks of gas leaks and carbon monoxide exposure. A qualified service tech will verify proper venting, clean or replace the flame rod, adjust gas pressure if needed, and make sure the unit meets all safety and manufacturer specifications before returning it to service.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
