## GE Washer E23 Error Code — What It Means

E23 is not a consistently documented GE factory code, but the most credible sources describe it as a drain timeout condition. The washer commanded a drain cycle but water did not leave within the expected time. Some repair videos also describe E23 as an overfill or water-level sensor fault, meaning the control believes the tub is too full even when it may not be. Because of this conflict, verify your exact model number and service literature before replacing parts. The practical takeaway is that either the machine is not draining fast enough or the control is seeing an invalid water-level signal.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain pump filter** Coins, lint, hair ties, or small debris block the pump trap and prevent water from draining within the timeout period.
- **Kinked or pinched drain hose** A sharp bend or crush in the drain line restricts flow and causes the washer to fail the drain cycle.
- **Faulty or jammed drain pump** The pump motor does not spin freely, the impeller is jammed, or the pump has failed and cannot move water out of the tub.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** If the tub is genuinely overfilling, the inlet valve may be allowing water to enter even when it should be closed.
- **Faulty water level sensor or pressure switch** If the tub is not actually overfilled, the pressure switch or its air hose may be disconnected or sending a false high-water signal to the control.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and protect the floor with towels before opening any drain components to avoid spills or shock.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** behind the washer for kinks, sharp bends, blockages, or incorrect routing height that could restrict flow.
3. **Open and clean the drain pump filter** (usually a twist-off cap at the front lower panel), remove all coins, lint, and debris, then reinstall the cap securely.
4. **Check the pump impeller** through the filter opening by rotating it by hand to confirm it spins freely and is not jammed or noisy.
5. **If the tub is actually overfilling**, unplug the machine and watch whether water continues entering. If it does, replace the water inlet valve assembly.
6. **If the tub is not overfilled** but the code persists, inspect the water level sensor or pressure switch and its air hose for disconnection, cracks, or clogs, and replace the sensor if testing confirms failure.
7. **Run a drain or rinse/spin test cycle** after repair to confirm normal draining and verify that the E23 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you have cleaned the filter and hose but the code returns, if you are unsure how to test the water inlet valve or pressure switch safely, or if the washer requires disassembly beyond the drain filter access panel. A pro can verify the exact meaning of E23 for your specific GE model, perform electrical diagnostics on the water level sensor and valve, and confirm that the control board is not at fault. If your washer is still under warranty, contact GE service before attempting any repair to avoid voiding coverage.
