## Navien Tankless Water Heater E303 Error — What It Means

The E303 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a flame loss or ignition failure during operation. The unit has detected that the burner flame extinguished unexpectedly or failed to ignite properly after the gas valve opened. This safety code shuts down the heater to prevent gas accumulation and alerts you to a problem with combustion, gas delivery, or flame sensing.

Because the exact meaning can vary slightly by Navien model and firmware version, consult your owner's manual or the label inside the front cover for a precise definition. In general, E303 points to an interruption in the flame signal that the control board uses to confirm safe combustion. Common causes range from a dirty flame rod that cannot conduct current properly to low gas pressure or a blocked inlet filter starving the burner of fuel.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or control board when the real problem is a fouled flame rod. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and check gas pressure at the meter before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or mineral buildup on the flame rod insulator prevent the sensor from conducting microamperes of current back to the control board, causing the board to think the flame is out.
- **Low gas supply pressure (~25%)** Insufficient gas pressure from a partially closed valve, regulator issue, or high demand from other appliances can cause the burner to light weakly or extinguish during operation.
- **Clogged inlet water filter (~15%)** Sediment or debris in the cold-water inlet filter reduces flow rate below the minimum threshold, triggering a flame cutout as the unit tries to protect the heat exchanger from overheating.
- **Blocked exhaust or air intake (~10%)** A bird nest, ice buildup, or lint blocking the vent pipe starves the burner of combustion air or prevents exhaust gas from leaving, causing incomplete combustion and flame loss.
- **Faulty gas valve or igniter (~5%)** A weak igniter or a gas valve that opens inconsistently can cause intermittent ignition failures, though this is less common than sensor and supply issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the isolation valves on both the cold-water inlet and hot-water outlet of the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover panel** by unscrewing the fasteners (typically two or four screws along the bottom edge) and lifting the panel up and off the hooks at the top.
3. **Locate the flame rod** inside the combustion chamber, a thin metal probe extending into the burner area with a white ceramic insulator at the base. Consult your model's service manual for the exact position.
4. **Disconnect the flame-rod wire** by gently pulling the push-on connector, then unscrew the mounting hardware (usually a single nut or screw) and slide the rod out of its bracket.
5. **Clean the rod and insulator** with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-metallic abrasive pad, removing all carbon deposits and white scale. Wipe with a clean cloth and avoid touching the cleaned surfaces with bare fingers.
6. **Reinstall the flame rod** in reverse order, ensuring the ceramic insulator seats fully in the bracket and the wire connector clicks firmly onto the terminal.
7. **Check and clean the inlet water filter** by closing the cold-water isolation valve, unscrewing the filter housing cap, and rinsing the screen under tap water. Reinstall the screen and cap, then open the valve slowly.
8. **Inspect the exhaust vent termination** outside your home for obstructions, ice, or debris. Clear any blockages and confirm that the vent cap louvers are open.
9. **Restore power and water flow** by opening both isolation valves fully and switching the circuit breaker back on. Open a hot-water tap to purge air from the system.
10. **Monitor the heater** through a full heating cycle. The burner should ignite within a few seconds and remain lit. If the E303 code reappears, measure gas pressure at the inlet with a manometer or call a licensed technician to verify supply pressure and gas-valve operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you have cleaned the flame rod and inlet filter, verified gas supply to other appliances, and the E303 code persists. A pro can measure gas pressure at the unit inlet with a manometer, test flame-rod microamp signal with a multimeter, inspect the gas valve for proper voltage and operation, and check exhaust draft with a combustion analyzer. Also call immediately if you smell gas, hear a continuous clicking from the igniter without ignition, or see soot or yellow flames in the burner window. Gas appliance work requires proper ventilation testing and code compliance, and many jurisdictions require a licensed contractor for any repair beyond cleaning external filters.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
