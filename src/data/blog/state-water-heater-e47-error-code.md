## State Water Heater E47 Error Code — What It Means

The E47 error code on State water heaters typically indicates a combustion air or venting problem that has triggered a safety lockout on the gas control. Because State manufactures multiple platform families, the exact definition of E47 can vary by model, so always check your specific model's manual (often accessible via the QR code on the rating plate) for the authoritative interpretation. In most cases, the unit has detected restricted airflow, blocked venting, or a combustion issue that prevents safe operation and shuts down the burner to protect your home.

This fault is not usually a single failed part but rather a system condition: something is blocking combustion air intake, restricting exhaust venting, or preventing the flame sensor from confirming clean combustion. The unit will remain in lockout until you identify and fix the root cause, then perform the prescribed reset procedure in your manual.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent** Debris, bird nests, ice, or improper vent termination can prevent combustion gases from escaping and trigger the safety lockout.
- **Restricted combustion air inlet or dirty filter** If your model draws combustion air through an inlet screen or filter, dust and lint buildup will starve the burner of oxygen and cause the fault.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor or thermocouple** Soot, oxidation, or carbon buildup on the flame-sensing element prevents the control from confirming ignition and forces a shutdown.
- **Inadequate or interrupted gas supply** Low gas pressure, a closed manual shutoff, or air in the line can produce weak or unstable combustion that the control reads as unsafe.
- **Burner contamination or poor combustion** Dust, spider webs, or misaligned burner jets create incomplete combustion, excess soot, and repeated lockouts over time.
- **Failed gas control valve or ignition module** If all air, vent, and sensor checks pass but the unit still locks out, the gas valve or electronic control may need replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your exact model number** from the rating plate and download the matching manual using the QR code or State's support page to confirm the E47 definition and reset procedure for your unit.
2. **Inspect the outdoor exhaust vent termination** for blockages, nests, ice, or damage, and clear any obstructions you find.
3. **Check the combustion air inlet** (if your model has one) and clean or replace any filter or screen that shows dust, lint, or debris buildup.
4. **Examine the flame sensor or thermocouple** inside the burner compartment for soot, corrosion, or oxidation, and gently clean it with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth if accessible.
5. **Confirm gas supply** by checking that the manual gas shutoff is fully open, the meter is delivering gas to other appliances, and there are no upstream supply issues.
6. **Perform the manufacturer reset procedure** from your manual only after correcting the underlying cause, then observe a full heating cycle to confirm the burner lights cleanly and stays lit.
7. **If E47 returns immediately or after one cycle**, test the flame sensor continuity (consult your model's manual for specs), verify gas pressure at the inlet, and consider replacing the gas control valve or calling a licensed technician for advanced diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you have cleared all vents and inlets, cleaned the flame sensor, confirmed gas supply, and the E47 fault returns after reset. Gas appliance diagnostics require pressure testing, electrical continuity checks, and sometimes specialized tools to verify combustion performance. If you are uncomfortable working around gas piping, burners, or electrical controls, or if your unit is still under warranty, professional service is the safest and often most cost-effective choice. Any work on gas controls or venting must meet local codes, and many jurisdictions require licensed contractors for gas-appliance repair.
