## Samsung Washer CE Error Code — What It Means

CE on a Samsung washer indicates a cooling or temperature-related error. The machine has detected that the water temperature is not what it expects during the cycle. This can mean hot water is entering when the washer called for cold, or that the temperature sensor is reporting an incorrect reading. In some cases, particularly on combo units or after a hot cycle, residual heat can trigger the fault until the tub cools down.

Samsung's diagnostic framework groups CE with other temperature codes. It is not a drain fault. The code reflects a mismatch between the expected and actual water temperature, either from incorrect plumbing connections or a failed sensor circuit. Different Samsung models and regions may present slightly different interpretations, so always confirm against your specific model documentation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first. Before ordering any electronics, verify the supply hoses are on the correct inlets and check sensor resistance at the connector.

## Common Causes

- **Hot and cold supply hoses reversed (~50%)** When the cold inlet hose is connected to the hot supply valve or vice versa, the washer receives hot water when it expects cold and throws a temperature fault.
- **Temperature sensor fault (~25%)** A failed thermistor or broken sensor wire sends an incorrect signal to the control board, causing the board to interpret the water as too hot or out of range.
- **Water inlet valve stuck or leaking internally (~15%)** If the hot-water solenoid in the inlet valve sticks open or leaks past the seat, hot water flows into the tub even when the control calls for cold only.
- **Kinked or restricted supply hose (~7%)** A pinched hose can reduce flow to the point where the washer cannot fill at the expected temperature, or it can cause pressure imbalance that mixes hot and cold supply lines.
- **Residual heat from a previous hot cycle (~3%)** On combo washer-dryers or after a sanitize cycle, leftover heat in the drum or plumbing can register as an over-temperature condition until the machine cools.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the washer** by unplugging it from the wall for at least five minutes to clear any transient control-board fault and allow residual heat to dissipate.
2. **Verify supply-hose connections** at the rear of the washer by matching the cold hose (usually marked with a blue collar) to the cold inlet and the hot hose (red collar) to the hot inlet.
3. **Inspect both hoses** for kinks, pinches, or blockages and straighten or replace any damaged sections to restore full flow.
4. **Run a cold rinse or delicate cycle** and feel the incoming water at the dispenser or tub to confirm it is cool rather than warm.
5. **Test the temperature sensor** by unplugging the washer, removing the rear or top panel, disconnecting the sensor harness, and measuring resistance across the sensor terminals with a multimeter (consult your model's service manual for the expected resistance at room temperature).
6. **Check the inlet valve** for internal leakage by shutting off both supply valves, removing the inlet hoses, and inspecting the valve screens and solenoid seats for debris or damage.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (sensor or inlet valve) if testing confirms it is out of specification, then reassemble the panels and run a test cycle to verify the code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you have confirmed that both supply hoses are correctly connected and in good condition, the inlet valve passes a visual inspection, and you are not comfortable using a multimeter to test sensor resistance or working inside the washer cabinet. A technician can quickly isolate whether the fault is in the sensor, the wiring harness, or the main control board and can access model-specific resistance tables and voltage maps that are not published in consumer documentation. Professional service is also the safer choice if your washer is still under warranty or if you encounter unfamiliar wire colors or connector types during disassembly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
