## Samsung Washer nF1 Error Code — What It Means

The nF or nF1 code on a Samsung washer signals a no-fill or insufficient-fill condition. The machine is not detecting that water is entering fast enough, or it is sensing the wrong inlet-water condition during the fill cycle. One common variant, nF1 specifically, indicates a hot/cold inlet mismatch where the washer senses water entering on the cold side as hot, usually because the supply hoses are reversed at the back of the machine.

This code does not always mean a part has failed. In most cases it points to a supply issue, such as closed valves, kinked hoses, clogged inlet screens, or reversed hose connections. The washer will pause or stop the cycle until it can detect proper water flow and temperature on the correct inlet ports.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the water inlet valve assembly first, but the real problem is often reversed supply hoses or clogged inlet screens. Disconnect the hoses and verify a strong flow from each supply line into a bucket before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Hot/cold hoses reversed (~35%)** The inlet hoses are connected to the wrong ports so the washer detects hot water on the cold inlet or vice versa, triggering nF1.
- **Clogged inlet screens (~25%)** Mesh filters at the washer water inlets are blocked by sediment or debris, restricting flow below the threshold the machine expects.
- **Water supply valves partially closed (~15%)** One or both shutoff valves at the wall are not fully open, reducing pressure and flow rate into the washer.
- **Kinked or pinched inlet hoses (~10%)** The supply hoses are bent sharply or crushed between the washer and the wall, choking off water flow.
- **Failed water inlet valve (~10%)** The solenoid coils inside the valve assembly are weak or open, preventing the valve from opening fully or at all.
- **Drain hose siphoning (~5%)** The drain hose is inserted too deeply into the standpipe or sealed airtight, causing water to siphon out as fast as it fills.

## Quick Diagnosis

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Hose routing is correct. Inspect the inlet screens and test the inlet valve solenoids next.<br><strong>No:</strong> Swap the hoses to match the correct ports, then run a test cycle. This usually clears the nF1 code immediately.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off both hot and cold water supply valves at the wall before moving the machine or touching any connections.
2. **Pull the washer forward** a few inches so you can see the back panel and the two inlet hoses clearly.
3. **Verify hose routing:** confirm the hot hose connects to the hot inlet port and the cold hose to the cold port (check any labels or your installation manual if the ports are not marked).
4. **Disconnect both inlet hoses** at the washer and inspect the mesh screens inside each inlet port for sediment, rust, or debris. Remove and rinse the screens under running water or gently brush them clean.
5. **Check for kinks or damage** along the entire length of each hose, and straighten any sharp bends. Replace any hose that shows cracks, bulges, or worn threads.
6. **Confirm drain-hose installation:** the drain hose should be inserted no more than 6-8 inches into the standpipe and should not be sealed airtight. Pull it out slightly if needed.
7. **Reconnect all hoses,** turn the supply valves fully open, plug in the washer, and run a rinse or test cycle to verify the code is cleared and water fills normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have confirmed both supply valves are fully open, the hoses are correctly connected and free of kinks, the inlet screens are clean, the drain hose is properly installed, and the household supply flow is strong, yet the nF1 code persists. At that point the inlet valve assembly or the water-level pressure switch may have failed, and testing those components requires a multimeter and familiarity with the control wiring. A pro can also diagnose any wiring or connector faults in the sensing circuit that a visual inspection will not reveal. If the machine is still under warranty, contact Samsung or an authorized service center before opening any panels or replacing parts yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (if screens or hoses need replacing), 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220 service call including diagnosis and valve replacement if needed.
