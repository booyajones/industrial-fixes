## Samsung Washer nF1 Error Code — What It Means

On Samsung washers, nF or nF1 signals a no-fill or fill fault. The machine is not getting enough water, or it is not detecting proper water entry during the fill portion of the cycle. The nF1 variant specifically often points to a hot/cold hookup problem where the washer detects the wrong water temperature at the inlet, typically because the hot and cold supply hoses have been reversed at the machine or at the wall.

The code may also appear when supply valves are closed, hoses are kinked or pinched, inlet screens are clogged with sediment, household water pressure is too low, or the water inlet valve itself has failed. In some cases the drain hose is installed too deeply in the standpipe and creates a siphon that prevents the washer from sensing a proper fill level.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve assembly when the real problem is simply reversed hot/cold hoses or clogged inlet screens. Always verify correct hose routing, clean the inlet mesh filters, and check that both supply valves are fully open before ordering a new valve.

## Common Causes

- **Reversed hot/cold hose connections (~35%)** The cold inlet receives hot water (or vice versa) because supply hoses are swapped at the back of the washer or at the wall valves.
- **Closed or partially closed supply valves (~25%)** One or both wall shutoff valves are not fully open, restricting flow and preventing proper fill.
- **Clogged inlet screens or mesh filters (~20%)** Sediment, rust, or mineral buildup blocks the fine screens inside the hose connections at the washer, reducing flow below the threshold the machine expects.
- **Kinked, pinched, or damaged supply hoses (~10%)** The rubber or braided-steel hoses are bent sharply, crushed behind the washer, or internally collapsed, choking off water delivery.
- **Drain hose siphoning or incorrect installation (~5%)** The drain hose is inserted too deeply into the standpipe or sealed airtight, creating a siphon that drains water as fast as it fills and prevents the pressure switch from sensing a proper level.
- **Failed water inlet valve assembly (~5%)** One or both solenoid coils inside the inlet valve are open-circuit or the valve body is mechanically stuck, blocking flow even when power is applied.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the washer** and unplug it from the wall outlet.
2. **Shut off both hot and cold supply valves** at the wall to stop water flow.
3. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the rear panel and hose connections.
4. **Verify hot-to-hot and cold-to-cold routing** by tracing each hose from the wall valve to the washer inlet (hot is typically the left inlet on the machine).
5. **Disconnect both supply hoses** at the washer and inspect the inlet screens inside each port for sediment, rust, or mineral buildup.
6. **Clean or replace the inlet screens** using needle-nose pliers to lift them out, then rinse under running water or replace if damaged.
7. **Check the drain hose installation** to confirm it is not inserted more than a few inches into the standpipe and is not sealed airtight.
8. **Reconnect the hoses** with the correct hot/cold orientation, turn on both supply valves fully, plug in the washer, and run a short test cycle to verify the fill.
9. **If the error persists**, use a multimeter to test continuity across each water inlet valve solenoid coil (typically a few hundred ohms); replace the valve assembly if a coil is open or the valve does not click when energized.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have tested and cleaned all supply-side components (hoses, screens, valves) and confirmed correct hot/cold routing, yet the nF1 error returns. The technician will use a multimeter to verify inlet-valve solenoid resistance, check for voltage at the valve during fill, and test the water-level pressure sensor and its air-tube connection. A pro can also diagnose wiring faults in the harness between the main control board and the inlet valve that a visual inspection might miss. If your washer is still under warranty, contact Samsung or an authorized service center before attempting valve replacement yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (if valve needed), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
