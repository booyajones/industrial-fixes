## Cooper and Hunter P5 Error Code — What It Means

The P5 error code on Cooper and Hunter mini splits signals that the outdoor condenser pipe temperature climbed above 65°C during operation, triggering a high-temperature protection shutdown. The unit will attempt to restart once the pipe temperature drops below the threshold. This fault typically points to restricted airflow around the outdoor unit, a failing condenser fan, a dirty heat exchanger, a faulty condenser temperature sensor, or an issue in the refrigerant circuit such as overcharge or a restriction. Cooper and Hunter's own troubleshooting flow treats P5 as a safety event tied to the outdoor condenser-pipe temperature sensor and the components that influence outdoor coil heat rejection.

## Common Causes

- **Poor outdoor airflow or obstructions** Blocked clearances, shrubs, or debris prevent the outdoor coil from rejecting heat and push pipe temperature above 65°C.
- **Failed or slow condenser fan** A weak or stopped outdoor fan cannot pull enough air across the coil, causing the condenser pipe to overheat.
- **Dirty outdoor heat exchanger** Dust, pollen, and dirt clog the outdoor coil fins and insulate the coil, trapping heat in the refrigerant circuit.
- **Faulty condenser temperature sensor or wiring** An out-of-range sensor resistance or broken wire tells the control board the pipe is hotter than it really is, or fails to detect a real overheat.
- **Refrigerant charge or system pressure problem** Overcharge, undercharge, a restriction, or a leak shifts operating pressures and raises discharge temperatures beyond normal limits.
- **Defective outdoor main PCB** When airflow, sensor resistance, and refrigerant checks pass but the fault persists, the outdoor control board may be misreading or mishandling temperature signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker and wait three minutes before opening any panels to avoid shock and allow pressures to equalize.
2. **Inspect the outdoor unit** for obstructions, verify at least 12 inches of clearance on all sides, and remove any leaves, grass clippings, or debris blocking the coil or fan grille.
3. **Turn power back on** and start a cooling cycle, then confirm the outdoor fan spins at full speed without unusual noise or hesitation.
4. **Clean the outdoor heat exchanger** by spraying coil cleaner from the inside out (fan side toward the coil back) and rinsing with low-pressure water to remove built-up dirt and restore airflow.
5. **Locate the condenser temperature sensor** on the outdoor unit's refrigerant piping (often a small thermistor clipped or strapped to the discharge line), unplug the two-pin connector, and use a multimeter to measure resistance across the sensor leads, comparing the reading to the temperature-resistance table in your service manual.
6. **Inspect the sensor wiring harness** from the sensor to the outdoor PCB for cuts, corrosion, or loose pins, and reseat both connectors firmly.
7. **If sensor resistance is abnormal or the wiring is damaged**, replace the condenser temperature sensor and retest the system through a full cooling cycle to verify the P5 code clears.
8. **Check refrigerant pressures** with manifold gauges while the unit runs in cooling mode, and compare suction and discharge readings to the nameplate subcool/superheat targets or pressure chart for your refrigerant type. If pressures are off-spec, recover, vacuum, and recharge to the factory weight or correct any leak or restriction before final testing.
9. **Replace the outdoor main PCB** only after confirming the sensor reads correctly, the fan operates properly, the coil is clean, and refrigerant pressures are in range but the fault code still appears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with line voltage, if you lack refrigerant handling certification and manifold gauges, or if the P5 code returns after you have verified airflow and cleaned the coil. Diagnosing refrigerant circuit faults and swapping control boards both require specialized tools and an EPA Section 608 license. A professional can also perform a full system pressure test, check for refrigerant leaks with electronic detectors, and update outdoor board firmware if Cooper and Hunter has released a patch for your model.
