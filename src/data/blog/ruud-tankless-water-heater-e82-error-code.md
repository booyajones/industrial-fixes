## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E82 Error — What It Means

The E82 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters generally indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition control. The unit has ignited but the control board is not receiving a proper signal from the flame-sensing rod, or the ignition sequence is failing to complete normally. This can happen even when a flame is present if the sensor cannot read it correctly.

Because the exact definition of error codes can vary slightly between Ruud models and software versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front cover for the precise meaning on your unit. In most cases the heater will lock out and require a reset after a certain number of failed attempts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire gas valve or ignition board when the real problem is a flame rod covered in carbon buildup or a loose wire connection. Clean the flame rod with fine steel wool and check all connectors before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~45%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame-sensing rod prevents it from reading the flame signal, causing the board to shut down even when ignition occurs.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~25%)** A poor connection at the flame rod, ground wire, or ignition board can interrupt the flame-detection circuit and trigger the error.
- **Faulty flame rod (~15%)** The flame-sensing rod itself can crack, break, or fail internally, preventing it from conducting the small current needed for flame detection.
- **Ignition board failure (~10%)** The control board may have a damaged flame-detection circuit or relay that cannot process the flame signal correctly.
- **Grounding issue (~5%)** A missing or corroded ground connection can interfere with the flame-detection circuit, making the board think no flame is present.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and lifting it away to access the burner assembly.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe near the burner that extends into the flame area, and disconnect the wire connector.
4. **Remove the flame rod** by unscrewing the mounting nut or bracket that holds it in place.
5. **Clean the rod** gently with fine steel wool or emery cloth to remove carbon deposits and corrosion, then wipe it with a dry cloth.
6. **Inspect all wire connections** to the flame rod, ignition board, and ground terminal for looseness or corrosion and clean or reseat as needed.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod** and reconnect the wire, making sure the connection is snug and the rod is positioned correctly in the flame path.
8. **Replace the front cover**, restore gas and power, and run hot water to test whether the error clears and the unit fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod and checking connections does not clear the error, or if you suspect a faulty ignition board or grounding problem. A pro can test flame-detection current with specialized meters, verify proper gas pressure and combustion air, and diagnose intermittent faults that are hard to reproduce. Any work involving gas-line connections or sealed combustion chambers should be handled by a qualified service technician.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
