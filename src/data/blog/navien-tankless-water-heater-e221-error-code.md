## Navien E221 Error Code — What It Means

The E221 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a fault condition that has interrupted normal operation. The exact meaning can vary by model and firmware version. Consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front cover for the specific definition on your unit. Common themes for E2xx codes on tankless heaters include flame sensing issues, airflow restrictions, or sensor faults that prevent safe ignition or operation. The unit will lock out until the condition is resolved and the error is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board first when the real problem is a dirty flame rod or a loose sensor connector. Clean the flame sensor and check all wiring connections before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Flame sensor fouling (~35%)** Carbon buildup or mineral deposits on the flame rod prevent the control from detecting ignition, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~25%)** Poor electrical contact at the flame rod, air-pressure switch, or thermistor connectors causes intermittent or missing signals.
- **Air-supply or exhaust restriction (~20%)** Blocked vent pipes, bird nests, or ice accumulation starve combustion or prevent proper draft, causing the unit to shut down.
- **Failed flame rod or igniter (~10%)** A cracked ceramic insulator or broken electrode wire stops ignition or flame detection altogether.
- **Low gas pressure or air in the line (~5%)** Insufficient fuel delivery or trapped air after recent service prevents stable combustion and trips the sensor.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** A failed circuit on the main PCB misreads sensor signals or refuses to energize the ignition sequence.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the dedicated circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve on the supply line to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners (typically two or four screws along the bottom edge).
3. **Locate the flame rod** inside the burner chamber. It is a thin metal probe extending into the flame zone, often held by a single screw or spring clip.
4. **Disconnect the flame-rod wire** by gently pulling the spade connector straight off the terminal. Unscrew the mounting hardware and slide the rod out.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-metallic abrasive pad to remove soot and scale. Wipe clean with a dry cloth.
6. **Inspect all sensor connectors** (flame rod, thermistors, air-pressure switch) for corrosion, moisture, or loose pins. Reseat each connector firmly.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod** and secure the mounting screw. Reconnect the wire and make sure it does not touch the burner or metal housing.
8. **Check vent pipes** at the outdoor termination for nests, leaves, or ice. Remove any blockage and confirm both intake and exhaust are clear.
9. **Restore gas and power**, then open a hot-water tap. Observe ignition through the sight glass and confirm the error does not return. If it does, call a licensed technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas connections, if the unit still faults after cleaning the flame rod and checking wiring, or if you suspect a failed gas valve, control board, or sealed combustion component. Tankless water heaters require precise gas-pressure settings and proper venting. A technician will use a combustion analyzer to verify air-fuel ratios and can safely test high-voltage ignition circuits and gas-valve operation. Any work on gas piping, venting, or the sealed combustion chamber should be left to a professional to maintain safety and warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
