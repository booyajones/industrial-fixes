## Samsung SUD Error Code — What It Means

The SUD error code (sometimes displayed as SUDS) means the washing machine's internal sensors have detected an excessive amount of foam or suds in the drum. This is a safety safeguard mechanism. When the over-sudsing condition is detected, the machine automatically stops the current cycle (usually pausing for a short period) to allow the suds to dissipate naturally. If the suds do not clear, the machine will not proceed to the rinse or spin phases, potentially leaving detergent on the clothes and causing mechanical strain due to the viscosity of the foam.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the pressure sensor or control board thinking the error is a sensor fault, when the real problem is simply detergent buildup or a clogged drain pump filter. Always clean the filter and run a flush cycle with no detergent before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Using non-HE detergent in a front-load washer (~45%)** Front-load washers require HE (High Efficiency) detergent because they use less water, and non-HE formulas create excessive foam that overwhelms the sensor.
- **Using too much HE detergent (~30%)** Even with HE detergent, exceeding the manufacturer's recommended amount (especially with 2x or 3x concentration formulas at the same volume as 1x) creates excessive suds.
- **Old detergent buildup in dispenser or hoses (~10%)** Residual detergent trapped in the dispenser drawer or internal hoses can create a bubble-machine effect when activated, triggering the error even with correct detergent use.
- **Clogged drain pump filter (~10%)** Debris (coins, lint, small objects) blocking the pump filter prevents proper water flow, leading to water retention and concentrated suds buildup in the drum.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~4%)** If the hose is kinked, crushed, or the standpipe is installed too high, water cannot drain fast enough and traps detergent-rich water in the drum.
- **Faulty pressure sensor or failing drain pump (~1%)** In rare cases, the pressure sensor may be miscalibrated or the drain pump may not generate enough pressure to clear the line, though this is uncommon compared to detergent issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off and unplug** the machine for 10 to 15 minutes to reset the electronic controls and clear the error memory.
2. **Verify detergent type and dosage** by checking that you are using HE (High Efficiency) detergent and following the concentration guidelines on the label (reduce volume if using 2x or 3x formulas).
3. **Remove and clean the detergent dispenser** drawer by pulling it out completely and flushing the drawer and housing cavity with hot water to remove soap sludge buildup.
4. **Run a maintenance cycle** (or a short rinse cycle) with NO clothes and NO detergent to flush residual suds from the drum, hoses, and internal chambers.
5. **Clean the drain pump filter** by locating the small access door at the bottom front of the machine, opening it, placing a shallow pan underneath to catch water, unscrewing the filter cap slowly, and removing any debris, lint, or residue.
6. **Inspect the drain hose** for kinks, crushing, or blockages, and verify the installation height is within the recommended range (typically 24 to 36 inches from the floor) per the installation manual.
7. **Restart the washer** with a small load and the correct amount of HE detergent to confirm the error does not return, and monitor the first few cycles to make sure proper sudsing levels.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have switched to HE detergent, cleaned the dispenser and drain pump filter, verified the drain hose is clear and properly installed, and the SUD error still appears on every cycle. A technician can test the pressure sensor for calibration issues, inspect the drain pump for mechanical failure, and check the control board for fault codes that indicate a sensor or wiring problem rather than a detergent issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-25 (cleaning supplies and HE detergent), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 if a service call is needed for pump or sensor inspection.
