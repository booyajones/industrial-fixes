## Whirlpool Oven Control Board Replacement — What This Part Does

The electronic range control (ERC) board is the main logic module in your Whirlpool electric oven. It receives input from the touchpad, monitors the oven temperature sensor, and switches the bake and broil elements on and off to hold setpoint. When it fails, you lose control of heating cycles or the appliance shuts down entirely to prevent unsafe operation.

Control boards fail from power surges, heat stress over time, corroded solder joints, or internal component breakdown. Moisture infiltration from spills or steam and repeated thermal cycling in the cabinet accelerate the damage. A failed board often throws fault codes like F6 or stops communicating with the keypad and sensor circuits.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **F6 or similar error code displayed** The control panel shows F6, indicating a communication or logic fault between the board and its input circuits.
- **Oven won't heat at all** You set a temperature and press Start, but no elements energize and the cavity stays cold.
- **Touchpad buttons unresponsive or erratic** Some or all keys do nothing when pressed, or the display lights up randomly without input.
- **Random beeping or alarm sounds** The oven beeps continuously or at intervals even when not in use, signaling an internal control error.
- **Display blank or garbled** The clock or readout is dark, flickers, or shows corrupted characters while the unit has power.
- **Code returns immediately after power reset** You flip the breaker off for one minute, turn it back on, and the same fault code reappears right away.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Turn off power at the circuit breaker or fuse panel feeding the range, then verify the display is dark and the unit is completely de-energized.
2. Pull the range away from the wall far enough to access the rear panel, or if it is a wall oven, remove the trim or front panel screws per your model's service access.
3. Remove the screws securing the rear or top access panel and lift it free to expose the control-board cavity.
4. Locate your model and serial number plate (usually inside the oven door frame or on the rear panel) and write down both numbers to order the correct replacement ERC board.
5. Photograph the wire harness connections on the old control board so you have a clear reference for reconnection.
6. Unplug each multi-pin connector from the board by releasing the locking tab or pulling straight out, then remove the mounting screws holding the board to the bracket.
7. Lift out the failed control board and position the new board on the same mounting bosses, aligning screw holes.
8. Reinstall the mounting screws snug but not over-torqued, then reconnect each harness plug to the matching socket on the new board, pressing firmly until the tabs click.
9. Replace the access panel, push the range back into place, restore power at the breaker, and verify the display lights and responds to touchpad input without throwing a code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a service technician if the F6 or control fault persists after you replace the board, if you measure line voltage at the board terminals and are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, or if the temperature sensor reads outside the 1030–1130 ohm range at room temperature and you want simultaneous sensor and harness diagnosis. A pro will also verify that the neutral and ground connections at the terminal block are tight and that no upstream wiring fault is back-feeding noise into the control circuit. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
