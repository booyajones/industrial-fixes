## Amana Dryer F4 E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F4 E1 code on an Amana dryer signals a heater relay or heater circuit problem. The electronic control board has detected a fault in the circuit that powers the heating element. This is commonly described as a heater relay fault, a connector issue, or a no-voltage-at-heater-relay condition in Whirlpool-family documentation used by Amana-branded dryers.

The dryer may still tumble but will not produce heat. The fault can stem from wiring problems, a failed heating element, a bad relay on the control board, or an inadequate power supply. Amana electric dryers require a 240-volt supply on a separate 30-amp circuit fused on both sides of the line. If one leg of 240 V is missing due to a tripped breaker, blown fuse, or miswired power cord, the heater circuit will fail and trigger this code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but loose or burned wiring and a shorted heating element are much more common. Always inspect the heater harness, connectors, and power-supply wiring before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose, damaged, or disconnected wiring (~35%)** Wiring between the control board and the heater circuit can work loose, corrode, or suffer heat damage at connectors, triggering the relay fault.
- **Failed heating element (~30%)** An internal short, open coil, or connector problem at the element itself will prevent the heater circuit from operating correctly.
- **Missing one leg of 240 V supply (~20%)** A tripped breaker, blown fuse, or miswired power cord can leave the dryer with only 120 V, preventing the heater from energizing.
- **Faulty control board or heater relay (~10%)** The relay on the main control board may fail to switch power to the heater even when wiring and element test good.
- **Burned or corroded terminal block (~5%)** The terminal block where the power cord connects can develop loose screws, burned terminals, or corrosion that interrupts power to the heater circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the dryer** by unplugging it from the wall (or turning off both breakers) for one minute, then restore power and run a timed heated cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Verify incoming power** by confirming both circuit breakers or fuses for the dryer are on, not tripped, and that the outlet delivers 240 V across both hot legs.
3. **Inspect the power cord and terminal block** at the rear of the dryer for loose screws, burned terminals, corrosion, or miswired connections, and tighten or repair as needed.
4. **Unplug the dryer and access the heater assembly**, usually located in the rear bulkhead or lower front panel, then inspect the wire harness and connectors to the heater for looseness, heat damage, or broken wires.
5. **Test the heating element** with a multimeter for continuity across its terminals and for shorts to the metal chassis, replacing the element if it is open or shorted.
6. **Check the control-board heater-relay connections** for corrosion or looseness, and test the relay output if possible, replacing the main control board if wiring and element are good but the relay does not switch power.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a complete timed heated cycle to confirm the F4 E1 code does not return and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt circuits, testing electrical components with a multimeter, or disassembling the dryer cabinet to reach the heater assembly. High-voltage work on electric dryers carries shock risk, and improper wiring or testing can damage the control board or create a fire hazard. A technician can safely verify incoming power, test the heater relay and element, trace wiring faults, and replace the correct component without guesswork. Professional service is also the best choice if the code persists after you have checked power and wiring, or if you suspect the control board relay itself has failed.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–300.
