## What this code means
The F41 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a fault in the door latch mechanism or its feedback circuit. The control board has detected that the motorized self-clean latch is not locking, not unlocking, or not reporting the correct position during a door-lock operation. This fault typically appears during or after a self-clean cycle, or when the oven tries to engage the door lock.

The code points to a problem in the lock assembly itself, the door-latch switch that signals position to the board, or the wiring that connects them. It does not indicate a temperature or sensor issue. Until the fault is cleared, the oven may refuse to start a self-clean cycle or display the error during normal operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but a stuck latch or loose harness connector is far more common. Check that the latch moves freely and that all connectors at the latch motor are seated firmly before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Jammed or obstructed latch mechanism (~40%)** Grease, food debris, or a misaligned door prevents the motorized latch from fully extending or retracting, so the control never sees a valid lock or unlock signal.
- **Defective latch motor or door-lock assembly (~35%)** The motor inside the latch assembly has failed and no longer drives the bolt, or internal gears have stripped and the latch cannot move.
- **Faulty latch switch or door-lock switch (~15%)** The microswitch that reports latch position to the control board is stuck, corroded, or broken, so the board sees an open or inconsistent signal even when the latch moves.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~7%)** A connector at the latch motor or control board has backed out, or a wire has frayed where the harness flexes near the hinge, breaking continuity in the lock circuit.
- **Failed main control board (~3%)** The relay or driver circuit on the electronic control board that powers and monitors the latch has failed, though this is less common when the latch itself is mechanically sound.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The mechanism is not jammed. Move to wiring and motor checks.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean around the latch, remove any debris, and verify the door closes squarely. If still binding, replace the latch assembly.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Do you hear a motor sound or clicking from the latch area when you start a self-clean cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor is trying to run. Check the latch switch and wiring for feedback faults.<br><strong>No:</strong> No motor actuation means a dead motor, open wiring, or a board that is not sending power. Inspect connectors first, then test the motor.</div>
</details>

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> A transient glitch or incomplete unlock is likely. Power-cycle the breaker and try a manual unlock if your model has a reset procedure.<br><strong>No:</strong> The fault is persistent. Proceed with mechanical and electrical diagnostics in order.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait 3–5 minutes, then restore power to reset the control board and see if the F41 clears.
2. **Open the oven door** and locate the latch assembly, usually on the front frame near the top of the door opening.
3. **Inspect the latch** for debris, grease, or any obstruction that prevents free movement, and clean around the bolt and guide slots with a damp cloth.
4. **Manually slide the latch bolt** in and out to confirm it moves smoothly without binding or sticking.
5. **Unplug the oven** (or turn off the breaker again), then remove the control-panel or access cover to reach the back of the latch motor and its wiring harness.
6. **Check all connectors** at the latch motor and the main control board for secure seating, corrosion, or pushed-back pins, and reseat any that are loose.
7. **Test latch-motor operation** by restoring power and starting a self-clean cycle, listening for motor sound and watching the bolt move; if the motor is silent or the bolt does not move, replace the latch assembly.
8. **Replace the latch motor assembly** if it is defective, or replace the control board only if the latch and wiring test good but the error persists.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring behind the control panel, if the oven is built in and requires cabinet disassembly to access the latch, or if you have replaced the latch assembly and checked all wiring but the F41 persists and you suspect a control-board fault. A technician can bring a service manual with model-specific resistance and voltage specs for the latch circuit, perform pinpoint meter tests at the board, and safely replace the control if needed. Also call if your oven is still under warranty, since DIY part replacement may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–180 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $200–400.
