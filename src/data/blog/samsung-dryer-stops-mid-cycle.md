## Samsung Dryer Stops Mid Cycle — What's Happening

A Samsung dryer that stops mid-cycle is usually not triggering a single fault code. It is a symptom caused by the machine entering a stop condition because it detects a problem that interrupts the cycle, most commonly overheating or loss of power. On Samsung dryers, a restricted vent can cause the high-limit thermostat to trip repeatedly, which leads the dryer to shut off before the cycle finishes.

If the dryer is not heating or the control loses power, the issue may be electrical supply related rather than a heat-cycle fault. If you see an actual fault code on the display, diagnose that code directly. If not, treat this as a shutdown or interruption problem.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged lint filter or vent duct** Restricted airflow from lint buildup causes the dryer to overheat and triggers the high-limit thermostat, shutting the machine down mid-cycle.
- **Blocked exterior vent flap or crushed duct** Excessive exhaust restriction from a blocked hood, crushed vent hose, or overly long duct creates back-pressure that leads to overheating shutdown.
- **Incorrect or unstable electrical supply** A tripped breaker, damaged power cord, or poor terminal-block connection can cause loss of power during the cycle.
- **Faulty door switch or latch** A defective door switch or worn latch causes the machine to think the door opened, stopping the cycle immediately.
- **Contaminated moisture sensors** Residue or corrosion on the sensor bars in the drum can cause false readings that end sensor-dry cycles prematurely.
- **Drive motor overheating or failure** A motor that overheats or fails will cause the dryer to stop running before the cycle completes.
- **Belt problems** A broken, slipped, or stretched drum belt prevents the drum from turning and can trigger a shutdown.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the complaint and check the display for any error code. If a code is present, diagnose that code directly. If not, proceed as a shutdown or interruption problem.
2. Check the power supply first. Confirm proper power at the outlet, reset the breaker, inspect the cord ends for discoloration or damage, and inspect the terminal block for heat damage or loose conductors. Samsung dryers require a 220 V, 60 Hz, 30 A dedicated supply.
3. Inspect airflow restrictions. Clean the lint filter, inspect the vent hose and rigid duct for kinks or buildup, check the exterior hood and flap for blockage, and temporarily run the dryer with the vent disconnected to see if it runs normally without the exhaust load.
4. Inspect the door switch and latch. Verify the door closes and latches positively, then test the door switch for electrical continuity if the machine stops as if the door opened.
5. Inspect the moisture sensors. Clean the two metal sensor bars inside the drum with a soft cloth and white vinegar or rubbing alcohol, and check harness connections for corrosion or loose wiring.
6. Check the drum drive system. Remove the front or top panel to access the drum belt, inspect the belt for damage or slack, and test the motor if drum drive or motor overheating is suspected.
7. Test safety components if overheating is confirmed. Check the thermal fuse, high-limit thermostat, and heater assembly for continuity and replace any open or failed component.
8. Reassemble and perform a supervised operational test. Verify heating, airflow, and complete cycle under a normal load.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with 220 V electrical connections at the terminal block or outlet, if you cannot access or test the thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat, or if the dryer continues to stop mid-cycle after you have cleaned all venting and verified power supply. A technician has the meters and experience to test safety components, motor windings, and control boards accurately and can replace failed parts safely.
