## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E59 Error — What It Means

The E59 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a gas-related fault or ignition sequence failure. The unit detects that gas flow is not adequate or that the ignition system cannot establish a flame within the expected time window. This code protects the system from unsafe operating conditions by shutting down the burner.

Because error-code definitions can vary across Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, consult your specific unit's owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for the exact meaning of E59 on your model. In general, the code points to issues in the gas-supply chain, ignition components, or flame-sensing circuitry.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame sensor first, but a closed manual gas valve or low inlet gas pressure is the usual culprit. Check that all gas shut-off valves upstream are fully open and that other gas appliances in the home are working normally before ordering ignition parts.

## Common Causes

- **Gas valve closed or supply interrupted (~40%)** The manual shut-off valve at the heater or meter may be partly closed, or the utility may have interrupted service.
- **Low inlet gas pressure (~25%)** Incoming gas pressure below the heater's minimum operating threshold prevents ignition or causes the code.
- **Flame sensor dirty or corroded (~15%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents the control board from detecting the flame signal.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition module (~10%)** A weak or failed igniter cannot create a spark, or the ignition module itself has failed.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The main PCB may log an E59 code if its internal ignition-timing logic or relay has failed.
- **Venting obstruction (~5%)** Blocked or restricted exhaust vent pipes can prevent proper combustion air flow and trigger ignition errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the manual gas shut-off valve on the unit's gas line.
2. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the main shut-off at the meter and at the heater are both fully open, then reopen the heater's gas valve.
3. **Power-cycle the unit** by switching the circuit breaker off for 30 seconds, then back on, to clear any transient fault codes.
4. **Inspect the exhaust vent** termination outside for blockages (bird nests, ice, debris) and make sure all vent joints are tight and properly sealed.
5. **Remove the front cover** of the heater and locate the flame sensor rod inside the burner chamber. Use fine sandpaper or a soft cloth to gently clean any carbon deposits.
6. **Check the igniter** for visible cracks or heavy carbon buildup. If damaged, mark it for replacement.
7. **Call a licensed technician** to measure inlet gas pressure with a manometer, test the gas valve's operation, and verify ignition-module output voltage if the code persists after the above checks.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E59 code persists after you have confirmed the gas valves are open, reset the unit, and cleaned the flame sensor. Gas-pressure testing requires a manometer and knowledge of your model's pressure specifications. Ignition-module diagnostics involve measuring high-voltage spark output, which can be dangerous without proper training. Any work on the gas valve, burner assembly, or control board should be performed by a qualified professional to maintain warranty coverage and comply with local codes.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
