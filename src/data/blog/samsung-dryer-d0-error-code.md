## Samsung Dryer d0 Error Code — What It Means

The d0 error code on a Samsung dryer indicates a door problem. The dryer's control board is not receiving a valid "door closed" signal from the door switch or latch circuit. This means the door is open, not fully latched, or the door status is not being sensed correctly by the machine.

The fault is not related to airflow or venting. Samsung groups d0 with door, latch, and sensor-related faults. The dryer will not start or will stop mid-cycle until the control confirms the door is securely closed and the switch circuit is complete.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real problem is a worn or misaligned door latch or a failed door switch. Test continuity through the door-switch circuit with a multimeter first before replacing any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Laundry or lint blocking the door** Clothing, dryer sheets, or accumulated lint caught in the door opening prevents the latch from engaging fully.
- **Misaligned or damaged door latch** The plastic latch or strike assembly is broken, loose, or worn so the switch is never actuated when the door closes.
- **Faulty door switch** The electrical switch that signals "door closed" has failed and no longer completes the circuit even when the latch engages.
- **Wiring or connector fault** A broken wire, loose connector, or corroded terminal in the door-switch harness interrupts the signal to the control board.
- **Control board misreading the circuit** The main PCB itself has a fault in the door-detection circuit and reports d0 even when the switch and wiring are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off and unplug the dryer.** Wait at least 60 seconds for a full power reset, then restore power and see if the code clears on its own.
2. **Open the door and inspect the opening.** Remove any clothing, dryer sheets, lint, or debris caught between the door gasket and the cabinet that would prevent a full seal.
3. **Examine the latch and strike assembly.** Close the door firmly and listen for a positive click. Check that the plastic latch on the door and the strike on the cabinet are not cracked, loose, or misaligned.
4. **Test the door switch with a multimeter.** Access the door switch (usually behind the top panel or inside the door frame). With the door closed, check for continuity across the switch terminals. If there is no continuity, replace the door switch.
5. **Inspect the wiring harness.** Trace the wires from the door switch to the control-board connector. Look for broken wires, loose pins, or corrosion. Repair or replace the harness as needed.
6. **Replace the door latch or strike if damaged.** If the mechanical components are broken or too worn to actuate the switch reliably, install a new latch assembly for your model.
7. **Reassemble and run a test cycle.** Close the door and start a short drying cycle. Confirm the d0 code does not reappear and the dryer runs normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical circuits or if the door-switch circuit tests good but the d0 code will not clear. A pro can verify continuity at the control-board harness, rule out intermittent faults, and replace the main PCB if needed. Also call for help if the door frame itself is bent or the hinges are so misaligned that mechanical adjustment is required. Most door-switch and latch replacements are straightforward DIY jobs with basic hand tools, but board-level troubleshooting and frame repairs benefit from experience.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $100–200.
