## Samsung Washer Door Boot Seal Replacement — What This Part Does

The door boot seal (also called the bellow gasket) is the flexible rubber barrier between the outer tub and the door opening on your Samsung front-load washer. It creates a watertight seal when the door latches shut so wash water stays inside the drum during fill, agitation, and spin cycles. Samsung designs the boot to prevent water from leaking out of the door.

The seal fails when clothing hardware, coins, or other sharp objects puncture or tear the rubber. Mold, soap residue, and trapped debris can degrade the gasket over time. If a previous repair left the inner or outer lip unseated from its groove or the retaining clamp loose, water will escape under pressure. Samsung's own cleaning guides point to foreign objects wedged under the seal lip and buildup in the rubber packing as common problems that lead to leaks or eventual boot damage.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **Water pooling at the front of the washer during or after a cycle** This is the classic sign of a torn or unseated door boot that can no longer hold wash water inside the drum.
- **Visible tear, cut, or hole in the rubber gasket** Inspect all surfaces of the boot, including the hidden areas when you pull back the seal, for any damage that breaks the water barrier.
- **Foreign objects or debris trapped under the door seal lip** Samsung tells users to check under the pulled-back seal for coins, buttons, hairpins, or other items that can puncture the rubber or prevent a tight seal.
- **Heavy mold, residue, or staining on the gasket** Severe buildup in the rubber packing can degrade the seal material and is a sign the boot may need replacement if cleaning does not restore it.
- **Gasket pulled out of its groove or hanging loose** If the inner lip has come off the tub groove or the outer lip is not seated in the cabinet groove, water will leak even if the rubber is not torn.
- **Water not draining from the boot area between cycles** Lint, hair, or debris blocking the drain holes in the seal prevents water from reaching the bottom drain path and can cause standing water or leaks.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Unplug the washer from the wall outlet and turn off the water supply valves to eliminate electrical and water hazards.
2. Open the door fully and inspect the entire boot by pulling back the seal to check for tears, cuts, foreign objects, mold, or debris on all surfaces.
3. Remove the outer retaining clamp (usually a spring-wire or band clamp) that holds the boot lip to the front cabinet or door frame by releasing the tension spring or loosening the fastener.
4. Pull the outer lip of the boot off the cabinet groove all the way around the door opening, then remove any hoses or sensor harness clips attached to the boot if your model has them.
5. Remove the front panel or access the inner retaining clamp as required by your model, then release the inner clamp and pull the boot lip off the outer tub groove.
6. Position the new boot with any alignment mark or arrow at the top, seat the inner lip fully onto the tub groove, and reinstall the inner retaining clamp with even tension all the way around.
7. Reattach any drain hoses or sensor fittings to the new boot, then pull the outer lip over the cabinet groove and seat it completely before reinstalling the outer retaining clamp.
8. Walk your fingers around the entire gasket to confirm the inner and outer lips are seated in their grooves with no twists or pinches, and verify both clamps are secure.
9. Reassemble the front panel if you removed it, restore power and water, and run a short test cycle to check for leaks at the door area during fill and agitation.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable removing the front panel or working with the inner and outer retaining clamps, call a qualified appliance technician. Incorrect installation of the boot (twisted gasket, unseated lips, or loose clamps) will cause immediate leaks and may damage the new part. If your washer also shows electronic error codes, won't drain, or has other symptoms beyond the door leak, a technician can diagnose multiple issues in one visit and confirm the boot replacement solves the problem.
