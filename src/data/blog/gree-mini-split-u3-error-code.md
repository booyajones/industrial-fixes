## Gree U3 Error Code — What It Means

On Gree mini-split systems, the U3 code signals a power-related fault in the outdoor unit. The exact definition varies by model family. In Gree VRF systems, U3 typically indicates power phase sequence protection, meaning the three-phase supply wiring is incorrect, reversed, or missing a phase. In other Gree families, U3 can mean DC bus voltage fluctuating, pointing to instability in the inverter power supply or rectifier circuit. Both definitions trace back to problems in the outdoor unit's electrical input or main control board, not indoor sensors or refrigerant issues. Always verify the specific meaning for your model in the service manual before troubleshooting.

## Common Causes

- **Incorrect three-phase wiring or reversed phase sequence** The outdoor unit receives power in the wrong phase rotation or one phase is miswired at the terminal block.
- **Loose, missing, or damaged power conductors** Terminal lugs at the outdoor unit are loose, corroded, or one phase is not connected properly.
- **Faulty outdoor main control board or detection circuit** The main PCB in the outdoor unit has failed and cannot correctly read or regulate incoming power.
- **Inverter power supply or rectifier instability** On models where U3 means DC bus voltage fluctuating, the rectifier, PFC section, or inverter board has internal faults causing voltage swings.
- **Unbalanced or out-of-spec incoming supply voltage** The building's electrical service delivers incorrect voltage or unbalanced phases to the outdoor unit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Lock out power** at the breaker or disconnect and confirm zero voltage at the outdoor unit terminal block with a multimeter before touching any wiring.
2. **Verify your exact indoor and outdoor model numbers** and consult the Gree service manual to confirm whether U3 means phase-sequence protection or DC-bus voltage fluctuation for your system.
3. **Inspect the outdoor unit terminal block** for loose lugs, corroded connections, reversed wires, or a missing phase conductor and re-torque or rewire as needed.
4. **Measure incoming supply voltage** at the outdoor terminals and verify all three phases are present, balanced, and match the nameplate rating before proceeding.
5. **Check phase rotation** with a phase-sequence meter on three-phase systems and swap any two supply conductors if the rotation is reversed.
6. **Inspect the outdoor main control board and inverter board** for visible damage, burned traces, or failed components and replace the outdoor PCB if wiring and supply are correct but the fault persists.
7. **Restore power and run the system** to confirm the U3 code clears and does not return during normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not qualified to work on three-phase electrical systems or if you cannot safely lock out and verify zero voltage. Misdiagnosing phase wiring or working on live inverter circuits can cause fatal shock or permanent equipment damage. A technician has phase-rotation meters, isolation tools, and access to Gree model-specific service literature to distinguish between phase-sequence faults and DC-bus voltage issues. If the fault returns after you correct wiring and supply voltage, the outdoor control board or inverter module likely needs replacement, which requires refrigerant recovery, board-level diagnostics, and system commissioning that only a certified pro should perform.
