## What this code means
GE's fault-code guidance states that C8 indicates the detergent cup or dispenser door is blocked from opening fully during the wash cycle. The dishwasher expects the dispenser to swing open at a specific point in the cycle, and when it cannot, the machine logs the C8 fault. This code is a mechanical obstruction or dispenser hardware issue, not a drain, fill, or pump problem. Some non-GE sources incorrectly link C8 to rinse-pump faults, but GE's published documentation confirms the code refers only to the detergent dispenser.

The fault is almost always caused by something physical: a utensil, tall pan, or dish rack interfering with the dispenser door's path, or less often, a damaged dispenser assembly that will not latch or open correctly. Because the code points to a mechanical blockage rather than an electrical failure, diagnosis focuses on visual inspection of the dispenser area and testing the door's travel.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some homeowners replace the control board or pump assembly after seeing online videos that mislabel C8 as a drain or rinse issue. GE's documentation confirms C8 is a dispenser obstruction, so inspect the cup area and remove any blockage before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Silverware or utensils blocking the dispenser door (~50%)** Knives, forks, or spatulas leaning against or jutting into the dispenser's swing path prevent the cup from opening fully.
- **Tall pans or baking sheets interfering with the cup (~25%)** Large cookware placed too close to the dispenser on the door or in the bottom rack can physically block the door's travel.
- **Damaged or warped detergent dispenser assembly (~12%)** The cup door, hinge, latch, or spring inside the dispenser may be broken, bent, or stuck so it cannot open even without obstruction.
- **Dish rack positioned incorrectly (~10%)** A misaligned upper or lower rack can push items into the path of the detergent cup as it tries to open.
- **Residue or hardened detergent on the dispenser latch (~3%)** Old detergent buildup can glue the dispenser shut or slow the release mechanism enough to trigger the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code.** Confirm that the dishwasher display shows C8 and note your GE model number from the door jamb or inside panel.
2. **Inspect the detergent cup area.** Open the dishwasher door and look at the dispenser on the inside of the door. Check for utensils, tall cookware, or any part of the dish racks within six inches of the cup that could block its swing path.
3. **Remove any obstruction.** Take out silverware, pans, or items near the dispenser and rearrange the load so nothing can interfere with the cup door when it opens during the cycle.
4. **Close the door and run a test cycle.** Start a normal wash or wait about 30 seconds for the dispenser to release. GE's guidance notes the cup should open freely once the obstruction is gone.
5. **Power-cycle the dishwasher if the code persists.** Unplug the machine or flip the breaker off for 30 seconds, then restore power. This can clear transient faults and allow the control to re-check the dispenser.
6. **Inspect the dispenser assembly for damage.** If the code remains, open the door and manually test the detergent cup. Press the latch and swing the door open and closed. Look for cracks, bent hinges, broken springs, or hardened detergent residue that could jam the mechanism.
7. **Replace the detergent dispenser assembly if damaged.** Order the correct part for your GE model and swap the assembly by removing the screws on the inside of the door panel. Re-test with an empty load to confirm the cup opens during the wash phase.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have removed all obstructions, power-cycled the machine, and the C8 code still appears. Also call if the dispenser assembly looks intact but will not open, or if you are uncomfortable removing the inner door panel to access the dispenser mounting screws. A qualified appliance service tech can verify the dispenser mechanism, test for less common faults in the door interlock or wiring harness, and replace the assembly with the correct GE part. Professional help is also wise if the dishwasher is under warranty or if you want to avoid accidentally damaging the door panel during disassembly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0 if obstruction removal, or $30-80 for a replacement dispenser assembly, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call plus parts if dispenser replacement is needed.
