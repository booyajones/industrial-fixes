## GE C2 Error Code — What It Means

The C2 code on GE dishwashers indicates that the total pump-out cycle exceeded 7 minutes. The dishwasher attempted to drain but could not complete the operation within the allowed time window. GE's appliance support documentation lists C2 as a drain timeout fault, not a washer code. Press RESET to stop the beeping alarm.

This fault points to a restriction somewhere in the drain path preventing water from leaving the dishwasher quickly enough. The control board monitors drain time and throws the code when the sump does not empty within the programmed limit. Common culprits include blocked hoses, clogged air gaps, forgotten disposer knockout plugs, debris in the sump filter area, or a weak drain pump.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the drain pump or control board before checking the external plumbing. Clear the drain hose, air gap, and disposer knockout plug first-these no-cost inspections solve most C2 faults.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked drain hose, air gap, or disposer connection (~45%)** Kinks, clogs, or debris in the external drain path prevent water from leaving the dishwasher within 7 minutes.
- **Garbage disposer knockout plug not removed (~25%)** Installers sometimes forget to punch out the disposer inlet plug, completely blocking drainage when the dishwasher is first connected.
- **Debris in the sump or filter area (~15%)** Food particles, glass, or foreign objects in the sump restrict flow and slow pump-out enough to trip the timeout.
- **Improper drain hose routing or elevation (~10%)** A hose looped too high or routed incorrectly forces the pump to work harder and may exceed the 7-minute limit.
- **Weak or failed drain pump (~5%)** If the plumbing path is clear but the pump cannot move water efficiently, internal blockage or motor failure is the cause.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press RESET** to silence the beeping alarm, then start a rinse cycle to confirm the C2 code returns and verify the symptom.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** from the dishwasher to the air gap or disposer connection for kinks, clogs, or pinched sections, and straighten or reposition as needed.
3. **Check the air gap** (if installed) by removing the cap and flushing debris from the internal passages, or verify the disposer knockout plug was removed if the hose connects directly to the disposer.
4. **Run the garbage disposer** for 15–20 seconds with cold water to clear any restriction in the shared drain line, as GE recommends for drain faults.
5. **Remove the lower rack and inspect the sump/filter area** for food debris, broken glass, or foreign objects that can obstruct the drain pump inlet.
6. **Verify proper hose routing** by ensuring the drain hose does not rise more than the maximum recommended height and has a smooth downward path to the drain point.
7. **Test the drain pump** by listening during a drain cycle or manually inspecting the pump housing. If the path is clear but the pump does not move water, replace the pump assembly and retest.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the C2 code persists after you have cleared the drain hose, air gap, disposer connection, and sump filter, and verified the drain pump runs but does not move water. A pro can test the pump motor windings, check control board outputs, and diagnose wiring or valve faults that are not obvious during a visual inspection. Also call for service if you are uncomfortable disconnecting plumbing or working around the dishwasher's lower access panel.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (hose, air gap, or pump if needed), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call and labor.
