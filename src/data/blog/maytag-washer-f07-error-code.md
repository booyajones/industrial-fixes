## Maytag F07 Error Code — What It Means

The F07 code appears on some Maytag dryers when the control board detects a problem with the drive motor or drum rotation system. The dryer may refuse to start, the drum may not turn even though the motor runs, or the drum may feel locked or very hard to rotate by hand. This code is not universal across all Maytag appliances. On washers, similar-looking codes like F7 E1 or F0E7 refer to entirely different faults and should not be confused with F07.

Because manufacturer documentation for F07 is limited in public sources, always confirm the exact code and its meaning in your dryer's service manual or wiring diagram. The fault typically points to a mechanical failure in the belt, pulley, or motor assembly rather than an electronic sensor issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drive motor when the real culprit is a broken belt or seized idler pulley. Before ordering a motor, remove the front or rear panel and visually inspect the belt for breakage, slack, or glazing.

## Common Causes

- **Broken or slipped drive belt (~50%)** The rubber belt that wraps around the drum, idler pulley, and motor pulley can snap, stretch, or slip off, allowing the motor to spin without turning the drum.
- **Seized or binding idler pulley (~25%)** The spring-loaded idler pulley maintains belt tension and can freeze up due to worn bearings, creating excessive drag that prevents the motor from starting or triggers the fault.
- **Jammed drum or mechanical obstruction (~15%)** Coins, small garments, or lint buildup around the blower wheel or drum rollers can lock the drum and prevent rotation.
- **Failing drive motor (~8%)** The motor itself may have burned windings, a stuck rotor, or a failed start capacitor that stops it from turning under load.
- **Control board or user interface fault (~2%)** After ruling out all mechanical causes, a defective main control board or interface assembly may falsely report an F07 error.

Move on to inspect the drive belt and idler pulley for breakage or slack.<br><strong>No:</strong> Something is physically jamming the drum. </details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you see or feel a drive belt on the motor and drum pulleys when you open the service panel?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Check whether the belt is intact, properly seated on all pulleys, and under correct tension from the idler. If the belt looks glazed or loose, replace it.<br><strong>No:</strong> The belt has broken or come completely off. Replace the belt and inspect the idler pulley and motor pulley for wear or damage that caused the failure.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the idler pulley spin smoothly when you turn it by hand, or does it feel rough or stuck?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The idler is working. Test the motor by advancing the dryer cycle and listening for motor hum or rotation. If the motor is silent, test for power at the motor terminals or replace the motor.<br><strong>No:</strong> Replace the idler pulley. A seized pulley will overload the motor and can cause the belt to slip or the code to appear even with a good belt.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker.
2. **Remove the service panel** (usually the front lower panel or the entire front cabinet) according to your model's disassembly instructions to expose the drum, belt, idler pulley, and motor.
3. **Inspect the drive belt** for breaks, glazing, or slack, and verify it is properly seated on the drum pulley, idler pulley, and motor pulley.
4. **Check the idler pulley** by spinning it by hand and feeling for roughness, wobble, or binding that would prevent proper belt tension.
5. **Clear any obstructions** around the drum, blower wheel, and drive path that could jam rotation.
6. **Test the motor** by advancing the cycle and listening for hum or rotation; if the belt and idler are good but the motor does not run, test for voltage at the motor terminals and replace the motor if necessary.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a short test cycle to confirm the drum turns smoothly and the F07 code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable disassembling the dryer cabinet, if the drum remains jammed after clearing visible obstructions, or if you have replaced the belt and idler pulley but the F07 code persists. A professional can test motor windings, check control-board signals, and diagnose wiring faults that are difficult to trace without a multimeter and service documentation. Also call for help if the dryer uses gas and you need to move the appliance away from the gas line during disassembly.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
