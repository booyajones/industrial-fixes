## Gree P7 Error Code — What It Means

On Gree mini-split systems, the P7 error code indicates an IPM (Intelligent Power Module) temperature sensor circuit malfunction. Gree's own support documentation describes P7 as an IPM temperature sensor circuit fault, while some maintenance manuals for specific model families also list it as an IPM or PFC (Power Factor Correction) temperature sensor error. The IPM is part of the inverter drive stage in the outdoor unit, and this fault means the control board either cannot read the IPM's temperature or has detected an overheating condition.

The code protects the compressor and electronics from thermal damage. It can be triggered by a faulty sensor, a loose connection, or by actual overheating of the drive module due to poor heat rejection. When P7 appears, the outdoor unit will typically shut down to prevent damage until the fault is cleared.

## Common Causes

- **IPM temperature sensor loose or not seated** The temperature sensor connector at the drive board or module can work loose over time, breaking the circuit and triggering P7.
- **Temperature sensor open, shorted, or out of range** The thermistor itself can fail with an open circuit, short circuit, or drift outside the expected resistance range.
- **Dirty condenser coil or blocked airflow** When the outdoor coil is clogged with debris or airflow is restricted, the IPM cannot shed heat and will overheat.
- **Outdoor fan not running or wiring fault** A failed fan motor, broken fan blade, or damaged wiring prevents air from cooling the condenser and drive module.
- **Poor thermal contact at the IPM heatsink** Dried thermal paste or a loose heatsink mounting allows heat to build up in the drive module even when airflow is normal.
- **Outdoor main PCB or drive board failure** If the sensor and all cooling components check good, the control board itself may have a fault in the sensor circuit or IPM driver stage.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the breaker for three to five minutes, then restore power and check if P7 returns after a test run.
2. **Inspect and clean the outdoor coil.** Remove any leaves, grass, or dirt blocking the fins and wash the coil with coil cleaner if heavily soiled.
3. **Check outdoor fan operation.** Turn the unit on and confirm the fan spins freely and moves air, and inspect the fan motor wiring and connectors for damage or corrosion.
4. **Reseat the IPM temperature sensor connector** on the outdoor control board or drive module, making sure it clicks firmly into place.
5. **Measure the sensor resistance.** Disconnect the sensor plug and use a multimeter to check resistance across the sensor leads, then compare the reading to your model's service data or resistance table.
6. **Inspect the IPM and heatsink area** for signs of overheating, burnt components, or poor thermal contact between the module and heatsink.
7. **Replace the outdoor main PCB** if the sensor measures within spec, airflow and cooling are normal, and the error persists after reseating connections.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working inside energized equipment or if the error returns after cleaning the coil and reseating connectors. Diagnosing sensor resistance, checking refrigerant pressures, and replacing control boards or the IPM module require specialized tools and knowledge of high-voltage inverter circuits. A pro can also cross-reference your exact model's service manual for resistance specs and fault trees that are not published in consumer-facing documents.
