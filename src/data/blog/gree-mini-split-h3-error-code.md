## Gree Mini Split H3 Error Code — What It Means

The H3 code on a Gree mini split indicates that the outdoor unit has activated compressor overload or overcurrent protection. In Gree's service documentation, the H0 through H3 family is described as a decrease in frequency due to overload, with H3 specifically tied to compressor overload protection logic. The outdoor control board detects the compressor or its circuit operating outside safe limits and reduces frequency or shuts the system down to protect the compressor from damage.

This code is a protection response, not a diagnosis of a failed compressor. It tells you that something is causing the compressor to run too hot or draw too much current. The most common real-world triggers are restricted airflow at the outdoor unit, problems with the outdoor fan, refrigerant charge issues, or electrical supply problems. A failed compressor or inverter board is possible but should only be suspected after ruling out all external conditions.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or blocked condenser coil** A clogged outdoor coil restricts airflow and forces the compressor to work harder, raising current draw and triggering overload protection.
- **Outdoor fan motor or control failure** If the fan does not spin at the correct speed or stops entirely, the condenser cannot reject heat and the compressor overheats or pulls high current.
- **Refrigerant overcharge, undercharge, or restriction** Too much refrigerant, a leak causing low charge, or a blockage in the metering device all alter operating pressures and can push compressor current beyond safe limits.
- **Low or unstable supply voltage** Inadequate voltage, loose wiring, burned connectors, or poor electrical connections cause the compressor to draw excessive current to meet load.
- **Compressor thermal protector or inverter board fault** A malfunctioning overload device, failed thermal protector, winding short, or inverter control issue can trigger H3 even when external conditions are normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code and power cycle the system.** Confirm that the indoor display shows H3 and that the outdoor unit is in protection mode, then turn off power at the breaker for two minutes to reset the control board and see if the fault recurs.
2. **Check the power supply at the outdoor unit.** Use a multimeter to measure voltage at the outdoor unit terminals and inspect all wiring, terminals, and connectors for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks, then correct any undervoltage or damaged connections.
3. **Inspect and clean the outdoor condenser coil.** Remove any debris, leaves, or obstructions around the outdoor unit and use coil cleaner and low-pressure water to clean the coil fins thoroughly, then straighten any bent fins.
4. **Test the outdoor fan motor and control.** Confirm the fan spins at proper speed when the unit runs, check the motor winding resistance if accessible, and inspect fan control outputs and any capacitor if your model uses one.
5. **Measure refrigerant operating conditions.** Connect gauges and check suction and discharge pressures against your model's service table, look for signs of overcharge or undercharge, and check for restrictions at the metering device or filter drier.
6. **Monitor compressor current draw and temperature.** Use a clamp meter to measure compressor current during operation and compare it to the nameplate rating, and feel the compressor body for excessive heat.
7. **Inspect the thermal protector and outdoor PCB.** Check compressor wiring and connectors for damage, test the overload protector resistance if accessible, and inspect the outdoor control board for burned traces, failed relays, or swollen capacitors, then replace any faulty component and retest the system under normal load.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live 220-240 volt power, if you do not own refrigerant gauges and recovery equipment, or if the code returns after cleaning the coil and confirming good voltage. Diagnosing compressor current, testing inverter boards, and handling refrigerant legally require EPA certification and specialized tools. If the compressor itself has failed, replacement on a mini split often costs as much as a new outdoor unit, so get a professional assessment and a quote for both repair and replacement before authorizing work.
