## Goodman Heat Pump E2 Error Code — What It Means

On Goodman and Amana furnace controls, E2 indicates a low-stage pressure switch stuck open or a pressure switch problem. The control board expects the inducer motor to create enough draft for the pressure switch to close before the heating sequence can continue. When the switch stays open, the board cannot verify safe venting conditions and locks out with E2. Note that E2 is not a universal code across all Goodman heat pump models, so the exact meaning can vary depending on your control board and unit. If you see E2 on an actual heat pump indoor or outdoor display, check your model number against the manufacturer literature to confirm the fault definition.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted vent piping** Obstructions in the intake or exhaust flue prevent the inducer from generating the draft needed to close the pressure switch.
- **Inducer motor not running or weak** A failed or underperforming inducer cannot move enough air to create the negative pressure required by the switch.
- **Condensate or debris in pressure switch tubing** Water, dirt, or calcium buildup in the tubing or ports blocks the draft signal to the pressure switch.
- **Failed pressure switch** The switch itself is defective and does not close even when draft is present and correct.
- **Wiring or connector fault** Broken wires or loose connectors between the control board and the pressure switch prevent the board from reading switch closure.
- **Plugged pressure port** The small sensing port at the inducer housing or on the switch body is blocked, preventing accurate pressure measurement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number and control board type** to confirm that E2 corresponds to a pressure switch fault on your specific unit, since code definitions vary across Goodman products.
2. **Check venting and air intake** by inspecting the flue and intake piping for blockages, disconnections, or restrictions, and replace or clean the air filter if dirty.
3. **Confirm the inducer motor starts and runs** by turning on the unit and listening for the inducer to spin up, then feel for airflow at the vent termination or inducer housing.
4. **Inspect and clear the pressure switch tubing** by disconnecting the tubing from the switch and blowing through it gently to remove water, dirt, or debris, then check both the inducer port and switch port for blockage.
5. **Measure draft with a manometer** if available by connecting it to the pressure switch port or tubing and comparing the reading to the switch rating stamped on the device (for example, one unit in the field used a 0.72 inch water column switch).
6. **Test the pressure switch electrically** by turning off power, disconnecting the switch wires, and checking continuity across the terminals while creating suction at the port to verify that the switch opens and closes.
7. **Replace the failed component** by installing a new pressure switch if draft is correct but the switch does not close, or by replacing the inducer motor if draft is absent or too weak, then restore power and verify the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, electrical components, or venting systems. A professional should handle any repair that involves combustion safety, pressure measurement with a manometer, inducer motor replacement, or control board diagnosis. If you clear tubing and verify the inducer runs but the code persists, a technician can perform detailed draft and electrical tests to pinpoint whether the pressure switch, wiring, or control board is at fault. Always call a pro if you smell gas, see rust or corrosion in the vent system, or if the unit has tripped multiple safety codes.
