## Navien E244 Error Code — What It Means

The E244 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a communication failure between the main printed circuit board (PCB) and the display panel or remote controller. When the control board cannot receive or transmit signals to the display, the unit flags the error and may stop operation to protect itself. This communication breakdown can stem from loose wiring, corroded terminals, damaged cables, or a failed component in the communication circuit.

Because this code involves internal electronics and low-voltage control circuits, the root cause is often a connection issue rather than a catastrophic part failure. Checking and cleaning all communication cable connectors and inspecting for physical damage usually comes first. If those steps do not resolve the fault, replacement of the display panel, cable assembly, or main PCB may be required.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main PCB or display panel immediately when the real problem is a loose or dirty connector at either end of the communication cable. Always inspect and reseat all cable connections before ordering expensive boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded connector (~50%)** The multi-pin plug or ribbon cable at either the main board or display panel has worked loose or developed corrosion, breaking the signal path.
- **Damaged communication cable (~25%)** The flat ribbon or multi-conductor cable running between the control board and panel is pinched, cut, or internally broken.
- **Failed display panel (~15%)** The display panel itself has a failed processor or communication circuit that prevents handshake with the main board.
- **Faulty main PCB (~10%)** The main control board has a failed communication port, relay driver, or related component that interrupts signal transmission.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and wait 30 seconds to allow capacitors to discharge.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the mounting screws, typically two or four Phillips-head fasteners, and lift the cover away.
3. **Locate the communication cable** connecting the main control board to the display panel or remote interface, usually a flat ribbon cable or multi-pin connector.
4. **Disconnect and inspect** both ends of the cable for corrosion, bent pins, moisture, or physical damage; clean terminals with electrical contact cleaner and a soft brush if needed.
5. **Reseat the cable** firmly into both the main board and display panel connectors, ensuring the locking tabs or latches click into place.
6. **Restore power** at the breaker and observe the display for 60 seconds to see if the E244 error clears and normal operation resumes.
7. **If the error persists**, power down again and replace the communication cable with a new part matching your model number; if a new cable does not resolve the fault, test or replace the display panel next, followed by the main PCB if necessary.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with low-voltage electronics, if reseating connectors and replacing the cable does not resolve the error, or if you need to test circuit board voltages with a multimeter. A pro can quickly isolate whether the main PCB or display panel has failed and can verify that no other wiring faults exist elsewhere in the unit. Also call for service if the heater is under warranty, since DIY board replacement may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
