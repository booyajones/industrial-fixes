## Electrolux Dryer E38 Error Code — What It Means

The E38 error code on Electrolux dryers indicates a communication or control system fault. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the back panel for confirmation. Generally, this code points to a problem with the main control board, a sensor connection, or internal communication between control components. The dryer may not start, or it may stop mid-cycle when this code appears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board immediately, but a loose connector or failed thermistor is often the real culprit. Inspect all wiring harnesses and test sensor continuity with a multimeter before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** Vibration and heat can loosen or corrode the plugs between the control board and sensors, interrupting communication.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** A broken or open-circuit thermistor will prevent the control from reading drum temperature and trigger a fault.
- **Main control board failure (~20%)** The electronic control board itself can develop solder cracks or component failures, especially after power surges.
- **Low or unstable line voltage (~10%)** Dryers require stable voltage to operate correctly, and a weak or fluctuating supply can cause intermittent control faults.
- **User interface board or keypad fault (~10%)** The front panel or UI board can lose communication with the main control, especially if moisture has entered the console.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the service panel** (usually the rear or front lower panel) using a screwdriver or nut driver to access internal components.
3. **Inspect all wiring connectors** at the main control board, user interface board, and thermistor or sensor locations for looseness, burn marks, or corrosion.
4. **Test the thermistor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance with a multimeter at room temperature (consult your model's table for expected values, typically a few thousand ohms).
5. **Check line voltage** at the dryer outlet or terminal block with a multimeter to confirm the appliance is receiving proper power (240 V for electric models in North America).
6. **Replace the faulty component** (thermistor, sensor, or control board) if testing confirms a failure, following the manufacturer's instructions and noting wire positions before removal.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify the error is cleared and the appliance operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components or if you lack a multimeter to test sensors and voltages. A professional should also handle the repair if you have replaced wiring connectors and sensors but the error persists, as advanced diagnostics and control board replacement may require specialized tools and experience. If the dryer shares a circuit with other high-draw appliances and you suspect a wiring or breaker issue, consult an electrician to inspect your home's electrical panel and outlet.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
