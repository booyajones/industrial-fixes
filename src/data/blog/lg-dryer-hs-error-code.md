## What this code means
The HS code on an LG dryer indicates the control has detected an error with the humidity sensor system. When this code appears, the dryer may continue running for about 2.5 hours, which can make it look like the dryer is not drying normally. The humidity sensor uses metal bars inside the drum to detect moisture in the load and determine when clothes are dry. When the sensor cannot read moisture correctly, the control flags HS and defaults to a timed cycle.

LG describes this as a humidity sensor circuit fault, not a general heating or airflow problem. The sensor bars are located inside the drum just below the lint filter opening. They rely on good electrical contact through moisture in the fabric to signal the control. Any residue or film on those bars will block the signal and trigger the code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the entire humidity sensor assembly or even the main control board when the real fix is simply cleaning the sensor bars with a damp cloth. Always clean the bars and perform a power reset before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty sensor bars (~60%)** Fabric softener residue, detergent film, or laundry additive buildup on the metal sensor bars inside the drum prevents accurate moisture detection.
- **Transient control or electrical contact issue (~20%)** A temporary glitch in the control board or a loose connector can register a false sensor error that clears with a power reset.
- **Failed humidity sensor assembly (~12%)** The sensor bars or the integrated sensor circuit can fail and lose continuity or develop incorrect resistance, requiring replacement of the moisture sensor assembly.
- **Damaged wiring or connector to the sensor (~5%)** Frayed wires or a corroded connector between the sensor and the control board can interrupt the signal and trigger HS.
- **Faulty main control board (~3%)** If the control board incorrectly interprets the sensor signal or has a failed input circuit, it may display HS even when the sensor is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify power supply.** Confirm the dryer is plugged into a grounded 240V outlet that matches the rating plate on the back of the unit.
2. **Inspect the lint filter and door.** Remove the lint filter, clean it, and check that it seats fully in its housing. Close the door firmly and confirm the latch engages.
3. **Locate and clean the humidity sensor bars.** Open the door and look inside the drum just below the lint filter opening. You will see two thin metal strips mounted horizontally. Wipe both bars with a soft cloth lightly dampened with water and a drop of mild dish soap to remove any film or residue. Dry the bars completely with a clean towel.
4. **Perform a power reset.** Unplug the dryer or turn off the circuit breaker. Wait about 5 seconds. While the unit is unpowered, press and hold the Start/Pause button for 5 seconds. Restore power at the outlet or breaker.
5. **Run a test cycle.** Close the door firmly, select a sensor dry cycle, and press Start. Observe whether the HS code returns.
6. **If the code persists, check wiring and sensor continuity.** Unplug the dryer. Remove the top or rear panel to access the sensor connector. Inspect the harness for damage and test the sensor for continuity according to your model's service manual. If readings are out of spec, replace the sensor assembly.
7. **Replace the humidity sensor or control board if diagnosed as faulty.** If the sensor tests good and wiring is intact, the control board may be misinterpreting the signal. Consult a technician or replace the board following the service manual for your model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the HS code returns after you have cleaned the sensor bars and completed a full power reset. Persistent HS indicates a failed sensor assembly, damaged wiring, or a faulty control board. A technician will use a multimeter to test sensor continuity and resistance, inspect the harness connector for corrosion, and verify the control board input circuit. If you are uncomfortable removing panels, handling 240V connections, or interpreting wiring diagrams, schedule service rather than guessing at part replacement. LG states that dryers displaying HS after basic cleaning and reset steps require professional repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-15 in cleaning supplies, 15-30 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call if sensor assembly or wiring needs replacement.
