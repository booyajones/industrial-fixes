## Whirlpool Dryer F72–F78 Error Code — What It Means

F72 through F78 error codes on Whirlpool dryers typically signal a problem with one or more temperature or moisture sensors, or the wiring and connectors that link them to the main control board. The exact meaning of each code number varies by model and platform, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the console to identify which sensor or circuit is being flagged.

Because Whirlpool uses these codes across many model families, the same F7x number may point to a different sensor or fault depending on the year and series. In general, the control board has detected a reading that is out of range, a broken wire, or a short circuit in the sensor harness. The dryer may stop mid-cycle or refuse to start until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, assuming the error means the board is bad. In most cases the actual problem is a loose connector, broken wire, or a failed sensor that costs far less to replace. Always inspect the wiring and test the sensor resistance before ordering a control.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~40%)** Vibration and heat can work sensor connectors loose or cause corrosion on the terminals, breaking the signal path to the control board.
- **Failed thermistor or moisture sensor (~30%)** Temperature or moisture sensors drift out of tolerance or fail open, sending readings the control interprets as a fault.
- **Broken or pinched wire in the harness (~15%)** Wires routed near moving parts or sharp metal edges can be cut, pinched, or burned through, creating an open circuit or short.
- **Faulty main control board (~10%)** A failed control board may misread valid sensor signals or lose the ability to power the sensor circuit correctly.
- **Water or lint contamination on sensor terminals (~5%)** Moisture from a vent leak or lint buildup on sensor connectors can create a false reading or short the circuit to ground.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the wall outlet or circuit breaker and leave the dryer unplugged for at least five minutes to clear temporary faults.
2. **Remove the top or front panel** by lifting the lid and removing the screws along the front edge, or by taking out the toe panel and lower front screws, depending on your model.
3. **Locate the sensors** referenced by your model's wiring diagram or service manual. Common sensors include the inlet thermistor, exhaust thermistor, and moisture sensor bars or strips.
4. **Inspect each connector** on the sensor wiring harness for loose pins, corrosion, burn marks, or moisture. Unplug and replug each connector to make sure a clean, tight fit.
5. **Check the wiring harness** from each sensor back to the control board. Look for pinched, frayed, or burned insulation, especially near cabinet edges, the blower wheel, or the heater housing.
6. **Test each sensor with a multimeter** set to resistance (ohms). Consult your model's table for the expected resistance at room temperature. Replace any sensor that reads open, shorted, or far outside the range.
7. **Reassemble the dryer** once all connectors are secure and any faulty sensor or damaged wire is replaced. Restore power and run a test cycle to confirm the error code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you cannot locate the sensor referenced by your specific code, or if you have tested all sensors and wiring and found them within specification but the code persists. A technician has access to the full service manual, wiring diagrams, and diagnostic software that can pinpoint intermittent faults or rare control board failures. Also call a pro if you discover evidence of arcing, burned wiring, or repeated component failures, which may indicate a deeper electrical problem in the home circuit or dryer grounding system.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
