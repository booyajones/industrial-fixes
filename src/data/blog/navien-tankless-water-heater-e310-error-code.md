## Navien E310 Error Code — What It Means

The E310 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame sensing or ignition failure. The unit attempts to light the burner but either cannot establish a flame or cannot detect that a flame is present. This can prevent hot water production entirely, or cause the heater to shut down after a brief attempt to ignite.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary slightly by model and firmware version, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover for the precise definition. In general, E310 points to a problem in the ignition or flame-detection circuit, which includes the flame rod, grounding, gas supply, and igniter assembly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the entire igniter assembly or gas valve when the real culprit is simply a flame rod covered in carbon or a loose ground wire. Clean the flame rod with fine steel wool and verify the ground connection before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon deposits or corrosion on the flame rod insulator prevent the sensor from detecting ionization current, so the control board thinks no flame is present even when the burner lights.
- **Poor or missing ground connection (~20%)** Flame sensing relies on a complete ground path; a loose ground wire, paint on the mounting screw, or corrosion at the bonding point will cause intermittent or constant E310 faults.
- **Weak or interrupted gas supply (~15%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or air in the line after a recent service can prevent reliable ignition or cause the flame to drop out immediately after lighting.
- **Faulty flame rod or rod wiring (~10%)** A cracked ceramic insulator, broken wire, or corroded connector at the flame rod can break the sensing circuit and trigger the code even when the rod is clean.
- **Failed igniter or ignition transformer (~5%)** If the spark is weak or absent, the burner will not light at all, and the control board will report a flame fault after several attempts.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the screws or clips and set it aside on a clean surface.
3. **Locate the flame rod**, a thin metal probe extending into the burner area, usually near the igniter.
4. **Disconnect the flame rod wire** at the connector and unscrew the rod from its mounting bracket.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine steel wool or a non-metallic abrasive pad until the metal and ceramic are bright and free of carbon or white deposits; avoid bending the rod.
6. **Inspect the ground wire** where it attaches to the chassis; remove any paint or corrosion at the contact point and tighten the screw firmly.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod** and reconnect the wire, then replace the front cover, open the gas valve, and restore power to test the unit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you do not feel comfortable working around gas connections, if cleaning the flame rod and verifying the ground does not clear the error after two attempts, or if you suspect a gas-pressure or control-board issue. A pro can measure gas pressure, test the flame-sensing microamps with a multimeter, and safely diagnose whether the gas valve or main control board has failed. Professional service is also recommended if you notice a strong gas odor, hear unusual clicking or rumbling, or if the unit repeatedly locks out with multiple error codes.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
