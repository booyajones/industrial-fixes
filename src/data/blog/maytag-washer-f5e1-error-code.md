## What this code means
The F5E1 error code indicates a door or lid switch failure on your Maytag washer. The control board cannot verify that the door has locked properly when a cycle starts or cannot confirm the door has unlocked when the cycle ends. This safety interlock prevents the machine from operating until the switch signals correctly. According to Maytag product help, the code appears when the door is not entirely closed or something is blocking the door switch, such as clothing, tags, or shipping material. The washer will not start a wash cycle until this condition is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when the real problem is a failed lid switch assembly. A simple multimeter resistance check on the switch (infinite resistance when open, near zero when closed) confirms whether the switch itself is defective before spending money on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Obstruction blocking latch (~45%)** Clothing items, fabric tags, or debris physically prevent the door hook from seating into the switch receiver, so the sensor never engages.
- **Failed lid switch assembly (~35%)** The internal sensor in the switch has failed electrically and no longer changes state when the actuator is pressed, a common wear item in top-load washers.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~10%)** The connector between the lid switch and control board is loose, corroded, or the wires are pinched or broken, interrupting the signal.
- **Misaligned latch mechanism (~7%)** The mechanical hook on the door or the receiver on the cabinet is bent or shifted, preventing the switch from fully engaging even when the door is closed.
- **Control board failure (~3%)** The main PCB cannot read the signal from the switch due to internal component failure, though this is rare and usually presents with other error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the washer from the wall outlet and wait 5 minutes to reset the control board and discharge residual voltage.
2. **Inspect the latch area** by opening the door and looking for clothing, tags, shipping material, or debris around the latch hook and the switch receiver on the cabinet, then remove any obstructions with your fingers or a brush.
3. **Test the mechanical latch** by closing the door firmly and listening for a distinct click, indicating the hook has seated into the receiver and the actuator has moved the switch.
4. **Access the lid switch** (typically by removing the rear panel or top console on top-load models, consult your model's service manual for exact location) and disconnect the wiring harness from the switch terminals.
5. **Test the switch electrically** using a multimeter set to resistance mode: probe the switch terminals with the actuator open (should read infinite or very high resistance) and then press the actuator (should read near zero ohms or continuity), replacing the switch if it does not change state.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the switch to the control board for loose connectors, corrosion, or pinched or broken wires, and reseat or repair connections as needed.
7. **Reinstall the new switch** (if defective) by connecting the harness, securing the mounting bracket, reassembling the panels, restoring power, and running a test cycle to confirm the error has cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the control board area or if you have replaced the lid switch and verified all wiring connections but the F5E1 error persists. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board to determine if the PCB itself has failed, which is a low-probability cause but requires board-level testing or replacement. Also call a pro if the latch mechanism is severely bent or broken and requires disassembly of the cabinet or door hinge, or if you lack the tools to safely remove panels and test electrical components with a multimeter.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-60 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
