## Maytag F5E1 Error Code — What It Means

The F5E1 code indicates a door switch or door lock detection fault. The washer control has detected a problem with the door lock circuit and will not proceed until it can verify a valid closed and locked condition. In plain terms, the machine thinks the door or lid is not secure, or the lock signal is not being received correctly by the control board.

This fault appears when the control cannot confirm that the door or lid is properly closed and locked after a power-up, reset attempt, or cycle start. The washer will remain stopped and display F5E1 until the lock circuit reads correctly.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first. Always test the door lock assembly for continuity and inspect the striker alignment before replacing any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Door or lid not fully closed (~40%)** Clothing, foreign material, or a misaligned door prevents the latch from engaging the strike and signaling the lock circuit.
- **Faulty door lock or lid switch assembly (~25%)** The lock assembly or lid switch has failed internally and does not change state or send the correct signal to the control board.
- **Debris or shipping material in the lock area (~20%)** Packing foam, lint, or other debris physically blocks the door lock mechanism or switch, preventing proper closure detection.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~10%)** Harness connectors between the lock assembly and the control board are loose, corroded, or damaged, interrupting the lock-status signal.
- **Temporary control glitch (~5%)** A transient software error or memory fault in the control board causes a false lock detection failure that clears after a power reset.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait at least five minutes to reset the control board, then restore power and attempt a new cycle to see if the fault clears.
2. **Open the door or lid fully** and inspect the strike area, latch, and lock mechanism for clothing, debris, shipping foam, or any obstruction that would prevent complete closure.
3. **Close the door firmly** and verify it latches flush with the cabinet, then start a cycle and listen for the lock mechanism to click or engage.
4. **Inspect the door lock or lid lock assembly** for visible cracks, broken tabs, or misalignment, and check that the striker engages the lock catch squarely.
5. **Disconnect power and remove the top or front panel** as needed to access the lock assembly wiring harness, then check all connectors for tight seating and inspect terminals for corrosion or damage.
6. **Test the lock assembly with a multimeter** by checking continuity across the switch contacts in both the locked and unlocked positions, replacing the assembly if it does not change state correctly.
7. **Reassemble the washer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the F5E1 code does not return and the door locks properly at cycle start.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cycled power, cleared all obstructions, and verified the door closes completely but the F5E1 code still appears. A technician can perform systematic continuity and voltage testing of the lock circuit, verify control-board communication, and replace the lock assembly or control board if required. Also call for service if you are uncomfortable working with panel removal, wiring harness connectors, or multimeter testing, or if your washer is still under warranty and unauthorized repairs would void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
