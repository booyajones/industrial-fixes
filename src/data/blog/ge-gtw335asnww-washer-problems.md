## GE GTW335ASNWW Washer Problems & Error Codes — Overview

The GE GTW335ASNWW is a top-load washer with basic electronic controls and onboard diagnostics accessible through a cycle-knob sequence. Like other GE top-loaders in this family, it stores fault codes that flash as LED patterns or display numbers to help you pinpoint failures. Common trouble spots include the lid lock mechanism, drain pump, and drive motor connections, with most issues tied to wiring faults or worn mechanical parts rather than board failures.

## Most Common Problems on This Model {#fix}

- **Lid lock error or won't start** Caused by a failed lid lock assembly, broken wiring to the lock, or a bad lid switch that prevents the cycle from starting.
- **Won't drain or stops mid-cycle** Usually a clogged or failed drain pump, kinked drain hose, or a stuck drain pump impeller blocking water removal.
- **Motor won't spin or makes noise** Drive motor failure, worn motor coupler, or a slipping belt on the pulley system that prevents the basket from turning.
- **No communication error or all lights flashing** Loss of signal between the motor control and main timer board, often caused by loose wire harness connectors or corroded terminals.
- **Mode shifter fault** The mode shifter actuator that moves the transmission between agitate and spin has failed or is jammed, preventing proper cycle transitions.
- **Overheated motor code** Motor thermal protection triggered by an overloaded tub, binding brake assembly, or a failing motor that draws excessive current.
- **Won't agitate but will spin** Agitator dogs inside the agitator are stripped or the drive coupler between motor and transmission is worn out.
- **Intermittent stopping or random pauses** Faulty wiring connections at the control board, corroded spade terminals, or a failing main control board storing false faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a tech if you cannot enter diagnostic mode using the cycle-knob sequence, if the error code you retrieve does not match any published GE fault list for this series, or if you see arcing or burnt traces on the main control board. Also call for motor or transmission work if you are not comfortable working with heavy mechanical assemblies or testing live motor circuits. Any repair involving the main tub seal, transmission, or suspension requires disassembly of the cabinet and is best left to someone with the proper tools and experience.
