## Trane E7 Error Code — What It Means

On Trane heat pumps using the Medium Foundation control system, E7 indicates a discharge temperature sensor fault for system A. The T5-A sensor circuit has either opened or shorted. This sensor monitors the temperature of refrigerant leaving the compressor. When the control board loses a valid signal, it throws E7 and may lock out or limit operation to protect the compressor. Note that E7 on other brands can mean something completely different, so always confirm your exact Trane model before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed T5-A discharge temperature sensor** The thermistor element inside the sensor has opened or shorted, which is the most common root cause according to Trane's troubleshooting chart.
- **Damaged or loose sensor wiring** Wires from the sensor to the main board can break, corrode at connectors, or pull loose during maintenance.
- **Connector corrosion or pin damage** Moisture or vibration can corrode the sensor plug or bend pins, creating intermittent or open circuits.
- **Faulty main control board** If the sensor and wiring both test good, the board input circuit itself may have failed and the board requires replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the fault code** on your Trane control display and note your exact model number, since E7 definitions vary across brands.
2. **Power off** the unit at the disconnect and breaker, then lock out the power source to work safely.
3. **Locate the T5-A sensor**, which is typically mounted on the discharge line near the compressor or on the reversing valve body, then inspect the connector and harness for cracks, burns, corrosion, or loose pins.
4. **Disconnect the sensor** from the harness and use a multimeter set to resistance to test the sensor across its two leads. Compare the reading to your model's service literature. An open (infinite resistance) or dead short (near zero ohms) means the sensor has failed.
5. **Replace the T5-A sensor** if it tests open or shorted, reconnect the harness, restore power, and clear the fault to see if E7 returns.
6. **Check wiring continuity** from the sensor connector back to the main board if the sensor itself tested good. Repair or replace any damaged wires or connectors.
7. **Replace the main control board** if the sensor and all wiring verify good and the code persists after reconnection and a power cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are not comfortable working with live 240 V power, refrigerant lines, or multimeter diagnostics, call a licensed HVAC technician. Replacing the sensor is straightforward for anyone with basic electrical skills, but misdiagnosing the board or damaging refrigerant lines during sensor removal can lead to expensive compressor damage or refrigerant loss. A technician can also verify refrigerant charge and run a full system checkout after the repair to confirm safe operation.
