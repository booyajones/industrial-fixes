## Navien Tankless Water Heater E313 Error — What It Means

The E313 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a flame loss or ignition failure during operation. The unit detected that the burner flame either failed to ignite properly or extinguished unexpectedly after starting. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent unburned gas from accumulating.

The code can appear during initial ignition or while the unit is running. The control board monitors flame sensing through the flame rod, and when it does not detect a stable flame signal, it triggers the E313 fault and locks out the system. Common triggers include interrupted gas flow, dirty or corroded flame sensors, inadequate combustion air, or venting problems that affect flame stability.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or flame rod when the real issue is simply a closed gas valve, empty propane tank, or debris blocking the burner orifices. Check that the gas supply valve is fully open and propane tanks have adequate fuel before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply interruption or low pressure (~35%)** The gas valve is partially closed, the propane tank is empty or in bypass mode, or the incoming gas pressure is below the minimum required for stable combustion.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensing rod prevents it from detecting the flame signal, even when the burner is firing normally.
- **Blocked burner orifices or heat exchanger (~20%)** Debris, dust, or carbon deposits restrict gas flow through the burner jets or reduce combustion efficiency, causing weak or unstable flames.
- **Insufficient combustion air or venting issues (~12%)** Blocked air intake, restricted exhaust vent, or improper vent installation creates negative pressure or starves the burner of oxygen, leading to flame rollout or extinguishment.
- **Faulty gas valve or igniter (~8%)** The gas valve fails to open fully or modulate correctly, or the igniter does not produce a strong enough spark to reliably light the gas.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and close the main gas supply valve to the unit.
2. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the main shutoff valve is fully open. For propane systems, verify the tank has adequate fuel and the regulator is not in low-pressure bypass mode.
3. **Inspect the air intake and exhaust vent** for blockages such as debris, insect nests, or ice buildup in cold weather. Clear any obstructions and confirm vent piping is properly sloped and supported.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to access the burner assembly. Consult your model's manual for the specific cover-removal procedure.
5. **Examine the flame rod** for carbon deposits, corrosion, or damage. Use fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to gently clean the rod surface until shiny, taking care not to bend or break it.
6. **Check the burner orifices** for blockages or debris. Use compressed air or a soft brush to clear any buildup. Do not enlarge the orifice holes.
7. **Reassemble the unit** and restore gas and power. Run a hot-water tap to initiate a call for heat and observe through the viewing window to confirm stable ignition and flame presence. If the error persists, call a qualified technician to test gas pressure, inspect the gas valve, and verify proper combustion air settings.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the gas supply and flame rod cleaning do not resolve the error, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a faulty gas valve. Professionals have the tools to measure inlet gas pressure, test the flame sensing circuit, and calibrate the gas valve after replacement. Navien tankless units also require periodic descaling and burner cleaning that should be performed by a trained technician to maintain warranty coverage and safe operation. Any work involving gas lines, regulators, or pressure testing must be done by a licensed professional to meet code requirements.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
