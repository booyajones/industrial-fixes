## Samsung Range E28 Error Code — What It Means

The E28 error code on Samsung ranges can indicate different issues depending on the specific model and year. Without documented specifications for this exact code, the safest approach is to consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel. Error codes in this range often relate to communication problems between the main control board and other components, or sensor faults. Some models use E28 to flag a temperature sensor reading that falls outside expected parameters, while others may use it for control-board communication errors.

Because Samsung uses different control architectures across its range lineup, the exact meaning of E28 varies. Always verify the code definition in your model's service manual before ordering parts. If the manual is unavailable, note the full model number from the label inside the oven door or on the back panel and contact Samsung support or a qualified technician for the correct interpretation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board first when the real fault is a loose connector or a failed sensor. Check all wiring harness connections at the control board and sensors before ordering any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wire connector (~30%)** Vibration and heat can loosen the multi-pin connectors between the main control board and sensors or touch panel, causing intermittent communication errors.
- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~25%)** The oven cavity temperature sensor (thermistor) can drift out of spec or develop an open circuit, sending readings the control interprets as invalid.
- **Main control board fault (~20%)** Internal failures in the electronic control board, including failed solder joints or damaged circuits, can generate spurious error codes.
- **Faulty touch control panel (~15%)** The membrane switch or touch-panel assembly can develop shorts or communication faults that trigger control errors.
- **Wiring harness damage (~10%)** Pinched, melted, or chafed wires between the control board and sensors can cause intermittent faults that register as error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait three minutes to allow the control board to fully reset.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether the E28 code reappears immediately or only during oven operation.
3. **Consult the owner's manual** or the service label inside the control panel for the documented meaning of E28 on your specific model number.
4. **Remove the rear access panel** or lower control panel (depending on model) to access the main control board and wiring harnesses.
5. **Inspect all connectors** at the control board, oven temperature sensor, and touch panel for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks; reseat any suspect connectors.
6. **Test the oven temperature sensor** by disconnecting it and measuring its resistance with a multimeter; compare the reading to the values in your service manual (typically 1000-1100 ohms at room temperature, but consult your model's specifications).
7. **Replace the failed component** (sensor, control board, or touch panel) based on your findings and the manual's guidance, then reassemble the range and test all oven functions.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the control-panel area or if your range uses a high-voltage (240V) control board that requires precise diagnostics. A qualified appliance technician can retrieve detailed fault logs from the control, cross-reference the E28 code with Samsung's service bulletins for your exact model, and perform board-level testing that goes beyond simple part swaps. Also call a pro if the code persists after you have reseated connectors and replaced the obvious suspect parts, since some E28 faults involve software issues or require a control-board flash update available only through service channels.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
