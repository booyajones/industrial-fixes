## Trane Furnace E07 Error Code — What It Means

The E07 error code on a Trane furnace generally indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition sequence. The control board expects to see a flame signal after the igniter glows and the gas valve opens, but it is not detecting one or the signal is too weak. This can happen because the flame sensor is dirty or corroded, the igniter is failing, or gas flow is interrupted. Some models may use E07 for related safety lockouts when the flame does not prove within the allowed window.

Because Trane uses different control boards across its model range, the exact definition of E07 can vary. Always consult your furnace's wiring diagram or owner's manual to confirm the code meaning for your specific model. The furnace will lock out to prevent unburned gas from accumulating, and you will need to restore power or press the reset button to clear the code after addressing the root cause.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the gas valve or control board when the real fix is a $0 cleaning of the flame sensor rod. Pull the sensor, polish it with fine steel wool or emery cloth, and reinstall it before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty flame sensor rod (~55%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the sensor prevents it from conducting the flame signal back to the control board.
- **Weak or cracked igniter (~20%)** The hot-surface igniter may glow but not reach the temperature needed to ignite gas, or it may crack and fail partway through the cycle.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or closed valve (~10%)** Low gas pressure or a partially closed manual shutoff valve will prevent the burner from lighting even if the igniter works.
- **Loose or corroded flame-sensor wiring (~8%)** A poor connection at the sensor terminal or control board can interrupt the microamp signal and trigger the code.
- **Blocked or restricted burner orifices (~5%)** Dust or debris in the burner ports can disrupt the flame pattern and prevent stable flame sensing.
- **Failed control board (~2%)** The flame-rectification circuit on the board itself can fail, though this is less common than sensor or igniter issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the furnace disconnect switch and at the breaker to make sure safe work.
2. **Remove the furnace door panel** and locate the flame sensor rod, a thin metal probe near the burner assembly, usually held by a single screw or bracket.
3. **Unplug the sensor wire** from the control board or sensor terminal and unscrew the mounting bracket to pull the sensor out.
4. **Clean the sensor rod** by gently rubbing the metal portion with fine steel wool, emery cloth, or a non-abrasive pad until it is bright and free of soot or oxidation.
5. **Reinstall the sensor** in its bracket, reconnect the wire, and make sure the rod sits in the flame path without touching the burner or any grounded metal.
6. **Restore power** and set the thermostat to call for heat, then watch through the burner window to confirm ignition and steady flame.
7. **Monitor the furnace** through a full cycle to verify the code does not return and the burner stays lit for at least two minutes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if cleaning the flame sensor and checking gas supply do not resolve the E07 code, if you smell gas at any point, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. A professional should handle gas-pressure testing, control-board diagnostics, and any work inside the sealed combustion chamber. If the igniter glows but you never see flame, the gas valve or pressure regulator may need adjustment or replacement, which requires proper metering equipment and knowledge of local codes. Intermittent E07 codes that come and go can indicate a cracked heat exchanger or drafting problem that only a trained eye can diagnose safely.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if sensor or igniter replacement needed, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350 for service call and part.
