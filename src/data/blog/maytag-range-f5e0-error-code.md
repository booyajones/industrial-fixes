## Maytag Range F5E0 Error Code — What It Means

The F5 E0 code on a Maytag range signals a fault in the oven door latch system. Maytag defines this as a problem with the oven door latch assembly, the door switch, or the wiring connecting them. The control board has detected that the door lock circuit is not responding correctly, which can prevent the oven from operating or trigger the error during self-clean cycles.

This code is specific to the door-lock mechanism and should not be confused with generic F5 errors listed on some third-party sites. Maytag's own documentation confirms that F5 E0 points directly to the latch assembly, door switch, or related wiring as the problem area.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, assuming the error means a failed electronic control. Check the door latch assembly and door switch first, since they are the parts Maytag identifies for this code and cost far less than a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Power disturbance (~40%)** A temporary fault or voltage spike can confuse the control, and Maytag recommends a simple power reset as the first step.
- **Failed door latch assembly (~30%)** The motor-driven latch mechanism can stick, jam, or fail electrically, preventing the control from reading the correct lock position.
- **Faulty door switch (~15%)** The switch that detects whether the door is closed can fail open or closed, sending incorrect signals to the control board.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~10%)** Wiring or connectors in the latch circuit can become loose, corroded, or damaged by heat, breaking the control circuit.
- **Corroded connectors (~5%)** Moisture or heat exposure can corrode the connector pins at the latch assembly or door switch, creating intermittent contact.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power** at the circuit breaker for 1 full minute to reset the control board.
2. **Restore power** and run the oven through a test cycle to see if the F5 E0 code returns.
3. **Pull the range** away from the wall and remove the rear access panel to reach the oven cavity back.
4. **Inspect the door latch assembly** for mechanical binding, broken parts, or signs of overheating, and check that the latch arm moves smoothly.
5. **Test the door switch** by disconnecting it and checking for continuity changes when the switch actuator is pressed and released.
6. **Examine all wiring** and connectors in the latch circuit for loose pins, corrosion, or heat damage, and reseat or repair as needed.
7. **Replace the failed component** (latch assembly, door switch, or wiring) and test the oven to confirm the code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt appliance wiring or if you cannot safely access the rear of the range. A pro should also handle the repair if the code persists after replacing the latch assembly and door switch, since that points to a control board fault or more complex wiring issue. If your range is still under warranty, contact Maytag or an authorized service center before opening the appliance yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
