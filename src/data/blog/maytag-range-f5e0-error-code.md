## Maytag Range F5E0 Error Code — What It Means

F5E0 on a Maytag range indicates a door latch or door switch circuit fault. The oven control is not seeing the expected state from the door lock and safety interlock system. When this happens, the oven will refuse to run or stop a cycle because the control believes the door latch circuit is not in the proper state. The code points to a problem with the oven door latch assembly, the door switch, or the wiring and connectors that link them to the control board. Because Maytag ranges share the same Whirlpool-built control architecture with KitchenAid models, the fault always involves the door safety circuit rather than a heating element or temperature problem.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but F5E0 almost always comes from the door latch assembly or door switch. Check the latch mechanism for binding and inspect the connectors at the latch and switch before ordering any control.

## Common Causes

- **Oven door latch assembly failure (~50%)** The latch mechanism binds, does not reach the fully locked position, or has worn internal contacts that prevent the control from reading the correct state.
- **Door switch failure (~25%)** The micro-switch mounted on or near the latch assembly has failed or lost contact, so the control cannot confirm the door position.
- **Loose or damaged harness connector (~15%)** The plug at the latch assembly or at the control board has backed out, or terminals inside the connector are corroded or heat-damaged.
- **Broken or heat-damaged wiring (~10%)** Conductors in the harness between the latch, switch, and control have broken strands or melted insulation from prolonged heat exposure.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker (both legs if a 240 V range) and wait a full minute.
2. **Restore power** and watch the range for 1 minute to see whether F5E0 returns, then verify oven operation.
3. **If the code returns**, pull the range forward and remove the rear access panel or top to reach the oven door latch assembly and wiring.
4. **Inspect the door latch** for binding, incomplete travel, broken linkage, or visible damage to the motor or switch mounted on it.
5. **Check every harness connector** at the latch assembly, door switch, and control board for loose fit, corrosion, or heat damage, and reseat each plug firmly.
6. **Operate the door lock** manually (or trigger a self-clean cycle if safe) and listen for the latch motor running and the mechanism reaching both locked and unlocked positions.
7. **Replace the failed component** (latch assembly, door switch, or damaged harness section) if testing confirms a fault, then restore power and verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working around 240 V power, if the range cannot be safely pulled out to access the rear panel, or if you have replaced both the latch assembly and door switch and the F5E0 code still returns. A pro can perform continuity and resistance checks on the latch circuit with the control board unplugged, trace intermittent wiring faults inside the range cabinet, and confirm that the control board itself has not failed. Although the control board is rarely the cause of F5E0, a technician can rule it out without buying the part first.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40–$120 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–$280.
