## Daikin F6 Error Code — What It Means

The F6 code on a Daikin heat pump signals a high-pressure control fault during cooling operation. The outdoor unit has detected unsafe pressure or temperature conditions on the refrigerant side and shuts down to protect the compressor. Daikin's service literature describes this as abnormal high pressure or refrigerant overcharged.

In simple terms, something is preventing the outdoor unit from releasing heat properly, causing refrigerant pressure to climb too high. The system stops itself before damage occurs. This fault typically points to either too much refrigerant in the system or a problem with airflow across the outdoor coil.

## Common Causes

- **Refrigerant overcharge** Daikin explicitly lists overcharged refrigerant as a cause, often after service work when refrigerant was added incorrectly.
- **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil** Leaves, cottonwood, grass clippings, or dust on the condenser coil prevent heat rejection and drive head pressure up.
- **Outdoor fan not running** A failed fan motor, bad capacitor, or control board issue stops airflow and causes the outdoor unit to overheat.
- **Restricted outdoor airflow** Fencing, bushes, recirculation, or improper clearance around the unit trap hot discharge air and raise condensing pressure.
- **Non-condensables in the system** Air or moisture introduced during installation or repair reduces heat transfer and raises system pressure.
- **High-pressure switch or sensor fault** A stuck or failing pressure control or sensor can trigger F6 even when actual pressures are normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the system** at the breaker or disconnect switch and wait three minutes before inspecting the outdoor unit.
2. **Inspect the outdoor coil** for dirt, leaves, debris, or blockage and clean thoroughly with a garden hose from the inside out if accessible (coil cleaner if heavily soiled).
3. **Check outdoor fan operation** by restoring power and entering cooling mode to confirm the fan spins and moves air freely across the coil.
4. **Verify clearance around the unit** is at least 12 inches on all sides and that no fencing, plants, or structures are blocking airflow or causing recirculation.
5. **Call a qualified technician** to check refrigerant charge against the nameplate specification using gauges and to correct any overcharge by recovery if present.
6. **Have the technician inspect** for non-condensables, restrictions, high-pressure switch operation, and control board signals if airflow and charge are correct.
7. **Reset the fault** after repairs by cycling power or using the controller's error-clear function, then run a full cooling cycle to confirm F6 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the outdoor coil is clean, the fan runs normally, and the fault persists. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification, recovery equipment, and gauges to verify charge against factory specifications. Any diagnosis involving pressure switches, control boards, or refrigerant-side restrictions also needs professional tools and training. If the unit is still under warranty, contact a Daikin-authorized service provider to avoid voiding coverage. Do not attempt to add or remove refrigerant yourself.
