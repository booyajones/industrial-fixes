## Frigidaire Washer E10 Error Code — What It Means

E10 on a Frigidaire washer is most commonly a fill problem. The machine is not getting water fast enough or within the expected fill time. Some Frigidaire model families also describe E10 as a water-supply or leak-related fault, so the exact wording can vary by platform and year. Because Frigidaire model families differ, confirm the exact definition against your model's tech sheet or service literature before replacing parts. The code is reported as an incoming water supply problem or not filling with water fast enough in most cases.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the water inlet valve first without checking the inlet screens at the hose connection and valve. Clean the screens and verify both supply valves are fully open before ordering a new valve.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens (~35%)** Sediment and debris from household water accumulate in the mesh screens at the hose connection and valve inlet, restricting flow so the washer cannot fill in time.
- **Water supply valves not fully open (~25%)** If the hot or cold supply valve at the wall is closed or only partly open, flow is too slow to satisfy the control's fill timer.
- **Kinked or crushed inlet hose (~15%)** A hose that is bent, pinched behind the machine, or crushed restricts water flow and triggers the fill timeout.
- **Low household water pressure (~10%)** If your home water pressure is below the minimum required by the washer, fill will be slow even when supply valves and hoses are good.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~10%)** The valve solenoids may not open fully or the valve body may be restricted internally, reducing flow even when supply and screens are clean.
- **Drain hose positioned too low or leak detection (~5%)** On some models where E10 is tied to leak sensing, a drain hose routed too low can siphon water out during fill, or an actual leak at the tub or door area may be detected.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the code against your model.** Check the tech sheet inside the cabinet or your service manual to confirm E10 definition, because some models tie it to leak detection rather than just fill timeout.
2. **Reset power briefly.** Unplug the washer for one minute to clear a transient fault, then plug it back in and start a fill cycle to see if the code returns.
3. **Confirm both supply valves are fully open.** Turn the hot and cold valves at the wall fully counterclockwise and verify water flows when you disconnect the hoses at the machine.
4. **Inspect and clean the inlet screens.** Remove both fill hoses at the back of the washer, pull out the small mesh screens with needle-nose pliers, rinse them under running water, and reinstall if intact or replace if torn.
5. **Check hoses for kinks or blockage.** Straighten any bent sections, verify hoses are not crushed behind the machine, and confirm internal flow by running water through them into a bucket.
6. **Test the water inlet valve.** If supply, screens, and hoses are good but fill is still slow, measure resistance across each valve solenoid coil and replace the valve assembly if it does not open properly or shows no continuity.
7. **Inspect the pressure-sensing system and wiring.** If the machine fills correctly by volume but still flags E10, check the pressure switch or sensor and its hose for blockage, and inspect wiring connectors to the inlet valve and pressure circuit for corrosion or looseness.
8. **Run a diagnostic cycle.** After correction, start a normal wash or run the machine's built-in diagnostics to confirm it completes fill without re-triggering E10.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified that both supply valves are open, screens are clean, hoses are not kinked, and household water pressure is adequate but the washer still will not fill or the E10 code returns. A pro can test the inlet valve solenoids electrically, inspect the pressure-sensing circuit and wiring for intermittent faults, and consult model-specific service literature to rule out leak-detection logic or control-board issues. Also call if you see active water leaks around the tub, door, or hoses that suggest the code is flagging a leak rather than a fill problem.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts (inlet valve if needed), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
