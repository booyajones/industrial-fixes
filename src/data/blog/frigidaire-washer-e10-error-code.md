## Frigidaire Washer E10 Error Code — What It Means

E10 is a water fill time or no-water-inlet fault. The washer control expected water to enter the tub within a set time window, but it did not detect adequate flow or level. In Frigidaire and Electrolux front-load machines, E10 is grouped with E11 and E12 as a no-water or insufficient-water-flow code.

This is not a drain problem. The control is flagging that water supply into the machine is restricted, absent, or that the pressure sensor cannot confirm the tub filled correctly. The code appears when the fill cycle times out before the washer sees the expected water level.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the inlet valve or control board when clogged inlet screens or closed supply taps are actually blocking water. Check and clean the inlet screens at the valve body and verify both hot and cold supply valves are fully open before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens or filters (~40%)** Sediment and mineral deposits collect in the small mesh screens where the hoses attach to the washer valve, starving the fill cycle of water.
- **Water supply valves closed or partially closed (~25%)** If the hot or cold tap behind the washer is not turned fully open, flow is restricted and the washer times out before reaching the target level.
- **Kinked or restricted inlet hoses (~15%)** A hose pinched behind the machine or blocked internally prevents water from reaching the inlet valve.
- **Low incoming water pressure (~10%)** House supply pressure below about 30 psi means the washer cannot fill quickly enough to satisfy the control timer.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~7%)** The valve solenoid coils may fail to open, or the valve body can stick closed mechanically even when power is applied.
- **Pressure sensor or switch problem (~3%)** The sensor that tells the control board the tub has filled may be stuck, disconnected, or reading incorrectly.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The inlet valve is stuck open and must be replaced.<br><strong>No:</strong> The valve closes correctly. Test the pressure sensor and control board logic.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off both hot and cold supply valves at the wall.
2. **Pull the washer forward** enough to access the back panel and the inlet hose connections.
3. **Disconnect both inlet hoses** from the water valve on the back of the washer and inspect the small mesh screens inside each valve port.
4. **Remove and rinse the screens** under running water or use a small brush to clear sediment, or replace them if they are torn or heavily calcified.
5. **Inspect the hoses** for kinks, collapses, or internal blockage, then reconnect them to the valve and hand-tighten the couplings.
6. **Turn both supply valves fully open** and plug the washer back in, then start a fill cycle to confirm water enters the tub promptly.
7. **Run a complete wash cycle** to verify the E10 code does not return and the machine fills and drains normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified full supply pressure (at least 30 psi at the machine), cleaned or replaced the inlet screens, confirmed the hoses are clear, and the E10 code still appears. At that point the inlet valve itself, the pressure sensor, or the main control board likely needs testing with a multimeter and replacement under warranty or by someone familiar with wiring diagrams. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with water connections or if your model requires removing the cabinet to reach the valve or sensor.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-80 in parts if a valve or sensor is needed, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 for a service call and part replacement.
