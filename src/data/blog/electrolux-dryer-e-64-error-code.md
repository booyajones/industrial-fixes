## Electrolux Dryer E64 Error Code — What It Means

The E64 code indicates a heating circuit fault in your Electrolux dryer. The control board has detected that the heating system is not working correctly, either because the element has burned out, shorted to the housing, or because a safety device has opened the circuit. In most field repairs, this code points directly to a failed heating element. The dryer may still tumble clothes but will not produce heat, leaving loads damp at the end of the cycle.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element** The coil inside the heater assembly can burn open or short against the metal casing, which is the most frequently reported cause of E64.
- **Blown thermal fuse** Overheating caused by restricted airflow can trip the one-time thermal fuse on the heater housing, opening the heating circuit permanently until replaced.
- **Clogged lint filter or exhaust vent** Blocked airflow forces the heating element to run hotter than designed, eventually damaging the element or tripping safety cutoffs.
- **Faulty cycling thermostat** The thermostat that regulates heater temperature can fail open or closed, preventing proper operation and triggering the fault code.
- **Damaged wiring or connectors** Heat exposure can melt spade terminals or burn connector housings at the heater assembly, breaking continuity in the heating circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and wait two minutes to make sure no stored voltage remains before opening any panels.
2. **Clean the entire airflow path** by removing and washing the lint filter, vacuuming the lint housing and blower area, and disconnecting the exhaust vent to clear any blockages.
3. **Access the heater assembly** by removing the lower front or rear panel (consult your model's service manual for exact location) and visually inspect the heating element for broken coils, scorch marks, or the element wire touching the metal housing.
4. **Test the heating element with a multimeter** set to resistance mode by removing both wire connectors and placing probes on the element terminals (a good element typically reads in the low tens of ohms, while a failed element reads open or infinite resistance).
5. **Check continuity on the thermal fuse and thermostats** mounted on or near the heater housing by disconnecting each component and testing across its terminals (a healthy fuse or thermostat reads near zero ohms, while a blown fuse reads open).
6. **Inspect all wiring and terminals** for heat damage, melted plastic, or loose connections, and replace any burned connectors or damaged wire segments.
7. **Replace the defective part** (heating element, thermal fuse, or thermostat), reassemble the panels, restore power, and run a timed dry cycle on high heat to confirm the dryer now heats properly and the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

If you are uncomfortable working with household voltage or accessing internal dryer components, call a qualified appliance technician. Also call a professional if you have replaced the heating element, thermal fuse, and thermostats but the E64 code persists, since the fault may lie in the main control board or internal wiring harness that requires diagnostic software and specialized tools to trace.
