## Electrolux Washer E66 Error Code — What It Means

E66 on an Electrolux washer points to a fault in the heating circuit, most commonly a heater relay problem or an overheat-protection event. The code is not consistently documented across all Electrolux washer models, but service training and related dryer documentation link E66 to the thermal limiter or heater control relays. In practice, the machine has detected either an open thermal cutoff, a failed relay that cannot energize the heater, or wiring damage in the heater circuit.

On Electrolux dryers the same code means the thermal limiter has tripped due to overheating, and the washer version follows similar logic. The fault prevents the washer from heating water properly or running heat cycles safely. Check your exact model's tech sheet for confirmation, because E66 may map to slightly different components depending on the washer platform.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heater relay on the control board** The relay that switches power to the heating element is stuck open or heat-damaged, preventing the heater from energizing.
- **Open thermal limiter or thermal cutoff** The safety device in the heater circuit has tripped or failed open due to an overheat condition or age-related failure.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness** Connections between the control board, thermal limiter, and heating element are frayed, pinched, or burnt, breaking the circuit.
- **Faulty electronic control board** The driver circuitry or relay sockets on the board are not switching correctly, even though the relays themselves may be intact.
- **Overheating caused by restricted airflow or venting issues** On some platforms, blocked vents or poor airflow can trip the thermal limiter before the heater completes its cycle.
- **Defective high-limit thermostat** The backup overheat sensor has opened, and on some designs it should be replaced together with the thermal fuse when one blows.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and pull the model and serial number, then locate the tech sheet (usually inside the front panel or on the back) to confirm the exact E66 definition for your platform.
2. **Remove the rear or top access panel** to expose the heater assembly, wiring harness, and control board, depending on your model's layout.
3. **Inspect all wiring** between the control board, thermal limiter, and heating element for pinched, burnt, loose, or frayed conductors, and repair or replace any damaged harness.
4. **Test the thermal limiter (thermal cutoff) for continuity** using a multimeter on the Rx1 scale. If the device is open, replace it and check whether a paired high-limit thermostat also needs replacement.
5. **Check the heater relay outputs** on the control board. If the relays do not close when commanded, or show heat damage around the sockets, replace the control board or relay assembly as your model requires.
6. **Verify normal airflow** and venting (if applicable) by removing any lint buildup or exhaust restrictions that could cause the limiter to trip during a heat cycle.
7. **Reassemble the washer, restore power, and run a full heat cycle** or service diagnostic test to confirm the E66 fault does not return and the heater operates correctly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working with live 120/240 V circuits, if you cannot locate the thermal limiter or relay assembly on your model, or if the fault returns after replacing the thermal cutoff and control board. A pro will have the model-specific service manual, correct replacement relays or safety components, and the tools to run factory diagnostic tests. If the heater element itself is damaged or the fault involves multiple interrelated heating components, professional diagnosis will save time and prevent repeated part swaps.
