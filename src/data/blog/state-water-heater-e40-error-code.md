## State Water Heater E40 Error Code — What It Means

The E40 code on State tankless water heaters indicates an ignition failure or combustion interruption. While State and A. O. Smith do not publish a universal E40 definition in widely available manuals, field experience shows this fault class stems from issues preventing the burner from lighting or staying lit. The control board has locked out the heater to prevent unsafe operation.

The most common root causes are blocked air intake vents, insufficient gas pressure or flow, dirty flame sensing components, or restricted water flow through a clogged inlet screen. The heater needs proper combustion air, adequate gas supply, and sufficient water flow to operate safely. When any of these conditions fail, the unit shuts down and displays the fault.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or vent termination** Lint, debris, leaves, or insect nests obstruct the combustion air intake screens or exterior vent pipe, starving the burner of oxygen.
- **Low gas pressure or insufficient gas flow** Undersized gas line, partially closed valve, empty propane tank, or regulator fault prevents adequate fuel from reaching the burner.
- **Dirty or fouled flame rod** Carbon buildup or mineral deposits on the flame sensing rod prevent it from detecting the flame, triggering a false lockout.
- **Clogged cold water inlet filter** Sediment or debris in the inlet screen restricts flow below the minimum threshold needed for ignition, and the unit refuses to fire.
- **Failed igniter or ignition circuit** A cracked ceramic igniter, loose wire, or faulty control board output stops spark generation and prevents the burner from lighting.
- **Venting defect or backdraft condition** Improper vent length, too many elbows, or wind-induced backdraft prevents proper exhaust flow and trips the combustion safety interlock.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and close the gas shutoff valve before beginning any inspection or cleaning work.
2. **Inspect the air intake screens** on the front or side of the heater and the exterior vent termination cap for blockage, then remove any lint, leaves, or debris by hand or with a soft brush.
3. **Remove and clean the cold water inlet filter screen** by closing the cold water isolation valve, unscrewing the inlet union, pulling the screen, rinsing it under running water, and reinstalling it.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve is fully open, checking propane tank level if applicable, and observing whether other gas appliances are operating normally to rule out low line pressure.
5. **Examine the flame rod and igniter** by removing the burner access cover and checking for carbon deposits, cracks, or misalignment, then gently clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper or a clean cloth.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then attempt to run a hot water tap and observe whether the unit ignites. If the error returns immediately, note any unusual sounds or smells and proceed to professional diagnostics.
7. **Reset the unit** by cycling power off for thirty seconds if the fault does not clear automatically after correcting airflow or water flow issues, then test hot water draw again.

## When to Call a Pro

Contact a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E40 code persists after you have cleared blockages, cleaned filters, and verified gas supply. Persistent ignition faults may require combustion analyzer readings, gas valve testing, or control board diagnostics that demand specialized tools and training. Any work involving gas line modifications, pressure testing, or control board replacement should be performed only by qualified personnel to maintain warranty coverage and meet local code requirements.
