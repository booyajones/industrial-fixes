## State Water Heater E115 Error Code — What It Means

State Water Heaters does not publish an official E115 definition in widely available manufacturer documentation, so the exact meaning depends on your specific model. The most likely interpretation, based on third-party State and A.O. Smith tankless references, is that E115 indicates an ignition lockout or combustion-air proving fault. This means the unit detected a problem with gas ignition, airflow verification, or the flame-sensing circuit and shut down to protect you and the equipment.

Before you proceed, locate the rating plate on your unit and scan the QR code to download the correct manual. That manual will confirm the E115 definition for your model and list manufacturer-approved troubleshooting steps. In the meantime, the repair workflow below addresses the most common causes of ignition and combustion-air faults across State tankless platforms.

## Common Causes

- **No gas or low gas pressure** The unit cannot ignite if the gas supply valve is closed, the meter is off, or pressure is too low for the burner.
- **Blocked air intake or vent termination** Leaves, snow, debris, or a dislodged vent pipe restrict combustion air or exhaust flow, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Clogged inlet water filter** A dirty filter reduces flow below the minimum threshold needed for ignition, causing the unit to fault out.
- **Scale buildup in the heat exchanger** Mineral deposits restrict water passages and overheat sensors, leading to ignition failures or flame-sensing errors.
- **Faulty flame sensor or igniter** A corroded flame rod or worn igniter electrode prevents the control board from confirming ignition, so the unit locks out.
- **Control board or wiring fault** Loose harness connectors, moisture intrusion, or a failed board can generate false lockout codes even when gas and air are correct.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Download your model-specific manual** by scanning the QR code on the rating plate or visiting the State Water Heaters support site with your full model number.
2. **Confirm gas supply** by checking that the manual gas shutoff valve at the unit is fully open, the meter valve is on, and other gas appliances in the home are operating.
3. **Inspect air intake and vent piping** for blockages, disconnected joints, or debris at the outdoor termination, and clear any obstructions.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** at the cold-water connection, rinsing away sediment and scale under running water.
5. **Check for scale in the heat exchanger** by listening for popping or kettling noises during heating, and perform a vinegar descale flush if the unit has hard water history.
6. **Reset the unit** by turning off power at the breaker or disconnect for 30 seconds, then restoring power and attempting a hot-water draw.
7. **If E115 returns immediately**, consult the manual fault table and use a multimeter to test the flame sensor, igniter continuity, and gas-valve coil resistance per the manufacturer specifications before replacing any components.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the E115 code returns after you have verified gas supply, cleared intake and vent obstructions, cleaned the filter, and completed a power reset. Persistent ignition faults require component-level diagnosis with a multimeter, gas-pressure gauge, and combustion analyzer. Any work involving gas-valve replacement, control-board programming, or vent-system modification must be performed by a qualified technician to meet local codes and preserve your warranty. If you smell gas at any point, shut off the gas supply immediately, evacuate the area, and call your utility or emergency services.
