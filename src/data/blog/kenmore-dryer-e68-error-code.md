## Kenmore Dryer E68 Error Code — What It Means

The E68 error code on Kenmore dryers typically indicates a communication problem between the main control board and one of the temperature sensors or thermistors, or it may point to a wiring fault. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cabinet for your specific dryer. In most cases, the control board is not receiving expected signals from a temperature sensor, or the wiring between components has been damaged or disconnected.

Because Kenmore sources dryers from multiple manufacturers, the E68 code is not always defined identically across all models. If your manual does not list E68, check for recent software updates or contact Kenmore support with your full model number. The code usually stops the dryer from running to prevent overheating or damage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the main control board when the real problem is a loose connector or a failed thermistor. Inspect all wiring harness plugs and test the thermistor with a multimeter for continuity before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring harness connection (~40%)** Vibration over time can loosen the push-on connectors between the control board and the thermistors or temperature sensors, causing intermittent signals.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~30%)** The thermistor may have drifted out of spec or developed an open circuit, preventing the control board from reading drum or exhaust temperature.
- **Damaged wiring or harness (~15%)** Wires near the blower wheel or drum can be pinched, melted by heat, or cut by sheet-metal edges, breaking the signal path.
- **Main control board communication fault (~10%)** The control board itself may have a failed trace, cold solder joint, or internal microcontroller error that prevents it from reading sensor data correctly.
- **Faulty user interface board (~5%)** In some models the user interface board relays sensor data, and a failure there can cause the main board to log a communication error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and shut off the gas supply if applicable.
2. **Remove the top panel or rear access panel** according to your model (usually two screws at the back and lift, or remove rear screws for cabinet access).
3. **Locate the main control board** (often behind the console or on the front bulkhead) and the thermistor or temperature sensor (typically clamped to the blower housing or exhaust duct).
4. **Inspect every wiring harness connector** leading to and from the control board, the thermistor, and any inline sensors. Press each plug firmly into its socket and check pins for corrosion or bent contacts.
5. **Test the thermistor with a multimeter** set to resistance (ohms). Disconnect the sensor, measure across its two terminals, and compare the reading to the temperature-resistance chart in your service manual. At room temperature most thermistors read between 10 and 50 kOhms, but consult your model's table for the exact value.
6. **Trace the wiring harness** from the thermistor to the control board, looking for pinched, burned, or frayed insulation. Pay special attention to wire routes near the drum seal, blower wheel, and any sharp metal edges.
7. **Reassemble the panels**, plug in the dryer, and run a test cycle. If the E68 error persists and all wiring and the thermistor test good, replace the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if the wiring diagram is unclear, or if you have tested all connectors and sensors without finding a fault. A pro has advanced diagnostic tools to measure signal voltages and can confirm whether the control board or a less common component is at fault. Also call if the dryer uses gas and you smell gas at any time, or if repeated part swaps have not cleared the code.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
