## Frigidaire F94 Error Code — What It Means

The F94 code on Frigidaire ranges and ovens signals a door lock system problem. The oven believes the door lock mechanism is not operating correctly or is not reporting the expected lock state. Frigidaire groups F94 with codes F90 through F93 under the general category of door lock issues.

This fault often appears during or after a self-clean cycle when the lock motor, latch, or position switch fails to respond as expected. The oven control will not allow normal operation until the lock system reports correctly. Frigidaire's official guidance is to reset power to the unit for up to 30 seconds. If the code remains after that reset, the manufacturer directs owners to service support rather than user-level troubleshooting.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when F94 persists, but the door lock assembly itself is almost always the culprit. Test the lock motor, latch movement, and position switch before condemning the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or sticking door lock motor/actuator (~45%)** The lock motor binds, burns out, or stalls so the control cannot drive the latch to the locked or unlocked position.
- **Binding latch mechanism or gears (~25%)** Heat, grease, or baked-on residue in the latch slot or gear train prevents free movement and the lock cannot complete its cycle.
- **Failed lock position switch (~15%)** The microswitch that tells the control whether the door is locked or unlocked is out of adjustment or has failed open or closed.
- **Wiring damage or loose connections (~10%)** A broken wire, corroded terminal, or loose connector between the lock assembly and the main control interrupts signals or power.
- **Control board fault (~5%)** The electronic oven control fails to send the correct drive signal or misreads feedback from an otherwise healthy lock assembly.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault was transient or heat-related. Monitor for recurrence. If F94 returns, proceed to lock hardware checks.<br><strong>No:</strong> The lock system has a persistent fault. Continue diagnosis of the lock motor, latch, switch, and wiring.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you hear the lock motor running or clicking when you start a self-clean cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor has power but the latch may be binding or the position switch may not be reporting. </details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the door aligned squarely and does it close without force or binding?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Door alignment is not the issue. Focus on the lock assembly, switch, and wiring inside the door frame.<br><strong>No:</strong> Adjust door hinges or check for obstructions. A misaligned door can prevent the latch from seating and trigger F94.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and wait 30 seconds, then restore power to reset the control.
2. **Allow the oven to cool** completely if the code appeared during or after a self-clean cycle, as the lock may remain engaged until cool.
3. **Inspect the door and latch area** for racks, debris, or baked-on residue that could block closure or latch movement.
4. **Access the lock assembly** by removing the oven door (consult your model's service manual) and the inner door panel or control console as needed.
5. **Test lock motor operation** by starting a self-clean cycle and listening for motor sound, or use a multimeter to check for voltage at the motor terminals during lock commands.
6. **Check the latch mechanism** for binding, broken gears, or grease buildup, and verify the latch moves freely by hand when power is off.
7. **Inspect wiring and connectors** between the lock assembly and the main control for breaks, corrosion, or loose pins, and repair or replace any damaged harness sections.
8. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor, latch, or position switch is faulty and the wiring and control output test good.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are not comfortable working inside the oven door or control panel, if wiring diagnosis requires a multimeter and schematic, or if the lock assembly and wiring both test good but the code persists (indicating a possible control board fault). High-voltage circuits are present even when the oven is cool, so always disconnect power before opening any panels. A professional will have the service manual, the correct replacement parts for your exact model, and the tools to test control board outputs safely. If your range is still under warranty, contact Frigidaire or an authorized service center before attempting any repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
