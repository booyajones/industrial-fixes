## Frigidaire F94 Error Code — What It Means

The F94 code indicates a door lock issue on your Frigidaire electric range. The oven's door lock system is not operating correctly, or the control board is not receiving the expected lock or unlock feedback from the door latch assembly. Frigidaire groups F90 through F94 together as a door lock fault family, so F94 does not indicate a unique sub-fault. It means the electronic control has detected a problem with the lock motor, the lock position switch, or the wiring between them.

The code can appear after a self-clean cycle, during normal use if the lock mechanism jams, or if the lock motor or feedback switch fails. The oven will not allow you to use the bake or broil functions while the door is locked or while the control believes the lock is stuck.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first. Always test the door lock motor resistance (should read about 2,000 ohms) and check wiring continuity before replacing the control.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock motor or actuator (~50%)** The lock motor inside the door lock assembly burns out, measures open or shorted instead of about 2,000 ohms, and cannot move the latch.
- **Bad door lock position switch (~25%)** The feedback switch that tells the control board whether the door is locked or unlocked fails and sends incorrect or missing signals.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness (~15%)** The wires between the control board and the lock assembly are loose, corroded, or damaged so the control cannot command or sense the lock.
- **Defective electronic control board (~10%)** The main oven control board has a failed relay or processor that incorrectly reads or sends lock signals even when the lock hardware is sound.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** to the range by unplugging it or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker for at least 30 seconds, then restore power to reset the control.
2. **Try the unlock command** by pressing and holding the OFF (or CANCEL) button for six seconds to cancel the oven lock feature and allow the control to unlock the door.
3. **Inspect the latch area** by opening the oven door (if possible) and looking for debris, broken pivot arms, or misalignment in the lock mechanism that could prevent smooth travel.
4. **Check the wiring harness** by pulling the range forward, removing the rear panel, and inspecting the connectors between the control board and the door lock assembly for loose, corroded, or damaged terminals.
5. **Test the lock motor resistance** by disconnecting the lock assembly plug and measuring across the motor terminals with a multimeter set to ohms. You should see about 2,000 ohms. An open-line (OL) or near-zero reading means the motor is bad.
6. **Verify wire continuity** by testing end-to-end resistance through the brown and white wires in the harness. A good wire reads near 0 ohms. An open-line (OL) indicates a break, and any reading between disconnected ends indicates a short that must be repaired.
7. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor or feedback switch tests bad and wiring is intact. If the new lock assembly still triggers F94, replace the electronic oven control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt wiring or removing the range rear panel. A technician should also be called if you have replaced the door lock assembly and verified wiring continuity but the F94 code returns, since diagnosing a faulty control board requires specialized tools and experience. If the oven door is physically jammed shut and you cannot safely access the lock mechanism, a service call will prevent damage to the door or frame.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80–150 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $200–350.
