## Electrolux Dryer E36 Error Code — What It Means

The E36 error code on an Electrolux dryer typically signals a communication problem between the main control board and one of the temperature sensors or other electronic components. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for precise troubleshooting steps.

This fault prevents the dryer from running because the control system cannot verify that temperature monitoring is working properly. The dryer will not start or will stop mid-cycle and display the code on the control panel.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when the actual problem is a loose connector or a failed thermistor. Check all wiring connections and test the thermistor resistance with a multimeter before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~40%)** Vibration over time can loosen the plug between the control board and the thermistor or heater assembly, breaking the signal path.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~30%)** The sensor that monitors dryer temperature can fail open or short, sending no signal or an out-of-range signal to the control board.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires can be pinched, chafed, or burned where they pass through sheet metal or near the heater box, causing intermittent faults.
- **Main control board failure (~10%)** The control board itself can develop a fault in the circuit that reads sensor inputs, though this is less common than wiring issues.
- **Moisture or lint contamination (~5%)** Lint or moisture can accumulate on connectors or circuit boards, creating false readings or shorts that trigger the error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker at the panel.
2. **Remove the top or front panel** depending on your model to access the control board and internal wiring (consult your service manual for disassembly steps).
3. **Inspect all wire harness connectors** at the control board, the thermistor (usually mounted on the blower housing or heater box), and any inline connectors along the harness for corrosion, bent pins, or loose fit.
4. **Reseat each connector** by pulling it apart, checking for debris or moisture, and pressing it back together until you hear or feel a click.
5. **Test the thermistor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance with a multimeter at room temperature (consult your model's table for expected values, typically several thousand ohms), and replace it if the reading is open or shorted.
6. **Check the wiring harness** for continuity between the control board and the thermistor, and inspect for pinched or burned wires along the entire length.
7. **Reassemble the dryer** and restore power, then run a test cycle to confirm the error is cleared and the dryer operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if the wiring harness is extensively damaged and requires splicing or soldering, or if you have tested the thermistor and connectors but the error remains and you suspect the control board. A technician can perform a full system diagnostic with specialized tools and safely handle high-voltage components. Also call a pro if the dryer uses 240-volt power and you are unsure how to safely disconnect and test the circuits.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
