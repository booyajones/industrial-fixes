## State Water Heater E12 Error Code — What It Means

The E12 error code on a State tankless water heater indicates flame loss or flame failure during operation. The unit successfully lights and detects flame, but then the flame goes out while the heater is running. This is part of the safety system that monitors combustion. State water heaters share architecture with A.O. Smith tankless units, and the E12 code follows the same flame-loss pattern seen across multiple tankless brands. The code triggers when the flame sensor stops detecting flame, shutting down the unit to prevent unsafe conditions.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or contaminated flame sensor** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents accurate flame detection and causes the unit to lose flame proof during operation.
- **Insufficient gas supply or low inlet pressure** Gas pressure that drops below the minimum required value under static, dynamic, or maximum load conditions starves the burner and causes flame loss.
- **Blocked air intake or restricted combustion air** Debris, screens clogged with lint or dirt, or intake termination blockage prevents proper combustion air from reaching the burner.
- **Improper venting or exhaust recirculation** Incorrect vent configuration, damaged concentric vent O-rings, or intake pulling in exhaust gases disrupts combustion and causes flame failure.
- **Burner or combustion chamber contamination** Carbon deposits, debris, or wear in the burner assembly or combustion path interfere with stable flame and proper ignition.
- **Gas line sizing or appliance fuel type mismatch** Undersized gas piping or incorrect conversion between natural gas and propane prevents adequate fuel delivery to maintain flame.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the exact code definition** in your unit's service manual or on the interior label, because State and A.O. Smith coding is not fully standardized across all models.
2. **Inspect all venting and combustion air paths** by checking the intake and exhaust terminations for blockage, debris, bird nests, or snow and ice, then confirming vent materials and concentric vent O-rings are intact and correctly installed.
3. **Test gas supply pressure** using a manometer at the unit's inlet with no call for heat (static), while firing (dynamic), and while other gas appliances run simultaneously (maximum dynamic), confirming pressure stays above the minimum listed on your model's rating plate under all three conditions.
4. **Clean the flame sensor and flame rod** by carefully removing the component and wiping it with a clean, dry cloth or fine non-abrasive pad to remove carbon and corrosion, then inspect for physical damage or heavy pitting.
5. **Inspect and clean the burner assembly** by accessing the combustion chamber per your service manual, checking for carbon buildup or debris on burner ports and in the combustion path, and replacing any gaskets or seals disturbed during access.
6. **Check gas shutoff valves and supply system** by confirming all manual shutoff valves upstream are fully open, that any seismic shutoff devices have not tripped, and that gas line sizing matches the requirements in your installation manual.
7. **Reset the unit and monitor** by cycling power or following the reset procedure in your manual, then running hot water for several minutes and watching for code recurrence or flame behavior through the viewing window if available.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if gas pressure testing shows values below specification, if the code returns after cleaning the flame sensor and venting, or if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances. Tankless water heater combustion diagnostics require specific tools including a manometer for gas pressure measurement and often a combustion analyzer for complete troubleshooting. A qualified service technician can perform component-level testing, handle gas line modifications if sizing is inadequate, and safely replace failed parts while maintaining manufacturer warranty coverage.
