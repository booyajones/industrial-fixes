## Weil-McLain Boiler A119 Error — What It Means

A119 is not confirmed as a standard fault code in Weil-McLain's published documentation. The exact meaning depends on your specific boiler model, series, and the control board installed on your unit. Weil-McLain boilers use different control platforms across their product lines, and each has its own fault-code table. Without the model number, CP barcode, and control manual, A119 cannot be reliably decoded.

Weil-McLain directs users to identify the exact model and serial number (found on the barcode sticker on the jacket or inside the jacket on models such as Ultra) and to consult the installation and service manual for that specific boiler. If your display shows A119, write down the full model name and control board type, then check the fault-code section of your owner's manual or contact a licensed heating contractor who can access the manufacturer's technical bulletins for your appliance.

## Common Causes

- **Unrecognized or custom control code (~40%)** A119 may be specific to a third-party or OEM control board not listed in Weil-McLain's general documentation, requiring the control manufacturer's manual to decode.
- **Model-specific fault (~30%)** Certain Weil-McLain series use proprietary control platforms with unique fault codes that appear only in the service manual for that series.
- **Display or communication error (~20%)** The control may be showing a garbled or incomplete code due to a wiring fault, low voltage, or a failing display module.
- **Outdated or replaced control (~10%)** If the control board has been replaced or upgraded in the field, the code table in your original manual may no longer apply.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify the exact boiler model and control type.** Find the CP number or WM serial barcode on the jacket or inside the jacket panel.
2. **Locate the installation and service manual** for your boiler model and look up A119 in the fault-code section. If the manual does not list A119, note the control board manufacturer and model printed on the board itself.
3. **Document any additional codes or fault history** displayed on the control. Many controls store multiple faults in a log that can help narrow down the issue.
4. **Do not reset the boiler repeatedly** if it is locked out. Repeated resets can mask the underlying fault and may cause further damage or unsafe operation.
5. **Call a licensed heating contractor** with your model number, CP/serial number, and the A119 code. The technician will use the correct service manual and diagnostic tools to decode the fault and identify the failed part.
6. **Provide the failed part description and part number** to the contractor for accurate ordering. Weil-McLain's warranty and parts-lookup process requires this information.
7. **Allow the technician to test and replace the identified component** and verify proper boiler operation before returning the system to service.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating professional immediately if your Weil-McLain boiler displays A119 and you cannot find that code in your manual. Boilers involve gas, high-voltage ignition, and safety controls that require proper diagnostics and code compliance. A technician will use the correct service documentation for your model and control platform to decode the fault, test the affected circuit, and replace the failed component safely. Do not attempt to bypass safety limits or replace parts by guessing. Weil-McLain's warranty and parts-lookup systems depend on accurate model identification and failed-part documentation, so a qualified contractor is the fastest and safest route to a reliable repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
