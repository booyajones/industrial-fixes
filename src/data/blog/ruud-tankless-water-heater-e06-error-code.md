## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E06 Error — What It Means

The E06 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem related to flame detection or ignition. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for the precise definition. In many cases, the unit has attempted to ignite but failed to establish or maintain a stable flame, or the flame sensor cannot confirm combustion is occurring.

Because tankless water heaters rely on precise gas flow, proper venting, and clean components to ignite reliably, an E06 can stem from gas delivery issues, a dirty flame rod, venting blockages, or faulty ignition components. The heater's control board monitors the flame sensor signal and will lock out if it does not detect the expected voltage during a burn cycle.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the igniter or gas valve first, when a dirty flame rod is the real culprit. Inspect and clean the flame rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty flame rod sensor (~40%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame rod prevent it from conducting current and confirming ignition, causing the unit to shut down and display the error.
- **Insufficient gas pressure or supply (~25%)** Low gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an empty propane tank starves the burner and prevents reliable ignition.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~15%)** Ice, bird nests, or lint in the intake or exhaust pipes disrupt combustion air and flue-gas flow, leading to flame-detection failures.
- **Failed igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked or fouled igniter cannot produce a strong spark, preventing the gas from lighting.
- **Faulty gas valve (~7%)** A stuck or malfunctioning gas valve may not open fully or modulate correctly, resulting in no flame or an unstable flame.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~3%)** Loose connections, corroded terminals, or a defective control board can interrupt the flame-detection circuit or ignition sequence.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power to the unit at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve at the water heater.
2. Remove the front service panel using a screwdriver to access the burner chamber and flame sensor.
3. Locate the flame rod, a thin metal probe near the burner assembly, and inspect it for carbon buildup or white mineral deposits.
4. Clean the flame rod gently with fine-grit sandpaper (220-grit or finer) or a soft cloth, wiping from base to tip until the metal is bright and smooth.
5. Inspect the igniter electrode for cracks or heavy fouling and clean it carefully with a dry brush if needed.
6. Check all wire connections to the flame rod, igniter, and gas valve for tightness and signs of corrosion, and reseat any loose connectors.
7. Reinstall the service panel, open the gas valve, restore power, and run a hot-water tap to initiate a call for heat and observe whether the unit ignites without error.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if cleaning the flame rod does not resolve the error, or if you smell gas at any time. Tankless water heater repairs require knowledge of combustion systems, gas pressure testing, and proper venting standards. A technician can measure inlet gas pressure, test the gas valve electronically, verify flame-sensor microamp readings, and inspect the heat exchanger for scale or blockages that may affect flame stability. Because natural gas and propane are hazardous, and incorrect repairs can lead to carbon monoxide hazards or fire, professional service is the safest choice for persistent ignition faults.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
