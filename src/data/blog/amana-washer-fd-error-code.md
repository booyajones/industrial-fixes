## Amana Washer Fd Error Code — What It Means

Amana does not publish "Fd" as a standard error code in current documentation. If your display shows "Fd," it may be a misread or model-specific variant. The most common Amana fill-related fault is F8 E1 or LO FL, which means the control is not detecting the correct amount of incoming water during the fill cycle. The washer attempts to fill but does not sense adequate flow or pressure within the expected time, so it stops and displays the error.

This code almost always points to a restriction in the incoming water path or an installation problem that lets water drain out as fast as it fills. It does not usually indicate an internal machine failure. The most frequent real-world cause is simply that one or both supply valves are not fully open or the inlet hose screens are clogged with sediment.

## Common Causes

- **Supply valves closed or partially open** One or both hot and cold water faucets behind the washer are not turned fully on, starving the machine of flow.
- **Kinked or collapsed inlet hose** The rubber fill hoses are bent, twisted, or pinched between the wall and the machine, blocking water.
- **Clogged inlet screens** Fine mesh filters inside the hose connections at the back of the washer are packed with sediment, rust, or scale.
- **Drain hose siphoning water out** The drain hose is pushed too far into the standpipe or sealed airtight, allowing gravity to pull water out as it fills.
- **Failed water inlet valve** The solenoid valve that opens to let water in is stuck closed or electrically faulty, even when commanded to open.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify both supply valves are fully open.** Turn the hot and cold shutoff faucets counterclockwise until they stop, then try a wash cycle to see if the error clears.
2. **Inspect the inlet hoses for kinks or damage.** Pull the washer forward and look behind it. Straighten any bends and replace any hose that is cracked, bulging, or collapsed.
3. **Test hose flow at a sink.** Disconnect both hoses from the back of the washer, hold each over a bucket or sink, and turn on the supply valve. Strong flow means the hose and house supply are fine. Low flow means the hose or house plumbing is restricted.
4. **Clean the inlet screens.** With hoses removed, look into the threaded ports on the back of the washer. You will see small plastic or metal screens. Pull them out gently with needle-nose pliers, rinse under water, and scrub with a toothbrush. Reinstall and reconnect hoses.
5. **Check drain hose installation.** The drain hose should not extend more than 4.5 inches into the standpipe or laundry tub. The top of the standpipe must be at least 39 inches above the floor. Leave an air gap so the hose is not sealed tight into the pipe.
6. **Run a test cycle.** Reconnect everything, push the washer back into place, and start a normal wash. If water flows normally and the code does not return, the repair is complete.
7. **Replace the water inlet valve if flow path is clear.** If valves are open, hoses are clean, screens are clear, and the drain is correct but the error persists, the inlet valve assembly on the back of the washer is likely faulty and must be replaced.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have confirmed both supply valves are fully open, hoses and screens are clean, the drain is installed correctly, and the error still appears every cycle. A persistent fill fault after those checks usually means a failed inlet valve or a control-board issue that requires diagnosis with a multimeter and part-specific testing. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working behind the washer, if water supply shutoffs are stuck or leaking, or if your model displays a different F# E# code that you cannot identify in your owner's manual.
