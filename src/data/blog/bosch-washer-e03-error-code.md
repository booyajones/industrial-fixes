## Bosch Washer E03 Error Code — What It Means

The E03 error code on many Bosch washers indicates a door-lock fault. The machine has detected that the door is not closing properly, the lock mechanism has failed to engage, or the control board cannot confirm a secure lock. Because the washer will not start a cycle until the door is safely latched, this code halts operation to prevent leaks and protect the user.

The exact meaning of E03 can vary slightly across Bosch washer models and markets, so always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the service panel for confirmation. In general, the fault points to the door interlock system rather than the motor or drain circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, assuming electronics are to blame. Test the door-lock continuity with a multimeter and inspect the striker before spending money on a board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty door-lock assembly (~50%)** The electromechanical lock that holds the door shut during a cycle wears out or the solenoid coil inside fails, preventing the control from confirming a closed door.
- **Broken or misaligned door striker (~25%)** The plastic latch catch on the door frame cracks, bends, or shifts so the lock cannot engage fully.
- **Wiring harness or connector corrosion (~15%)** Moisture enters the plug between the door lock and the control board, creating high resistance or an open circuit.
- **Control-board relay failure (~10%)** The relay that powers the door lock on the main control board burns out or sticks, though this is less common than a lock failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply at the shut-off valves behind the machine.
2. **Open the door** and inspect the plastic striker on the door frame for visible cracks, chips, or looseness. Wiggle it gently to confirm it is seated properly.
3. **Remove the top panel or front service panel** (consult your model's service manual for screw locations) to access the door-lock assembly mounted on the front of the tub.
4. **Disconnect the wiring harness** from the lock assembly and use a multimeter set to ohms to test continuity across the lock terminals. Compare readings to the values shown on the wiring diagram inside the cabinet.
5. **Replace the door-lock assembly** if continuity is out of spec or if the lock does not click when 120 V AC is applied during a test cycle (a pro can perform a live voltage test).
6. **Inspect all connectors and wiring** for corrosion, burnt pins, or moisture. Clean contacts with electrical contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
7. **Reassemble the panels**, plug in the washer, turn on the water, and run a rinse-and-spin cycle to verify the door locks and the error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V AC circuits or if the error persists after replacing both the lock and the striker. A technician can perform live voltage checks on the control board's relay output, trace intermittent wiring faults inside the door hinge, and confirm that the main board is sending the correct lock signal. If the board itself has failed, a pro can also reflash firmware or replace the board with the correct software version for your model year.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
