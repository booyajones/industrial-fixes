## State Water Heater E167 Error Code — What It Means

State Water Heaters does not publish a universal E167 error code definition across all product lines. The exact meaning of E167 depends on your specific State model and control board revision. State directs users to retrieve model-specific manuals and troubleshooting guides using the QR code on the unit's rating plate, because error codes are documented per product family rather than as a single cross-model table.

Before attempting any repair, locate your unit's model number on the rating plate and scan the QR code or visit State's support site to download the correct manual. This step is necessary because the sensors, thresholds, and component tests for E167 will differ by series. Once you have the manual, cross-reference E167 in the fault code table to identify the affected sensor or system, then proceed with the troubleshooting steps outlined for that specific fault.

## Common Causes

- **Model-specific code definition** State publishes error codes by product line, so E167 may indicate different sensors or failures depending on your exact water heater model and control platform.
- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Lint, debris, or ice on the intake screen or a kinked exhaust pipe can trigger sensor faults on tankless units.
- **Low water flow or closed fixture** Insufficient flow through the heat exchanger prevents proper operation and can set off temperature or flow sensor codes.
- **Failed sensor or wiring fault** A disconnected, corroded, or out-of-range sensor reading will log a fault code and halt ignition or heating.
- **Gas supply interruption** Closed manual valve, empty propane tank, or low inlet pressure can cause ignition-related codes to appear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate the rating plate** on your State water heater and note the complete model number and serial number.
2. **Scan the QR code** on the rating plate or visit State's support site to download the installation and service manual for your exact model.
3. **Look up E167** in the fault code table in your model's manual to identify the specific component or system the code refers to.
4. **Inspect air intake and exhaust** for blockages, remove any debris from the intake screen, and confirm the exhaust pipe is clear and properly sloped.
5. **Verify water flow** by opening a hot fixture fully and confirming at least the minimum flow rate specified in your manual (typically around 0.5 gallons per minute for tankless units).
6. **Check gas supply** by confirming the manual valve is open, the tank has fuel, and inlet pressure meets the spec listed in your manual.
7. **Reset the unit** by cycling power off for thirty seconds, then restore power and observe whether E167 returns or clears after normal operation resumes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot locate your model's manual, if E167 returns after clearing external blockages and resetting the unit, or if the manual directs you to test sensor resistance or gas pressures that require a multimeter or manometer. Any work involving gas connections, control board replacement, or heat exchanger inspection should be performed by a qualified technician to maintain warranty coverage and make sure safe operation.
