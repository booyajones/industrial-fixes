## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E85 Error — What It Means

The E85 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a control board communication issue or a sensor fault. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the unit for the precise definition. In general, this code appears when the main control board cannot read signals from one or more sensors, when there is a wiring problem, or when the board itself has failed.

The error may prevent the heater from firing or cause it to shut down mid-cycle. Because tankless heaters rely on multiple sensors to regulate temperature and flow, a broken wire, corroded connector, or failed sensor can all trigger this fault. In some cases the control board may need replacement, but many E85 codes are resolved by reseating connections or replacing an individual sensor.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the control board immediately, but a loose or corroded wire connector is often the real culprit. Inspect and clean all sensor plugs and harness connections before buying a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can cause connectors at temperature and flow sensors to lose contact or develop corrosion, interrupting the signal to the control board.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~25%)** Inlet, outlet, or heat-exchanger temperature sensors can fail over time, sending no signal or an out-of-range reading that the board interprets as a fault.
- **Faulty control board (~20%)** The main printed circuit board may develop cracked solder joints, failed components, or moisture damage that prevents it from reading sensor inputs correctly.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~12%)** Rodent damage, pinched wires during installation, or heat exposure can break conductors in the low-voltage harness between sensors and the board.
- **Power supply issue (~8%)** Low or fluctuating line voltage, a weak transformer, or a loose AC connection can cause the control board to malfunction and report sensor errors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and shut the gas valve to the off position for safety.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the tankless heater by loosening the screws at the bottom and lifting the panel away.
3. **Inspect every wire connector** at the control board and at each sensor (inlet, outlet, flame rod, flow sensor) for loose fit, bent pins, or green corrosion.
4. **Disconnect and reconnect** each sensor plug firmly, and spray contact cleaner into corroded connectors if needed.
5. **Check for damaged wires** along the harness, looking for pinched insulation, rodent chew marks, or burn marks near the heat exchanger.
6. **Restore power** and reset the unit by pressing the reset button or cycling the breaker, then observe whether the E85 code returns.
7. **Test sensor resistance** with a multimeter if the code persists, comparing readings to the values in your model's service manual or wiring diagram, and replace any sensor that reads open or out of range.
8. **Replace the control board** only after confirming all sensors and wiring are intact and the error remains after sensor replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the wiring diagram is difficult to interpret, or if the error persists after you have cleaned connections and tested sensors. A pro can perform voltage and resistance checks across the entire harness, diagnose intermittent faults with specialized meters, and safely handle any gas-line work if a repair requires moving the unit or accessing internal components. Professional service is also recommended if the control board needs replacement and you are not confident soldering or programming firmware settings that some models require after a board swap.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-400.
