## Electrolux Washer E62 Error Code — What It Means

The E62 error code on Electrolux and Frigidaire-family washers indicates a heating or overheat fault in the wash-water temperature circuit. Electrolux groups E60, E61, and E62 together as general heating problems. Repair sources describe E62 specifically as water overheating during the wash cycle, pointing to the temperature sensor, heater, wiring, or control board as the fault path.

This is not a drain error. The machine has detected that the wash water temperature is outside the expected range, either because the heater is staying on too long, the temperature sensor (NTC thermistor) is reading incorrectly, or the control board is not managing the heater properly. In some cases, improper fill or circulation can contribute to abnormal heating behavior, so water inlet or pressure-sensing issues may also trigger this code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but a simple resistance check of the NTC sensor and heater element with a multimeter will identify the failed component and save hundreds of dollars.

## Common Causes

- **Defective NTC temperature sensor (~40%)** The thermistor on or near the heater assembly sends incorrect temperature readings to the control board, causing the heater to overheat the water or stay on too long.
- **Faulty wash heater element (~25%)** The heating element itself has shorted, failed open, or developed internal resistance problems that prevent proper heating control.
- **Wiring or connector issues (~15%)** Loose, corroded, or burned terminals between the heater, sensor, and control board interrupt the temperature feedback loop or heater control signal.
- **Control board or relay failure (~10%)** The main control board or heater relay stays energized too long, overheating the water even when the sensor reports the correct temperature.
- **Water inlet valve or pressure sensor fault (~10%)** Improper fill level or pressure-sensing errors can contribute to abnormal heating behavior by keeping the heater on while water flow is restricted.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the washer or switching off the breaker for at least 60 seconds, then restore power and retest to confirm the fault is repeatable.
2. **Access the rear panel** by pulling the washer away from the wall, removing the back cover screws, and exposing the heater assembly (typically located at the bottom rear of the wash tub).
3. **Inspect the heater and sensor connectors** for visible damage, corrosion, burned terminals, or loose plugs, and disconnect both the heater and NTC sensor harnesses.
4. **Test the heater element** with a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) by measuring across the two heater terminals, and consult your model's service manual for the correct resistance range.
5. **Test the NTC temperature sensor** by measuring resistance across its terminals and comparing the reading to the manufacturer's table for ambient temperature (the sensor may be integrated into the heater or separate).
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the heater and sensor back to the main control board for opens, shorts, chafing, or heat damage, and repair or replace any damaged sections.
7. **Replace the failed component** (heater assembly, NTC sensor, or harness), reconnect all plugs securely, reassemble the rear panel, and run a test cycle to verify the code clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections, if the heater and sensor both test correctly but the code persists (indicating a control board or relay fault), or if you cannot access the rear panel safely. A technician can use the machine's diagnostic mode to verify the exact subcode behavior, test the control board heater output, and confirm that water inlet, pressure switch, and drain components are not contributing to the fault. If the main control board needs replacement, professional installation will make sure correct programming and avoid damage to other components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
