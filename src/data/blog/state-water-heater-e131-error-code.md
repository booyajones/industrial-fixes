## State Water Heater E131 Error Code — What It Means

The E131 code on a State water heater is not universally documented in available service literature, so the exact meaning depends on your specific model. In similar appliances, E131 flags an overheating lockout caused by restricted water flow, failed circulation components, or faulty temperature sensors. The unit shuts down to prevent unsafe temperatures in the heat exchanger or flue. Always confirm the fault definition in your model's tech sheet or service manual before proceeding with repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Pump failure or air in the system** A stopped or weak pump, or trapped air pockets, prevents proper water circulation and causes localized overheating.
- **Blocked heat exchanger** Scale, sediment, or sludge buildup inside the heat exchanger restricts flow and raises operating temperature.
- **Closed or partially closed valves** Isolation valves or service valves left closed block circulation and trigger an overheat lockout.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermistor** A failing sensor sends incorrect temperature readings to the control board, causing false lockout.
- **Blocked flue or vent obstruction** Restricted exhaust prevents hot gases from leaving, raising flue temperature above safe limits.
- **Low system pressure** Insufficient water pressure in the system reduces flow rate and allows hot spots to form.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Record the error code** and model number from the rating plate, then consult the model-specific service manual to confirm E131 definition.
2. **Reset the control** once by cycling power or pressing the reset button, then monitor to see if the fault returns immediately or during the next heating cycle.
3. **Check system pressure** on the gauge (typically 1.0 to 1.5 bar when cold for closed-loop systems) and top up through the fill valve if low.
4. **Verify all isolation valves** are fully open, including supply, return, and any service or balancing valves in the loop.
5. **Inspect the pump** for power, unusual noise, and warm casing, and bleed the pump purge screw to release trapped air if the impeller is not spinning freely.
6. **Examine the flue and vent termination** for blockages, bird nests, or debris, and confirm the fan (if equipped) is running and wired correctly.
7. **Test temperature sensors** with a multimeter against the resistance table in your service manual, and inspect wiring for corrosion or loose connections at the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the fault returns after one reset, if you find low system pressure that drops again after refilling, if the pump does not run even with power present, or if you lack the service manual for your specific State model. Overheat lockouts involve combustion safety and sealed water circuits, so misdiagnosis can damage the heat exchanger or create a scalding or carbon-monoxide hazard. A qualified service tech has model-specific fault trees, pressure-test equipment, and access to OEM parts databases to pinpoint the root cause quickly.
