## Daikin A5 Error Code — What It Means

The A5 code is Daikin's freeze-up protector fault for the indoor unit. It means the system has detected that the evaporator coil is becoming too cold or is at risk of icing, so the unit has stopped or limited operation to protect itself. This is not a single-part failure. Instead, it signals that something is causing the indoor coil to drop to dangerously low temperatures, usually tied to restricted airflow, low refrigerant charge, or a faulty temperature sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted airflow through the indoor unit** Dirty filters, blocked intake or discharge grilles, or a weak or failed indoor fan motor prevent enough air from crossing the coil and cause freeze-up.
- **Low refrigerant charge** A refrigerant shortage drives coil temperature down and encourages ice formation on the evaporator.
- **Dirty or blocked evaporator coil** Heavy dirt or contamination on the coil reduces heat transfer and allows the coil to freeze.
- **Faulty indoor thermistor or coil temperature sensor** A bad sensor gives incorrect temperature readings and triggers false freeze protection or prevents proper control.
- **Refrigerant circuit issues** Problems with the expansion device or valve can cause improper refrigerant metering and freeze the coil.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the fault code using the Daikin remote self-diagnosis method to confirm A5 is active and retrieve any additional malfunction codes stored in the system.
2. Inspect and clean the air filters and check that all intake and discharge grilles are unobstructed, then verify the indoor fan runs at proper speed.
3. Examine the evaporator coil for frost, ice, or heavy dirt buildup, and clean the coil if contamination is present.
4. Measure refrigerant-side conditions by checking operating pressures, superheat, and subcooling to confirm the system has proper refrigerant charge before adding refrigerant or replacing parts.
5. Test the indoor coil thermistor and its wiring for correct resistance and connection if the freeze-up condition does not match actual coil temperature.
6. Inspect electrical connections and the indoor control board if the symptom is intermittent or if other causes have been ruled out.
7. Repair the root cause, clear the fault code, and retest the system in cooling or heating mode to confirm the coil no longer triggers freeze protection.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you have cleaned the filters and coil but the A5 code returns, or if you are not trained in refrigerant work. Diagnosing low refrigerant charge, leak repair, thermistor testing, and expansion-device problems requires gauges, recovery equipment, and knowledge of refrigerant circuit behavior. Electrical and control-board diagnostics also require specialized tools and service manuals. A technician can retrieve stored fault codes, measure operating conditions, and isolate the true cause without guessing or causing further damage.
