## State Water Heater E87 Error Code — What It Means

The E87 error code on State water heaters is not explicitly defined in widely available manufacturer documentation, but it typically falls into the category of ignition, flame detection, or venting faults common to tankless and power-vent models. When the unit cannot ignite the burner, loses flame during operation, or detects unsafe combustion conditions, it locks out and displays a code. Most E87 instances are triggered by blocked air intake or exhaust venting, low gas pressure, restricted water flow, or dirty flame sensors.

Because State is part of the A. O. Smith family, error-code behavior often mirrors other A. O. Smith and Takagi platforms. The code forces a safety shutdown until the underlying condition is cleared and the unit is reset. Always consult your specific model's service manual or the label inside the access panel for the precise definition and troubleshooting chart.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Lint, leaves, snow, or corrosion restrict airflow and prevent proper combustion or safe venting.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Closed manual valve, empty propane tank, kinked flex line, or insufficient gas pressure starves the burner.
- **Dirty or fouled flame sensor** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame rod prevent the controller from confirming ignition.
- **Clogged inlet water filter** Sediment or debris reduce flow below the minimum GPM threshold needed to trigger the burner.
- **Scale buildup on heat exchanger or burner** Hard water leaves mineral deposits that block flame ports, slow ignition, or distort the flame pattern.
- **Faulty gas valve or igniter** A weak igniter spark or stuck gas valve coil prevents fuel from reaching the burner chamber.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the unit** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and turn the gas control knob to OFF, then wait two minutes for any residual gas to clear.
2. **Inspect the air intake and exhaust vent terminals** outside the building for obstructions like leaves, wasp nests, or ice, and clear any blockage with a flashlight and brush.
3. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the manual shutoff valve upstream of the heater is fully open, verifying your propane tank gauge shows adequate fuel, and inspecting the flex connector for kinks or damage.
4. **Remove and clean the inlet water filter** located on the cold-water inlet by unscrewing the screen housing, rinsing the mesh under tap water, and reinstalling it snugly.
5. **Access the burner compartment** by removing the front cover panel (usually four screws), then use a vacuum with a soft brush to remove dust and a damp cloth to gently wipe the flame sensor rod until shiny.
6. **Restore power and gas**, turn the control knob to ON, open a hot-water tap to initiate flow, and listen for the igniter clicking and the burner lighting within a few seconds.
7. **Monitor for a clean blue flame** through the sight glass and confirm the error does not reappear after one full heating cycle; if E87 persists, consult your service manual diagnostic flowchart or call a licensed technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the error returns after you have cleared vents, confirmed gas supply, cleaned the filter, and wiped the flame sensor. Persistent E87 codes often require combustion analysis with a flue-gas meter, gas-pressure testing with a manometer, or replacement of the gas valve, igniter, or main controller board. Any work involving gas-line disconnection, pressure adjustment, or electrical diagnostics on the control board should be handled by someone with the proper certifications and liability coverage. If you smell gas at any point, evacuate immediately and call your utility or fire department.
