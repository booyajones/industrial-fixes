## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E10 Error — What It Means

The E10 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a flame or ignition-related fault. The unit may fail to light, or the flame sensor may not detect combustion after ignition. This code protects the system by shutting down operation when proper flame establishment or sensing cannot be confirmed.

Because error codes can vary slightly between models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover for the exact meaning of E10 on your specific model. The code often appears when gas flow is interrupted, the igniter is weak, or the flame rod is fouled and cannot confirm ignition.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is a dirty flame rod. Remove and inspect the flame rod for buildup before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Flame rod fouled or corroded (~40%)** Carbon, mineral scale, or corrosion on the flame rod prevents it from sensing ignition current, causing the unit to shut down and throw the E10 code.
- **Gas supply interrupted or pressure too low (~25%)** A closed valve, kinked flex line, or low inlet pressure prevents the burner from lighting or staying lit.
- **Igniter failure or weak spark (~15%)** A worn or cracked igniter cannot produce a strong enough spark to light the gas-air mixture reliably.
- **Flame rod grounding or wiring issue (~10%)** A damaged flame-rod wire, corroded connector, or short to ground interrupts the sensing circuit and mimics a no-flame condition.
- **Gas valve stuck or solenoid fault (~7%)** Internal debris or a failed solenoid coil prevents the valve from opening fully, starving the burner of gas.
- **Control board relay or output fault (~3%)** A failed relay on the control board may not energize the igniter or gas valve, blocking ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve upstream of the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by loosening the retaining screws and sliding or lifting the panel free.
3. **Locate the flame rod** near the burner assembly (consult your model's manual for exact position) and inspect it for carbon buildup or white mineral scale.
4. **Remove the flame rod** by loosening the mounting screw or bracket and disconnecting the wire; lightly sand the sensing tip with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth until clean and shiny.
5. **Inspect the igniter electrode** for cracks or heavy carbon; clean gently with a soft brush if accessible, or replace if visibly damaged.
6. **Check gas inlet pressure** with a manometer if you have the training and tools, or confirm the manual shutoff valve is fully open and the flex line is not kinked.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod and igniter**, reconnect wiring, and replace the cover; restore gas and power and test the unit by calling for hot water.
8. **Monitor the display** through several hot-water cycles; if the E10 persists, measure voltage at the gas valve and flame-rod circuit, or call a licensed technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas-appliance technician whenever you smell gas, lack the tools to measure gas pressure, or are uncomfortable working near live ignition components. If cleaning the flame rod and confirming gas supply do not clear the E10 code, a technician should test the gas valve, measure microamp flame current, and check control-board outputs. All work involving gas piping, pressure regulators, or combustion adjustments requires proper licensing and permits in most jurisdictions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
