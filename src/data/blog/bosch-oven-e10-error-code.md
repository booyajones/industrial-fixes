## Bosch Oven E10 Error Code — What It Means

The E10 error code on Bosch ovens typically indicates a communication or electronic fault within the control system. The exact meaning can vary by model, so always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the oven panel for the specific definition. In many cases, E10 points to a problem with the electronic control board, a temperature sensor, or the wiring between them. The oven will usually refuse to heat or operate until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board immediately, but a loose connector or damaged wire harness between the board and sensors is often the real culprit. Inspect all connectors and continuity-test the wiring before buying a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** Vibration or heat can loosen plug connections between the control board and sensors, interrupting signals and triggering the error.
- **Failed electronic control board (~30%)** The main control board may develop solder cracks, capacitor failures, or circuit damage from power surges.
- **Faulty temperature sensor (~20%)** The oven temperature sensor (RTD or thermistor) can drift out of range or fail open, causing the control to log a fault.
- **Damaged wire harness (~10%)** Wires can fray, melt against hot surfaces, or break at flex points, creating intermittent or open circuits.
- **Power supply glitch (~5%)** A momentary voltage sag or spike can scramble the control memory and leave a stored error code even after the issue clears.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the oven or confirm zero voltage at the terminal block with a multimeter.
2. **Remove the rear or lower access panel** using a screwdriver, then locate the main control board and temperature sensor wiring.
3. **Inspect every connector** on the control board and sensor, pressing each one firmly to reseat; look for burnt pins, green corrosion, or melted plastic.
4. **Check the wire harness** for pinched, melted, or chafed insulation, especially near sharp metal edges or the hinge area.
5. **Test the temperature sensor** with a multimeter set to ohms; compare the reading at room temperature to your model's resistance table in the service manual.
6. **If wiring and sensor are good**, remove the control board, inspect solder joints and capacitors for cracks or bulges, and replace the board if damaged.
7. **Reassemble the panels**, restore power, and run a bake cycle to verify the error is gone and the oven heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt circuits or if you cannot locate the control board or sensor. A pro can run factory diagnostics through the service mode to pinpoint whether the board, sensor, or wiring is at fault. Also call if the oven is still under warranty, since self-repair may void coverage. If you have already replaced the sensor and reseated connectors but the error persists, a qualified service tech can test the board in place and check for hidden wiring faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-150 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
