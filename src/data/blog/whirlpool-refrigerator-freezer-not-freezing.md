## Whirlpool Freezer Not Freezing — What's Happening

A Whirlpool freezer that is not freezing is a symptom, not a single error code. It points to problems with airflow, temperature controls, defrost operation, door sealing, fan function, or compressor-side components depending on your model. Whirlpool's own troubleshooting starts with temperature settings, blocked vents, condenser coil cleanliness, frost buildup, and door seal integrity before moving to internal parts like the evaporator fan, condenser fan, compressor start relay, or defrost system.

If your unit displays an error code like E2 or F2, that can narrow the diagnosis. Whirlpool and iFixit documentation list E2 or F2 as a freezer sensor fault or evaporator fan error, and E3 as a defrost sensor fault. Without a code, you work through the most common external and internal causes until the faulty component is identified.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Blocked airflow** Overpacked shelves or blocked vents prevent cold air circulation and stop the freezer from reaching temperature.
- **Dirty condenser coils** Dust and debris on the coils at the bottom or back reduce cooling capacity and prevent proper heat rejection.
- **Faulty door gasket** Torn, bent, or dirty door seals allow warm air into the freezer and compromise cooling.
- **Frost or ice buildup on evaporator coils** Heavy frost behind the rear freezer panel blocks airflow and signals a defrost system failure such as a bad heater, timer, or control.
- **Failed evaporator fan** If the evaporator fan does not run or is obstructed by ice, cold air cannot circulate through the freezer.
- **Incorrect temperature setting or control fault** The thermostat or electronic control may be set incorrectly or failed, preventing the compressor from running long enough.
- **Faulty compressor start relay** A failed start relay keeps the compressor from starting or causes abnormal cycling and loss of cooling.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm the freezer is actually warm and verify the temperature control is set to the correct position per the owner's manual.
2. Remove excess food and check that all interior vents and airflow paths are clear and not blocked by packaging or ice.
3. Unplug the unit, pull it away from the wall, and vacuum or brush all dust and debris from the condenser coils at the bottom or back.
4. Inspect the door gasket around the entire perimeter using the paper-slip test and look for tears, bends, or contamination that would prevent a tight seal.
5. Remove the rear interior freezer panel if accessible and check the evaporator coil for heavy frost or ice buildup that blocks airflow.
6. Test the evaporator fan by listening for operation when the door is closed or by manually triggering the door switch, and check that the fan spins freely without ice obstruction.
7. If frost is blocking the coil, inspect the defrost heater, defrost timer or control board, and related sensor or wiring for faults.
8. Check the compressor start relay and overload for signs of failure if the compressor does not start or cycles abnormally, and test the relay per service instructions if available.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have checked airflow, cleaned the coils, and verified the door seal but the freezer still will not freeze. Defrost system diagnosis, sealed refrigeration system work, compressor testing, and control board replacement require tools and refrigerant handling that are not safe or practical for most owners. If your unit shows an error code like E2, F2, or E3, a technician can test the sensor circuits, wiring, and control board to pinpoint the electronic fault quickly.
