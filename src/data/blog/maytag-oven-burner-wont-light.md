## Maytag Oven Burner Won't Light — What's Happening

When a Maytag oven burner won't light, you're describing a symptom rather than a specific fault code. For a cooktop or surface burner, this typically shows up as clicking without ignition. For an oven bake or broil burner, the oven simply won't heat. Maytag's factory guidance points to gas supply interruptions, air trapped in the line after new installation, control lockout settings, moisture from cleaning, clogged burner ports, misaligned burner caps or bases, and igniter failures as the primary culprits.

The exact cause depends on whether you're working with a surface burner or an oven cavity burner. Surface burners often fail to light because of positioning or blockage issues in the burner assembly itself. Oven burners that won't ignite usually trace back to a weak or failed igniter, a tripped thermal fuse, or a fault in the control board. No single universal error code ties to this symptom across Maytag gas ranges.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Gas supply shutoff closed or interrupted** Maytag instructs that the appliance gas shutoff valve must be open and the supply line connected for any burner to light.
- **Air in the gas line after installation** Maytag notes that air trapped in the line following a new install or reconnection can delay ignition until the line is purged by running one burner.
- **Control Lock engaged** Maytag states that Control Lockout prevents burner operation and must be deactivated before ignition is possible.
- **Clogged burner ports or misaligned burner cap** Maytag identifies blocked ports and improper seating of the burner cap or base as common reasons a surface burner clicks but does not light.
- **Moisture in switches or burner area** Maytag says water from cleaning or spills can interrupt the spark circuit and recommends drying the area with cool air.
- **Weak or failed igniter (oven burner)** Maytag lists igniter malfunction as a leading cause when an oven bake or broil burner will not ignite or heat.
- **Blown thermal fuse or control board fault (oven)** Maytag identifies thermal fuse and control board issues among the reasons an oven does not heat, especially in electric-ignition gas models.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm whether the problem is a surface burner that clicks but won't light or an oven bake burner that won't heat, since the diagnostic path differs.
2. Check that the gas shutoff valve behind or beneath the range is fully open and that power is present at the outlet or breaker.
3. Verify that Control Lock is off by consulting your model's control panel or owner's guide, then test the burner again.
4. If the range was recently installed or reconnected, turn one surface burner to Ignite for 30 to 60 seconds to purge air from the gas line.
5. Remove the grate and burner cap from the affected surface burner, inspect the cap and base for proper alignment, and clean any clogged ports with a soft brush or toothpick without enlarging the holes.
6. If moisture is present from cleaning or a spill, allow the burner area to air-dry or use a fan with cool air, then test ignition again.
7. For an oven that won't heat, open the oven cavity and visually inspect the igniter for cracks, breaks, or heavy carbon buildup that indicates wear.
8. Use a multimeter to test continuity across the igniter, thermal fuse, or bake element if you have electrical testing experience and the oven remains cold after all other checks.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician or gas-certified service provider if you've verified the gas supply is open, purged air from the line, cleaned and aligned the burner assembly, and the symptom persists. Any work involving gas connections, igniter replacement in the oven cavity, thermal fuse testing, or control board diagnosis requires specialized tools and carries burn, shock, and gas-leak hazards. Maytag recommends professional service for component-level electrical testing and for any repair that involves disconnecting or modifying gas fittings. For gas line, burner, or igniter work, or if you ever smell gas, stop and call a licensed technician.
