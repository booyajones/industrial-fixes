## Cooper & Hunter H3 Error Code — What It Means

The H3 error code on a Cooper & Hunter mini split signals that the compressor overload or overheating protection system has activated in the outdoor unit. The control board has detected that the compressor is drawing excessive current or running too hot, and it has shut down or limited operation to prevent permanent damage to the compressor. This code is part of the Gree-derived code set used by Cooper & Hunter systems, and it indicates a real fault condition in the outdoor unit that requires diagnosis and repair.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or dirty condenser coil** Dirt, cottonwood seed, leaves, or other debris restrict airflow through the outdoor coil, preventing heat rejection and driving compressor temperature up until the overload trips.
- **Failed or slow outdoor fan motor** When the condenser fan does not spin at full speed or has stopped completely, the outdoor coil cannot dissipate heat and the compressor overheats under load.
- **Refrigerant-side restriction or imbalance** A stuck expansion valve, clogged capillary tube, or incorrect refrigerant charge forces the compressor to work harder and run hotter than design conditions allow.
- **Loose wiring or damaged connectors** Poor connections at the compressor terminals, outdoor board, or power supply can create high resistance, voltage drop, or intermittent operation that registers as an overload condition.
- **Faulty compressor thermal protector** The overload or thermal cutout device mounted on the compressor may be failing and opening the circuit prematurely, even when the compressor is operating within normal temperature and current ranges.
- **Internal compressor failure** A mechanically seized, shorted, or internally damaged compressor will draw excessive current and trip the overload protection repeatedly under any load.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Power down the system at the breaker and the outdoor disconnect before opening any panels or touching wiring to make sure your safety and prevent arc flash.
2. Inspect and clean the outdoor condenser coil by removing the top grille and side panels, then using a garden hose and coil cleaner to flush dirt and debris from between the fins.
3. Check the outdoor fan operation by restoring power briefly and watching the fan spin at full speed without wobble, grinding, or delay when the unit calls for cooling.
4. Inspect all wiring and connectors at the outdoor unit by opening the electrical box and checking for burned terminals, loose screws, corroded pins, or damaged insulation on the compressor and fan leads.
5. Test the compressor thermal protector and overload device with a multimeter set to continuity mode, confirming that it closes when cool and that resistance values match manufacturer guidance for your model.
6. Evaluate refrigerant-circuit conditions by looking for frost on the suction line, unusual pressure readings if you have gauges, or signs of restriction at the expansion valve, and call a licensed technician if refrigerant work is needed.
7. Clear the fault code after repairs by cycling power off for two minutes, then restarting the system and monitoring for stable operation without the H3 code returning under normal load.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live 240-volt power, if the condenser coil and fan are clean but the H3 code persists, or if you suspect refrigerant-side problems that require recovery, evacuation, and recharge. Refrigerant work and compressor replacement both require EPA-certified tools and licensing. A technician will also have the exact service manual, pressure targets, and compressor current specifications for your model to confirm the diagnosis and replace the correct components safely.
