## What this code means
F9 E1 on an Amana washer indicates a long drain condition. The washer is taking longer than expected to drain water from the tub. On front-load models, Amana defines this as drain times typically over eight minutes. The machine has detected that water is not evacuating at the normal rate, so it throws the fault and stops the cycle to prevent overfilling or damage.

Amana's official troubleshooting prioritizes the drain path outside the machine. The most common culprits are installation errors (drain hose pushed too far into the standpipe, causing siphoning or backflow), kinked or clogged hoses, and blocked standpipes or utility sinks. On front-load washers, excessive suds from non-HE detergent can also slow drainage. Only after those checks come up clean should you suspect the drain pump or its circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump immediately, but most F9 E1 codes clear once you correct the hose installation or clear a kinked section. Always inspect the entire drain path and hose routing before ordering pump parts.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose installed too far into standpipe (~40%)** If the hose is pushed beyond the elbow in the standpipe, it can siphon water back into the tub or prevent proper drainage, triggering the long-drain fault.
- **Kinked or crushed drain hose (~25%)** A sharp bend, kink, or external crushing of the hose restricts flow and slows the drain cycle beyond the eight-minute threshold.
- **Blocked standpipe or utility sink (~15%)** A partial or full clog in the household drain line backs up water and prevents the washer from emptying at normal speed.
- **Excessive suds from non-HE detergent (~10%)** Front-load washers require HE detergent; regular detergent creates thick suds that slow drainage and can trigger the long-drain code.
- **Clogged or failed drain pump (~10%)** If all hose and drain-path checks pass and the fault persists, debris in the pump impeller or a failed pump motor is the likely machine-side cause.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** or disconnect power at the breaker to work safely.
2. **Pull the washer forward** and inspect the drain hose where it exits the back of the machine and enters the standpipe or utility sink.
3. **Check hose installation.** Verify the hose is not pushed more than a few inches into the standpipe (Amana specifies standpipe height must be between 39 and 96 inches for front-load models). Pull the hose back if it is too deep. Do not tape the hose to the drain pipe or alter the factory U-shaped hose form.
4. **Straighten kinks and clear blockages.** Walk the length of the hose looking for sharp bends, crushing, or debris. If the hose is damaged or permanently kinked, replace it.
5. **Test the household drain.** Disconnect the hose from the standpipe and place the end in a bucket. Run a drain or spin cycle. If water flows freely into the bucket, the blockage is in the standpipe or sink line. Snake or clear that drain. If water does not flow well, the restriction is in the washer's hose or pump.
6. **Clear the error code.** Press Pause/Cancel twice, then Power once on front-load models. Alternatively, unplug the machine for five minutes, then plug it back in and start a rinse-and-spin cycle to retest.
7. **Inspect the drain pump** (if the drain path is clear but the error returns). Access the pump filter or housing (location varies by model), remove any debris from the impeller, and check that the pump runs and does not hum or bind. Replace the pump if it is damaged or does not operate.
8. **Verify detergent type** on front-load washers. Use only HE detergent to prevent excessive suds that delay drainage.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have corrected the drain hose installation, cleared the standpipe, confirmed HE detergent use, and the F9 E1 code still returns after a five-minute power reset. At that point the fault is likely in the drain pump, its wiring harness, or the control board's drain-pump circuit. A pro can test pump continuity, verify voltage at the pump terminals, and replace failed components. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable moving the washer, accessing the pump housing, or using a multimeter to diagnose electrical faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-30 in hose or clamps if replacement needed, 15-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 for a service call and pump replacement if needed.
