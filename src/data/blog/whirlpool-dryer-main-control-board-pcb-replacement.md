## Whirlpool Dryer Main Control Board Replacement — What This Part Does

The main control board (electronic control or EOC) is the dryer's central computer. It reads inputs from the user interface and sensors, then switches power to the motor, heating element, and other components to run your selected cycle. Whirlpool dryers show F01 (electronic circuit board fault) or F1E1 (stuck motor relay on the control) when the board itself fails, often after a power surge, age-related component breakdown, or a relay burning out internally.

The board can fail from line voltage spikes, moisture intrusion into the control area, or simple wear over years of cycling. When a relay or microprocessor on the board goes bad, the dryer may refuse to start, show an error immediately, or click repeatedly without running. Heat damage and visible burn marks on the board are common during failure. Loose or corroded connectors can mimic board faults, so inspecting harnesses first is always part of the diagnosis.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **F01 or F1E1 error code on the display** Whirlpool uses F01 for electronic circuit board faults and F1E1 for stuck motor relay on the control, both pointing to main board failure.
- **Dryer powers on but won't start the cycle** The user interface lights up normally, but pressing Start does nothing or the drum doesn't turn.
- **Clicking or chattering from the control area** A stuck or burned relay on the board will click rapidly without engaging the motor.
- **Visible burn marks or scorching on the control board** Power surges or failed relays leave dark spots, melted plastic, or charred traces on the circuit board.
- **Intermittent start or random mid-cycle stops** The board may work for a few seconds then fault out, or the dryer stops and displays an error partway through.
- **Error returns immediately after a power reset** Unplugging for two to ten minutes clears the fault temporarily, but the code comes back as soon as you try to run a cycle again.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Unplug the dryer from the wall outlet (or shut off the breaker) and wait at least two minutes to discharge capacitors and perform a hard reset.
2. Pull the dryer away from the wall so you can access the rear or top panel, depending on your model's control board location (consult your service manual for exact panel removal steps).
3. Remove the screws securing the control console or rear access panel, then lift or tilt the panel to expose the main control board and its wire harnesses.
4. Take a clear photo of every harness connector and its position on the old board before you disconnect anything, so you have a wiring reference during reassembly.
5. Inspect all harness connectors and terminals for heat damage, melting, or corrosion (if you see charred wires or melted pins, replace the damaged harness before installing the new board).
6. Unplug each wire harness from the old control board by pressing the locking tab and pulling straight out, then remove the mounting screws and lift the board out of its plastic cradle.
7. Place the new control board into the mounting cradle and secure it with the original screws, making sure the board sits flat and all screw holes line up.
8. Reconnect each harness connector to the matching terminal on the new board, using your reference photo to verify correct positions (press each connector firmly until you hear or feel the lock click).
9. Reinstall the console or access panel with all screws, slide the dryer back into place, and restore power.
10. Run a short test cycle (timed dry or air fluff) to verify the dryer starts, the drum turns, and no error code appears on the display.

## When to Call a Pro

If you see extensive heat damage to multiple harnesses, scorched wiring inside the cabinet, or you're uncomfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, call a qualified appliance technician. If the new board still throws the same error code after installation and you've verified all connectors are seated correctly, the fault may lie in a damaged motor relay circuit, a shorted wire, or a problem elsewhere in the dryer that requires diagnostic tools and a schematic. Professional diagnosis can save you the cost of unnecessary parts and prevent further damage from incorrect repairs.
