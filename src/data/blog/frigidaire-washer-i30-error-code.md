## Frigidaire Dishwasher i30 Error Code — What It Means

The i30 code on a Frigidaire dishwasher signals that water has been detected in the leak pan at the bottom of the unit, beneath the main tub. This is an overflow protection alarm, not a drain fault. When the float in the base pan lifts, the machine stops the wash cycle and typically runs the drain pump to protect your floor from flooding. The code will not clear until you remove the water from the pan and fix whatever caused it to fill.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or drain pump when the real problem is a loose hose clamp or split rubber hose. Pull the machine, dry the base pan, and inspect every water connection before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Leaking hose or loose clamp (~40%)** Drain hoses, circulation hoses, or inlet-valve connections can split, crack, or work loose and drip into the base pan.
- **Circulation or drain pump seal failure (~25%)** A worn shaft seal on the pump lets water seep out during the cycle and pool in the leak tray.
- **Oversudsing from wrong detergent (~15%)** Excessive foam or regular dish soap instead of dishwasher detergent can overflow the tub and reach the base pan.
- **Door gasket or tub seal leak (~10%)** A torn, misaligned, or dirty door gasket lets water escape from the front edge during spray cycles.
- **Inlet valve leak or stuck open (~5%)** A faulty inlet valve may drip continuously or fail to close, sending extra water into the tub that overflows into the pan.
- **One-time spill during loading or service (~5%)** Water spilled into the machine base during installation, repair, or aggressive loading can trip the float without an ongoing leak.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the dishwasher before you begin any inspection or repair work.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** from the cabinet so you can access the entire base and rear area beneath the tub.
3. **Remove all water from the base pan** using a shop vacuum, towels, or a sponge, and dry the pan and float mechanism completely.
4. **Inspect every water connection** including the drain hose, inlet hose, circulation hoses, pump housings, and door gasket for cracks, loose clamps, or wet spots.
5. **Check for suds or detergent issues** by looking inside the tub and confirming only dishwasher detergent was used, not hand soap or excess product.
6. **Tighten or replace any leaking components** such as hose clamps, split hoses, a dripping pump seal, or a torn door gasket.
7. **Restore power and run a short test cycle** with the machine pulled out so you can watch for any new leaks or water reaching the base pan.
8. **Clear the i30 code** by cycling power once the pan is dry and the leak is repaired, then slide the dishwasher back into place and monitor the next few loads.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot safely pull the dishwasher out, if the leak source is not obvious after a full inspection, or if the pump or inlet valve needs replacement and you are not comfortable with plumbing or electrical connections. Also call if the i30 code returns repeatedly after you have dried the pan and checked every hose, because an intermittent leak inside the tub or a hidden crack in the sump area requires disassembly and parts-level diagnosis. A technician can pressure-test the system, identify hidden leaks, and replace sealed components such as the pump or tub assembly if needed.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-50 in hoses or clamps, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300 including diagnostics and parts.
