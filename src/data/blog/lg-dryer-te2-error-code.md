## LG Dryer tE2 Error Code — What It Means

The tE2 code on an LG dryer indicates a thermistor or temperature-sensing fault. LG support describes it as a problem with the dryer's thermistor, the sensor that monitors air temperature inside the drum and heater assembly. The code typically appears when the thermistor reads open, shorted, or otherwise out of the expected range.

LG notes that tE2 most often occurs during cold conditions, particularly in winter when cold outdoor air is pulled back into the dryer through an open or poorly sealing exhaust vent. Restricted airflow from lint buildup, crushed ducting, or a blocked vent run can also distort temperature readings and trigger the fault. Less commonly, the thermistor itself, its wiring connector, or the main control board may be at fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the thermistor or control board first. Check the exterior vent hood for stuck louvers or ice, clean the lint screen and duct, and confirm strong airflow at the outside termination before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Cold air backdraft through the exterior vent (~40%)** Cold outdoor air enters through an open, stuck, or poorly sealing vent hood when the dryer is idle, causing the thermistor to read abnormally low.
- **Restricted airflow from lint buildup (~30%)** A clogged lint screen, lint housing, or vent duct reduces airflow and distorts the temperature profile, triggering an out-of-range thermistor reading.
- **Failed or out-of-spec thermistor (~15%)** The thermistor itself reads open, shorted, or outside the normal resistance range, even when airflow and venting are correct.
- **Loose connector or damaged harness (~10%)** The thermistor connector has corroded pins, a loose fit, or broken wires, causing an intermittent or open circuit.
- **Main control board fault (~5%)** The control board misinterprets a valid thermistor signal or has a failed input circuit, though this is less common.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and reset** by unplugging the dryer or opening the circuit breaker, waiting five minutes, then restoring power and starting a normal timed dry cycle to see if the fault clears.
2. **Clean the lint screen** and inspect the lint housing at the front or rear of the drum, removing any accumulated lint that could restrict airflow.
3. **Inspect the exhaust duct** from the dryer to the exterior termination for kinks, crushed sections, or blockages, and straighten or replace damaged ducting.
4. **Check the exterior vent hood** for stuck louvers, ice, snow, or an overly open design that allows cold air to enter when the dryer is idle, and replace or seal as needed.
5. **Locate the thermistor** (consult your model's service manual for the exact location on the blower housing or heater assembly), unplug the dryer, and inspect the sensor connector for corrosion, loose pins, or damaged wires.
6. **Test the thermistor resistance** using a multimeter at room temperature and compare the reading to your model's specification table (consult the wiring diagram or service sheet), replacing the sensor if it reads open, shorted, or out of range.
7. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the thermistor to the main control board for cuts, pinches, or poor connections, and repair or replace damaged wiring.
8. **Replace the main control board** only if the thermistor, connector, and harness all test good and the fault persists after verifying airflow and venting are correct.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt electrical connections, cannot locate the thermistor on your model, or if the code persists after you have confirmed good airflow, tested the thermistor in-spec, and inspected all wiring. A qualified appliance technician can perform model-specific resistance checks, trace harness faults with advanced test equipment, and safely replace the control board if needed. Also call for help if the dryer is still under warranty or if you suspect a gas leak (on gas models).

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 for a thermistor, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 for diagnosis and repair.
