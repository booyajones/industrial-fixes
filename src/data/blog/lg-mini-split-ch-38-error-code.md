## LG Mini-Split CH38 Error Code — What It Means

The CH38 code on an LG mini-split indicates that the system has detected a low refrigerant level and has entered protection mode to prevent compressor damage. LG technical support identifies this code as a refrigerant gas-level fault, meaning the unit is sensing an insufficient refrigerant charge in the sealed refrigeration circuit. This is not a transient error or display glitch. The system will not operate normally until the underlying refrigerant loss problem is found and corrected. In nearly all cases, CH38 points to a real leak or installation issue rather than a failed sensor or electrical fault.

## Common Causes

- **Refrigerant leak at flare connections** Loose, improperly torqued, or poorly formed flare nuts at the indoor or outdoor unit are the most common source of refrigerant loss in mini-split systems.
- **Damaged or pinched line set** Physical damage to the refrigerant tubing during installation or from external impact can create small leaks that slowly deplete the charge.
- **Leaking service valve or Schrader port** The service valves on the outdoor unit and any Schrader access ports can develop leaks over time or after improper service work.
- **Faulty brazed joint or coil leak** Factory or field-brazed connections and the indoor or outdoor coil itself can develop pinhole leaks, especially in corrosive environments.
- **Improper initial charge or service undercharge** If the system was not charged correctly during installation or after a repair, it may run low and trigger the protection code.
- **Expansion valve malfunction** A stuck or faulty metering device can cause abnormal refrigerant distribution that mimics a low-charge condition, though this is less common than actual leaks.

## When to Call a Pro

CH38 requires refrigerant system diagnosis, leak detection equipment, vacuum pump, recovery machine, and EPA Section 608 certification to handle refrigerant legally. Homeowners should not attempt to open the refrigerant circuit or add refrigerant without proper tools and licensing. Call a qualified HVAC technician if you see this code. The repair will include leak location, proper evacuation, and precise recharge, all of which require specialized gauges and skills. Attempting a DIY refrigerant fix risks personal injury, environmental harm, and permanent damage to the compressor.
