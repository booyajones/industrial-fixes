## Electrolux Dryer E40 Error Code — What It Means

The E40 error code on Electrolux dryers typically signals a door-latch or door-switch problem, though the exact meaning can vary by model. The control board expects a signal from the door mechanism confirming the door is closed and latched. When that signal does not arrive or is interrupted, the dryer will not start or will stop mid-cycle and display E40.

Because Electrolux uses this code across many dryer platforms, always consult your model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the precise definition. In most cases the fault traces to a mechanical latch that has worn or shifted, a door switch that no longer makes contact, or wiring between the switch and the control board. Occasionally the control board itself misreads a good signal.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board when the real culprit is a worn door-latch striker or a switch with corroded terminals. Test continuity across the door switch with the door closed before ordering electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Worn or misaligned door latch or striker (~50%)** Plastic latch assemblies and strikers wear over thousands of cycles, preventing the switch from closing fully.
- **Failed door switch (~25%)** The micro-switch inside the latch assembly can develop poor contacts or break internally.
- **Loose or corroded wiring at the door switch (~15%)** Vibration and moisture can degrade the spade terminals or push-on connectors at the switch.
- **Control-board misread or fault (~10%)** The main control may fail to interpret a valid door-closed signal due to capacitor drift or a failed input circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall or switch off the circuit breaker.
2. **Open the door** and examine the latch striker (the plastic or metal tongue on the door) and the catch in the cabinet frame for cracks, wear, or debris.
3. **Close the door firmly** and listen for a solid click. If the latch feels loose or does not engage, adjust the striker screws or replace the worn component.
4. **Locate the door switch** at the top or side of the door opening (consult your model diagram). Pull the wire connector off the switch.
5. **Test continuity** across the switch terminals with a multimeter while holding the door closed. You should see near-zero resistance when closed and infinite resistance when open.
6. **If the switch fails the test**, remove the mounting screws and replace the door-switch assembly with a new one matched to your model number.
7. **Reconnect all wiring**, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the E40 code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have tested the door switch and latch and both check out, yet the E40 error persists. The control board may need bench testing or replacement, which requires board-level diagnostics and proper part programming. Also reach out if you are uncomfortable working with multimeters or removing cabinet panels, or if your dryer is still under warranty and you want factory-authorized service.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220.
