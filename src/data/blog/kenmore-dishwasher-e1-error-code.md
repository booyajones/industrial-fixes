## Kenmore Dishwasher E1 Error Code — What It Means

The E1 error code on Kenmore dishwashers can indicate different faults depending on the model series. On many models it points to a water-fill issue where the machine is not detecting proper water entry within the expected time frame. On other models it may relate to a drain fault or a sensor issue. Because Kenmore sources dishwashers from several manufacturers, the exact definition can vary. Check your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the door for your specific model to confirm what E1 means for your unit.

Regardless of the specific fault, the code generally means the control board has detected a condition outside normal operating parameters related to water flow, level sensing, or drainage. The machine halts the cycle to prevent flooding or damage. Most cases stem from a blockage, kinked hose, or a failed sensor rather than a control board fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a clogged filter, kinked hose, or faulty water-inlet valve or float switch are far more common. Test the fill and drain systems and inspect sensors before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Kinked or restricted water-fill hose (~30%)** A bend or closed valve at the supply line prevents adequate water flow and triggers a fill timeout error.
- **Clogged drain filter or pump (~25%)** Food debris or broken glass blocking the filter or pump impeller prevents proper drainage and can cause a fault.
- **Faulty water-inlet valve (~20%)** The solenoid valve that controls incoming water can stick closed or fail electrically, stopping water entry.
- **Stuck or faulty float switch (~15%)** The overfill float in the base pan can stick in the up position or fail, signaling a false flood condition.
- **Failed water-level sensor or pressure switch (~8%)** The sensor that detects water level in the tub may be clogged with debris or have failed connections.
- **Control board communication error (~2%)** Loose wiring or a circuit fault on the main control board can produce spurious error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water shut-off valve under the sink.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** enough to access the fill hose and base pan, or open the lower kickplate if your model allows access from the front.
3. **Inspect the water-fill hose** where it connects under the sink. Straighten any kinks and confirm the shut-off valve is fully open.
4. **Remove the lower dish rack** and unscrew the cylindrical drain filter at the bottom of the tub. Rinse it under the tap and clear any debris from the sump.
5. **Check the base pan** under the tub for standing water. If present, soak it up with towels and inspect the float switch (a small dome or cup on a lever). make sure it moves freely and is not stuck in the raised position.
6. **Test the water-inlet valve** by disconnecting the fill hose and checking the valve screen for debris. Use a multimeter to test the solenoid coils for continuity (typically 500-1500 ohms). Replace the valve if it is clogged or shows no continuity.
7. **Inspect the pressure-switch hose** (a small air tube running from the tub to the control area). Remove it and blow through to confirm it is clear. Clean or replace if blocked.
8. **Reassemble the dishwasher**, restore power and water, and run a short test cycle to confirm the error is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have checked all hoses, cleaned the filter and sump, tested the inlet valve, and confirmed the float switch moves freely, yet the E1 code persists. A technician can perform voltage checks at the control board, test sensor circuits with specialized meters, and safely diagnose wiring faults. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with water connections, electrical testing, or pulling the dishwasher out from under the counter. If the control board itself is at fault (rare), replacement requires careful connector work and programming on some models.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
