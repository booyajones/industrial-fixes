## State Water Heater E83 Error Code — What It Means

E83 does not appear in official State Water Heaters documentation as a standard error code. If you see E83 on your display, it may be a misread fault code, an installer notation, or a code from a control platform not documented in public manuals. State gas water heaters typically use flame-sensing and thermocouple circuits to confirm safe ignition, and many field issues involve sensor fouling or gas-valve communication failures rather than alphanumeric fault codes.

Because no manufacturer definition for E83 exists in available State materials, treat any E83 display as a prompt to inspect flame-sensing components and verify that your model's actual fault-code legend matches what you see. Check your unit's rating plate and control-board label for the exact model number, then consult the installation and service manual for the correct code table.

## Common Causes

- **Misread or non-standard code** E83 may be a misread display, a code from a third-party control module, or shorthand not found in State's published fault lists.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor** Carbon and mineral deposits on the sensor rod prevent proper flame detection and can shut down the burner or trigger generic faults.
- **Failed thermocouple or flame-rod circuit** A degraded thermocouple or broken wire interrupts the safety signal to the gas valve, mimicking or causing fault conditions.
- **Gas control valve communication fault** Internal valve electronics may fail to communicate status correctly, leading to unverified or custom error displays.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Insufficient inlet pressure or a closed manual shutoff can prevent ignition and cause the control to log a fault.
- **Control-board firmware or wiring issue** Loose connectors, corrosion on terminals, or outdated board firmware can produce nonstandard or phantom codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power and gas** at the unit shutoff and the circuit breaker or disconnect to make sure safe access to internal components.
2. **Locate and photograph the rating plate** on the side of the tank and any displayed code to confirm your exact model number and compare against the manual's code table.
3. **Remove the burner-access door** and inspect the flame sensor or thermocouple rod for soot, white mineral crust, or corrosion.
4. **Clean the sensor** with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-abrasive pad until the metal is bright, then wipe away all dust and debris.
5. **Check all wire connections** at the gas valve, sensor terminals, and control board for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks and reseat or clean as needed.
6. **Restore gas and power**, then initiate a call for heat or press the unit's reset button and observe whether ignition succeeds and the code clears.
7. **If the fault persists**, consult the service manual for your specific model or contact State technical support with the exact code and model number to verify whether E83 is valid for your control platform.

## When to Call a Pro

If cleaning the flame sensor and verifying wiring do not clear the fault, or if you cannot locate E83 in your model's manual, call a licensed water-heater technician or State's technical-support line. Gas-appliance diagnostics require combustion testing and sometimes require valve or control-board replacement that involves pressure measurement and code-compliant venting. A technician can cross-reference your exact control platform, download the correct service bulletin, and confirm whether E83 is a valid code or a sign of a non-original control module that needs replacement.
