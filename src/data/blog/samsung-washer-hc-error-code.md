## Samsung Washer HC Error Code — What It Means

Samsung groups HC, HE, HE1, Hr, and 1 HC together as heater errors on its washer support page. The code signals that the machine has detected an abnormal heating condition in the wash system. This can be a heating element circuit fault, a temperature sensor problem, or control logic seeing an invalid reading during the heating process. Because Samsung's public documentation does not define HC separately for every washer model, always verify your exact model number and consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for model-specific interpretation.

In most washer-service contexts, HC indicates a water heating or sensor fault, meaning the heating element, thermistor, wiring, or control board is reporting a condition outside normal parameters. The machine stops the cycle to prevent damage or unsafe operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first. Test the heater element for continuity and the thermistor for correct resistance before ordering any control electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty heater element or heating circuit (~40%)** The heating element itself may be open, shorted, or corroded, preventing proper energization or causing the control to see a fault.
- **Temperature sensor or thermistor failure (~30%)** The sensor monitoring water temperature may be open, shorted, or reading unstable values, triggering an invalid heating condition.
- **Damaged wiring or connector at the heater or sensor (~15%)** Loose terminals, corroded pins, or frayed harness wires can interrupt the circuit or create intermittent faults that the control interprets as a heater error.
- **Low line voltage or poor power quality (~10%)** Samsung notes that low voltage can contribute to heater errors on electric dryers, and the same principle may affect washer heater circuits on some models.
- **Control board heater-relay or logic fault (~5%)** The main control may fail to command or monitor the heater correctly, though this is less common than a failed heater or sensor.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error appear immediately when you start a heated cycle, before any water fills?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The control is likely detecting a pre-existing fault in the heater circuit or sensor wiring. Inspect connectors and test the heater and thermistor for shorts or open circuits.<br><strong>No:</strong> The error appears after the cycle begins, which suggests the fault develops during heating. Check for intermittent wiring or a sensor that drifts out of range under load.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Have you recently moved the washer or had a power surge in your home?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Physical movement can loosen connectors or crack solder joints, and surges can damage the heater relay or control board. Inspect all heater-circuit connectors and test the heater element.<br><strong>No:</strong> The fault is likely age or wear related. Focus on the heater element and thermistor, which are common wear items in heated wash cycles.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the washer fill with water normally and is the water supply adequate?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Water supply is not the issue. The fault is in the heating circuit, sensor, or control logic.<br><strong>No:</strong> Confirm both hot and cold supply valves are fully open, hoses are not kinked, and inlet screens are clean. Some Samsung washers require proper fill before heating, and low flow can indirectly affect heating operation.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off and reset the washer** by unplugging it or turning off the breaker for one minute, then restore power and attempt a heated cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Verify your exact model number** from the label inside the door or on the back panel, because Samsung's code interpretation can vary by platform and product family.
3. **Unplug the machine and access the heater assembly** by removing the front or rear service panel according to your model's service manual.
4. **Inspect the heater element and all connectors** for evidence of arcing, burning, corrosion, loose terminals, or damaged insulation on the wiring harness.
5. **Test the heater element for continuity** using a multimeter; the element should show a path to ground through its rated resistance, not an open or direct short.
6. **Test the temperature sensor or thermistor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance; compare the reading to your model's service specification if available, or replace if the reading is open, shorted, or erratic.
7. **Check incoming line voltage** at the wall outlet or terminal block to confirm it meets the nameplate requirement; low voltage can prevent proper heater operation on some models.
8. **Reassemble the washer and run a controlled test cycle** with a heated wash selection; monitor that the heater energizes only when commanded and that the temperature rises normally without re-triggering the code.
9. **Replace the failed component** based on your test results: heater assembly if the element is faulty, sensor assembly if the thermistor is out of range, or control board only if all other circuits test good and the relay or logic is proven defective.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with line-voltage wiring or testing heating circuits. The heater element and its control relay operate at 120 or 240 volts depending on the model, and improper testing or reassembly can cause shock or fire. A professional has the correct service manual, wiring diagram, and test equipment to isolate the fault quickly and safely. If you have already replaced the heater or sensor and the code returns, the control board or a deeper wiring fault is likely, and that work is best handled by a trained service provider.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
