## What this code means
On Maytag wall-oven and range units that include a microwave, the F1E5 error code signals a problem with the microwave inverter, magnetron tube, or the wiring that connects them. Maytag's official guidance begins with a power reset to clear the fault. If the code reappears, there is an active hardware failure in the microwave's high-voltage circuit rather than a simple glitch.

This code does not refer to oven temperature sensors or cooking elements. It is specific to the microwave portion of the appliance. The inverter supplies controlled power to the magnetron tube, which generates the microwaves for cooking. When either component fails or their harness connections loosen or corrode, the control board detects the fault and posts F1E5.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board or oven sensor when they see an F-code, but F1E5 on Maytag ranges points to the microwave inverter or magnetron. Always verify the connectors at the inverter and magnetron harness before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed microwave inverter (~40%)** The inverter board that powers the magnetron tube burns out or develops internal shorts, triggering F1E5.
- **Failed magnetron tube (~35%)** The magnetron itself shorts, opens, or loses vacuum, causing the control to detect a fault in the microwave circuit.
- **Loose or corroded harness connections (~15%)** Connectors at the inverter, magnetron, or appliance manager (P21, P22 on some models) work loose or corrode, interrupting signals or power.
- **Damaged high-voltage wiring (~10%)** Wiring between the inverter and magnetron cracks, arcs, or melts from heat or age, breaking continuity or causing intermittent faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the range or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker for at least 60 seconds to reset the control board.
2. **Restore power** and observe whether F1E5 reappears immediately or only when you attempt to use the microwave.
3. **Access the service area** by removing the rear panel or bottom cover (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations), and disconnect power again before touching any components.
4. **Inspect the microwave inverter** for burn marks, swollen capacitors, or loose connectors, and check that the harness is firmly seated.
5. **Inspect the magnetron tube** and its thermal cutout for physical damage, corrosion on terminals, or a loose ground strap.
6. **Check wiring and connectors** along the path from the inverter to the magnetron and from the inverter to the appliance manager or control board, looking for cracks, arcing, or corrosion.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (inverter or magnetron) if testing or visual inspection isolates it, then reassemble the unit, restore power, and run a microwave cook cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician immediately if F1E5 persists after a power reset or if you are not comfortable working around high-voltage microwave components. The inverter and magnetron operate at voltages that can remain stored in capacitors even after the unit is unplugged, and accidental contact can cause severe injury or death. A technician has the tools, training, and service data to safely discharge the high-voltage circuit, test the inverter and magnetron, and replace the failed part. Professional service is also the best choice if you see any arcing, burning smells, or repeated intermittent faults, because those symptoms indicate active electrical damage that must be traced and repaired under controlled conditions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200–450.
