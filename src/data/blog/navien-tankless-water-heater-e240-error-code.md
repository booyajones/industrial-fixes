## Navien E240 Error Code — What It Means

The E240 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals an exhaust overheating condition. The unit's internal sensor has detected that exhaust gases are hotter than safe operating limits, and the heater shuts down to prevent damage or a safety hazard.

This fault typically points to airflow problems that prevent hot combustion gases from leaving the unit properly. When exhaust cannot vent freely, heat builds up inside the heat exchanger and triggers the overheat protection. The system will not resume normal operation until the underlying blockage or restriction is cleared and the unit is reset.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust temperature sensor or flame rod when the real problem is a physical blockage in the vent pipe. Inspect the entire vent run for obstructions, snow or ice, bird nests, and sagging sections before ordering sensors.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked exhaust vent (~45%)** Snow, ice, leaves, bird nests, or other debris obstruct the outdoor vent terminal and trap hot exhaust gases inside the unit.
- **Restricted or improperly pitched vent pipe (~20%)** The exhaust pipe has a sag, dip, or low spot that collects condensate or debris, reducing airflow and causing heat buildup.
- **Undersized or excessively long vent run (~15%)** The vent pipe diameter is too small for the heater's BTU output, or the total length and number of elbows exceed the manufacturer's maximum, creating back pressure.
- **Failed combustion blower or weak fan motor (~12%)** The blower cannot move exhaust gases at the required speed, allowing heat to accumulate in the heat exchanger.
- **Faulty exhaust temperature sensor (~8%)** The sensor reads artificially high or sends an erratic signal, triggering the overheat code even when exhaust temperature is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or dedicated switch.
2. **Inspect the outdoor exhaust vent terminal** for visible blockages such as snow, ice, leaves, or bird nests. Clear any obstructions by hand or with a brush.
3. **Check the intake air vent** (often concentric or nearby) for blockages as well, since restricted intake can also cause incomplete combustion and overheating.
4. **Follow the exhaust pipe indoors** and look for sags, dips, or low spots where condensate or debris might collect. Vent pipe should pitch slightly upward toward the outdoors with no horizontal runs that sag.
5. **Measure the total vent length and count elbows** if you suspect the run is too long. Consult your model's installation manual for maximum allowable length and elbow equivalents.
6. **Restore power and reset the heater** by pressing the reset button or cycling power. Run a hot-water tap and observe whether the unit fires normally and the code stays cleared.
7. **If the code returns immediately**, turn off the unit and call a qualified technician to test the combustion blower motor, exhaust temperature sensor, and heat exchanger for internal blockages or component failure.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared all visible vent obstructions and confirmed proper vent pitch but the E240 code persists. A technician can measure blower pressure, test the exhaust temperature sensor with a multimeter, and inspect the heat exchanger for internal scale or debris. Because tankless heaters rely on precise gas-to-air ratios and sealed combustion, incorrect repairs can create carbon monoxide hazards. If you smell gas, see soot around the unit, or notice yellow flames instead of blue, shut off the gas supply and call for service immediately.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-50 in parts if you need a vent terminal or gasket, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
