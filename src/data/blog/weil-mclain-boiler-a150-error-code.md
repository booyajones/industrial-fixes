## Weil-McLain A150 Error Code — What It Means

The A150 code is not a universally published Weil-McLain fault code found in the manufacturer's available documentation. Weil-McLain boilers use model-specific control boards, and the same-looking code can mean different things depending on your exact boiler model and control type. Without the specific model number and control platform, the exact meaning cannot be verified.

To identify what A150 means on your boiler, locate the model number and control/display type on the appliance label, then consult that model's service manual or access the control's fault history through the diagnostics menu. Common fault categories on Weil-McLain boilers include ignition or flame-proving problems, low system pressure or circulation issues, condensate or venting restrictions on condensing models, and sensor faults such as thermistor or low-water-cutoff errors. The correct path is to pull the fault history from your control rather than guessing from the display.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace control boards or igniters without checking system pressure, circulation, or pulling the fault history first. Always verify water pressure (should be 12–15 psi on many systems) and access the diagnostics menu to read the actual stored fault before replacing any part.

## Common Causes

- **Low system pressure or water level (~30%)** Pressure below 12 psi can trigger a shutdown, and many codes appear until the system is refilled and bled of air.
- **Ignition or flame-proving failure (~25%)** Failed ignition sequence, dirty flame sensor, or interrupted gas supply prevents the burner from lighting or staying lit.
- **Circulation or air-lock issue (~20%)** A stuck circulator pump, closed valve, or air trapped in the piping stops heat from moving and can cause nuisance faults.
- **Sensor fault (thermistor or low-water cutoff) (~15%)** A failed temperature sensor or low-water-cutoff input sends incorrect readings to the control board and triggers a lockout.
- **Condensate or venting restriction (~10%)** On condensing models, a blocked condensate trap, frozen drain line, or obstructed intake/exhaust can cause the boiler to shut down.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off power and fuel** at the boiler's disconnect switch and gas valve before opening any panels or touching components.
2. **Record the exact boiler model number** from the appliance label and identify the control board or display type installed on your unit.
3. **Check system pressure** on the gauge. If it is below 12 psi, open the fill valve to bring it to about 15 psi, then bleed air from radiators or zone piping until water flows freely.
4. **Access the control's diagnostics or fault-history menu** following the steps in your model's manual. Write down all stored codes and timestamps to decode the actual fault.
5. **Verify circulation** by confirming the circulator pump is running and warm, all zone valves are open, and water is moving through the system when the boiler calls for heat.
6. **Inspect ignition and flame proving** if the fault history points to combustion. Check the igniter for cracks, clean the flame sensor rod, verify gas supply is on, and watch the burner light sequence.
7. **Test suspect sensors with a multimeter** if the diagnostics indicate a thermistor, low-water-cutoff, or pressure-switch fault. Compare resistance or continuity readings to the values in your model's service manual and replace only the failed component, then refill, purge air, restore power, and verify normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician if you cannot locate your model's fault-code definition, if the diagnostics menu is locked or unfamiliar, or if the fault points to gas supply, ignition timing, venting, or sealed combustion components. Also call a pro for any work involving gas piping, pressure-switch calibration, control-board programming, or if you have refilled the system and bled air but the code returns immediately. A technician has the model-specific manuals, meters, and combustion-analysis tools to decode A150 on your exact platform and to safely test igniters, flame sensors, thermistors, and gas valves under live conditions.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–400.
