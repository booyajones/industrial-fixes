## Maytag Range F8 Error Code — What It Means

F8 on a Maytag range signals a meat probe fault. The oven control has detected a problem with the meat probe circuit or the connection between the probe and its receptacle. Maytag's product help directs you to verify the probe is securely inserted in the receptacle, reset power, and recheck operation.

If your display shows F8 E0 instead, that is a different fault. F8 E0 means the cooling fan speed is too low. Maytag states it can be caused by airflow restriction, a faulty oven cooling fan, or associated wiring. Always check the full code on your display because the meaning changes depending on whether you see F8 alone or F8 E0.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes order a new control board when the problem is simply a dirty or loose meat probe receptacle. Always remove, inspect, and reseat the probe connection before replacing any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Meat probe not fully seated (~50%)** The probe is loose or partially unplugged from the receptacle, breaking the circuit.
- **Dirty or corroded probe receptacle (~25%)** Food residue or oxidation on the receptacle pins prevents good contact.
- **Damaged meat probe (~15%)** The probe wire or connector is bent, cracked, or has internal wire damage.
- **Faulty receptacle or wiring (~10%)** The receptacle itself or the wire harness behind it has a loose terminal or break.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker for 1 minute to reset the oven control.
2. **Remove the meat probe** from the receptacle on the oven wall or cavity.
3. **Inspect the probe connector** and receptacle pins for bent contacts, corrosion, or food debris.
4. **Clean the receptacle** with a dry cotton swab or compressed air if you see any residue.
5. **Reinsert the probe** firmly into the receptacle until it seats completely.
6. **Restore power** at the breaker and monitor the display for 1 minute.
7. **Test oven operation** without the probe. If the code does not return, test again with the probe installed and verify the oven reads probe temperature correctly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the F8 code returns after you have verified the probe is fully seated and you have reset power. A technician can test continuity in the probe circuit wiring and check whether the receptacle has a broken solder joint or loose terminal on the control board. Also call if your display shows F8 E0 instead of F8 alone. F8 E0 is a cooling fan fault that requires inspecting the fan motor, checking for airflow restrictions, and testing the fan wiring and connectors. If you are not comfortable working behind the oven or testing electrical circuits with a multimeter, a service call will save you time and avoid misdiagnosis.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-50 in parts if a new probe is needed, 10-20 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call if the receptacle or wiring needs attention.
