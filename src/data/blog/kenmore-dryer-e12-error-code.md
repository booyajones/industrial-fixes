## Kenmore Dryer E12 Error Code — What It Means

The E12 error code on Kenmore dryers is not universally defined across all models. Different Kenmore dryer lines may use E12 to indicate different faults, ranging from control board communication errors to wiring or sensor issues. Some models use it for a thermistor or temperature sensor problem, while others may flag a control board internal fault or a wiring harness issue. Because Kenmore sources dryers from multiple manufacturers, the exact meaning depends on your model number and the underlying platform. Always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dryer cabinet to confirm what E12 means for your specific unit before replacing parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board when E12 appears, but the real cause is frequently a loose wire harness or corroded connector. Inspect and reseat all harness plugs at the control board and sensors before buying any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wire harness connector (~35%)** A poor connection between the control board and sensors or other components can trigger a communication fault code.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** On some Kenmore models, E12 indicates an open or shorted thermistor circuit that monitors drum temperature.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~20%)** Chafed or broken wires between the control board and sensors can create intermittent or permanent fault signals.
- **Faulty main control board (~15%)** Internal failures in the electronic control board can generate false error codes or prevent proper communication.
- **Incorrect voltage or power supply issue (~5%)** Low or unstable voltage at the dryer outlet can cause the control board to throw errors during startup or mid-cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and turn off the circuit breaker to eliminate all power before working inside.
2. **Remove the top panel or front panel** to access the control board (consult your model's service manual for the correct disassembly sequence).
3. **Locate and photograph** all wire harness connectors on the main control board so you can reassemble them correctly.
4. **Disconnect and reseat** each connector firmly, inspecting the pins for corrosion or damage and cleaning with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Check the wiring harness** along its entire length for chafing, cuts, or pinch points where wires run near sharp metal edges or moving parts.
6. **Test the thermistor or temperature sensor** (if your model's diagram shows E12 relates to temperature) by measuring resistance at room temperature and comparing to your model's specification table.
7. **Reassemble the dryer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to see if the error clears; if E12 persists and all connections are good, replace the control board or the specific sensor identified in your wiring diagram.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connectors or if the wiring diagram is unclear for your model. A technician can quickly identify whether E12 is a sensor, harness, or control board issue and has the tools to test components under load. Also call a pro if you have already reseated connectors and tested sensors but the error persists, since control board replacement on some Kenmore models requires soldering or special programming.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
