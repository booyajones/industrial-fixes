## Frigidaire Washer EB1 Error Code — What It Means

EB1 on a Frigidaire washer indicates the control board believes the incoming line frequency is outside its acceptable range. This is a power-supply fault, not a water-fill, drain, or door-lock issue. North American washers expect 60 Hz supply. The code appears when the washer detects frequency variation, unstable power, or when the board itself has become sensitive or failed and reports a false fault even when household power is normal.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many replace the main control board first without checking household power. Measure the supply frequency and voltage at the receptacle with a meter before ordering any part, because EB1 is fundamentally a line-frequency detection fault.

## Common Causes

- **Incorrect or unstable power supply frequency (~40%)** The branch circuit or utility supply delivers power outside the 60 Hz standard or with significant variation, triggering the fault.
- **Control board sensitivity or failure (~35%)** The main control board falsely reports EB1 even when household power is acceptable, due to failed components or poor solder joints.
- **Loose power connections or intermittent wiring (~15%)** Damaged power cord, loose plug fit, or intermittent wiring at the inlet causes brief interruptions the control interprets as invalid supply.
- **Power-quality issues (~10%)** Abnormal frequency variation, voltage sag, or intermittent supply problems from the utility or shared branch circuits affect washer operation.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and unplug the washer from the wall receptacle.
2. **Measure the receptacle** with a multimeter capable of checking AC frequency and voltage, comparing against the washer's nameplate requirements and 60 Hz North American standard.
3. **Inspect the power cord and plug** for damage, loose fit, or signs of arcing, and check that the receptacle itself is tight and the breaker is not weak or tripped.
4. **Plug the washer back in** and restart it to see if EB1 clears after a power cycle, which can reset a transient fault.
5. **If household power is normal and EB1 persists**, turn off power again, remove the washer's rear or top panel, and locate the main control board.
6. **Inspect the control board** for visible damage, burnt relay contacts, or poor solder joints, and replace the board if defects are found or if all supply checks pass.
7. **Reassemble the washer**, restore power, and run a test cycle to confirm the fault is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you do not own a meter capable of measuring AC frequency, if you are uncomfortable working with 120 V or 240 V supply circuits, or if EB1 persists after you have verified normal household power and completed a power cycle. A technician can confirm the supply frequency against the washer's requirements, inspect the control board for subtle failures, and replace it safely. Because EB1 is a line-frequency detection fault rather than a mechanical component failure, professional diagnosis of both the household electrical system and the washer's internal control is often the fastest path to a reliable fix.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $150-250 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
