## Weil-McLain Boiler A173 Error — What It Means

A173 is not a universal field service error code documented across all Weil-McLain boilers. The exact fault behind A173 depends on your specific model and control board. Weil-McLain uses model-specific diagnostic code structures, so you must consult your boiler's manual or retrieve the stored fault history from the control menu to identify what A173 means on your unit.

Based on typical Weil-McLain lockout patterns, codes in this family often point to ignition failures, flame-proving faults, sensor errors, or combustion airflow problems. The control may display A173 as a stored fault rather than the current active condition, so checking the fault-history menu is the first step before testing any component.

## Before You Replace Anything

Technicians sometimes replace the ignitor or flame sensor by guesswork. Always retrieve the exact fault from the control's stored history and measure gas inlet pressure with a manometer during the ignition attempt before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Ignition or flame-sensor failure (~35%)** A dirty, damaged, misaligned, or weakened ignitor or flame rod prevents the control from proving flame during the start sequence.
- **Low or no gas pressure (~25%)** Insufficient gas inlet pressure, a partially closed shutoff valve, or regulator problems stop the burner from lighting or maintaining flame.
- **Combustion airflow or pressure-switch fault (~20%)** A blocked vent, failed blower, or stuck pressure switch prevents the control from proving safe combustion air before ignition.
- **Temperature or water-level sensor error (~12%)** A faulty thermistor or low-water sensor sends a false high-limit or dry-fire signal that locks out the boiler.
- **Poor grounding or polarity (~8%)** Missing or corroded ground connections and reversed line polarity interfere with reliable flame sensing and ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control board** by reading the rating plate and control label, then locate the matching service manual online or in your installation packet.
2. **Retrieve the stored fault history** from the control menu by following the button sequence in your manual, and write down the exact fault name or number alongside A173.
3. **Verify the thermostat is calling for heat** and all external safeties (rollout switches, low-water cutoffs, manual resets) are closed and not tripped.
4. **Observe a full start-up attempt** and note where the sequence stops: during pre-purge, at ignitor spark, after the gas valve opens, or during flame proving.
5. **Measure gas inlet pressure** with a manometer while the boiler attempts to fire, and compare the reading to the spec on your rating plate (do not rely on smelling gas).
6. **Inspect the ignitor and flame sensor** for cracks, corrosion, soot buildup, correct gap, and secure wire connections, and clean or replace as needed.
7. **Check combustion air and venting** by confirming the blower spins freely, the vent is clear of blockage or ice, and the pressure switch hose is not cracked or loose.
8. **Test the ground connection** by verifying continuity between the boiler chassis and the grounding screw on the electrical panel, and confirm correct line polarity with a plug tester.
9. **Clear and inspect the condensate trap** on condensing models for blockage, freezing, or a dry trap that breaks the seal.
10. **Reset the control** only after correcting the identified fault, then monitor the next few cycles to confirm the lockout does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician if you cannot retrieve the exact fault behind A173 from your control, if gas pressure measurement or adjustment is required, or if the ignition sequence fails after you have cleaned the ignitor and flame sensor. Gas appliance work demands proper combustion testing, pressure regulation, and venting knowledge to avoid carbon monoxide hazards and code violations. A qualified technician will use a manometer, combustion analyzer, and your model's service manual to diagnose the root cause, test each component in the manufacturer's recommended order, and verify safe operation after repair.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–400.
