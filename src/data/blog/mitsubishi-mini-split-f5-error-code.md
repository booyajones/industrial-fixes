## Mitsubishi Mini Split F5 Error — What It Means

An F5 error code on a Mitsubishi mini split typically signals a communication issue between the indoor and outdoor units, or a sensor fault within the system. The exact definition can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for your specific unit. The fault may be triggered by loose or damaged wiring in the control cable that links the indoor evaporator to the outdoor condenser, by a failed temperature sensor, or by a problem on the main control board.

Because mini splits rely on low-voltage communication circuits between the two halves of the system, even a small break in the signal path will interrupt operation and throw the code. The system will typically shut down or refuse to start until the fault is cleared. Diagnosing an F5 requires checking connections, testing sensor resistance, and sometimes swapping control boards.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the indoor control board when the real problem is a loose terminal or corroded wire at the outdoor unit. Inspect and clean all low-voltage connections before ordering any boards.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or damaged communication wiring (~40%)** The control cable between indoor and outdoor units may have a loose terminal, a corroded connection, or physical damage that interrupts the signal.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~25%)** A thermistor on the indoor coil or outdoor heat exchanger can drift out of range or fail open, causing the control board to throw the code.
- **Indoor control board failure (~20%)** The main PCB in the indoor unit can fail due to power surges, moisture intrusion, or age, disrupting communication with the outdoor unit.
- **Outdoor control board failure (~10%)** The outdoor board may stop responding if damaged by lightning, voltage spikes, or component wear over time.
- **Transient glitch or voltage sag (~5%)** A brief power interruption or brownout can confuse the microprocessor and trigger a false fault that clears after a full power cycle.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch for both the indoor and outdoor units, and wait at least two minutes to let the system reset.
2. **Remove the indoor unit cover** by lifting the front panel or unscrewing side fasteners, depending on your model, to access the control board.
3. **Inspect the communication wire terminals** on the indoor board for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks, and re-seat or clean any suspect connections.
4. **Check the outdoor unit** by removing the service panel and inspecting the low-voltage terminal strip where the control cable from the indoor unit connects.
5. **Test temperature sensors** with a multimeter set to resistance mode, comparing readings to the thermistor table in your service manual for your operating temperature range.
6. **Replace faulty sensors or boards** as indicated by your testing, taking care to match model and part numbers exactly.
7. **Restore power** and observe the unit through one full cycle to confirm the error code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical connections or if the error persists after re-seating wiring and cycling power. Diagnosing control boards and sensors requires a multimeter and knowledge of the correct resistance values for your model. Because mini splits use refrigerant and high-voltage components, attempting repairs beyond basic wiring checks can be dangerous and may void your warranty. A technician will have the service manual, proper test equipment, and genuine Mitsubishi parts to resolve the fault safely.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
