## Frigidaire Oven F3 Error — What It Means

The F3 error code on a Frigidaire oven indicates a temperature-sensing fault in the oven sensor circuit. Frigidaire groups F3 as a communication or sensor-related error on ranges. In practical terms, the electronic oven control is not receiving a valid signal from the oven temperature sensor (RTD). This can happen because the sensor itself has failed, because the wiring harness between the sensor and the control board is damaged or has a loose connection, or because the electronic oven control board has a failed input circuit.

The fault may appear during normal baking, during self-clean, or at startup. The oven will usually stop heating and display F3 when the control detects an out-of-range resistance value or an open or shorted sensor circuit. The code points to a problem somewhere in the temperature-sensing loop, and isolating the failed component requires testing the sensor resistance, inspecting the harness connections, and checking continuity back to the control.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic oven control board first without testing the sensor. Measure the oven temperature sensor resistance at room temperature (should be around 1100 Ω) and inspect the harness connections before buying a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Defective oven temperature sensor (RTD) (~55%)** The sensor may drift out of range, fail open, or short internally so the control sees an invalid resistance and throws F3.
- **Loose, oxidized, or burnt connector (~20%)** The connector at the sensor or at the control board may have loose terminals, corrosion, heat damage, or burnt pins that interrupt the signal.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Frayed insulation, broken wires, or an open circuit between the sensor and the control board will prevent the control from reading the sensor even when the sensor itself is good.
- **Failed electronic oven control (EOC) board (~10%)** If the sensor and harness both test correctly but F3 persists, the input circuit on the control board has likely failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** to the oven at the circuit breaker or by unplugging the range before touching any wiring or components.
2. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** (RTD). It is typically mounted on the rear wall inside the oven cavity with a metal probe and a two-wire connector.
3. **Inspect the sensor connector and the harness** for loose terminals, burnt pins, corrosion, frayed insulation, or heat damage at both the sensor end and where the harness plugs into the electronic oven control board on the back of the range.
4. **Measure the sensor resistance** at room temperature using a multimeter set to ohms. Disconnect the sensor plug and measure across the two sensor terminals. The RTD should read about 1100 Ω at room temperature.
5. **If the sensor resistance is wrong, replace the oven temperature sensor.** If the sensor reads correctly at the sensor but the reading is wrong at the control-board connector, the problem is in the harness between the two points.
6. **If the sensor and harness both test good, check continuity** of each wire from the sensor plug back to the control-board plug to confirm there is no open or high-resistance fault in the wiring.
7. **If sensor, connections, and wiring all check out but F3 returns, replace the electronic oven control board.** Verify the new control is programmed or configured for your model if required, then restore power and test.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with the oven's rear panel and wiring, if the error returns after you have replaced the sensor and inspected the harness, or if you are uncertain how to safely disconnect power and test continuity with a multimeter. A qualified appliance technician has the tools to isolate sensor, harness, and control-board faults quickly and can confirm the repair without risking further damage to the oven control or wiring. If your range is still under warranty, contact Frigidaire or an authorized service provider before replacing any parts yourself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-60 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
