## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E75 Error — What It Means

The E75 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with system sensors or water flow detection. Because error code definitions vary by model and firmware version, the exact meaning can differ. Consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover to confirm what E75 means for your specific model.

In many cases, the code appears when the unit cannot verify proper flow or temperature conditions. This can stem from a clogged inlet screen, a faulty flow sensor, a temperature sensor reading outside normal range, or scale buildup restricting water passages. The unit stops heating to protect itself until the condition clears.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a dirty inlet filter or a flow sensor that just needs cleaning. Always inspect and clean the inlet screen and check flow rates before ordering any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screen (~35%)** Sediment and mineral deposits block the mesh screen at the cold water inlet, reducing flow below the minimum threshold the unit needs to fire.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~25%)** The turbine or hall-effect flow sensor fails to send accurate signals to the control board, preventing the heater from confirming water movement.
- **Temperature sensor out of range (~20%)** An inlet or outlet thermistor reads extreme or implausible values due to drift, corrosion, or a loose connection, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Scale buildup in heat exchanger (~12%)** Hard water deposits restrict flow passages inside the heat exchanger, causing uneven flow rates or hot spots that confuse the sensors.
- **Low incoming water pressure (~8%)** Supply pressure below the unit's minimum specification prevents sufficient flow to activate the heater, and the board logs an error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close both the cold water inlet and hot water outlet isolation valves on the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners, then locate the inlet filter screen on the cold water supply fitting.
3. **Unscrew the inlet filter housing** using an adjustable wrench, pull out the mesh screen, and rinse it thoroughly under running water to remove sediment.
4. **Inspect the flow sensor** (usually mounted in-line near the inlet) for debris or mineral buildup on the turbine or magnetic rotor, and clean gently with a soft brush if accessible.
5. **Check all thermistor connectors** at the inlet and outlet pipes for corrosion or loose pins, and reseat each plug firmly.
6. **Reinstall the inlet screen** and tighten the housing, then open the isolation valves slowly and check for leaks at all fittings.
7. **Restore power** at the breaker, open a hot-water faucet to initiate flow, and observe the display panel to confirm the E75 code has cleared and the unit fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you cannot access the inlet filter or flow sensor, if the error persists after cleaning and sensor inspection, or if you smell gas or see signs of a water leak inside the unit. Professional help is also warranted when you lack a multimeter to test sensor resistance, when the heat exchanger needs descaling with pump equipment, or when the control board shows physical damage. A technician can run full diagnostics, flush the heat exchanger with descaling solution, and safely replace sealed components without voiding your warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
