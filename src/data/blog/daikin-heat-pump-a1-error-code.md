## Daikin A1 Error Code — What It Means

The A1 error code on a Daikin heat pump indicates an indoor PCB defect or malfunction of the indoor unit control board. This code appears when the indoor microcomputer either fails to boot properly, detects an internal board fault, or is disrupted by external electrical problems such as power supply abnormalities or electrical noise. The code can be retrieved from the indoor unit display or through the remote control's self-diagnosis function.

In most cases, A1 points to a hardware failure within the indoor control board itself, though external electrical disturbances can sometimes trigger the fault. Daikin's official error code documentation identifies the indoor PCB as the defective component, making board replacement the standard corrective action once other contributing factors have been ruled out.

## Common Causes

- **Defective indoor PCB or microcomputer failure** The control board has suffered component failure, damaged solder joints, or internal circuit faults that prevent normal operation.
- **External electrical noise or interference** Power line disturbances, nearby electrical equipment, or electromagnetic interference disrupt the microcomputer's operation and trigger the fault.
- **Unstable or incorrect incoming power supply** Voltage fluctuations, brownouts, or incorrect supply voltage stress the PCB and can cause the microcomputer to malfunction or shut down.
- **Moisture intrusion or corrosion on the board** Water leaks, high humidity, or condensation damage PCB traces and components, leading to intermittent or permanent control failures.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections at the PCB** Poor terminal contact or oxidized connectors prevent proper signal transmission and can mimic or cause genuine PCB faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the error code** by checking the indoor unit display or using the remote control's self-diagnosis function to confirm A1 is the active fault.
2. **Cycle power** by turning off the unit at the circuit breaker for at least 60 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the fault reappears immediately or after operation.
3. **Check incoming power supply** to the indoor unit with a multimeter, confirming voltage is stable and within the manufacturer's specified range for your model.
4. **Inspect the indoor PCB** by removing the cover and looking for burnt components, discolored areas, loose connectors, visible corrosion, or signs of moisture damage.
5. **Reseat all wiring connectors** at the indoor PCB and indoor unit assembly, ensuring each terminal is clean, dry, and making firm contact, then retest the system.
6. **Replace the indoor control board** if the fault persists after confirming proper power and wiring, as A1 indicates a PCB defect that cannot typically be repaired in the field.
7. **Clear the fault and run a test cycle** after replacement by restoring power and operating the unit through a full heating or cooling cycle to confirm normal operation and no return of the A1 code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical components, if you cannot safely access the indoor unit control board, or if the fault returns after a power cycle. PCB replacement requires handling sensitive electronics, matching the correct part number to your model, and verifying proper system operation afterward. A qualified technician has the tools to test incoming power quality, inspect for hidden moisture or wiring damage, and make sure the new board is installed and configured correctly. If your unit is under warranty, professional installation of the replacement PCB is often required to maintain coverage.
