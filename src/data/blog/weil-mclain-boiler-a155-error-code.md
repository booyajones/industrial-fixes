## Weil-McLain A155 Error — What It Means

A155 is not a universal fault code across all Weil-McLain boilers. The exact meaning depends on your boiler model and control family. In most cases where technicians see this code or similar display, it points to a failed ignition or light-off attempt. The boiler called for heat, opened the ignition sequence, but did not prove flame and locked out. Your boiler's fault-history screen and model-specific manual will show the actual lockout reason rather than a generic shorthand.

Common triggers include no gas flow, a blocked or dirty flame sensor, a failed ignitor, gas valve issues, or control and wiring problems in the ignition chain. Always check your manual's diagnostics section and read the stored fault history instead of guessing from a shortened code. Repeated resets without fixing the root cause can damage components or create a safety hazard.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the ignitor or gas valve first. Pull and inspect the flame sensor for corrosion or soot buildup before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~40%)** Soot, corrosion, or mineral deposits on the flame rod prevent the control from proving flame even when the burner lights.
- **No gas supply or closed valve (~25%)** The manual shutoff is closed, the meter is off, or the gas line has air in it after recent work.
- **Failed ignitor or hot surface ignitor (~15%)** The ignitor is cracked, corroded, or has burned out and cannot light the gas.
- **Gas valve not opening (~10%)** The valve coil is faulty, the valve is stuck, or the control is not sending the signal to open.
- **Ignition control or board fault (~7%)** The control module is not sequencing properly, has a failed relay, or bad wiring connections.
- **Grounding or wiring fault (~3%)** Loose connectors, corroded terminals, or a missing ground path interrupt the flame-proving circuit.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model and control** from the rating plate or inside the manual cover.
2. **Read the fault history or status screen** on the control to confirm the actual lockout reason instead of relying on shorthand.
3. **Check the manual gas shutoff valve** at the boiler and at the meter to confirm both are fully open.
4. **Power-cycle the boiler** and attempt a single reset if your manual permits it. Watch the startup sequence.
5. **Observe the ignitor and burner** during startup. The ignitor should glow bright orange, the inducer should run, and the gas valve should click open.
6. **Pull and inspect the flame sensor** if the burner lights but locks out immediately. Clean the rod with fine sandpaper or steel wool and reinstall.
7. **Test or replace the ignitor** if it does not glow or is cracked. Verify wiring connections and resistance if you have a meter.
8. **Stop repeated resets** if the fault returns. Use your model's fault tree or call a licensed technician to avoid damaging the control or creating a safety hazard.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas, if the fault returns after cleaning the flame sensor, or if you cannot safely access the ignition components. Professional diagnosis is required for gas valve replacement, control board faults, or any situation where the boiler has locked out more than twice. Repeated resets without fixing the root cause can damage the control or create a carbon-monoxide hazard. Weil-McLain recommends using the model-specific fault tree and service literature rather than guessing, and warranty parts claims require a contractor to document the serial number and failed component.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
