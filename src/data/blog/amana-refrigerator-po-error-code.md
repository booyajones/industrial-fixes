## Amana Refrigerator PO Error Code — What It Means

On Amana refrigerators, PO is a Power Outage indicator. The control detected that power to the refrigerator was interrupted and that the freezer temperature rose to 18°F (-8°C) or higher before power returned. This is usually not a failure code by itself. It is an alert that the unit lost power and may need acknowledgment on the user interface. On these Whirlpool-family platforms used by Amana and KitchenAid, the alert typically clears after the user acknowledges the outage on the control panel. If the refrigerator is cooling normally after power returns, no repair is required.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some owners replace the main control board thinking PO is a failure code. In most cases, PO is simply a notification that a real power interruption occurred and the alert clears by pressing Confirm on the control panel.

## Common Causes

- **Utility outage or brownout (~40%)** A brief or extended power interruption from the local utility triggered the outage detection and the alert remains until you acknowledge it.
- **Tripped breaker or home electrical interruption (~30%)** A circuit breaker trip or other home electrical event cut power to the refrigerator long enough for the freezer to warm past 18°F.
- **Refrigerator unplugged during cleaning or moving (~15%)** The unit was briefly unplugged for cleaning, repositioning, or service, allowing the freezer temperature to rise and trigger the PO alert.
- **Loose outlet connection or damaged cord (~10%)** A loose wall receptacle, worn plug, or damaged power cord created an intermittent supply that caused short power losses.
- **False detection by control board (~5%)** If PO repeats without any known outage, the main control or power-monitoring circuit may be falsely detecting supply interruptions.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the display reads PO** and not a different flashing fault code or cooling-off state.
2. **Check whether a real power interruption occurred** by reviewing your home for a recent utility outage, tripped breaker, or any time the refrigerator was unplugged.
3. **Acknowledge the outage** by pressing the MEASURED FILL button under Confirm on the control panel until the PO code clears from the display.
4. **If the code does not clear**, cycle power to the refrigerator by unplugging it or turning off the dedicated breaker for at least one minute, then restore power and attempt to clear the alert again.
5. **Inspect the power cord and wall receptacle** for looseness, burned marks, damage, or an intermittent connection that could cause brief power losses.
6. **Verify the refrigerator is cooling** by checking that the freezer and fresh-food compartments return to normal temperatures within a few hours after power is restored.
7. **If PO keeps returning without a known outage**, consult a technician to evaluate the main control board or user interface for false outage detection or a power-monitoring circuit fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the PO code returns repeatedly without any known power outage or if you cannot clear the alert by pressing Confirm and cycling power. A pro can test the main control board and power-monitoring circuit to determine whether the control is falsely detecting outages. Also call for service if the refrigerator is not cooling after the PO alert, because the power interruption may have uncovered or caused a separate refrigeration issue such as ice build-up or a failed evaporator fan that requires diagnosis beyond the scope of the PO alert itself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about No cost if clearing the alert resolves the issue. A pro service call runs about $100–250 if a control board or power cord needs replacement for recurrent false alerts.
