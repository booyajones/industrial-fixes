## Maytag d7 Error Code — What It Means

The d7 error code on a Maytag washer signals a Slow Fill fault. The control board has detected that the water level inside the tub is not rising to the target level within the expected time window (typically 10 to 15 minutes depending on your model). When the inlet valves open and the water level sensor or pressure switch does not register the required fill volume after that duration, the cycle stops and displays d7.

This code is almost always caused by restricted water supply or inlet valve issues rather than electronic sensor failures. The washer is trying to fill but something is limiting the flow rate so severely that it cannot complete the fill cycle on time.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the entire inlet valve assembly when the real problem is just sediment blocking the mesh screens at the valve inlet ports. Always disconnect the hoses and inspect the screens before ordering new valves.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet screens (~45%)** Sediment, sand, or filter debris from the building plumbing accumulates on the fine mesh screens inside the washer's inlet valve ports and restricts water flow below the minimum fill rate.
- **Supply valves not fully open (~25%)** The hot or cold water shut-off valves at the wall are partially closed or have internal sediment buildup that limits flow even when turned.
- **Kinked or frozen inlet hoses (~15%)** The supply hoses are physically pinched behind the washer or frozen internally in cold locations, blocking water flow.
- **Low building water pressure (~10%)** The home's water pressure is below the minimum required (typically below 20 psi) or a flood-safe mixing valve is restricting flow.
- **Failed inlet valve solenoid (~5%)** The hot or cold inlet valve solenoid is not opening fully or is mechanically stuck, preventing normal water flow even when powered.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Cancel the cycle and perform a hard reset.** Press Power or Cancel to stop the washer. Unplug the machine (or turn off the breaker) for one full minute, then restore power. Restart the cycle and watch for d7. If it clears and fills normally, the fault was transient.
2. **Verify both supply valves are fully open.** Go to the wall shutoff valves behind the washer. Turn each valve counter-clockwise until it stops to confirm full flow. If either was partially closed, open it completely and restart the cycle.
3. **Inspect and clean the inlet screens.** Turn off the supply valves. Disconnect the hot and cold inlet hoses from the back of the washer. Look inside each inlet port on the washer for a fine mesh screen. Use a small pick, needle-nose pliers, or a soft brush to remove any visible sediment, rust, or debris. Flush the screens with water if needed. If screens are damaged, replace the inlet valve assembly.
4. **Check the inlet hoses for kinks or freezing.** Examine the full length of both hoses. Straighten any kinks or twists. If the washer is in a cold location (garage, basement), feel the hoses for ice or freezing. Thaw or replace frozen hoses.
5. **Test the inlet valves during a fill cycle.** Reconnect the hoses and turn the supply valves back on. Start a new wash cycle and listen at the back of the washer. You should hear both inlet valves click open and water flowing. If one valve is silent or water flow is very weak, that valve solenoid may be stuck or failed and needs replacement.
6. **Measure building water pressure if available.** If you have a water pressure gauge, attach it to an outdoor spigot or laundry faucet. Normal residential pressure is 40 to 80 psi. If pressure is below 20 psi, the building supply is the problem and you may need a plumber to address low pressure or a faulty pressure regulator.
7. **Replace the inlet valve assembly if all screens and hoses are clear.** If the screens are clean, hoses are not kinked, supply valves are open, and building pressure is adequate but d7 persists, the inlet valve solenoids are likely faulty. Order a replacement inlet valve assembly for your model, disconnect power and water, remove the old valve (usually two screws and hose clamps), install the new valve, and reconnect everything. Test the cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have checked and cleaned the inlet screens, confirmed the supply valves are fully open and the hoses are not kinked, yet the d7 error still appears and you measured adequate building water pressure. A technician can test the inlet valve solenoids with a multimeter, verify the pressure switch or water level sensor is functioning correctly, and rule out control board faults. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with water connections or if the washer is still under warranty, since DIY repairs can void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if valve replacement is needed, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-220 service call.
