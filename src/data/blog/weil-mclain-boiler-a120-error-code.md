## Weil-McLain Boiler A120 Error — What It Means

A120 is not a universally documented error code across all Weil-McLain boilers. It appears as a fault history entry on certain control platforms, and the exact meaning depends on your specific boiler model and control board. Weil-McLain stores past lockouts and faults in a diagnostics menu, and the technician must retrieve the full fault name from that menu rather than relying on the shorthand code alone.

Because fault codes are control-specific, the first step is to identify your exact boiler model and CP number from the rating plate, then enter the diagnostics or fault history screen to read the complete fault description. Once you know the full fault name, consult your model's installation and service manual for the approved corrective steps. Common underlying issues include safety limit trips, temperature sensor faults, pressure or flow proving errors, venting problems, condensate system blockages, or gas supply issues, but the exact cause will be clear only after you decode A120 on your specific unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Do not replace control boards or limit switches until you decode the full fault name from the diagnostics menu and test the specific sensor or safety device the fault identifies.

## Common Causes

- **Control-specific fault not decoded (~40%)** A120 is a history entry whose meaning varies by model and control, so the exact cause cannot be determined without accessing the diagnostics menu.
- **Safety limit trip (~25%)** High-limit or blocked-vent switches may have opened and logged a fault, requiring manual reset and inspection of venting or airflow.
- **Temperature sensor fault (~15%)** A faulty supply, return, or outdoor temperature sensor can generate a fault code that appears in the history log.
- **Pressure or flow proving error (~10%)** Circulator failure, low water pressure, or a failed pressure switch can trigger a lockout that is recorded as a history entry.
- **Condensate system blockage (~5%)** On condensing models, a clogged condensate trap or drain can cause a fault related to pressure or venting.
- **Gas supply or ignition fault (~5%)** Interrupted gas flow or ignition problems may generate a fault that is stored in the control's history.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify the exact boiler model and control.** Locate the rating plate on the boiler and write down the complete model number and CP number.
2. **Enter the diagnostics or fault history menu.** Follow the control's instructions to access the fault log and scroll to the A120 entry to read the full fault name.
3. **Consult the model-specific service manual.** Use the complete fault description to find the approved corrective steps, safety checks, and any reset procedures in the manual.
4. **Inspect the related safety and operating inputs.** Depending on the decoded fault, check temperature sensors, limit switches, pressure or flow proving devices, venting, condensate drainage, gas supply, and circulator operation.
5. **Test and replace only the identified failed component.** Use a multimeter or other diagnostic tool to verify the faulty part, then replace it with the correct Weil-McLain part number for your model.
6. **Clear the fault history and verify operation.** After the repair, reset the control, confirm the boiler runs through a full cycle without error, and check that A120 no longer appears in the active fault list.
7. **Document the repair.** Record the fault name, the part replaced, the part number, and the date for warranty and future service reference.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed heating professional if you cannot access the diagnostics menu, if the full fault name points to a gas, venting, or sealed-system problem, or if testing sensors and switches requires tools or expertise you do not have. Weil-McLain directs contractors to use the fault history and model-specific manuals to diagnose and repair faults, and warranty service requires documentation of the failed part description and part number. A qualified technician will decode A120 on your specific control, perform the necessary safety checks, replace only the component identified by testing, and make sure the boiler returns to safe operation without introducing new faults.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
