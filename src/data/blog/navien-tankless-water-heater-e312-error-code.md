## Navien Tankless Water Heater E312 Error — What It Means

The E312 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with the combustion air or exhaust system. The unit has detected that the fan is not operating correctly, there is insufficient airflow, or exhaust gases cannot vent properly. This safety interlock prevents the burner from firing when proper ventilation cannot be confirmed.

The code may appear during startup or while the unit is running. Because tankless water heaters require precise combustion air management, the control board monitors fan speed and pressure switches to verify safe operation. When readings fall outside normal parameters, the system shuts down and displays E312 to protect against incomplete combustion or carbon monoxide risks.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the combustion fan motor when the real problem is a blocked vent or a failed pressure switch. Inspect all intake and exhaust pipes for obstructions and test the pressure switch continuity with a multimeter before ordering a new fan.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~35%)** Birds' nests, ice dams, snow accumulation, or lint buildup can obstruct the vent termination or horizontal runs, preventing proper exhaust flow and triggering the error.
- **Failed combustion air pressure switch (~25%)** The pressure switch monitors differential pressure across the fan and may fail to close if the diaphragm is damaged or the switch contacts are worn.
- **Faulty combustion fan motor (~20%)** Worn bearings, a failed capacitor, or motor winding failure can prevent the fan from reaching the required RPM, and the control board detects insufficient airflow.
- **Loose or disconnected vent pipe (~10%)** PVC or stainless exhaust pipes may separate at joints due to improper glue application, thermal expansion, or vibration, causing a pressure drop the board reads as a fault.
- **Control board communication fault (~10%)** Corrosion on wiring terminals or a failing control board can misread fan speed signals or pressure switch inputs, even when components are working correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Inspect the exterior vent termination** for blockages such as ice, snow, leaves, or animal nests and clear any obstructions you find.
3. **Remove the front cover** of the unit and locate the combustion fan assembly, usually mounted near the top or side of the heat exchanger.
4. **Check the vent pipes** inside the unit for any disconnected joints, cracks, or condensation pooling that could affect airflow.
5. **Test the pressure switch** by disconnecting one wire and using a multimeter set to continuity; with the fan off the switch should be open, and with the fan running it should close.
6. **Inspect the combustion fan** by spinning the impeller by hand to confirm it rotates freely without grinding or resistance.
7. **Restore power** and observe whether the fan spins up when the unit calls for heat; if the fan does not run and voltage is present at the connector, replace the fan motor, and if the fan runs but the error persists, replace the pressure switch.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical connections or gas appliances, or if you have cleared all vents and tested the pressure switch and fan but the error persists. A pro can use a manometer to measure actual pressure differentials, verify control board signals with diagnostic software, and make sure proper combustion after any repair. If the unit is still under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-200 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $180-400.
