## Samsung Dryer CG Error Code — What It Means

The Samsung dryer CG (or Clg) error code indicates the dryer has detected a clogged or restricted vent during its vent-check logic. Samsung's support page identifies Clg and Cg as a clogged vent condition. This is an airflow restriction problem, not an internal electronic fault. The dryer's sensors monitor exhaust performance and trigger the code when air cannot move freely through the lint filter, duct, or exterior hood.

The code appears when the dryer detects vent restriction during a blockage test. It does not point to a failed electronic component or control board. The machine is alerting you that something in the exhaust path is preventing proper airflow, and continuing to run the dryer in this state can cause longer dry times, overheating, and potential fire hazard from lint buildup.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or moisture sensors when they see a CG code, but Samsung guidance makes clear this is a venting issue. Before ordering any electronic parts, inspect and clean the entire exhaust path from the lint filter to the outdoor hood.

## Common Causes

- **Lint filter not fully clean** Even a thin layer of lint or fabric-softener residue on the screen reduces airflow enough to trigger the code.
- **Crushed or kinked vent hose** The flexible connector behind the dryer can collapse, crimp, or be pinched when the machine is pushed back against the wall.
- **Blocked exterior hood or stuck damper flap** Lint, bird nests, or ice can obstruct the outdoor termination, and the damper flap may stick closed or partially closed.
- **Heavy lint buildup inside the duct** Years of use without professional cleaning can pack the rigid or flex duct with lint, severely restricting airflow.
- **Disconnected or improperly routed duct** A loose joint or sagging section in the exhaust path allows air to escape and reduces flow at the sensors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the dryer** at the circuit breaker or by unplugging the cord before touching any part of the vent system.
2. **Remove and inspect the lint filter** under bright light, looking for any lint, fabric-softener residue, or blockage, then wash it with warm soapy water, rinse, dry completely, and reinstall.
3. **Pull the dryer away from the wall** and disconnect the flexible vent hose from the back of the cabinet, checking for crushing, kinks, collapse, or heavy lint buildup inside the connector.
4. **Trace the entire exhaust duct** from the dryer outlet to the exterior termination, looking for disconnected joints, sagging sections, or packed lint, and use a vent brush or vacuum to clean the interior of the duct if accessible.
5. **Inspect the exterior vent hood** outside your home for lint buildup, stuck damper flaps, bird nests, or ice, and clear any obstruction so the flap moves freely.
6. **Reconnect the vent hose securely** with metal clamps or foil tape (not plastic or vinyl duct, and avoid screws that protrude into the airflow), keeping the path as short and straight as possible.
7. **Restore power to the dryer** and run a short timed-dry cycle, monitoring airflow at the exterior hood and checking whether the CG code reappears.
8. **If the code persists after verifying a clean vent**, power off the dryer for 2-3 minutes to reset the control, then restart and re-test before calling a technician to inspect airflow sensors and related wiring.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the CG code returns after you have thoroughly cleaned the lint filter, inspected and cleared the entire duct path, verified strong airflow at the exterior hood, and power-cycled the dryer. A technician will use airflow-measurement tools and test the dryer's temperature and airflow sensors, wiring harnesses, and control logic to identify any internal fault. Also call a pro if your vent duct is very long, runs through walls or ceilings you cannot access, or if you are uncomfortable disconnecting and reconnecting ductwork. Professional vent-cleaning services use rotary brushes and high-volume vacuums to remove years of compacted lint that hand tools cannot reach.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (if duct or hood needs replacement), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for vent cleaning and inspection.
