## Maytag Oven AB Error Code — What It Means

The AB display on a Maytag oven is not a standard manufacturer error code. Maytag's official error-code documentation does not list AB as a fault. The most likely explanation is that the display is showing Sab or 5ab, which indicates Sabbath mode is active. Sabbath mode is a special operating feature that changes normal oven behavior, keeping the display minimal and disabling certain controls. It is not a failure or fault.

If the display is truly showing a different code that looks like AB, consult your model's wiring diagram or service sheet for the exact meaning. Maytag does document other fault codes such as F9 and F9 E0, but AB itself does not appear in their published fault tables.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board thinking AB is a failure code. Before ordering parts, confirm whether the display is Sabbath mode (Sab) and try canceling it from the settings or power-cycling the unit.

## Common Causes

- **Sabbath mode activated (~70%)** The control is displaying Sab or 5ab, which is often misread as AB, and simply needs to be canceled through the keypad or settings menu.
- **Power glitch or brief outage (~15%)** A voltage dip or momentary loss of power can leave the control in an unexpected state, requiring a reset to clear the display.
- **Stuck or shorted keypad button (~10%)** A stuck key or moisture in the membrane can trigger mode-selection sequences, turning on Sabbath mode without the user realizing it.
- **Misread display characters (~5%)** Partial segments or dim LEDs can make other codes or status indicators look like the letters AB.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the exact display characters.** Stand directly in front of the control panel and note whether the display shows AB, Sab, 5ab, or another combination. Take a photo if the characters are unclear.
2. **Attempt to cancel Sabbath mode.** Consult your owner's manual for the button sequence or menu option to disable Sabbath mode. Many Maytag models use a long press of a specific button or a settings menu to toggle the feature off.
3. **Power-cycle the oven.** Turn off the circuit breaker or pull the fuse for the range for at least one minute. Restore power and check whether the display clears and normal operation resumes.
4. **Inspect the keypad for stuck buttons.** Press each control button firmly and listen for a click. Look for moisture, spills, or debris around the buttons. Wipe the panel with a damp cloth and dry it thoroughly.
5. **Check electrical connections.** If the display persists, turn off power again and inspect the harness connectors at the back of the control board and at the oven temperature sensor. Reseat any loose plugs.
6. **Enter service diagnostics if available.** Some Maytag models allow you to read stored fault codes by pressing a button combination. Consult your service sheet or wiring diagram for the procedure and note any codes displayed.
7. **Contact service if the display remains.** If canceling Sabbath mode and power resets do not clear the display, and you cannot identify a true fault code, schedule a technician visit to inspect the control board and wiring harness for internal faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you cannot cancel Sabbath mode using your owner's manual instructions, if power resets do not clear the display, or if the oven fails to heat after the display is cleared. Also call for service if you do not have your model's wiring diagram and cannot identify the true fault code. A qualified appliance technician can enter manufacturer diagnostics, read stored fault histories, and test control-board outputs and sensor circuits with proper meters. If the range was recently installed and shows F9 or F9 E0 codes alongside the AB display, have a licensed electrician verify the outlet wiring before replacing oven parts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0, 2-5 min to cancel mode or reset. A pro service call runs about $100-200 service call if a true fault code requires diagnosis.
