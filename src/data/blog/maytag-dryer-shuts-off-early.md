## Maytag Dryer Shuts Off Early — What's Happening

A Maytag dryer that shuts off early is not usually tied to a specific fault code. It is a symptom that points to restricted airflow, overheating protection, moisture sensor misreading, or a power or control interruption. If the dryer stops during Auto Dry or Sensor Dry cycles, the control board thinks the load is dry based on readings from the moisture-sensing system. If the unit stops mid-cycle after a short run, it may be protecting itself from overheating or losing power to part of the machine.

On many models there is no fault code at all. The dryer simply ends the cycle or stops because of a functional shutdown rather than a diagnostic error. The issue can appear in sensor cycles only or across all cycle types depending on the root cause.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Restricted venting or poor airflow** Lint blockage in the vent hose, wall duct, or exterior hood forces the dryer to overheat and shut down early or prevents proper drying and airflow for sensor operation.
- **Lint filter contamination** Fabric softener residue or fine lint can clog the screen even when it looks clean, restricting airflow and causing early cycle termination.
- **Faulty moisture sensor** The sensor bars inside the drum can become coated with residue or fail electrically, telling the control the clothes are dry when they are still damp.
- **Overloaded drum** Too much laundry reduces tumbling and airflow, preventing accurate moisture sensing and causing the dryer to stop before the load is actually dry.
- **Thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat open** Overheating from restricted airflow or a failing component can open the thermal fuse or cycling thermostat, shutting the dryer down mid-cycle.
- **Broken drive belt** A snapped or slipping belt stops drum rotation and can interrupt the cycle, making the dryer appear to shut off early.
- **Intermittent power supply or door switch** Loose electrical connections, a tripped breaker, or a failing door switch can cut power mid-cycle and mimic an early shutoff.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify whether the issue happens in Auto Dry or Sensor Dry cycles only, or also in Timed Dry mode.
2. Clean the lint screen thoroughly, then wash it with warm soapy water if fabric softener residue is present and let it dry completely.
3. Inspect and clean the entire vent path including the vent hose, wall duct, and exterior exhaust hood for lint blockage, kinks, or crushed ducting.
4. Reduce the load size to no more than three-quarters full and confirm proper tumbling and airflow during operation.
5. Clean the moisture sensor bars inside the drum with rubbing alcohol and a soft cloth, then test a sensor cycle again.
6. Check the thermal fuse, cycling thermostat, and high-limit thermostat for continuity if overheating or airflow restriction is suspected.
7. Verify proper power at the outlet, breaker, and cord connections, and test the door switch for continuity.
8. Inspect the drive belt and drum rotation if the unit starts and then stops abnormally or the drum does not turn.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if cleaning the vent system and lint filter does not resolve the issue, if you cannot safely access or test electrical components like the thermal fuse or moisture sensor, or if the dryer continues to shut off early in all cycle types after basic troubleshooting. A qualified appliance technician can test the control board, wiring harness, and thermostat circuits and repair the underlying electrical or mechanical fault safely.
