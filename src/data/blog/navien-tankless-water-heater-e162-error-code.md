## Navien E162 Error Code — What It Means

The E162 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a problem with combustion or flame sensing. The unit has detected an unsafe condition where the flame is not stable, not being sensed correctly, or the exhaust system is compromised. The heater will shut down to prevent unsafe operation.

This code can appear when the flame rod fails to confirm proper combustion, when the air/fuel mixture is incorrect, or when exhaust gases cannot vent properly. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual for model-specific details.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the flame rod or gas valve when the real problem is simply carbon buildup on the flame rod or blocked intake/exhaust vents. Clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper and inspect venting before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Flame rod contaminated (~40%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame rod prevent it from sensing the flame signal and the unit shuts down thinking combustion has failed.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~25%)** Exhaust or intake pipes obstructed by debris, ice, or animal nests cause improper combustion and the flame sensor triggers a fault.
- **Gas pressure issue (~15%)** Low or fluctuating gas supply pressure results in an unstable flame that the sensor cannot reliably detect.
- **Failed flame rod (~10%)** The flame rod itself has failed electrically and no longer generates the signal needed to confirm flame presence.
- **Combustion chamber dirty (~5%)** Soot buildup inside the chamber or on the burner surfaces alters the flame pattern and causes erratic sensing.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~5%)** Corroded connections or a failing PCB cannot process the flame signal correctly and throws the error even when combustion is normal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws; set the panel aside.
3. **Locate the flame rod** inside the combustion chamber; it is a thin metal probe near the burner assembly.
4. **Disconnect the wire** from the flame rod terminal and unscrew the rod from its mounting bracket.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or emery cloth until shiny, removing all carbon and deposits; wipe with a dry cloth.
6. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside for blockages, ice, or debris; clear any obstructions and verify proper termination clearances.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod** securely, reconnect the wire, and replace the front cover; open the gas valve and restore power, then monitor for normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and clearing vents, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a control board fault. A technician can measure gas inlet pressure, test flame current with a microamp meter, and diagnose wiring or PCB issues safely. Also call a pro if the combustion chamber shows heavy soot buildup, which indicates a fuel-air ratio problem that requires adjustment or burner service.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
