## What this code means
F21 on a Whirlpool front-load washer indicates a drain problem. The control has detected that water is not leaving the tub within the expected time window. Whirlpool states that F21 or F21/F02 codes mean drain times are longer than expected. This does not necessarily mean a single component has failed, but rather that the drain path or process is being slowed or blocked somewhere in the system.

The washer may pause mid-cycle, leave water standing in the drum, or refuse to advance to spin. The code appears because the control board waited for the water level to drop and it did not happen quickly enough. Addressing the blockage or suds issue usually clears the fault without replacing any parts.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump without checking the hose or pump filter. Pull and inspect the drain hose for kinks and clean the pump filter before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Drain hose kinked or clogged (~40%)** A pinched, twisted, or debris-blocked drain hose is the most frequent real-world cause and prevents water from leaving the tub quickly enough.
- **Pump filter or coin trap blocked (~30%)** Lint, hair, coins, and small objects accumulate in the pump filter and reduce or stop flow through the drain pump.
- **Excessive suds from non-HE detergent (~15%)** Using regular (non-HE) detergent or too much detergent creates thick foam that slows drainage and can trigger the code.
- **Drain pump failure or weak impeller (~12%)** The pump motor may run but fail to move water if the impeller is cracked, bound by debris, or the volute is worn.
- **Blocked drainpipe or utility sink (~3%)** A clog in the household standpipe or sink drain can back up and prevent the washer from draining normally.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press Pause or Cancel** on the control panel to attempt to clear the fault and allow the washer to begin draining.
2. **Unplug the washer** for at least 5 minutes to reset the control board, then restore power and re-select your cycle.
3. **Inspect the drain hose** from the back of the washer to the standpipe or sink for kinks, pinches, or internal clogs. Pull the hose off and flush it with a garden hose if needed. Confirm the hose is installed in the U-shaped holder and not submerged in standing drain water.
4. **Check detergent type and dose.** Confirm you are using only HE (high-efficiency) detergent and that you are not over-measuring. Excessive suds will slow drainage and can cause the code to return.
5. **Access the drain pump filter** (usually behind a small door at the front lower corner). Place towels on the floor, open the door, and slowly unscrew the filter cap. Remove lint, coins, hair, and any debris from the filter cavity and pump inlet.
6. **Inspect the drain pump impeller** by reaching into the filter opening (with power off). Spin the impeller by hand to check for binding, broken blades, or objects wrapped around the shaft. If the impeller does not turn freely or shows damage, the pump assembly should be replaced.
7. **Run a drain/spin or rinse/spin cycle** with the washer empty to verify water drains quickly and the F21 code does not reappear. If the code returns and all paths are clear, the drain pump motor or control board may need service-level diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a pro if you have cleaned the hose, filter, and impeller but the washer still will not drain or the F21 code returns immediately. A technician can test the drain pump electrical circuit, check the pressure switch and air trap for proper water-level sensing, and verify control board operation. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable tilting the washer to access the pump from below, or if the pump or control board needs replacement and you prefer not to disassemble the cabinet. Professional diagnosis is especially useful when multiple codes appear together or the washer has a history of intermittent faults, since those patterns can point to wiring or board-level issues rather than a simple clog.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if pump needed, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call plus parts.
