## Navien Tankless Water Heater E191 Error — What It Means

The E191 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically indicates a communication fault or flow-related problem within the system. This code can appear when the control board is not receiving proper signals from sensors or when water flow conditions are not being detected as expected. Because Navien uses multiple codes for different faults, the exact meaning of E191 may vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel for the specific definition. The heater will usually shut down or fail to ignite when this code is active, leaving you without hot water until the fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when the real problem is a dirty flow sensor or a loose connector. Clean the flow sensor and check all wiring plugs before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or failed flow sensor (~40%)** Scale, debris, or a worn turbine inside the flow sensor prevents accurate flow measurement, triggering a communication or sensor fault.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~30%)** Vibration or moisture can cause connectors at the control board or sensors to work loose, interrupting signal paths.
- **Faulty control board (~15%)** The main PCB may have a failed component or solder joint that prevents it from reading sensor signals correctly.
- **Low water flow or closed isolation valves (~10%)** Partially closed service valves or a clogged inlet filter reduce flow below the threshold needed for the heater to operate.
- **Faulty temperature sensor (~5%)** A failing inlet or outlet thermistor can send out-of-range readings that the board interprets as a communication error.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault was transient or caused by momentary low flow. Monitor for recurrence and check inlet filter screens.<br><strong>No:</strong> The issue is persistent. Proceed to inspect wiring and sensors.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are all wiring connectors at the control board and sensors firmly seated and free of corrosion?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Wiring is likely sound. Test or replace the flow sensor next.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean or reseat the loose connector. If pins are corroded, replace the connector or harness.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the flow sensor turbine spin freely when you blow through the sensor body (removed from the line)?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The sensor may still be electrically faulty. Test resistance across its terminals per the service manual or replace it.<br><strong>No:</strong> The turbine is jammed with scale or debris. Clean or replace the flow sensor.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the inlet and outlet isolation valves on the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners to access the control board and sensor wiring.
3. **Inspect all wiring connectors** at the control board, flow sensor, and temperature sensors. Reseat any loose plugs and clean corroded pins with electrical contact cleaner.
4. **Locate the flow sensor** on the cold water inlet line. Disconnect the wiring plug and unscrew the sensor from the brass housing.
5. **Remove and inspect the flow sensor**. Flush out any debris and check that the turbine spins freely. If it is stiff or the sensor body is heavily scaled, replace the sensor.
6. **Reinstall the flow sensor** with a new O-ring or gasket, reconnect the wiring plug, and open the isolation valves slowly to purge air.
7. **Restore power** and open a hot water tap. Observe the display. If the E191 code persists, test or replace the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien service technician if you are uncomfortable working with water and electrical connections at the same time, if the error returns after replacing the flow sensor and checking all wiring, or if you need to isolate a fault in the gas valve or ignition system. A pro can run board-level diagnostics, verify sensor resistance values with a multimeter, and access factory service bulletins for your specific model. Most tankless water heater repairs require both plumbing and electrical skills, so professional service is often the safest route for persistent codes.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-120 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
