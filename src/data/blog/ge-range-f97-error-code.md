## GE Range F97 Error Code — What It Means

GE's own support page states that F97 on a range or wall oven usually means that an internal component needs to be replaced, though the generic page does not name the exact failed part. In the context of GE double wall ovens, technician sources consistently tie F97 to the lower oven cooling fan or its feedback circuit. The control board flags F97 when it detects the lower cooling fan is not running, running too slowly, or the fan sensor circuit is not reporting correctly.

This code is sometimes transient and can clear after a power reset. When it returns immediately or persists after a power cycle, the lower cooling fan assembly is the most commonly replaced component. Less often, the fan sensor or feedback circuit attached to the fan is at fault, or the control board itself is misinterpreting a good fan signal.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first because F97 is vague. Always test the lower cooling fan for proper operation and check its wiring before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Lower cooling fan motor failing (~50%)** The fan motor itself may be mechanically dragging, stalling, or failing electrically so it does not reach the speed the control board expects.
- **Fan sensor or feedback circuit fault (~25%)** The sensor integrated with or attached to the fan may fail, causing the control board to see the fan as too slow or invalid even if the motor is physically turning.
- **Loose, damaged, or corroded wire connections (~10%)** Poor contact between the fan harness and the control board can interrupt the feedback signal or supply voltage to the fan.
- **Contamination or buildup binding the fan (~10%)** Grease, dust, or debris may cause the fan blades or motor to bind and run slowly, triggering the code.
- **Control board output or interpretation fault (~5%)** If the fan and wiring test good but the code persists, the control board itself may be misreading the fan signal or failing to drive the fan correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power at the circuit breaker** and wait two minutes, then restore power to see if the code clears and stays away.
2. **If F97 returns**, turn off power again and remove the oven's outer panels or access covers to reach the lower cooling fan area (consult your model's service manual for safe disassembly).
3. **Inspect the lower cooling fan** for obstructions, grease buildup, dust, or binding by gently turning the blades by hand and looking for mechanical resistance or damage.
4. **Check all wire connectors** at the fan motor and at the control board for looseness, corrosion, heat damage, or broken conductors, and repair or replace any damaged wiring.
5. **Test fan operation electrically** by verifying the fan receives the correct supply voltage when the oven calls for cooling (use a multimeter and follow your model's wiring diagram).
6. **Replace the lower cooling fan assembly** if the motor does not run when voltage is present, runs sluggishly, or if the fan sensor is integrated and cannot be serviced separately (GE part # WB26X35089 is used on several models).
7. **Replace the control board** only if the fan and wiring test good but the F97 code still appears after reassembly and a power cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt power, if you cannot safely access the lower cooling fan area behind panels, or if the fan appears to run normally but the code persists and you do not have the equipment to measure fan speed or sensor output. A technician can test fan RPM, fan feedback voltage, and control board signals to pinpoint whether the fan, sensor, or board is at fault. If your oven is still under warranty, contact GE or an authorized service center before opening the unit.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on part and labor.
