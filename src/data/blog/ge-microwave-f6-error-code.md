## GE Microwave F6 Error Code — What It Means

On GE microwaves, the F6 code means the control has detected a shorted temperature probe circuit. The electronic control believes the probe wiring or sensor is electrically shorted, so the unit may block cooking or report a fault during any temperature-cook function that relies on the probe. This is distinct from GE's separate "Probe" code, which signals an open probe or a probe not plugged in during temp cook.

The fault can appear during a probe cook cycle or sometimes at power-up if the control runs a self-check. GE explicitly documents F6 as "shorted temperature probe" and recommends service if a power reset does not clear it. The code does not apply to other brands that may use F6 for entirely different faults, so always confirm your exact GE model documentation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Always test the probe harness and connector for shorts and inspect the probe jack before replacing the board, since a damaged probe or pinched wire is far more common.

## Common Causes

- **Shorted temperature probe (~50%)** The probe element itself has an internal short circuit, causing the control to read near-zero resistance and throw F6.
- **Probe connector not fully seated or damaged (~25%)** The probe plug at the microwave wall jack is loose, corroded, or has bent pins that create a short across terminals.
- **Pinched or damaged probe harness wiring (~15%)** The wire between the probe jack and the control board is pinched, chafed, or melted, shorting hot to ground or to another wire.
- **Electronic control board fault (~10%)** The control misreads a good probe circuit as shorted because of a failed input circuit or corrupted firmware.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The probe or its connector is the likely fault. </details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Did the code clear after unplugging the microwave at the breaker for 30 seconds and plugging it back in?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault was transient or caused by a momentary short. </details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is there visible damage, melting, or corrosion at the probe plug or wall jack?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Replace the damaged component (probe or jack assembly) and clean any corrosion from the mating connector before reassembly.<br><strong>No:</strong> The short is likely internal to the probe element or in hidden wiring. ## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by switching off the circuit breaker or unplugging the microwave for at least 30 seconds to attempt a control reset.
2. **Restore power** and check if F6 reappears immediately or only during a probe cook cycle.
3. **Remove the temperature probe** from the wall jack and inspect both the probe plug and the jack receptacle for bent pins, corrosion, or melted plastic.
4. **Test the probe for a short** using a multimeter set to resistance: with the probe unplugged, measure across the probe plug terminals. A reading near zero ohms indicates a short (consult your model's service manual for the exact specification if available).
5. **Inspect the probe harness** by removing the outer wrap or cabinet panel to trace the wire from the jack to the control board, looking for pinch points, chafing, or heat damage.
6. **Replace the temperature probe** if it tests shorted or shows physical damage, ensuring the new probe is firmly seated in both the jack and the food during temp cook.
7. **Replace the control board** only if the probe and all wiring test good but F6 persists after a full power cycle and reconnection.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the microwave cabinet or if the code persists after replacing the probe and inspecting all visible wiring. GE explicitly recommends trained service for fault codes that do not clear with a power reset. A technician will use a service manual with model-specific resistance tables, perform circuit-level diagnostics at the control board, and safely handle any high-voltage or sheet-metal disassembly. Always call a pro if you lack a multimeter, cannot access the probe harness without removing screws or panels, or if the microwave is still under warranty.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–40 for a probe, 15–30 min. A pro service call runs about $100–200 including service call and probe.
