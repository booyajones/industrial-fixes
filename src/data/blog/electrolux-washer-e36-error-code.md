## Electrolux Washer E36 Error Code — What It Means

The E36 error code on an Electrolux washer indicates the machine is not draining water correctly or the control is detecting a drain-system problem. The code points to a blockage or fault somewhere in the drain path, from the filter and pump through the hose and outlet connection. In some Electrolux model families, closely related drain faults may also involve pressure-sensor or overfill issues, so the exact behavior can vary by series.

Electrolux support documentation lists the relevant causes as a blocked drain pipe, blocked or dirty filter, defective drain pump, pressure switch or sensor defect, or in rare cases a water inlet valve issue if the machine is overfilling or misreading the water level. The practical meaning is that water is not leaving the drum at the expected rate, and the machine has stopped the cycle to prevent overflow or damage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump without checking the filter and hose first. Clean the drain filter and inspect the full drain path for debris before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked drain filter or fluff filter (~45%)** Lint, coins, buttons, and debris accumulate in the filter and prevent the pump from moving water out of the tub.
- **Clogged drain hose or kinked outlet (~25%)** The drain hose may be kinked, crushed, or filled with lint and soap residue, restricting flow to the sink or standpipe.
- **Defective drain pump (~15%)** The pump may be jammed by debris, have a burned-out motor, or have failed bearings that prevent it from spinning and moving water.
- **House drain or sink trap obstruction (~10%)** The standpipe, wall connection, or sink trap may be clogged, causing backpressure that prevents the washer from draining normally.
- **Pressure switch or sensor fault (~5%)** The pressure sensor may be stuck or giving incorrect feedback, telling the control board the tub is full when it is not, or vice versa.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Switch off power** at the wall outlet or circuit breaker and unplug the washer. Wait about 30 seconds, then plug back in to perform a hard reset and clear transient faults.
2. **Locate and remove the drain filter** at the front lower panel (consult your model's manual for the exact location). Place a shallow pan underneath and slowly unscrew the filter to catch water and debris.
3. **Clean the filter thoroughly** under running water, removing all lint, coins, buttons, and any trapped objects. Inspect the filter housing and pump intake for additional debris or foreign objects.
4. **Check the drain hose** from the back of the washer to the standpipe or sink connection. Straighten any kinks, look for internal clogs, and confirm the outlet end is not submerged or blocked by a house drain clog.
5. **Test the house drain** by placing the drain hose end into a bucket or sink and running a short drain cycle. If the washer drains into the bucket, the house plumbing is the problem. If it still fails, the pump or internal path is blocked.
6. **Inspect the drain pump** through the filter opening or by tilting the machine and removing the lower panel. Spin the impeller by hand to check for jams, then test pump power (if you have a multimeter) or listen for pump hum when the drain cycle starts.
7. **Replace the drain pump** if it is jammed, noisy, or does not run when power is applied. If the pump runs but the error persists, check the pressure sensor, sump hose, and wiring for faults, or schedule a service call for advanced diagnostics.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the filter, inspected the full drain path, and confirmed the house drain is clear, but the E36 error persists. A technician can test the drain pump electrically, inspect the internal sump hose and pump connections, and check the pressure sensor and control board for faults that are not visible from the outside. Electrolux recommends an approved service visit for unresolved drain faults, especially if the machine is still under warranty or if you are not comfortable working with wiring and internal plumbing. If the error clears but returns frequently, a deeper hydraulic or sensor fault may require diagnostic tools and model-specific service documentation.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–80 in parts (filter clean is free, pump if needed), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $120–250 service call and labor.
