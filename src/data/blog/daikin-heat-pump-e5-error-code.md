## Daikin E5 Error Code — What It Means

The E5 error code on Daikin heat pumps indicates an inverter compressor motor protection or overheat condition. Daikin's diagnostic documents identify this fault as a response to compressor lock, high differential pressure, defective inverter PCB, or problems with the UVW connection between the inverter and compressor motor. The system shuts down to prevent damage when it detects the compressor is overloaded, mechanically locked, or receiving improper drive signals from the control board.

This is a protection fault, not a simple sensor reading. It means the outdoor unit has detected a real electrical or mechanical problem with the compressor circuit. The fault can stem from a failed compressor, a bad inverter board, poor wiring connections, or secondary issues like restricted airflow or abnormal refrigerant pressures that overload the compressor during operation.

## Common Causes

- **Compressor mechanical lock or overload** The compressor motor is seized, has failed internally, or cannot start due to mechanical resistance, triggering immediate protection.
- **Defective inverter PCB or outdoor control board** The inverter board sends improper drive signals to the compressor or misreads compressor current, causing a false or real overload condition.
- **UVW compressor wiring or connector contact problems** Loose, corroded, or damaged connections between the inverter and the compressor motor create high resistance or intermittent contact, triggering the fault.
- **High discharge or differential pressure** Restricted airflow, dirty outdoor coil, or refrigerant-side abnormalities increase compressor load beyond safe operating limits.
- **Wiring or sensor faults in the compressor circuit** Damaged cables, poor grounds, or faulty current sensors make the control board read an abnormal compressor condition and shut down for protection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Confirm the E5 code** from the remote control or self-diagnosis mode and note whether the fault appears immediately at startup or after the unit runs.
2. **Turn off power** at the disconnect and wait three minutes for capacitors to discharge before opening any outdoor unit panels.
3. **Inspect the compressor UVW wiring and connectors** between the inverter board and compressor terminals for corrosion, burn marks, loose pins, or damaged insulation.
4. **Check for compressor lock** by observing startup behavior when power is restored (listen for hum without rotation or immediate shutdown), then turn off power again if the fault returns instantly.
5. **Examine the outdoor coil and fan** for dirt, debris, or airflow restrictions that would cause high discharge pressure and compressor overload.
6. **Test the inverter PCB** if wiring and mechanical checks are clear, following your model's service manual for voltage and resistance values at the compressor terminals.
7. **Replace the failed component** (compressor, inverter board, or connector), clear the fault code from the system, and monitor a full heating and cooling cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician for E5 faults. This code involves high-voltage inverter circuits, refrigerant system diagnostics, and compressor testing that require specialized tools and EPA certification. Misdiagnosis can lead to expensive part replacements that don't fix the root cause. A qualified technician will use the model-specific service manual to measure compressor winding resistance, inverter output voltages, and system pressures to pinpoint whether the compressor, inverter board, or a secondary cause is at fault. Compressor replacement also requires refrigerant recovery, vacuum procedures, and proper charging, all of which are regulated and best handled by a professional.
