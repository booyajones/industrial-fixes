## Weil-McLain Boiler A56 Error — What It Means

The A56 fault is not a universal error code across all Weil-McLain boiler models, and the exact meaning depends on your specific control board and model series. Weil-McLain documentation shows that different control systems use different fault-code sets, so A56 may indicate an ignition failure, a flame-proving problem, or another sensor issue depending on whether you have an Ultra, CGa, GV90+, Evergreen, or WTGO model.

Because the code definition is not standardized, the correct first step is to identify your exact boiler model from the rating plate and consult that model's service manual or control diagnostics screen. In practice, most lockout faults on Weil-McLain boilers involve ignition problems (no flame established), flame-sensor contamination, gas-supply interruptions, or failed ignitors, and technicians typically start with those components when troubleshooting any A-series or numeric lockout code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve when the real cause is a dirty flame rod or a closed manual gas shutoff. Clean the flame rod and verify gas supply before ordering any valve.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty flame rod or flame sensor (~40%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the rod prevent the control from detecting flame, causing a lockout even when the burner lights.
- **No gas flow or closed gas valve (~25%)** A manual shutoff left closed or an empty propane tank stops fuel delivery, so the ignition sequence fails and the boiler locks out.
- **Failed ignitor (~20%)** A cracked or burned-out ignitor will not spark, the burner never lights, and the control times out on ignition attempts.
- **Low or unstable gas pressure (~10%)** Insufficient inlet pressure or a partially restricted regulator can prevent reliable ignition, triggering repeated faults.
- **Control board or wiring issue (~5%)** A loose flame-sensor connection or a control-board fault can register false lockouts even when ignition components are clean and working.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your exact boiler model** from the rating plate (Ultra, CGa, GV90+, Evergreen, WTGO, etc.) and locate the matching Weil-McLain service manual to confirm what A56 means for that control board.
2. **Turn off power and gas** to the boiler at the service switch and manual shutoff valve before opening any panels or inspecting components.
3. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual valve is fully open and that fuel is available at the meter or tank, then turn the gas back on.
4. **Remove and inspect the flame rod** (and ignitor if applicable) from the burner assembly, looking for carbon deposits, oxidation, or cracks.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a Scotch-Brite pad until the metal is bright and clean, then wipe off any residue with a dry cloth.
6. **Check the ignitor** for cracks or burn marks and replace it if damaged, then reinstall both components securely in the burner.
7. **Restore power and gas**, reset the fault through the control, and run a full heating cycle to confirm the boiler lights, proves flame, and holds steady without relocking.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if the fault returns after you have cleaned the flame rod and verified gas supply, if you are not comfortable shutting off gas or working inside the burner assembly, or if the boiler uses a sealed-combustion or direct-vent configuration that requires special tools to access ignition components. Also call a pro if you see repeated fault codes in the control's history that point to gas-valve or pressure-switch issues, because those components require a combustion analyzer and pressure gauges to diagnose safely. Any work involving the gas valve, gas pressure adjustment, or control-board replacement should be handled by a qualified heating contractor to maintain warranty coverage and local code compliance.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
