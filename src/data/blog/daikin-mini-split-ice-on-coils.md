## Daikin Mini Split Ice on Coils — What's Happening

Ice forming on your Daikin mini split's indoor coil is a symptom of an underlying problem, not a fault code by itself. Daikin systems use protection codes like A5 (freeze protection or abnormal indoor coil temperature) when the coil temperature drops outside its normal range during cooling. The system enters this protection state to prevent damage, but the ice itself tells you that either air isn't moving over the coil properly or the refrigerant side is running too cold.

The most common scenario is restricted airflow. When the indoor coil doesn't get enough air moving across it, the refrigerant inside stays cold longer and moisture from the air freezes on the fins. Less often, low refrigerant charge or a faulty coil temperature sensor can also cause icing. Daikin's own documentation points to dirty filters, blocked return grilles, failed indoor fan motors, loose fan wiring, and mechanical obstructions as the leading contributors to coil freeze conditions.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Dirty or clogged air filter** A blocked filter is the most common cause of coil icing because it cuts off airflow and lets the coil temperature drop below freezing.
- **Failed or weak indoor fan motor** If the indoor blower isn't spinning or is running too slowly, airflow drops and the coil ices up even with a clean filter.
- **Blocked return air path or supply louvers** Furniture, curtains, or closed louvers can starve the unit of return air and cause the coil to freeze.
- **Faulty indoor coil thermistor or temperature sensor** A bad sensor can report an incorrect coil temperature and trigger freeze protection or allow the coil to ice without stopping the compressor.
- **Low refrigerant charge** If the system is low on refrigerant due to a leak, the coil can run too cold and ice over, but this is less common than airflow problems.
- **Loose or damaged fan motor wiring or connectors** A poor connection to the indoor fan can cause intermittent or no fan operation, leading to icing.
- **Foreign matter or debris blocking the fan** Dust buildup or an object caught in the fan wheel can stop the blower from moving air and cause the coil to freeze.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Check the Daikin error code on the indoor unit display or remote to confirm whether the system is showing A5 (freeze protection) or another fault related to airflow or temperature.
2. Turn the system off and let the coil thaw completely before doing any further work (typically 2 to 4 hours), and do not try to scrape or chip ice off the coil.
3. Remove and inspect the return air filter for dirt, dust, or blockage, then clean or replace it if needed.
4. Check that all supply and return air paths are clear, including louvers, grilles, and the area around the indoor unit, and remove any furniture or obstructions.
5. With the system off, visually confirm that the indoor fan blade spins freely by hand and that nothing is jamming it.
6. Power the unit back on and watch the indoor fan to confirm it starts and runs at full speed during operation.
7. Inspect the wiring harness and connectors between the indoor PCB and fan motor for loose pins, corrosion, or damage.
8. If airflow is good and the fan runs normally but icing returns, measure the indoor coil temperature sensor resistance (if service specs are available) or call a technician to check refrigerant charge and for leaks.

## When to Call a Pro

If you've cleaned the filter, confirmed the fan is running, and cleared all obstructions but ice still forms on the coil, the problem is likely in the refrigerant circuit or the coil temperature sensor. Low refrigerant requires leak detection, evacuation, and precise recharge with the correct Daikin refrigerant type, which needs gauges and recovery equipment. Faulty sensors often require resistance testing against factory specs and careful PCB connector work. A licensed HVAC technician can also retrieve detailed fault logs from the Daikin control board to pinpoint intermittent fan or sensor failures that aren't obvious during a quick visual check.
