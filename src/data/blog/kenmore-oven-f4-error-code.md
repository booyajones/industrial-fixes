## What this code means
The F4 error code on a Kenmore oven indicates a temperature sensor circuit fault. The control board is seeing the oven sensor as open, shorted, or otherwise out of the acceptable range, or it is losing the signal through the wiring or control connection. This fault can stem from a failed sensor probe itself, a broken or shorted wiring harness, loose or corroded connectors, or a failing electronic range control board.

The control cannot safely regulate oven temperature without a valid sensor reading, so it throws F4 and typically shuts down heating. While the exact wording of the code may vary slightly across Kenmore model families, the underlying issue is always an abnormal reading in the sensor circuit. A technician will isolate the sensor, test its resistance, inspect the harness, and verify the signal at the control board before condemning any component.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the electronic control board first when the real culprit is the sensor or a corroded connector. Measure the sensor resistance at room temperature (should read around 1100 Ω) and inspect the plug at the control before spending money on a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~60%)** The sensor probe inside the oven cavity drifts out of specification, reads open, or shorts internally so the control sees an invalid resistance.
- **Damaged or shorted wiring harness (~20%)** Wires between the sensor and control melt, pinch against a sharp cabinet edge, or short together from heat or vibration.
- **Loose or corroded connector terminals (~10%)** The plug where the sensor harness meets the control board oxidizes, loosens, or burns, creating intermittent or high resistance.
- **Failing electronic range control board (~10%)** The ERC itself has a bad sensor input circuit or solder joint, misreading a good sensor as faulty.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power at the circuit breaker** and wait five minutes to allow the control board to reset and clear any transient faults.
2. **Pull the range away from the wall** and remove the rear lower access panel or the oven back panel to expose the temperature sensor probe and its harness.
3. **Disconnect the sensor plug** at the sensor end or at the control board (whichever is easier to reach) so you can test the sensor in isolation.
4. **Set your multimeter to ohms** and measure the resistance across the two sensor terminals at room temperature. You should see approximately 1100 Ω, with an acceptable window of about 1050 to 1150 Ω.
5. **Check for continuity** between each sensor terminal and the metal mounting bracket or oven wall. You should see infinite resistance (open circuit) because the sensor must be electrically isolated from ground.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** from the sensor to the control board for melted insulation, pinched sections near cabinet edges, or burn marks. Wiggle connectors and look for corrosion or loose pins.
7. **Measure resistance at the control board connector** (sensor still disconnected at the sensor end) to verify the harness and plug are carrying the same reading. If the resistance jumps or reads open, repair or replace the harness.
8. **Replace the sensor** if it reads outside the acceptable range or shows a short to ground. Install the new probe in the same mounting hole and reconnect the plug.
9. **Restore power and run a test bake cycle** to confirm the F4 code does not return. If it does and the sensor and wiring tested good, replace the electronic range control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Most homeowners can safely replace the oven temperature sensor because it involves only disconnecting a plug, removing a mounting screw, and reversing the process with the new part. Power must be off at the breaker during the entire repair. Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working inside the range cabinet, if the harness damage extends into areas you cannot access without disassembling the control panel or oven door, or if you have replaced the sensor and inspected the wiring but the F4 code persists and you need a technician to diagnose the control board with specialized tools. Pros also carry the proper service manuals and connector repair kits for uncommon harness faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–60 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
