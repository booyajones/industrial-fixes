## Navien E024 Error Code — What It Means

The E024 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals that the exhaust temperature has exceeded safe limits. This is a safety lockout designed to prevent damage to the heat exchanger and venting system. The unit will not operate until the fault is cleared and the system resets.

Overheating exhaust usually points to restricted airflow, either on the intake side or the exhaust side. The heater needs a steady supply of combustion air and a clear path to expel exhaust gases. When either is blocked, combustion becomes inefficient and temperatures climb. The error can also appear if the venting system is too long, improperly installed, or partially obstructed by debris, ice, or blockage.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flame rod or igniter when they see an error code, but E024 is almost always an airflow or venting issue. Check and clean the intake filter and inspect the vent termination outdoors before ordering any combustion parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged air intake filter (~45%)** A dirty filter starves the burner of oxygen, causing incomplete combustion and high exhaust temperatures.
- **Blocked vent termination (~25%)** Snow, ice, leaves, or animal nests can obstruct the outdoor intake or exhaust pipes and choke airflow.
- **Vent pipe too long or improperly sized (~15%)** Exceeding maximum vent run length or using incorrect pipe diameter reduces draft and traps heat in the exhaust.
- **Faulty exhaust temperature sensor (~10%)** A failing sensor can report false high temperatures even when the exhaust is within normal range.
- **Heat exchanger scale buildup (~5%)** Mineral deposits on the heat exchanger reduce heat transfer efficiency and force exhaust temperatures higher.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit to access the air intake filter, typically located at the lower front or side of the cabinet.
3. **Pull out the air intake filter** and inspect it for dust, lint, and debris. Rinse it under warm water if reusable, or replace it if damaged or heavily soiled.
4. **Go outside** and locate the vent termination. Check both the intake and exhaust pipes for obstructions such as snow, ice, leaves, or animal nests. Clear any blockages.
5. **Inspect the vent piping** inside the home if accessible. Look for sagging, kinks, or disconnected sections. Verify that the total horizontal and vertical run does not exceed the limits in your installation manual.
6. **Reinstall the clean or new filter**, replace the front cover, restore power, and open the gas valve. Wait for the unit to complete its startup sequence.
7. **Observe the display** for the E024 code. If the code clears and the unit fires normally, the repair is complete. If the code returns, call a qualified technician to test the exhaust sensor and inspect the heat exchanger for scale.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the error persists after you have cleaned the filter and verified the vents are clear. Exhaust sensor testing requires a multimeter and knowledge of the correct resistance values for your model. If the sensor tests good, the issue may be scale buildup on the heat exchanger, which requires descaling with a pump and cleaning solution. Venting errors that stem from incorrect pipe size, excessive run length, or improper termination also require a licensed technician to measure, diagnose, and correct according to local code and manufacturer specifications.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-40 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
