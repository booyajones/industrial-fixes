## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E57 Error — What It Means

The E57 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a communication issue or sensor malfunction within the unit's control system. Because error code definitions can vary by model and firmware version, the exact meaning of E57 may differ across Ruud's product line. Some models use this code for internal communication failures between the control board and other components, while others may assign it to specific sensor circuits. Always consult your model's owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's service panel to confirm the precise meaning for your heater.

The code usually appears when the control board cannot receive proper signals from connected sensors or modules, or when a sensor reports values outside expected parameters. This can stem from loose connections, corroded terminals, damaged wiring, or a failed sensor or board component.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when the actual problem is a loose connector or corroded terminal. Inspect and clean all wiring connections and sensor plugs before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~40%)** Vibration, moisture, or age can cause connector pins to oxidize or work free from the control board or sensor terminals.
- **Failed temperature or flow sensor (~25%)** A sensor that has drifted out of calibration or developed an open circuit will send invalid signals to the control board.
- **Control board fault (~20%)** Internal circuit failures on the main control board can prevent proper communication with sensors and other modules.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Rodent damage, pinching during installation, or heat exposure can break conductors or short wires together.
- **Intermittent power supply issue (~5%)** Low or fluctuating voltage at the unit can cause the control board to log communication errors as sensors reset or drop offline.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and shut off the gas supply to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners (typically Phillips-head screws).
3. **Locate the control board** and identify all wire connectors, sensor plugs, and cable harnesses entering the board.
4. **Inspect each connector** for corrosion, burn marks, or looseness; reseat every plug firmly and spray contacts with electronics cleaner if oxidation is visible.
5. **Check the wiring harness** along its entire length for pinched, chafed, or damaged insulation, paying attention to areas near sharp edges or heat exchangers.
6. **Test each sensor** (inlet, outlet, and any flow sensors) with a multimeter set to resistance mode; compare readings to the specifications in your service manual.
7. **Replace any sensor** that reads open, shorted, or far outside the expected range; note the sensor type and connector style before ordering.
8. **Reassemble the cover**, restore power and gas, and monitor the display for a few heating cycles to confirm the error has cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot identify which sensor or connection is at fault, or if the error persists after you have reseated all connectors and tested sensors. A professional has specialized diagnostic tools to interrogate the control board's memory and pinpoint intermittent faults that are hard to replicate. Also call a pro if you see evidence of water intrusion into the control board enclosure, which can require both board replacement and leak repair to prevent recurrence.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-150 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-400.
