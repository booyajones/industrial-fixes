## State Water Heater E129 Error Code — What It Means

The E129 error code on State water heaters (part of the A. O. Smith family) indicates a combustion-air or combustion-control fault tied to the fan and air-supply side of the unit. The heater cannot establish or sustain proper combustion because airflow, gas delivery, or water flow is not in the expected range. This is treated as an ignition or combustion abnormality, not a simple temperature issue.

In practical terms, the unit has detected that something in the combustion sequence is wrong. It might be attempting ignition but failing to light, or lighting briefly and then shutting down because the air-fuel mixture is incorrect. The code protects the unit from unsafe operation when combustion conditions are out of spec.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake screens or path** Lint, dust, insects, or debris accumulate on the intake screens or inside the air inlet path, starving the combustion chamber of proper airflow.
- **Vent restriction or improper venting** Exhaust vent blockage, incorrect vent length, or termination issues prevent proper combustion air delivery or exhaust flow, causing the unit to fault.
- **Insufficient gas supply or low manifold pressure** A partially closed gas valve, undersized gas piping, or low supply pressure from the utility prevents the unit from getting enough fuel to ignite or sustain combustion.
- **Dirty inlet water filter or low flow** A clogged inlet screen or filter restricts water flow below the threshold needed for the startup sequence, triggering a combustion fault before ignition even begins.
- **Fan motor or combustion-air system problem** Debris inside the fan assembly, a failed fan motor, or a damaged blower housing prevents the unit from establishing the correct combustion-air conditions.
- **Igniter or flame-sensing issue** A cracked igniter, fouled flame sensor, or loose wiring connection causes the unit to attempt ignition without establishing or proving flame, resulting in lockout.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify model and code definition** by checking the unit's data plate and service manual to confirm E129 matches a combustion-air or ignition fault for your specific State model, since code mapping can vary by platform.
2. **Confirm safe operating conditions** by checking for any gas odor, ensuring power is on, and inspecting the exterior vent termination and air intake for visible blockage, snow, or debris.
3. **Inspect and clean air intake screens and fan inlet path** by removing the intake cover and brushing or vacuuming away lint, dust, insect nests, or other obstructions that restrict combustion airflow.
4. **Check inlet water filter and flow path** by shutting off the cold water supply, removing the inlet filter screen, and cleaning any sediment or debris that could limit water flow below the startup threshold.
5. **Verify gas supply is fully open and adequate** by confirming the manual gas valve is wide open and checking for any kinks or restrictions in the gas line (a licensed technician should then test inlet and manifold pressure with a manometer during startup).
6. **Inspect igniter, flame sensor, and wiring connections** by visually checking the igniter for cracks, wiping the flame sensor with fine sandpaper or a clean cloth, and ensuring all combustion-side connectors are secure.
7. **Reset the unit once after corrective checks** by power-cycling the heater or pressing the reset button, then monitoring one full startup cycle to see if E129 clears or returns (if it returns, internal parts-level diagnosis is required).

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot access or clean the air intake and filters safely, or if the error returns after you have cleared visible blockages and cleaned the inlet filter. Gas pressure testing, combustion analysis, and fan motor replacement require specialized tools and training. If the unit fails to ignite after basic checks, or if you smell gas at any point, shut off the gas supply and call a professional immediately. Internal control board, gas valve, or flame-proving circuit diagnosis should only be performed by qualified service personnel with the manufacturer's service manual and proper instrumentation.
