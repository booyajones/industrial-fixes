## Fujitsu E:31 Error Code — What It Means

The E:31 error code on Fujitsu mini-split systems is not a universal fault definition across all models. Fujitsu uses model-specific diagnostic tables, so the exact meaning of E:31 depends on your indoor and outdoor unit model numbers and must be looked up in the corresponding service manual or diagnostic app. Generic fault charts do not confirm what E:31 represents.

Based on Fujitsu troubleshooting architecture, codes in this format typically point to sensor circuit problems, which can involve the room air thermistor, pipe or coil thermistor, sensor wiring and connectors, or the indoor control board. Without your specific model number, a technician cannot determine which sensor or circuit is flagged.

## Common Causes

- **Model-specific sensor fault** E:31 may reference the room air sensor, pipe sensor, or another thermistor depending on your exact indoor unit model.
- **Faulty thermistor** The sensor itself may have drifted out of range, failed open, or shorted internally.
- **Wiring or harness damage** The sensor cable may be pinched, corroded, or broken between the sensor and the control board.
- **Loose or corroded connector** Poor contact at the sensor plug or board socket can mimic a sensor failure.
- **Control board issue** The indoor PCB may have a failed sensor input circuit or corrupted firmware.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Record your exact model numbers from the indoor unit nameplate and outdoor condenser label, then look up E:31 in the manufacturer service instructions or diagnostic app to identify the specific sensor or circuit involved.
2. Power off the system at the breaker or disconnect, wait thirty seconds, then restore power to see if the fault clears after a full reboot.
3. Remove the front cover and locate the sensor (usually a small black thermistor with two wires) identified in your model's fault table, then check that the connector is fully seated and free of corrosion.
4. Use a multimeter to measure continuity and resistance across the sensor leads with the connector unplugged, comparing the reading to the specification in your service manual (if the manual does not list a value, note whether the sensor reads open or shorted).
5. Inspect the sensor harness for pinches, cuts, or damage along its full length from the sensor to the indoor control board.
6. Check the reference voltage at the sensor connector on the board side (one Fujitsu diagnostic procedure notes a 5 V reference as the expected value, though this depends on your model).
7. If all wiring and connectors are intact and the sensor reads out of range, replace the thermistor with the correct part number for your unit, then clear the fault and verify operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you do not have access to the manufacturer service manual or diagnostic app for your model, if you are uncomfortable working with live electrical circuits, or if sensor and wiring checks do not resolve the fault. Fujitsu mini-split diagnostics require model-specific fault tables and proper refrigerant handling if any disassembly is needed, so professional service is often the fastest and safest path to a correct repair.
