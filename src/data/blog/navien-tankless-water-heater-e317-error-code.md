## Navien E317 Error Code — What It Means

The E317 error code on a Navien tankless water heater indicates a communication failure between the main control board and another electronic module in the system. This could involve the flow sensor, temperature sensors, or other circuit boards depending on your specific model. The control board is unable to send or receive signals properly, which prevents the unit from operating safely.

Because the exact component involved can vary across Navien models, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the inside cover of the unit to identify which module corresponds to the E317 code on your particular heater. The fault is usually related to wiring, connectors, or a failed sensor rather than the main board itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board when the real problem is a corroded pin or loose plug on the sensor harness. Inspect and reseat all low-voltage connectors before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded connector (~50%)** Vibration or moisture causes a plug to work loose or develop corrosion on the pins, breaking the signal path.
- **Failed flow sensor or thermistor (~25%)** The sensor itself stops responding, so the board cannot read flow or temperature data.
- **Damaged or pinched wiring harness (~15%)** Wires are cut, pinched, or shorted during installation or service, interrupting the signal.
- **Control board failure (~10%)** The main board or a secondary circuit board has a failed communication chip or trace.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker or disconnect switch and close the gas shutoff valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners to access the control board and wiring.
3. **Inspect all wire connectors** at the control board, flow sensor, and thermistors for looseness, corrosion, or moisture.
4. **Disconnect and reconnect** each low-voltage connector firmly, wiping pins with electrical contact cleaner if you see any discoloration.
5. **Check the wiring harness** along its entire length for pinched, cut, or abraded insulation and repair or replace damaged sections.
6. **Test sensor resistance** with a multimeter if the error persists, comparing readings to the values in your service manual.
7. **Replace the faulty sensor** if readings are out of specification, securing it with the original hardware and reconnecting the wiring.
8. **Restore power and gas**, then run hot water to verify the error is cleared and the heater fires normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error returns after reseating connectors and replacing sensors, or if you suspect the main control board or wiring harness has failed. A technician can use diagnostic software to read detailed fault logs and perform voltage and continuity tests on the circuit boards. They also have access to updated firmware and can check for recalls or service bulletins specific to your model.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
