## Samsung Dryer Ct Error Code — What It Means

The Ct error code on a Samsung dryer indicates the unit is operating in an ambient temperature that is either below freezing (32°F) or above 104°F. Samsung publishes this as a temperature-range fault, not a vent-blockage code, though the company does recommend addressing vent restrictions as part of the repair process. The dryer's internal sensors detect conditions outside the safe operating window and halt the cycle to prevent damage or unsafe operation.

## Common Causes

- **Dryer installed in a freezing or very hot location** The unit is in a garage, unheated utility room, or other space where ambient temperature falls below 32°F or rises above 104°F.
- **Blocked or crushed exhaust vent duct** Lint buildup, a kinked flex hose, or crushed metal duct restricts airflow and raises internal temperatures, contributing to the fault.
- **Clogged lint filter or screen housing** A dirty or improperly seated lint filter reduces airflow and allows heat to build inside the cabinet.
- **Exterior vent hood blocked or damper stuck closed** Debris, bird nests, or a jammed damper flap at the outside termination prevent exhaust from leaving the duct system.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermistor** The sensor that monitors dryer temperature sends incorrect readings to the control board, triggering a false Ct code even when conditions are normal.
- **Failed thermal fuse or thermostat** A safety device or thermostat in the heater circuit has opened or is reading out-of-range, signaling an over-temperature condition to the board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Check the installation location temperature** with a thermometer and confirm the space is between 32°F and 104°F. If the dryer is in an unheated garage or hot attic, move it to a climate-controlled area.
2. **Remove and clean the lint filter**, then inspect the filter housing pocket for packed lint or debris. Reinstall the filter firmly and verify it seats completely.
3. **Disconnect power and pull the dryer away from the wall**, then disconnect the exhaust vent hose. Inspect the hose and the wall duct for kinks, crushing, or heavy lint accumulation. Vacuum or replace the vent hose if needed.
4. **Go outside and inspect the exterior vent hood** for blockages, bird nests, or a stuck damper flap. Clear any obstructions and confirm the damper moves freely.
5. **Reconnect the vent, restore power, and run a test cycle** with a load of dry towels to verify the code clears and airflow is strong at the exterior hood.
6. **If the code returns after airflow and ambient conditions are corrected**, unplug the dryer and access the rear panel or top. Locate the thermistor (temperature sensor) on the blower housing or heater duct and test its resistance at room temperature, consulting your model's service manual for expected ohm values.
7. **Test the thermal fuse and any limit thermostats** in the heater circuit for continuity using a multimeter. Replace any component that reads open or out-of-specification, then reassemble and retest.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the Ct code persists after you have confirmed the dryer is in a normal-temperature environment, cleaned the lint filter, and verified the entire vent path is clear and unrestricted. Component-level diagnosis of the thermistor, thermal fuse, limit thermostats, and heater circuit requires a multimeter, a model-specific service manual with resistance and voltage tables, and safe disassembly of the cabinet. A qualified appliance technician has the wiring diagrams and test procedures to isolate the faulty part quickly and can source the correct replacement components for your dryer's exact model number.
