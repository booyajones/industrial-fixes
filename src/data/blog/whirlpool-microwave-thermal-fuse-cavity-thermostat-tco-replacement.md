## Whirlpool Microwave Thermal Fuse / Cavity Thermostat Replacement — What This Part Does

The thermal fuse (also called the cavity thermostat or thermal cutoff, or TCO) is a safety device that cuts power to your Whirlpool microwave if the unit overheats. When the fuse senses excessive temperature inside the cavity or around critical components, it opens the circuit and shuts down the oven to prevent fire or further damage.

The fuse itself rarely fails on its own. It usually trips because of an actual overheating condition caused by blocked airflow, a failed cooling fan, a defective door switch or interlock problem, or broken high-voltage components like the magnetron or diode. Replacing the fuse without finding and fixing the root cause will just result in another blown fuse.

## Signs It Needs Replacing

- **Microwave is completely dead and won't turn on** The unit has no display, no lights, and does not respond when you press any buttons because the thermal fuse has opened and cut all power.
- **Oven runs but does not heat food** The display and turntable work normally, but the magnetron circuit is disabled by the open thermal cutoff so no microwave energy is produced.
- **Microwave shuts off mid-cycle and will not restart** The unit stops heating or turns off completely during operation and cannot be restarted because the fuse tripped due to overheating.
- **Fan runs but heating stops after a few seconds** The cooling fan may continue to operate, but the microwave stops producing heat immediately because the thermal fuse opened the magnetron circuit.
- **Unit worked intermittently before failing completely** You noticed the microwave cutting out or stopping during use over several days or weeks before it finally stopped working altogether.
- **No continuity across the thermal fuse terminals** Testing the fuse with a multimeter shows an open circuit (infinite resistance), confirming the device has tripped and needs replacement.

## How to Replace It {#fix}

1. Unplug the microwave from the wall outlet and wait at least five minutes to allow the high-voltage capacitor to discharge before opening the cabinet.
2. Remove the screws securing the outer cabinet (typically along the top, sides, and rear panel) and slide or lift the cover off to expose the internal components.
3. Locate the thermal fuse or cavity thermostat, usually mounted on the oven cavity wall, near the magnetron, or on the blower housing (consult the wiring diagram on the inside of the cabinet or tech sheet for exact location).
4. Test the fuse with a multimeter set to continuity or resistance to confirm it is open (no continuity means the fuse has blown and must be replaced).
5. Inspect the cooling fan, air vents, magnetron, high-voltage diode, door switches, and surrounding wiring for signs of overheating, blockage, or component failure that caused the fuse to trip.
6. Disconnect the wire connectors from the thermal fuse terminals and remove any screws or clips holding the fuse to its mounting bracket or cavity wall.
7. Install the new thermal fuse in the same position, secure it with screws or clips, and reconnect the wire terminals in the original configuration.
8. Reassemble the cabinet, slide the outer cover back into place, and replace all screws to restore the microwave enclosure.
9. Plug the microwave back in, run a short heating test with a cup of water, and confirm the unit heats normally and does not shut down or trip the new fuse.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional appliance technician if you are not comfortable working around high-voltage microwave components, if you cannot safely discharge the capacitor, or if the thermal fuse keeps blowing after replacement (which means there is an underlying overheating or electrical fault that requires diagnosis with a wiring diagram and component testing). High-voltage microwave repairs carry a risk of serious shock even when unplugged, so professional service is recommended if you are uncertain about any step of the process.
