## Navien E181 Error Code — What It Means

The E181 error code on Navien tankless water heaters indicates a communication or signal problem between the main control board (PCB) and the flow sensor. The flow sensor monitors water movement through the unit and tells the control board when to fire the burner. When the board cannot read the sensor signal properly, it triggers E181 and the unit will not heat water.

This code can result from a faulty flow sensor, damaged wiring between the sensor and board, loose connectors, or a failing control board. Because the exact definition of error codes can vary between models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's front cover to confirm the meaning for your specific model.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main PCB when the real problem is a loose connector or a failed flow sensor. Check and reseat all connections to the flow sensor before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed flow sensor (~40%)** The flow sensor turbine or Hall-effect sensor inside can wear out or fail, sending no signal or an erratic signal to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded flow sensor connector (~35%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen the plug or corrode the pins where the flow sensor connects to the control board, interrupting the signal.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires between the flow sensor and PCB can break, chafe, or short if pinched during installation or service.
- **Control board failure (~10%)** The main PCB itself can develop a fault in the input circuit that reads the flow sensor, though this is less common than sensor issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the water isolation valves on both the cold inlet and hot outlet of the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by lifting it up and off the mounting tabs, exposing the internal components and wiring.
3. **Locate the flow sensor** on the water inlet side (it is typically a cylindrical component with a turbine inside and a wiring harness plug).
4. **Inspect the connector** where the flow sensor plugs into the main control board or wiring harness. Look for corrosion, bent pins, or moisture. Disconnect and reconnect firmly.
5. **Check the wiring harness** for any visible damage, pinches, or breaks along its length from the sensor to the board.
6. **If wiring is intact**, remove the flow sensor by unscrewing the mounting bolts or clips and disconnecting the plug. Inspect the turbine inside for debris or damage.
7. **Test the sensor** with a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) if you have the specification from your service manual, or replace it if no reading is obtained or if it is visibly damaged.
8. **Reinstall** the new or cleaned sensor, reconnect the plug, restore water flow, and turn power back on. Run a hot water tap and verify the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical connections, if you cannot identify the flow sensor or its wiring, or if replacing the sensor does not clear the E181 code. A technician has diagnostic software and multimeter tests to pinpoint whether the control board input circuit has failed. Professional help is also wise if you see evidence of water intrusion inside the unit, which can damage multiple components at once.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-180 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
