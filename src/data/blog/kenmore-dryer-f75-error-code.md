## Kenmore Dryer F75 Error Code — What It Means

The F75 error code on Kenmore dryers indicates an abnormal airflow or temperature-sensing condition detected by the control board. The control believes it is not receiving a valid response from the outlet thermistor or exhaust temperature sensor, which monitors heat at the dryer's exhaust path. This code can appear when airflow is restricted, the sensor fails, or wiring to the sensor is damaged. The exact definition can vary slightly by model platform, but the core issue is always related to the dryer's ability to sense proper airflow and temperature during operation. The dryer may stop mid-cycle or refuse to start until the fault is cleared.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted exhaust venting** Crushed, kinked, blocked, or excessively long vent hoses prevent proper airflow and cause the control to flag an abnormal temperature pattern.
- **Clogged lint screen or internal lint buildup** Lint accumulation in the screen, blower housing, or internal air path blocks airflow and triggers the sensor fault.
- **Failed outlet thermistor** The exhaust temperature sensor can fail open, short, or drift out of spec, sending invalid resistance readings to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections** Damaged harness connections, loose pins, or heat-induced corrosion at the thermistor or control board interrupt the sensor circuit.
- **Faulty main electronic control board** If airflow and sensor circuits check good, the control board itself may be misinterpreting valid sensor signals.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet or turn off the circuit breaker before opening any panels or touching wiring.
2. **Check the exhaust vent system** by disconnecting the vent hose from the back of the dryer and running a test load. If the error clears or drying improves, the problem is airflow restriction. Inspect the entire vent run, outside hood, and any elbows for blockages or damage.
3. **Clean the lint screen and internal air path** by removing the lint filter, vacuuming the filter housing, and accessing the blower housing to clear any lint buildup around the fan and exhaust ducting.
4. **Enter service diagnostics mode** (consult your model's manual for the button sequence) to confirm the F75 code, then clear stored codes and retest after airflow corrections.
5. **Test the outlet thermistor** by locating it near the exhaust duct, disconnecting the wire harness, and measuring resistance with a multimeter. Compare readings to your model's service data. A common nominal value is around 10,000 ohms at room temperature, but verify against your exact model's chart. Replace the sensor if it reads open, shorted, or far out of range.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness** between the thermistor and the main control board for breaks, heat damage, loose connector pins, or corrosion. Repair or replace damaged connectors and wiring.
7. **Replace the main control board** only if airflow is verified unrestricted, the thermistor tests within spec, and the harness is intact. Confirm the board is not correctly reading a known-good sensor circuit before ordering the part.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components, cannot safely access the thermistor or control board, or if the error persists after you have verified clear airflow and tested the sensor circuit. A pro can run model-specific diagnostics, measure exact thermistor response curves, and confirm whether the control board is misreading valid signals. If your dryer is still under warranty or service contract, contact Kenmore or the retailer before opening panels or replacing parts.
