## GE Dishwasher C3 Error Code — What It Means

The C3 code on a GE dishwasher indicates a drain problem. The machine has detected that water is not emptying properly from the tub or sump area after a drain attempt. This is a water-flow fault, not a specific sensor error.

When you see C3, the dishwasher is telling you it cannot complete the drain cycle. The most common real-world causes are blockages in the drain path: a clogged filter, debris in the sump or pump area, a kinked drain hose, a blocked garbage disposal inlet, or a clogged air gap. Less often, the drain pump itself has failed or there is a control circuit issue.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump or control board before checking for simple blockages. Always remove and clean the filter, inspect the sump for debris, and verify the drain hose is not kinked before ordering any electrical parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filter or sump debris (~40%)** Food particles, labels, broken glass, or other debris block the filter or pump inlet and prevent water from draining.
- **Kinked or pinched drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose routing under the sink has a sharp bend, kink, or is compressed by cabinetry, restricting flow.
- **Garbage disposal knockout plug not removed (~15%)** If the dishwasher drains through a new disposal, the inlet plug was never knocked out and blocks the connection.
- **Clogged air gap (~10%)** If your installation has an air gap on the countertop, sludge or buildup inside restricts the drain path.
- **Failed drain pump (~8%)** The pump motor hums, runs weakly, or does not turn on at all despite a proper command from the control.
- **Control board or wiring issue (~2%)** A failed connector, broken wire, or control output problem prevents the drain pump from receiving power.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the breaker or by unplugging the dishwasher to work safely.
2. **Remove the lower dish rack** and pull out the filter assembly from the tub floor. Rinse it under running water and clear any debris.
3. **Inspect the sump area** below the filter for food particles, broken glass, labels, or small objects that could jam the pump impeller.
4. **Check the drain hose** under the sink for kinks, pinches, or sharp bends. Straighten the hose and verify it is routed in a high loop or through an air gap as required by local code.
5. **Verify the garbage disposal inlet** is open. If the disposal is new, confirm the knockout plug was removed. If an air gap is installed, remove the cap and flush it with water.
6. **Restore power and run a drain cycle**. Listen for the drain pump. If it hums without moving water or is silent, test for voltage at the pump terminals. Replace the pump if voltage is present but the pump does not run.
7. **If the fault persists** after clearing all mechanical blockages and the pump runs normally, inspect wiring connectors and consult the wiring diagram for control-board output tests.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleared all visible blockages, the drain pump runs and sounds normal, but the C3 code returns. That scenario points to a control-board communication issue, a faulty pressure switch, or an intermittent wiring fault that requires diagnostic tools and schematics. Also call a pro if you are not comfortable working around 120-volt wiring, testing pump voltage, or disassembling the lower sump area of the dishwasher.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (if pump or hose needed), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
