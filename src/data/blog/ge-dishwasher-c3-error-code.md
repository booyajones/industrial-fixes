## GE Dishwasher C3 Error Code — What It Means

C3 on a GE dishwasher signals a drain failure. The control board has detected that water is not leaving the tub as expected, so the dishwasher cannot complete its drain sequence. In practical terms, you will see standing water in the bottom of the tub after a cycle.

GE's own fault-code guide lists C3 as a no-drain condition and recommends a power reset first. If the code returns after reset, basic drain-path checks are the next step. If those checks do not clear the fault, service is recommended.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump when the real problem is a kinked hose or a forgotten knockout plug in the garbage disposal. Always inspect the entire drain path and clear debris from the filter and sump before ordering a pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged filter or sump area (~35%)** Food debris, grease, or plastic fragments in the filter or sump restrict water flow and prevent the pump from clearing the tub.
- **Kinked or blocked drain hose (~25%)** A hose that is pinched, looped too high, or obstructed by debris will stop water from exiting the machine.
- **Garbage disposal or sink drain blockage (~20%)** A clogged disposal inlet, a forgotten knockout plug, or a backup in the sink line prevents the dishwasher from draining into the household plumbing.
- **Debris jamming the drain pump impeller (~12%)** Small objects or hard food particles can lodge in the pump housing and stop the impeller from turning.
- **Faulty drain pump (~8%)** When the drain path is clear and the impeller spins freely but the pump does not expel water, the motor or pump assembly itself has failed.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset the dishwasher** by switching off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds, then restore power and run a short rinse cycle to see if the code clears.
2. **Remove the lower dish rack** and take out the filter assembly from the floor of the tub, then rinse it under running water to clear any food debris, grease, or fragments.
3. **Inspect the sump area** at the bottom of the tub for loose objects, plastic labels, or other debris, and wipe it clean with a damp cloth.
4. **Check the drain hose** under the sink or behind the kickplate for kinks, pinches, or blockages, and straighten or reposition the hose so it slopes downward without high loops.
5. **Verify the garbage disposal connection** by confirming the knockout plug has been removed from the disposal inlet and that the disposal itself is not clogged.
6. **Access the drain pump** (usually behind a lower kickplate or access panel) and look for debris jamming the impeller, then try rotating the impeller by hand to confirm it spins freely.
7. **Run a drain test** by starting a short rinse cycle and listening for the pump to activate and water to exit the tub. If the code persists after all these checks, the pump assembly or control wiring likely needs professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the C3 code returns after you have completed a power reset, cleaned the filter and sump, verified the drain hose is not kinked, and confirmed the garbage disposal or sink drain is clear. At that point the likely causes are a failed drain pump motor, a jammed impeller that cannot be freed, or a wiring or control-board fault that requires diagnostic tools and component-level testing. GE's own guidance recommends service when basic drain-path checks do not resolve the no-drain condition.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts (hose or pump if needed), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
