## Amana Refrigerator Running Constantly — What's Happening

An Amana refrigerator running constantly means the compressor and cooling system stay energized longer than expected because the cabinet is not reaching the target temperature or a control or sensor issue is preventing the cycle from ending. This is a symptom, not a displayed fault code. Amana notes that longer run times can be normal after large food loads, frequent door openings, or on newer high-efficiency units, but persistent operation points to a cooling-performance problem.

If the refrigerator cannot reject heat properly or if warm air is leaking into the cabinet, the compressor will run continuously trying to satisfy the thermostat. When fans and compressor are running but cooling is poor, the problem is typically airflow, defrost system issues, or a failed sensor telling the control board the compartment is too warm.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Dirty condenser coils** Dust and debris on the coils reduce heat rejection and force the compressor to run longer to maintain temperature.
- **Blocked or defective condenser fan motor** An obstructed fan blade or bad motor prevents proper airflow over the condenser, keeping the system from rejecting heat.
- **Door gasket leakage** Worn or damaged door seals allow warm air infiltration, forcing extended cooling cycles to compensate.
- **Evaporator airflow restriction or defrost problem** Frost buildup on the evaporator coils blocks airflow, so the unit runs without satisfying the temperature setpoint.
- **Defective thermistor or temperature control** A bad thermistor can report the cabinet is too warm, and a defective control board may keep supplying voltage to the compressor continuously.
- **Normal extended run time after load or door openings** Recent warm food loads, frequent door openings, or high ambient temperature can extend run time normally for 24 to 48 hours.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm whether long run time is normal by asking about recent warm loads, door openings, ambient temperature, and whether the refrigerator is new or recently relocated.
2. Measure actual fresh food and freezer temperatures to determine whether the unit is over-running or simply recovering from a heat load.
3. Unplug the refrigerator, pull it away from the wall, and clean the condenser coils thoroughly with a coil brush and vacuum.
4. Inspect the condenser fan blade for obstructions and verify the fan motor spins freely and runs when the compressor is on.
5. Check all door gaskets for tears, gaps, or poor sealing, and verify doors close and align properly.
6. Remove the evaporator cover inside the freezer and inspect for heavy frost buildup, blocked airflow, or a failed defrost heater.
7. Test the thermistor with a multimeter for correct resistance at room temperature if cooling is weak but the compressor runs constantly.
8. Unplug the unit for five minutes to reset the control board after any repairs, then monitor run cycle behavior for 24 hours.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if cleaning the coils and checking the condenser fan do not restore normal cycling, if you find heavy frost on the evaporator and are not comfortable testing the defrost heater and control, or if you suspect a control board or sealed-system issue. Refrigerant work and control board diagnosis require specialized tools and training. If the compressor runs constantly but temperatures remain high despite good airflow and clean coils, the sealed system may have a restriction or low charge that only a licensed technician can address safely.
