## LG Dryer D90 Error Code — What It Means

LG's D90 code is a Flow Sense or duct-blockage warning. LG states it means the dryer has detected about a 90% blockage in the ductwork and needs immediate attention. In practical field terms, it is almost always an exhaust-airflow restriction problem, not a bad board problem.

LG pairs this with D95 as a 95% blockage warning, which reinforces that D90 is an airflow-restriction threshold rather than a generic fault code. The dryer is warning that exhaust airflow is severely restricted from the lint screen to the outside termination.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or moisture sensors, but LG's own guidance emphasizes vent restriction first. Check airflow at the outside termination while the dryer runs before replacing any electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Lint filter blockage or film coating (~35%)** The lint screen or housing is packed with lint or coated with fabric-softener residue that still looks clean but restricts airflow.
- **Crushed, kinked, or dented flex vent behind the dryer (~25%)** The exhaust hose is compressed, bent sharply, or damaged where it connects to the dryer outlet or wall.
- **Exterior vent hood blockage or stuck flap (~20%)** Lint clogs the outside vent hood, or the flapper does not open freely during operation.
- **Disconnected or loose vent connections (~10%)** The duct has come apart at a joint, or a clamp has loosened, restricting airflow or venting into the wall cavity.
- **Duct run too long or too many elbows (~8%)** The total vent length or number of 90-degree bends exceeds design limits, causing persistent low airflow even when the vent looks clean.
- **Hidden lint accumulation in blower housing or internal ducting (~2%)** Lint has built up inside the dryer's blower wheel or internal exhaust path after the obvious sections are cleaned.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** or turn off the circuit breaker before you begin any disassembly or vent removal work.
2. **Remove and clean the lint filter** thoroughly, then inspect the lint-screen housing for packed lint or fabric-softener film that restricts airflow.
3. **Go outside and check airflow at the exterior vent hood** while the dryer runs (have a helper start a cycle). You should see and feel strong airflow. If airflow is weak or absent, there is a restriction that must be removed.
4. **Disconnect the flex vent from the dryer outlet** and inspect both the dryer's exhaust port and the connected duct for visible obstructions, lint clogs, or damage.
5. **Inspect the flex vent behind the dryer** for crushing, kinks, sharp bends, dents, or loose connections. Correct the routing or replace damaged ducting to restore full airflow.
6. **Inspect the exterior vent hood and flapper** for lint clogging, damage, or a flap that does not open freely. Replace the hood if it won't open properly or is heavily clogged.
7. **Evaluate the overall vent design** for excessive length and too many 90-degree turns. If the code persists after cleaning and the vent run is long or complex, consult LG's installation manual or hire a professional duct-cleaning service to inspect hidden sections.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the lint filter, inspected the flex vent, and cleared the exterior hood but the D90 code persists. A technician can inspect hidden duct sections, evaluate the overall vent design for excessive length or too many bends, and perform internal blower-housing cleaning or duct replacement. Also call a pro if you are not comfortable working behind the dryer, disconnecting ductwork, or accessing the exterior vent from the roof or a high wall. LG recommends professional duct cleaning when the restriction is not obvious and the vent run is long or complex.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (vent hood or flex duct), 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for duct cleaning or vent repair.
