## KitchenAid Oven PF Error Code — What It Means

PF on a KitchenAid oven stands for power failure. The control board detected that the appliance lost electrical power or was powered up for the first time. On most models the display can be cleared by pressing Cancel or another front-panel button. Some models will also require you to reset the clock after power is restored.

This code does not point to a sensor or heating-element problem. It simply tells you the control experienced an interruption in supply voltage. The code may appear after a utility outage, a tripped breaker, unplugging the unit, or during initial installation. If PF does not clear or returns immediately after clearing, the issue may be an unstable household circuit or a failing control board rather than a cooking-performance fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace temperature sensors or igniters when PF appears, but those parts do not cause a power-failure code. Check your breaker panel and press Cancel before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Utility outage or tripped breaker (~60%)** The most common cause is a genuine loss of power to the oven from the home electrical system during a storm, overload, or when the unit was unplugged.
- **First power-up after installation (~20%)** A new or recently moved oven will show PF when energized for the first time until you clear the display and set the clock.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~10%)** A poor connection at the terminal block, outlet, or junction box can cause intermittent power loss that the control reads as a failure.
- **Incorrect voltage or noisy supply (~5%)** Miswired outlets, incorrect voltage, or electrical noise on the circuit during an outage event can trigger the power-failure flag on some models.
- **Failing control board or power supply (~5%)** If PF returns immediately after clearing and the breaker is sound, the main control board or internal power-supply circuit may be failing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Press Cancel or touch any front-panel button to clear the PF display from the control screen.
2. Reset the clock or time-of-day if your model requires it after a power interruption.
3. Check your home breaker panel to confirm the oven circuit breaker is fully on and has not tripped to the middle position.
4. Turn off the oven circuit breaker for one full minute, then turn it back on to reset the appliance power and watch for the code to return.
5. Inspect the wiring connections at the oven terminal block and wall outlet for loose, burned, or corroded terminals if PF returns immediately after clearing.
6. Verify incoming voltage, polarity, and ground at the receptacle if the oven was recently installed or if you suspect a household wiring issue.
7. Replace the main control board if all supply-side checks are normal and PF reappears without any external power event or breaker trip.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the PF code returns immediately after clearing and you have confirmed the breaker is sound and the outlet is not loose. Also call a licensed electrician if the oven is newly installed and you suspect incorrect voltage, reversed polarity, or missing ground at the wall supply. High-voltage wiring inside the oven and control-board diagnosis require proper tools and safety training. If you are uncomfortable working inside your home electrical panel or behind a 240-volt appliance, professional help will save time and prevent shock hazards.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0 if clearing the code or resetting a breaker solves it; $150-300 if a control board must be replaced. A pro service call runs about $150-400 including labor if a control board or wiring connection repair is required.
