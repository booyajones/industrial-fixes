## Electrolux Washer E60 Error Code — What It Means

E60 on an Electrolux washer indicates a heating fault. The machine has detected that water is not reaching the programmed temperature, or the heating system is not responding correctly. Electrolux support materials confirm that E60 generally refers to a heating problem, usually involving the temperature sensor or heating element.

The code may also appear if the washer cannot establish the correct water level or drain conditions needed for safe heating. In those cases, the control board flags a heating error even though the root cause is a blocked filter, faulty drain pump, defective pressure switch, or restricted drain hose. Always verify basic water flow and drainage before testing the heat circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when E60 appears, assuming the electronics have failed. Always test the heating element and temperature sensor for opens or shorts first. Both are inexpensive to test with a multimeter and far more likely to fail than the board itself.

## Common Causes

- **Defective heating element (~45%)** The heating element has an open circuit, short to ground, or corroded terminals and cannot warm the water.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermistor (~30%)** The sensor sends an out-of-range or open signal to the control board, preventing the heating cycle from starting or completing.
- **Blocked drain pump filter or clogged drain hose (~12%)** Water cannot drain fully, so the control board will not energize the heater to protect against overheating or low-water conditions.
- **Defective pressure switch or sensor (~8%)** The control board cannot confirm the correct water level and inhibits heating as a safety measure.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~5%)** Insufficient or incorrect fill prevents the washer from reaching conditions needed to heat safely.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait 30 seconds to reset the control board, then restore power and check if the code clears.
2. **Clean the drain pump filter** by opening the small access door at the front lower corner, unscrewing the filter cap, and removing lint and debris.
3. **Run a hot wash cycle** without laundry or detergent to verify that water drains completely and the drum empties at each step.
4. **Disconnect power at the wall**, then remove the rear or front service panel (consult your model's service manual for fastener locations).
5. **Locate the heating element** at the bottom of the tub and disconnect its wiring harness, then test the element with a multimeter for continuity (typically a few tens of ohms) and for shorts to the chassis (should be infinite resistance).
6. **Test the temperature sensor** by unplugging its connector and measuring resistance across its terminals (consult your model's table for the expected value at room temperature).
7. **Replace the failed component** (heating element or temperature sensor), reconnect all wiring, reinstall panels, restore power, and run a diagnostic cycle or normal hot wash to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with mains-voltage components inside the washer cabinet, if the heating element and temperature sensor both test good but E60 persists, or if you suspect the pressure switch, inlet valve, or control board. Authorized Electrolux service technicians can run factory diagnostics, read stored fault logs, and measure real-time signals that are not accessible to basic multimeter tests. Professional service is also the safest choice if the washer has recently been moved or installed and E60 appears on the first cycle, since installation errors (kinked fill hoses, incorrect drain height, or poor ventilation around the cabinet) can mimic component failures.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280.
