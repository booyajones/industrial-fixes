## What this code means
The E60 error code on an Electrolux washer signals a heating fault. The machine detects that the water temperature has not reached the required level within the expected time frame. This fault points to a problem with either the heating element (the coil that warms the water) or the temperature sensor (thermistor) that monitors water temperature. When E60 appears, the wash cycle may continue without hot water, or the machine may stop if the heating fault is critical. The control board flags this code when it receives incorrect resistance readings from the sensor or detects no current flow through the heater.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when the real fault is a broken heating element or sensor. Always test the resistance of the heater and thermistor before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Defective heating element (~45%)** The heater coil is broken or burnt out, showing infinite resistance and preventing any heat generation during the wash cycle.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~30%)** The thermistor provides incorrect resistance readings, causing the control board to misjudge water temperature and flag a heating fault.
- **Wiring issues (~15%)** Loose, corroded, or disconnected wires between the control board and the heater or sensor interrupt the signal and current flow.
- **Water inlet restriction (~10%)** Blocked inlet hoses or a malfunctioning inlet valve reduce water flow, indirectly impacting heating efficiency and cycle timing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and wait 30 seconds to reset the control board, then restore power and attempt a test cycle to see if the error clears.
2. **Access the heating element** by removing the rear or bottom service panel (consult your model's service manual for exact location).
3. **Disconnect the heater terminals** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure resistance across the element. A functional heater typically shows 20-50 Ω (a 2000W heater on 230V reads approximately 24 Ω). Infinite resistance means the element is broken.
4. **Test the temperature sensor** by disconnecting it and measuring resistance at room temperature. Expect 10-20 kΩ at 25°C. Readings of 0 Ω or infinite Ω indicate a faulty sensor.
5. **Inspect wiring and connectors** between the control board and both the heater and sensor. Look for loose plugs, corrosion, or burnt wires. Check continuity in the harness.
6. **Replace the defective component** (heater or sensor) with a genuine Electrolux part matching your model number. Secure all connectors and reassemble the panels.
7. **Run a diagnostic test cycle** by selecting the Normal cycle, then pressing and holding the Temperature and Select buttons for 5 seconds to enter diagnostic mode. Verify the error does not return and the water heats properly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with electrical components or using a multimeter to test resistance and continuity. If you replace both the heating element and temperature sensor and the E60 error persists, the control board may have a fault that requires diagnostic equipment and board-level repair. A technician can also check for less common issues like faulty relays on the board or internal wiring faults that are difficult to trace without schematics. Most E60 faults are straightforward component swaps, but persistent codes after replacement point to deeper electrical problems best handled by a qualified appliance repair tech.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-90 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
