## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E33 Error — What It Means

The E33 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a flame detection or combustion problem. The unit is attempting to ignite but either cannot establish a flame or cannot sense the flame once ignited. This interrupts the heating cycle and shuts down the unit as a safety measure. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the inside panel for your specific unit. Common causes include gas supply issues, igniter wear, flame rod contamination, or air intake and exhaust blockages that interfere with proper combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter when the flame rod is simply dirty or the gas valve filter is clogged. Clean the flame rod and check gas pressure before ordering an igniter.

## Common Causes

- **Insufficient gas supply (~35%)** A partially closed valve, low pressure at the meter, or a clogged inlet screen prevents enough gas from reaching the burner.
- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensor prevents it from detecting the flame, even when combustion is normal.
- **Failed igniter (~20%)** A worn or cracked hot-surface igniter cannot produce enough heat to light the gas-air mixture reliably.
- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent (~15%)** Ice, nests, or debris in the vent piping or termination restricts airflow and prevents proper combustion.
- **Faulty gas valve (~5%)** The gas valve may not be opening fully or modulating correctly, starving the burner of fuel during ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch, then close the gas shutoff valve to the unit.
2. **Check the gas supply** by confirming the valve is fully open and other gas appliances are working; if you have a manometer, verify inlet pressure matches the rating plate.
3. **Inspect vent terminations** outside for nests, ice, or debris; clear any obstructions and check that vent piping has the correct slope and no sags.
4. **Remove the front panel** and locate the flame rod (a thin metal probe near the burner) and igniter (a ceramic element); photograph wire positions before disconnecting anything.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a Scotch-Brite pad until shiny, then wipe with a clean cloth; do not touch the polished surface with bare hands.
6. **Inspect the igniter** for cracks or breaks; if damaged, disconnect the wire connector and unscrew the mounting bracket to remove it.
7. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, and monitor the startup cycle; if E33 returns after these checks, call a licensed technician to test gas pressure, valve operation, and control-board output.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and clearing vents, or if you lack a manometer to verify gas pressure. Professionals have the tools to measure inlet and manifold pressure, test the gas valve modulation, and diagnose control-board signals that are difficult to check safely at home. Any work involving gas-line fittings, valve replacement, or control-board troubleshooting should be left to a qualified technician to avoid safety hazards and maintain warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
