## Navien E335 Error Code — What It Means

The E335 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals that the unit has detected a combustion or flame sensing abnormality. The control board expects to see a stable flame signal during operation, and when that signal is weak, intermittent, or absent, the heater shuts down and displays this code. This can happen because the flame rod sensor is coated with carbon deposits, because the combustion chamber or venting is obstructed, or because the gas supply is insufficient. The unit is protecting itself from unsafe operation.

Because combustion problems can involve gas and carbon monoxide risk, careful diagnosis is important. A weak or dirty flame rod is often the culprit, but venting blockage and gas pressure issues also trigger this code. Check the flame rod and venting first, then move to gas supply and ignition components if the problem persists.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or gas valve when the real issue is a dirty flame rod that costs nothing to clean. Always inspect and clean the flame rod before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame rod insulate it from the flame current, so the board cannot confirm ignition.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~20%)** Obstructions in the intake or exhaust pipe starve the combustion chamber of air or trap exhaust gases, disrupting the flame.
- **Low gas pressure or gas valve issue (~15%)** Insufficient gas flow or a sticky valve prevents the burner from establishing a stable flame strong enough to be sensed.
- **Faulty flame rod or wiring (~10%)** A cracked ceramic insulator or loose wire prevents the flame signal from reaching the control board.
- **Condensate or water in the burner (~5%)** Moisture from condensation or a leak can short the flame rod circuit or interfere with ignition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the breaker and shut off the gas supply valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners and carefully setting the panel aside.
3. **Locate the flame rod** inside the combustion chamber, a thin metal probe near the burner assembly.
4. **Disconnect the flame rod wire** and unscrew the mounting bracket to remove the rod.
5. **Clean the flame rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a clean cloth to remove carbon deposits and corrosion, taking care not to damage the ceramic insulator.
6. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside the home for blockage, bird nests, ice, or debris and clear any obstructions.
7. **Reinstall the flame rod** and reconnect the wire, ensuring a snug connection and proper grounding.
8. **Restore gas and power**, then run a hot-water tap to call for heat and observe whether the unit ignites and runs without error.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod and clearing the vents does not resolve the error, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a faulty gas valve. Combustion problems can create carbon monoxide risk, and diagnosing gas flow, ignition timing, and control board signals requires specialized tools and training. A professional can measure gas pressure, test flame rectification current, and replace internal components safely.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
