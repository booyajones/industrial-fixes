## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E78 Error — What It Means

The E78 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters indicates a flame failure or ignition fault. The unit is detecting a condition where the burner flame either fails to ignite, does not remain stable, or shuts off unexpectedly during operation. This safety feature prevents unburned gas from accumulating and protects the unit from operating outside safe parameters.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary across different Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the diagnostic chart on the inside cover of the unit for model-specific guidance. The code typically points to problems in the ignition sequence, flame sensing, gas delivery, or combustion air supply.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many homeowners replace the igniter or flame sensor first when gas pressure or a closed gas valve is the real problem. Check that the gas shutoff is fully open and the gas meter shows flow before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~40%)** Insufficient gas pressure, a partially closed valve, or an empty propane tank prevents the burner from lighting or staying lit.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents the control board from detecting the flame, causing shutdown.
- **Failed igniter (~15%)** A cracked or worn spark igniter no longer produces a strong enough spark to light the gas.
- **Blocked combustion air intake or exhaust vent (~10%)** Obstructions or lint in the intake or exhaust piping starve the burner of oxygen or create backdraft conditions.
- **Faulty gas valve (~7%)** A stuck or failed gas valve coil or solenoid does not open to allow gas flow when the control board calls for heat.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~3%)** Corroded connections, loose wiring, or a failed relay on the control board interrupt the ignition signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the water heater before beginning any inspection.
2. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the shutoff valve is fully open and checking that other gas appliances operate normally.
3. **Inspect intake and exhaust vents** outside the home for blockages such as bird nests, ice, or debris, and clear any obstructions.
4. **Remove the front cover** of the tankless unit and locate the flame sensor rod and igniter, usually near the burner assembly.
5. **Clean the flame sensor** with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits or corrosion, then reinstall it snugly.
6. **Check wiring connections** to the igniter, flame sensor, and gas valve for corrosion, looseness, or damage, and repair or reseat as needed.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then attempt to run hot water and observe whether the unit ignites and the error clears. If the code persists, call a licensed technician to test gas pressure and component voltages.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician immediately if you smell gas, if the unit repeatedly fails to ignite after basic checks, or if you are uncomfortable working around gas lines and electrical components. Tankless water heater repairs require accurate gas pressure testing, combustion analysis, and safe handling of both gas and electrical connections. A qualified pro will use a manometer to verify inlet and manifold pressures, test igniter spark gap and voltage, check flame sensor microamp readings, and inspect the heat exchanger for blockages or damage. Because gas leaks and improper combustion pose serious safety risks, professional diagnosis and repair are strongly recommended for any persistent flame or ignition fault.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
