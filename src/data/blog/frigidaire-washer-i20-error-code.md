## Frigidaire Dishwasher i20 Error Code — What It Means

The i20 code on a Frigidaire dishwasher means the control detected that water was not removed from the tub as expected during the drain cycle. This is a drain-restriction or pressure-sensing fault, not a heater or leak-pan issue.

The machine monitors drain performance through a pressure-sensing system. When drainage is incomplete or too slow, the i20 code appears and the cycle stops. The root cause is almost always a physical blockage in the drain path, incorrect hose installation, or a restriction in the pressure-sensing circuit that prevents the control from detecting proper water evacuation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump first when the i20 code appears. Before ordering a pump, remove and clean the filter and sump, then run a cancel-drain test. Most i20 faults clear after cleaning debris from the drain path, not by replacing the pump.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked filter or glass trap (~50%)** Food debris, labels, glass fragments, or sludge accumulate in the filter assembly or sump screen and prevent water from reaching the drain pump.
- **Kinked or obstructed drain hose (~20%)** The hose is pinched behind the machine, routed below the proper 32-inch minimum height from the floor, or has internal buildup restricting flow.
- **Disposer knockout plug not removed (~15%)** When the dishwasher drains into a garbage disposer, the factory knockout plug inside the disposer inlet must be punched out or water cannot exit.
- **Debris in the sump or drain opening (~10%)** Small objects or food particles lodge in the sump area beneath the filter, blocking the path to the pump even when the filter itself looks clean.
- **Contaminated pressure-sensing path (~3%)** Grease, mineral deposits, or debris restrict the small pressure tube or channel that the control uses to detect water level, causing a false i20 even when drainage is working.
- **Failed or mechanically blocked drain pump (~2%)** The pump impeller is broken, jammed by a foreign object, or the motor has failed and cannot run, preventing water evacuation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Kill power** at the breaker or unplug the dishwasher to reset the control and make sure safe access.
2. **Remove the lower rack** and locate the cylindrical filter assembly or glass trap in the floor of the tub. Twist or lift it out per your model's design.
3. **Clean the filter and sump** thoroughly under running water, removing all food debris, grease, labels, and any small objects lodged in the mesh or the sump cavity below.
4. **Check the drain hose** from the dishwasher to the sink or disposer. Confirm the hose is not kinked, that it rises to at least 32 inches from the floor, and that the disposer knockout plug was removed if applicable.
5. **Reinstall the filter** and close the lower access panels. Restore power and start a cancel-drain or rinse cycle to test whether water evacuates and the code clears.
6. **If the i20 returns**, inspect the drain pump for blockage or damage by removing the pump cover (usually accessible from inside the tub or beneath the unit) and checking the impeller for debris or broken vanes.
7. **Inspect the pressure-sensing circuit** if the drain path is clear and the pump runs. Look for a small tube or channel near the sump that can become clogged with grease or mineral buildup, preventing the control from detecting proper water evacuation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the filter and sump, verified the drain hose routing and disposer connection, and the i20 code still returns after a test cycle. The fault may be in the pressure-sensing tube, the pressure switch itself, or the wiring between the sensor and the control board. Diagnosing pressure-sensing circuits requires disassembly and test equipment that most homeowners do not have. Also call a pro if the drain pump needs replacement and you are not comfortable working beneath the dishwasher or handling the water and electrical connections at the pump.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts if a pump or filter is needed, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 including diagnosis and labor.
