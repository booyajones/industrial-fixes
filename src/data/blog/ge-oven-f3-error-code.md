## GE Oven F3 Error Code — What It Means

The F3 error on most GE ovens and ranges signals that the oven temperature sensor circuit is reading out of range. The electronic control interprets the sensor's signal as unsafe or invalid. This fault can stem from the sensor probe itself, broken or corroded wiring and connectors between the sensor and the main control board, or a failed control board. The code appears when the control cannot verify safe oven temperature, so it halts heating and displays F3 to prevent damage or hazard.

In the field, the temperature sensor is the part that fails most often. Loose or oxidized connections at the sensor plug are the next common culprit. If both the sensor and its wiring check out, the electronic range control board is usually at fault. Because the control relies on a precise resistance reading from the sensor, even a minor wiring defect or sensor drift will trigger F3.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the expensive main control board first. Always test the sensor's resistance (should read about 1,080 ohms at room temperature) and inspect the connector before condemning the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~60%)** The sensor probe inside the oven cavity drifts out of spec, reads open, or shorts, sending an invalid resistance to the control.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged wiring and connectors (~25%)** Burned insulation, frayed wires, oxidized terminals, or a poorly seated plug between the sensor and control board mimic a bad sensor.
- **Failed electronic range control (ERC) board (~12%)** If the sensor and harness test correctly, the control board's sensor-input circuit has failed and the board must be replaced.
- **Intermittent connection at the sensor terminals (~3%)** Heat cycling and vibration can loosen spade terminals or cause micro-cracks in solder joints, producing an intermittent F3.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely before opening any panels or touching any wiring.
2. **Remove the oven racks** and locate the temperature sensor probe inside the oven cavity, usually protruding from the upper rear or side wall.
3. **Pull the range away from the wall** to access the rear panel or lower access cover, depending on your model, and trace the sensor wires to the connector at the control board or junction block.
4. **Inspect the sensor wiring and connector** closely for burned insulation, frayed strands, corrosion on spade terminals, or a loose plug, and clean or repair as needed.
5. **Disconnect the sensor plug** and measure resistance across the sensor terminals with a multimeter set to ohms; at room temperature the reading should be close to 1,080 ohms.
6. **Replace the sensor** if it reads open (infinite resistance), shorted (near zero), or far outside the expected range, and verify the new sensor measures correctly before installation.
7. **Reassemble and restore power**, then run a bake cycle; if F3 returns and the sensor and wiring are known good, replace the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working inside a 240-volt appliance, if you cannot safely pull the range out to access the rear panel, or if you have replaced the sensor and verified all wiring but F3 continues to appear. A pro can perform live-circuit checks on the control board's sensor input, confirm that line voltage is stable, and swap the control board without risk of further damage. If your range is still under warranty or part of a high-end slide-in installation that requires precise reinstallation, professional service is the safer choice.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20-50 in parts, 30-45 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
