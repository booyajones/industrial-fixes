## State Water Heater E165 Error Code — What It Means

The E165 error code on State water heaters typically indicates an ignition failure or flame detection problem. This code appears when the control board either cannot ignite the burner or cannot detect a stable flame after ignition. State water heaters are manufactured on the A. O. Smith platform, and this fault family is related to combustion system issues rather than a single failed component.

Before starting any repair, verify your exact model number from the rating plate or QR code on the unit. State directs users to model-specific manuals and troubleshooting procedures, and the exact diagnostic steps may vary by model. The code generally points to airflow restrictions, gas supply issues, or dirty flame-sensing components.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or vent** Restricted intake screens, lint buildup, bird nests, or improper vent termination prevent proper combustion airflow and are the most common cause of ignition faults on State and A. O. Smith gas water heaters.
- **Insufficient gas supply or pressure** A closed manual shutoff, low gas pressure from the utility, incorrect regulator setting, or kinked supply line prevents the burner from lighting or maintaining flame.
- **Dirty flame sensor or rod** Carbon deposits or oxidation on the flame-sensing rod prevent the control from detecting the flame, causing the system to shut down even when ignition occurs.
- **Low water flow on tankless models** A clogged inlet screen filter or low system flow rate prevents flow-proved ignition from completing the start sequence on tankless units.
- **Failed pressure switch or blocked tubing** On models with combustion air proving switches, a stuck switch, blocked pressure-sensing tube, or weak combustion blower can prevent ignition authorization.
- **Faulty gas control valve** If all other components test correctly, the gas valve itself may have failed and require replacement per the service manual.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model number** by locating the rating plate or scanning the QR code on the water heater, then download the correct service manual from the State website for model-specific diagnostics.
2. **Inspect the air intake and vent** for any blockage, lint, nests, or debris, and clear all obstructions from intake screens, vent terminations, and the combustion air path.
3. **Confirm gas supply** by checking that the manual gas shutoff is fully open, the supply line is not kinked, and gas is available at other appliances in the home.
4. **Check the inlet water filter** on tankless models by shutting off the cold supply, removing the inlet screen or filter, cleaning it thoroughly, and reinstalling it.
5. **Clean the flame sensor** by shutting off gas and power, removing the burner access panel, locating the flame rod, and gently cleaning it with fine sandpaper or a clean cloth.
6. **Inspect pressure switch tubing** on models with a combustion air prover by checking all rubber tubing for cracks, blockage, or disconnection, and testing the pressure switch per the service manual if accessible.
7. **Reset the unit** after correcting the issue by restoring power and gas, initiating a call for heat or hot water, and observing a full ignition cycle to confirm stable flame and no return of the error code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot locate or clear the blockage causing the fault, or if the error returns after cleaning the intake and flame sensor. Professional help is required for gas pressure testing, combustion analysis, control board diagnostics, and gas valve replacement. If your unit is under warranty, contact State customer service or an authorized service provider before attempting repairs to avoid voiding coverage. Any work involving gas piping, venting modifications, or electrical control replacement should be performed by a qualified technician.
