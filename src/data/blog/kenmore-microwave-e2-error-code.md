## What this code means
E2 is a thermistor error code on Kenmore microwaves. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that monitors the cavity or cabinet temperature so the control board can prevent overheating. When the control board sees the thermistor signal out of range, shorted, open, or otherwise invalid, it displays E2 and stops operation to protect the unit.

Kenmore is a brand label, not a manufacturer. The first three digits of your model number identify who actually built the microwave. That means the exact thermistor location, resistance specification, and wiring colors can vary by platform. Always verify the fault code against your specific model's service documentation to confirm you are working on the thermistor circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first because they assume all error codes mean a bad board. Before ordering a board, unplug the unit and test the thermistor resistance with a multimeter at room temperature and compare it to your model's service sheet. Most E2 faults resolve with a new thermistor.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The temperature sensor itself drifts out of specification or fails open or shorted, sending an invalid signal to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded connector (~20%)** The plug between the thermistor harness and the control board becomes loose, oxidized, or pushed out during a move or repair, breaking the signal path.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires to the thermistor are pinched, cut, or melted from heat or physical damage inside the cabinet, creating an open or short circuit.
- **Control board input failure (~10%)** The main control board's thermistor input stage has failed, so even a healthy sensor reads as a fault.
- **Intermittent connection (~5%)** Vibration or thermal cycling causes a wire crimp or solder joint to make and break contact, triggering the error sporadically.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the microwave** and wait at least two minutes for the high-voltage capacitor to discharge before opening the cabinet (microwave capacitors can retain dangerous voltage even when unplugged).
2. **Record your full model number** from the label inside the door frame or on the back panel, then look up the thermistor location and resistance specification in the service sheet or wiring diagram for your specific platform.
3. **Remove the outer cabinet** by taking out the mounting screws (usually along the back and sides) and sliding or lifting the cover off, exposing the control board and magnetron area.
4. **Locate the thermistor**, typically a small metal probe or bead with two wires clipped to the magnetron casing, cavity wall, or air duct, and trace its harness back to the control board connector.
5. **Inspect the connector and wiring** for loose pins, corrosion, burn marks, pinched or cut wires, and reseat the plug firmly; wiggle the harness gently to check for intermittent breaks.
6. **Disconnect the thermistor plug** from the control board and measure the thermistor resistance with a digital multimeter set to ohms at room temperature, then compare the reading to your model's specification (common values range from 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ at 77°F, but you must verify yours).
7. **Replace the thermistor** if the resistance is significantly out of range, open (infinite resistance), or shorted (near zero ohms); order the correct part using your model number and install it in the same clip or mount position.
8. **Reassemble the cabinet**, plug the microwave back in, and run a brief test cycle; if E2 persists after thermistor and wiring checks pass, replace the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Microwave repair involves high-voltage components that can store lethal charge even when unplugged. If you are uncomfortable working near the magnetron and capacitor, or if you cannot locate the thermistor or interpret resistance readings, call a qualified appliance technician. A pro will also have access to the exact service documentation and OEM part numbers for your specific Kenmore platform, ensuring the correct thermistor specification and reducing the risk of ordering the wrong part. If replacing the thermistor does not clear E2 and you suspect the control board, professional diagnosis can confirm the board fault before you invest in an expensive replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–300.
