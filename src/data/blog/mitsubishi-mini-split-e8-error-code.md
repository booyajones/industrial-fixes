## Mitsubishi Mini Split E8 Error — What It Means

The E8 error code on a Mitsubishi mini split system typically indicates a problem with the indoor evaporator coil temperature sensor or abnormal refrigerant flow across the coil. The system monitors coil temperature to prevent freeze-up and maintain efficiency. When the control board detects a reading outside the expected range or erratic sensor behavior, it displays E8 and may shut down to protect the compressor and coil.

The exact definition can vary slightly across Mitsubishi model families, so consult your owner's manual or the service label on the indoor unit for model-specific diagnostics. In most cases, the code points to either a blocked airflow condition (dirty filter, clogged coil, obstructed blower) that changes coil temperature, or a faulty thermistor sensor that feeds incorrect data to the board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the indoor control board first, assuming electronics have failed. Check and clean the filter and coil before ordering any boards, since restricted airflow accounts for most E8 codes and costs nothing to fix.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty air filter or coil (~50%)** Restricted airflow raises or lowers coil temperature beyond normal limits, triggering the sensor fault.
- **Faulty evaporator coil thermistor (~25%)** The sensor itself drifts out of range or develops an open or short circuit, sending bad data to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~15%)** Vibration or moisture causes the thermistor plug to lose contact, resulting in intermittent or invalid readings.
- **Low refrigerant charge (~7%)** A leak or undercharge reduces coil temperature enough that the sensor reads outside programmed thresholds.
- **Indoor blower motor failure (~3%)** If the fan runs slowly or not at all, coil temperature swings abnormally and the sensor reports a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker or disconnect switch feeding the indoor and outdoor units.
2. **Remove and clean the air filter** using warm water and mild detergent, or replace it if torn or heavily clogged.
3. **Open the front cover** of the indoor unit (typically clips or screws) and inspect the evaporator coil face for dust, pet hair, or debris.
4. **Gently vacuum or brush** the coil fins and use coil cleaner if necessary, then let it dry completely.
5. **Locate the evaporator coil thermistor**, a small sensor clipped to or inserted into the coil piping, and check its two-wire connector for corrosion or looseness.
6. **Restore power** and run the system in cooling mode for at least 10 minutes to see if the E8 code reappears.
7. **If the code persists**, measure the thermistor resistance with a multimeter (consult your model's service manual for the correct ohm range at room temperature) and replace the sensor if out of spec or open-circuit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the E8 code returns after you have cleaned the filter and coil, verified sensor connections, and power-cycled the system. Persistent errors often require refrigerant pressure testing, electronic diagnostics with manufacturer software, or replacement of the indoor control board. Any work involving refrigerant recovery, leak repair, or sealed-system brazing must be performed by an EPA-certified technician. If the blower motor is not spinning or the system repeatedly trips the breaker, stop troubleshooting and schedule professional service to avoid compressor damage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts if a sensor needs replacing, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on whether cleaning or sensor replacement is needed.
