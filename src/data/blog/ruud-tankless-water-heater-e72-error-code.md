## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E72 Error — What It Means

The E72 error code on a Ruud tankless water heater typically indicates a problem with flame detection or sensing. The unit has successfully ignited but cannot verify that the flame is present, or the flame-sensing circuit is reporting an inconsistent or weak signal. This safety feature shuts down the burner to prevent gas flow without proper combustion.

Because error-code definitions can vary between Ruud model families and firmware revisions, always consult your specific unit's installation and service manual or the diagnostic chart on the inside cover to confirm the exact meaning for your model. In general, flame-detection errors point to either a dirty or failing flame rod, poor grounding, or a faulty ignition control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the ignition control board when the real problem is a flame rod coated in carbon or mineral deposits. Clean the flame rod with fine steel wool or sandpaper before ordering any circuit board.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~55%)** Carbon deposits or mineral buildup on the flame-sensing rod insulate it from the flame and prevent the control board from detecting ionization current.
- **Loose or corroded ground connection (~20%)** The flame-detection circuit requires a solid chassis ground, and a loose ground wire or corroded ground screw can prevent proper flame-current measurement.
- **Failed flame rod (~12%)** The flame rod itself can crack or lose its insulation over time, especially in high-temperature environments, and must be replaced.
- **Faulty ignition control board (~8%)** The board that reads the flame-rod signal can develop capacitor or relay failures and misread or ignore a valid flame current.
- **Intermittent flame or poor combustion (~5%)** Low gas pressure, a partially blocked burner, or inadequate air supply can cause the flame to flicker or burn incompletely, triggering inconsistent flame sensing.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the tankless heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws, then set the panel aside.
3. **Locate the flame rod** near the burner assembly; it is a thin metal probe with a ceramic insulator and a wire connector.
4. **Disconnect the flame-rod wire** by pulling the spade connector straight off the rod terminal.
5. **Remove the mounting screw** securing the flame rod and slide the rod out of its bracket.
6. **Clean the flame rod** with fine steel wool or 400-grit sandpaper until the metal is bright and free of deposits, then wipe it with a dry cloth.
7. **Inspect the ceramic insulator** for cracks or carbon tracking; replace the rod if the insulator is damaged.
8. **Reinstall the flame rod** into its bracket, secure the mounting screw, and reconnect the wire.
9. **Check and tighten all ground connections** on the chassis and burner assembly, cleaning any corrosion with a wire brush.
10. **Replace the front cover**, restore gas and power, and observe the heater through a complete heating cycle to verify the E72 error does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and checking all grounds, or if you suspect low gas pressure or combustion-air problems. A technician can measure flame-rod microamp current, test gas pressure at the inlet and manifold, inspect the burner and heat exchanger for blockages, and safely diagnose control-board faults. Professional service is also recommended if the unit is still under warranty, since DIY repairs can void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-45 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
