## A.O. Smith Tankless E25 Error Code — What It Means

The E25 error code on A.O. Smith tankless water heaters generally indicates a problem with flame detection or ignition. The unit may be attempting to ignite but failing to detect a stable flame, or it may not be igniting at all. This safety feature shuts down the heater to prevent gas buildup.

Because error code meanings can vary slightly across different A.O. Smith tankless models, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover for the exact definition. Common triggers include gas supply issues, a dirty or corroded flame sensor, igniter problems, or venting restrictions that affect combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve or igniter when the flame sensor rod simply needs cleaning. Inspect and clean the flame rod with fine sandpaper before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor rod (~45%)** Carbon deposits or mineral buildup on the flame rod prevent it from detecting the flame, triggering the E25 code even when the burner ignites.
- **Interrupted or inadequate gas supply (~25%)** A closed or partially closed gas valve, low gas pressure, or empty propane tank stops the burner from lighting or maintaining a flame.
- **Faulty igniter or ignition cable (~15%)** A cracked ceramic igniter or damaged wire prevents the spark that lights the burner, or creates an intermittent connection.
- **Blocked or restricted venting (~10%)** Obstructions in the exhaust vent, intake vent, or condensation blockage starve the burner of oxygen or cause incomplete combustion.
- **Failed gas valve or control board (~5%)** A stuck gas valve coil or a control board that cannot send the ignition signal will prevent flame establishment and set the error code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off the gas supply** at the shutoff valve near the water heater and switch off electrical power at the breaker or disconnect.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the fasteners and carefully setting it aside to access the burner and control components.
3. **Locate the flame sensor rod** near the burner assembly, a thin metal rod extending into the combustion chamber.
4. **Clean the flame rod** by gently sanding it with 400-grit or finer sandpaper to remove carbon and mineral deposits, then wipe it dry with a lint-free cloth.
5. **Inspect the igniter** for cracks in the ceramic tip and check that the wire connection is secure and undamaged.
6. **Check the exhaust and intake vents** outside the home for blockages, bird nests, ice, or lint, and clear any obstructions.
7. **Reinstall the cover**, restore gas and power, and observe the startup sequence to confirm the burner ignites and the error clears.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the error persists after cleaning the flame rod and confirming gas supply, or if you suspect the gas valve or control board has failed. Professionals have manometers to measure gas pressure, multimeters to test electrical continuity, and the training to diagnose complex ignition and venting problems safely. If the unit is still under warranty, contact A.O. Smith or an authorized service provider to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
