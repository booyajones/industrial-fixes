## Mitsubishi P4 Error Code — What It Means

The P4 error code on Mitsubishi Electric heat pumps and mini-splits signals a condensate drain sensor or float-switch fault. The indoor unit has detected that condensate water is not draining properly, or the safety float switch has opened to prevent overflow. This is not a refrigerant or compressor problem. It is a drainage system issue, meaning water has backed up in the drain pan, the condensate pump has stopped working, or the float switch itself has failed or is stuck.

The system shuts down to protect your home from water damage. Until the drain path is clear and the float switch resets, the unit will not run in cooling or heating mode. P4 and P5 codes are both tied to the same condensate safety circuit in Mitsubishi's design.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged condensate drain line or trap** Algae, slime, dust, or debris blocks the drain tube or trap, causing water to back up into the pan and trip the float switch.
- **Failed or unpowered condensate pump** The pump motor has burned out, is mechanically stuck, or is not receiving power from the control board.
- **Faulty float switch or drain sensor** The safety float switch is stuck in the open position, has a broken arm, or the wiring harness has an open circuit or corroded connector.
- **Improper drain installation or trapped air** The drain line has insufficient pitch, an air lock, a kink, or a poorly installed trap that prevents gravity drainage or pump discharge.
- **Indoor control board output problem** The PCB does not energize the pump relay or misreads the float-switch input, triggering a false P4 alarm even when drainage is clear.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power down the unit** at the thermostat and the breaker, then wait two minutes before opening the indoor air handler or service cover to access the drain pan and components safely.
2. **Inspect the drain pan and line** for standing water, slime buildup, blockages, or kinks. Remove the drain hose from the unit and check whether water flows freely when you pour a cup into the pan.
3. **Test the float switch and pump** by slowly filling the drain pan with clean water until the float rises. The pump should start automatically and discharge the water. If it does not, note whether the pump hums, is silent, or whether the float does not move.
4. **Disconnect the drain line downstream** from the pump or unit outlet and test whether the pump can discharge into a bucket when you trigger it. This isolates a downstream clog from a pump or electrical fault.
5. **Locate and activate the service test jumper** (often labeled SWE on Mitsubishi boards) to command a manual pump and blower test cycle. Observe whether the pump runs and water is expelled.
6. **Check the float-switch harness and connectors** for corrosion, loose pins, or broken wires. Use a multimeter to verify continuity across the float switch when the float is down and an open circuit when it is raised.
7. **Replace the failed component** after confirming the root cause. Clear the drain line with a wet-dry vacuum or flush, replace the pump or float switch as needed, restore power, and monitor the unit through a full cooling cycle to confirm the P4 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components, cannot locate the float switch or pump inside your indoor unit, or if the P4 code returns after you have cleared the drain line and tested the pump. A technician has the service manual, the correct test jumpers, and the diagnostic tools to measure board outputs and verify the float-switch circuit. If the control board or pump wiring harness is damaged, or if your unit is still under warranty, professional repair protects your coverage and ensures safe, compliant work.
