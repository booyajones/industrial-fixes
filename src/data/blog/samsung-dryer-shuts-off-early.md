## Samsung Dryer Shuts Off Early — What's Happening

When a Samsung dryer stops mid-cycle or ends a load before clothes are dry, you are seeing a symptom of either an airflow restriction, an electrical supply fault, or a failed safety component. Samsung support documentation identifies restricted airflow and trapped heat as the most common cause of unexpected shutdowns, especially when the dryer stops after only a few minutes or displays a temperature-related error.

If the machine stops only on sensor-dry cycles but runs fine on timed dry, the moisture sensor bars may be dirty and falsely signaling that clothes are already dry. If the unit loses heat partway through or will not start a cycle correctly, the power supply or a thermal safety part has likely failed. Samsung also flags door latch issues, Child Lock activation, and jammed control panel buttons as possible causes of start and stop problems.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged lint screen or exhaust vent** Blocked airflow causes heat buildup that trips the safety thermostat and forces the dryer to shut down.
- **Failed thermal fuse or thermal sensor** These safety parts commonly fail when overheating occurs and will prevent the dryer from running normally until replaced.
- **Dirty moisture sensor bars** Residue on the sensor bars inside the drum causes the dryer to think clothes are dry when they are still damp, stopping the cycle early on sensor modes.
- **Improper electrical supply or bad power cord connection** Loose or burned terminals at the power cord or terminal block, or incorrect voltage, can cause the dryer to lose power or stop unexpectedly.
- **Door not latching properly or Child Lock enabled** If the door does not close fully or Child Lock is active, the dryer may not start or may stop during the cycle.
- **High-limit thermostat tripping repeatedly** Excessive vent restriction can cause the high-limit to open and close repeatedly, shutting the dryer off each time it overheats.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify whether the dryer stops only on sensor-dry cycles, stops on all cycles, shuts off with an error code, or loses heat partway through the run.
2. Remove and clean the lint screen, then inspect the lint housing, flex connector, duct run, and outside vent hood for blockage, crushed pipe, or debris.
3. Disconnect the exhaust vent temporarily and run a short test cycle to see if the dryer completes normally. If it does, the vent system is blocked and must be cleaned or repaired before reconnecting.
4. Check the power supply by confirming the breaker is not tripped, inspecting the power cord terminals for discoloration or looseness, and examining the rear terminal block for heat damage or broken wires.
5. Open the dryer door and locate the two metal moisture sensor bars inside the drum. Wipe them with a damp cloth and dry completely if the complaint is early stopping on sensor cycles.
6. Check that the door closes securely and that Child Lock is not enabled on the control panel.
7. If airflow and power are correct but the dryer still shuts off early, test the thermal fuse, thermal sensor or thermistor, and high-limit thermostat for continuity and proper operation.
8. Replace any failed thermal safety part only after confirming and correcting the root cause, because repeated failures usually mean the venting problem was not fixed.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleaned the entire vent system and checked the power supply but the dryer still shuts off early, or if you are not comfortable testing electrical components with a multimeter. Repeated thermal fuse or sensor failures after replacement usually mean there is still an airflow restriction or a control board fault that requires professional diagnosis. If your dryer requires disassembly to access internal safety parts or the terminal block shows heat damage, a qualified service provider should perform the repair.
