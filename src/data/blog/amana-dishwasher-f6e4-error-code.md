## Amana Dishwasher F6E4 Error — What It Means

The F6E4 code on Amana dishwashers (which share Whirlpool-family platform logic) indicates the control has detected an overfill or float-switch condition. The machine thinks there is a water-level problem, either because the overfill float switch in the base has been triggered or because the control sees an abnormal condition in the water management circuitry. This can happen when water actually accumulates in the base pan, when the float assembly is stuck or obstructed, or when the appliance is not sitting level and the float behaves as though the tub is overfilled.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but the float switch and level of the machine are far more likely culprits. Always inspect the float assembly for free movement and verify the dishwasher is level side-to-side and front-to-back before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck or obstructed overfill float switch (~35%)** The float assembly in the base can be held up by food debris, mineral buildup, or a broken pivot, keeping the switch open and the code active.
- **Dishwasher not level (~30%)** If the unit is tilted side-to-side or front-to-back, the float can shift position and trigger the switch even when no actual overfill is present.
- **Standing water or leak in the base pan (~20%)** Water from a hose leak, door seal, or tub crack accumulates in the base and lifts the float, which is exactly what the switch is designed to detect.
- **Corroded or loose wiring and connectors (~10%)** The harness to the float switch or control can develop corrosion, burns, or loose pins that mimic an open float circuit.
- **Failed control board or wiring fault (~5%)** If the float circuit and wiring check good but the code persists after reset, the control itself may be misreading the input or have an internal fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Switch off power** at the circuit breaker and wait one full minute, then restore power to reset the control and see if the code clears on its own.
2. **Check the dishwasher level** using a bubble level placed on the door sill side-to-side and on top of the tub front-to-back. Adjust the leveling legs until the appliance is level in both planes.
3. **Remove the lower access panel or kick plate** and inspect the base pan for standing water, dampness, or signs of a leak. If water is present, identify the leaking component (hose, door seal, pump) and repair it before continuing.
4. **Locate the overfill float assembly** in the base pan (it looks like a small cup or dome on a pivot). Lift it by hand and confirm it moves freely up and down without binding, then release it and verify it drops back to the bottom. Clean any debris or mineral buildup.
5. **Inspect the wiring harness and connectors** to the float switch and the control board for corrosion, loose pins, burns, or damage. Reseat all connectors firmly and look for signs of moisture intrusion.
6. **Test the float switch** if accessible. With power off, disconnect the switch and use a multimeter to check continuity. The switch should be closed (continuity) when the float is down and open (no continuity) when the float is lifted. Replace the switch if it does not change state or is always open.
7. **Reassemble and retest** the dishwasher. Run a short cycle and monitor for the code. If F6E4 returns and all mechanical and wiring checks passed, the control board may need replacement or professional diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you find standing water in the base but cannot locate the leak, if wiring damage extends into areas you cannot safely access, or if the code returns after you have verified the float moves freely, the machine is level, and all connectors are clean and tight. A technician can perform circuit-level testing of the float switch and control board, identify hidden leaks with specialized tools, and replace the control if needed. Also call a pro if you are not comfortable working around 120 V wiring or removing panels to access the base area.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts (float switch or wiring), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280 service call and diagnosis.
