## Hoshizaki F1 Error Code — What It Means

The F1 error on Hoshizaki ice machines indicates abnormal drain pump operation — the drain pump either failed to start, ran longer than expected without draining the water trough, or the control board detected abnormal current in the drain pump circuit. Hoshizaki uses drain pumps on many KM and IM series models to remove water during harvest; F1 means that process failed.

## Common Causes

- **Drain pump motor failed** — The pump motor's impeller is seized or the motor windings have failed, preventing the pump from moving water.
- **Drain line blocked** — Ice, scale, or debris has blocked the drain line, causing the pump to run against a blocked outlet. The pump runs but water doesn't drain, and the cycle times out.
- **Drain pump check valve stuck** — Some models have a check valve in the drain line that prevents backflow. A stuck valve blocks drainage even with a functional pump.
- **Control board not energizing pump** — A failed relay or output on the control board prevents the pump from receiving power.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Run a manual drain cycle** — Press and hold the drain button (if available on your model) to trigger a drain. Listen for the pump motor running and check if water flows from the drain line.
2. **Inspect the drain line** — Check the drain hose from the machine to the floor drain for kinks, ice blockage (especially in cold rooms), or scale buildup. Clear any obstruction.
3. **Test the drain pump** — Disconnect the pump and apply 120V directly (or use a multimeter to check for voltage at the pump terminals during drain cycle). No voltage = board/relay fault. Voltage with no pump operation = failed pump motor.
4. **Check the drain check valve** — Locate the check valve in the drain circuit and verify it moves freely. Replace if stuck.
5. **Reset and test** — After repair, cycle power and run the machine through a full cycle. Confirm F1 does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

If drain pump, line, and valve are all clear and F1 persists, the control board may have a failed relay output. Board diagnostics and replacement require a certified refrigeration technician.
