## What this code means
The E13 error code on a Bosch dishwasher signals a high-water-temperature or overheat fault. The machine has detected that the wash water in the tub has risen above the safe limit of approximately 75°C (167°F). This is not a drainage problem. Instead, it points to an issue in the temperature-sensing or heating control circuit.

The fault is designed to protect the dishwasher from damage caused by excessively hot water. The control board monitors the thermistor and shuts down operation when the reading exceeds the threshold. Common triggers include a faulty temperature sensor sending a false high reading, a stuck heater relay that cannot turn off the element, or incoming water that is already too hot at the supply connection.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first. Instead, test the temperature sensor with a multimeter and verify the incoming water supply temperature before spending on electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty temperature sensor or thermistor (~45%)** The sensor near the heater assembly sends a false high-temperature signal to the control board, triggering the E13 alarm even when water is not actually overheated.
- **Stuck heater relay or control board fault (~30%)** The relay on the main control board fails to cut power to the heating element, allowing the water to continue heating past the safe limit.
- **Incoming water supply temperature too high (~15%)** The dishwasher is connected to a hot-water line or the household water heater is set abnormally high, so water entering the tub is already near or above the threshold.
- **Damaged or loose wiring and connectors (~10%)** Heat-affected or corroded connections in the sensor-heater-control circuit create intermittent faults or erroneous readings that the board interprets as an overheat condition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the dishwasher, then allow the unit to cool completely before opening any panels or touching internal components.
2. **Verify the water supply connection** by confirming the dishwasher inlet hose is attached to a cold-water valve and that your household water heater is set below 60°C (140°F) to rule out incoming water that is already too hot.
3. **Remove the lower access panel** or kick plate and locate the temperature sensor or thermistor, typically mounted near the heating element at the bottom of the tub.
4. **Inspect the sensor and its connector** for physical damage, corrosion, heat discoloration, or loose terminals, and disconnect the sensor harness to measure resistance with a multimeter (consult your model's service data for the expected reading at room temperature).
5. **Check the heater control path** by visually inspecting the heater relay on the main control board for burnt contacts or signs of overheating, and examine all wiring between the sensor, heater, and board for heat damage or broken strands.
6. **Replace the failed component** identified by your diagnosis (sensor, control board, or heater relay), reconnect all harnesses, and restore the lower panel.
7. **Restore power and run a monitored test cycle** to confirm the dishwasher heats normally without re-triggering E13, watching for stable operation and proper shutoff of the heating element.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with electrical diagnostics, if multimeter testing and visual inspection do not reveal an obvious fault, or if the E13 error persists after replacing the temperature sensor and verifying correct water supply temperature. A technician has factory service data, board-level diagnostic tools, and the experience to isolate intermittent faults in the heater control circuit. Also call a pro if you need the control board replaced and your model requires programming or if local codes require a licensed appliance technician for warranty or insurance purposes.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25–75 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
