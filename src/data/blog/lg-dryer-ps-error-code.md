## LG Dryer PS Error Code — What It Means

The PS code on an LG dryer signals improper voltage at the power cord and terminal block connection. LG documentation states this code appears when the dryer detects incorrect electrical supply conditions, most commonly caused by reversed white and red wire connections on the dryer's terminal block. This is a power-supply and installation fault, not a drum, sensor, or heating element problem.

Because the dryer requires 240 V to operate correctly, the PS code will trigger if the supply voltage is wrong, the cord is incorrectly wired, or the connection is loose. LG specifically instructs users to verify the outlet matches the dryer's 240 V rating, reset the breaker, and check for reversed conductors at the terminal block.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board thinking it is faulty. Before ordering any parts, inspect the terminal block wiring and measure the outlet voltage with a multimeter.

## Common Causes

- **Reversed white and red wires at the terminal block (~50%)** LG states this is the documented cause of the PS code, typically occurring during installation or after service work when the power cord is reconnected incorrectly.
- **Loose power cord connection (~25%)** A poor or loose connection at the dryer terminal block or wall outlet can cause intermittent voltage issues and trigger the PS code.
- **Incorrect or unstable supply voltage (~15%)** If the outlet does not deliver 240 V (for example, if one leg of the circuit is out or the breaker is tripped), the dryer will display PS.
- **Tripped or faulty branch circuit breaker (~10%)** LG instructs users to reset the dryer breaker, since a partially tripped or weak breaker can cause low or incorrect voltage at the outlet.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer or switch off its dedicated circuit breaker** and wait at least 10 seconds to reset the control board and clear transient errors.
2. **Restore power** and run a short test cycle to see if the PS code reappears.
3. **Verify the wall outlet** is a properly grounded 240 V dryer receptacle matching the dryer's rating plate and that the circuit breaker is fully on (reset it if needed).
4. **Disconnect power again**, then remove the dryer's rear access panel to expose the terminal block where the power cord connects.
5. **Inspect the terminal block wiring** and confirm the white (neutral) and red (hot) conductors are connected to the correct terminals per the dryer's wiring diagram (usually found on the back panel or in the manual).
6. **Correct any reversed connections** by loosening the terminal screws, swapping the white and red wires if they are reversed, and retightening securely.
7. **Check all connections** at the terminal block for tightness and signs of arcing or corrosion, then reassemble the panel, restore power, and test the dryer again.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable working with 240 V wiring or if you cannot identify which wires belong on which terminal. An electrician or appliance technician can measure the outlet voltage with a multimeter, inspect the terminal block for correct wiring, and verify the home circuit breaker and branch wiring are sound. If the PS code persists after confirming correct wiring and supply voltage, LG states the dryer will need service, which may involve internal control or wiring faults that require part-level diagnosis and replacement by a qualified technician.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $80–180 (service call to correct wiring or replace power cord).
