## Bosch Washer E06 Error Code — What It Means

The E06 error code on Bosch washing machines generally indicates a problem with the door lock mechanism. The washer's control system has detected that the door cannot be locked or that the lock assembly is not communicating properly. This safety feature prevents the machine from running when the door cannot be secured, protecting against leaks and imbalance during the spin cycle.

Because the exact meaning of error codes can vary between Bosch model families and production years, always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the appliance to confirm the specific definition for your unit. In most cases, the fault involves the door latch, the electronic door lock assembly, or the wiring between the lock and the main control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board when the door lock assembly or a loose connector is actually at fault. Always test the lock for continuity and inspect the harness plug before ordering expensive electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock assembly (~55%)** The electronic interlock mechanism inside the door lock wears out or the solenoid coil fails, preventing the control from detecting a secure lock.
- **Misaligned or broken door latch (~20%)** The plastic strike or catch on the door frame is cracked, bent, or blocked by lint, so the latch cannot engage fully.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness (~15%)** The multi-pin connector at the lock assembly works loose from vibration or develops corrosion, interrupting the signal to the control board.
- **Main control board fault (~8%)** The relay or microprocessor on the control board that drives or reads the door lock signal has failed.
- **Obstruction in the door seal (~2%)** Clothing, coins, or other debris caught in the rubber boot prevents the door from closing tightly enough to trigger the lock.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and shut off the water supply valves to make sure safe working conditions.
2. **Open the door** and inspect the rubber boot and door catch for foreign objects, lint buildup, or cracks in the plastic latch.
3. **Remove the door lock assembly** by reaching inside the boot, releasing the retaining clips or screws, and unplugging the wiring harness.
4. **Test the lock with a multimeter** set to continuity mode by probing the terminals on the lock assembly according to your model's wiring diagram.
5. **Inspect the harness connector** for bent pins, corrosion, or moisture and clean with contact cleaner if necessary.
6. **Install the new or tested lock** by snapping it into place, reconnecting the harness, and verifying the door closes and latches properly.
7. **Restore power and run a test cycle** to confirm the E06 error is cleared and the door locks during operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have tested the door lock assembly and harness and the error persists, since the fault may lie in the main control board or internal wiring that requires disassembly of the cabinet and diagnostic equipment. Also seek service if you are uncomfortable working with electrical testing tools or if your washer is still under warranty, as DIY repairs can void coverage. A technician can use manufacturer software to read detailed fault logs and pinpoint intermittent issues that do not show up during simple continuity tests.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $40-90 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
