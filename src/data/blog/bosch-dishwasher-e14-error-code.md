## What this code means
Bosch identifies E14 as a flow meter failure or an issue with water flowing into the dishwasher filling system. The dishwasher detects that water is not entering at the expected rate or that the flow sensor in the inlet path is not signaling correctly. This code halts the wash cycle to prevent damage from insufficient water.

The flow meter, also called a reed sensor or flow switch, sits in the inlet hose or inlet pipe assembly and monitors the stream of incoming water. When it fails or becomes obstructed, the control board cannot confirm that water is flowing and throws E14. A kinked inlet hose, closed supply valve, or faulty water inlet valve can also trigger the code by restricting flow.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the water inlet valve first, assuming poor fill means a bad valve. Test water supply pressure and inspect the flow meter and its wiring before swapping the valve, since a dirty or loose sensor is cheaper and more commonly at fault.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty flow meter or reed sensor (~45%)** The sensor in the inlet path fails to signal water movement, either from internal wear, contamination, or a loose magnet inside the flow chamber.
- **Restricted or kinked inlet hose (~20%)** A bent, crushed, or partially blocked inlet hose reduces flow below the threshold the sensor expects, triggering the fault even though the sensor itself is good.
- **Low or closed water supply (~15%)** The supply valve is not fully open or household water pressure is too low to deliver adequate flow through the meter.
- **Corroded or loose wiring at the flow sensor (~12%)** Moisture or vibration damages the connector pins or harness at the sensor, breaking the signal path to the control board.
- **Defective water inlet valve (~8%)** The solenoid valve sticks partially closed or opens weakly, starving the dishwasher and preventing the flow meter from reading normal flow.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power and water.** Unplug the dishwasher or switch off the breaker, then close the supply valve under the sink and remove the inlet hose from the valve.
2. **Verify supply conditions.** Turn the supply valve back on briefly into a bucket to confirm strong flow and adequate pressure, then close it again and reconnect the hose without kinks.
3. **Access the flow meter.** Pull the dishwasher forward and locate the flow sensor assembly in the inlet hose or inlet pipe near the water valve, typically a small cylindrical housing with a wiring connector.
4. **Inspect and clean the sensor.** Disconnect the wiring plug and check for corrosion or moisture on the pins. Remove the flow meter from its housing if possible and flush out any debris or mineral buildup.
5. **Test or replace the flow meter.** If the sensor is cracked, the internal magnet rattles, or the contacts look burnt, replace the flow meter assembly with a genuine Bosch part matching your model E-Nr.
6. **Check the inlet valve.** If the flow meter is clean and wired correctly but the code persists, test the inlet valve for proper opening by applying voltage per your wiring diagram or replace it if weak flow is observed.
7. **Reassemble and test.** Reconnect all hoses and wiring, restore power and water, and run a short cycle to confirm normal fill operation and that the E14 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have verified good water supply, cleaned the flow meter, checked all wiring connections, and the code still appears. A pro can perform electrical continuity tests on the sensor circuit, measure control-board signal output, and diagnose whether the main control board is misinterpreting a good sensor. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water connections inside a built-in appliance or if your dishwasher is still under warranty, since some Bosch warranties require authorized service for electronic faults.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
