## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E97 Error — What It Means

The E97 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters indicates a fault detected by the unit's control system. Because error code definitions vary by model and firmware version, the exact meaning of E97 is not universal across all Ruud tankless models. Some models use this code to flag internal communication errors, sensor faults, or power supply irregularities. Your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit's cover will provide the specific definition for your model.

Before attempting repairs, consult your model's documentation to confirm what E97 represents on your particular unit. Common underlying issues include loose wiring connections, voltage fluctuations, faulty sensors, or control board communication problems. Do not assume the code means the same thing as on other brands or even other Ruud models.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board without first checking power supply voltage and wiring connections. A multimeter test of incoming voltage and a visual inspection of harness connectors often reveal the real problem.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connection (~30%)** Vibration and temperature cycling can loosen terminal screws or corrode spade connectors, interrupting communication between sensors and the control board.
- **Low or unstable supply voltage (~25%)** Inadequate voltage from the breaker panel or a weak connection at the disconnect can trigger fault codes as the board struggles to maintain stable operation.
- **Failed temperature sensor (~20%)** Inlet, outlet, or combustion temperature sensors can drift out of range or fail open, causing the board to log a fault and halt operation.
- **Control board communication fault (~15%)** Internal firmware glitches or component wear on the board can produce spurious error codes that clear with a power cycle or require board replacement.
- **Faulty flame rod or ignition system (~10%)** An intermittent flame signal or ignition fault can register as a system error on some models, especially if the code is tied to combustion safety.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply valve to the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the unit by unscrewing the fasteners (usually Phillips-head screws) and carefully lifting the panel away.
3. **Inspect all wire connectors** on the control board and sensors for secure seating, corrosion, or burn marks. Reseat any loose connectors.
4. **Measure incoming voltage** at the terminal block using a multimeter set to AC voltage. Compare the reading to the nameplate specification.
5. **Check each temperature sensor** by disconnecting its connector and measuring resistance with a multimeter (consult your model's service manual for the expected resistance at room temperature).
6. **If all wiring and voltage are correct**, consult your owner's manual to identify which sensor or component the E97 code flags on your specific model, then replace the faulty part.
7. **Restore power and gas**, then run a test cycle to verify the error does not return. If it does, the control board may need replacement by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after basic wiring and voltage checks, or if you need to replace the control board or combustion components. Gas-fired tankless heaters require precise combustion tuning and venting checks that only trained professionals should perform. A technician will have the model-specific service codes, diagnostic software, and replacement parts to resolve intermittent faults safely and correctly.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
