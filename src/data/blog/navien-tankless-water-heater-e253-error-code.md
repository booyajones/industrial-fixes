## Navien E253 Error Code — What It Means

The E253 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with the combustion fan circuit or venting system. The unit's control board has detected that the fan is not spinning at the expected speed, that airflow is restricted, or that the pressure switch is not signaling proper combustion air movement. This safety lockout prevents the unit from firing until the issue is resolved.

Because combustion fan and venting codes protect against dangerous exhaust conditions, the heater will not operate until the fault is cleared. Common triggers include blocked intake or exhaust pipes, a failing combustion fan motor, a dirty fan blade assembly, or a faulty air pressure switch. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit for model-specific details.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the air pressure switch or control board when the real problem is a dirty or failing combustion fan motor. Inspect the fan blades for dust buildup and listen for abnormal noise before ordering electrical parts.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted venting (~40%)** Debris, ice, or condensation in the exhaust or intake pipes prevents proper airflow and triggers the pressure switch.
- **Combustion fan motor failure (~30%)** The fan motor bearings wear out or the windings fail, causing the fan to spin too slowly or not at all.
- **Dirty or obstructed fan blades (~15%)** Dust and lint accumulate on the fan assembly, reducing airflow and preventing the pressure switch from closing.
- **Faulty air pressure switch (~10%)** The switch that confirms proper combustion airflow fails to close or signal the control board correctly.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~5%)** Connections at the fan motor or pressure switch become oxidized or vibrate loose, interrupting the control signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Inspect the exhaust and intake vents** on the exterior of your home for obstructions, ice buildup, or animal nests, and clear any blockages.
3. **Remove the front panel** of the unit to access the combustion fan assembly, usually mounted on the left or top of the burner chamber.
4. **Check the fan blades** for dust, lint, or corrosion, and clean them gently with a soft brush or compressed air if dirty.
5. **Test the fan motor** by spinning the blades by hand to confirm they rotate freely without grinding or resistance.
6. **Inspect all wiring** to the fan motor and air pressure switch for loose, corroded, or burnt connections, and repair or replace as needed.
7. **Replace the combustion fan motor** if it does not spin freely or if you detect electrical burn marks or unusual noise, following the manufacturer's replacement procedure for your model.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if the combustion fan requires replacement, or if clearing vents and resetting the unit does not resolve the error. Combustion and venting faults involve carbon monoxide risk, so professional diagnosis is recommended when the cause is not obvious. A technician can measure fan RPM, test the pressure switch with a manometer, and verify proper combustion air settings. If the error persists after fan and switch replacement, the control board may need replacement or reprogramming, which requires factory training and diagnostic tools.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
