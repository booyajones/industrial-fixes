## What this code means
The PO code on a Whirlpool refrigerator stands for Power Outage. It means the unit detected that electrical power was interrupted long enough for the freezer temperature to rise to 18°F or higher. On side-by-side models the display shows PO, an alert tone sounds three times, and the Confirm prompt blinks until you acknowledge it. This is an informational alert, not a component failure code. The refrigerator is telling you it lost power and that food safety may have been compromised during the outage.

In most cases the code appears after a utility outage, a tripped breaker, or a temporary loss of power to the appliance. Once you restore power and the refrigerator runs normally, you simply need to clear the alert by pressing the MEASURED FILL button. If the PO code keeps returning without any known outage, the problem is usually an unstable electrical supply, a loose plug, a faulty outlet, or a damaged power cord rather than a refrigeration system fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or user interface when the PO code reappears, but the real cause is almost always an electrical supply problem. Check the outlet, breaker, and power cord for damage or loose connections before replacing any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Actual power outage or utility interruption (~50%)** A loss of utility power or a brief brownout allows the freezer temperature to rise above 18°F, triggering the alert.
- **Tripped circuit breaker (~25%)** The breaker supplying the refrigerator tripped due to overload or a fault, cutting power until it is manually reset.
- **Loose or damaged power cord (~15%)** A plug that is not fully seated or a cord with internal breaks causes intermittent power loss.
- **Faulty wall outlet or receptacle (~8%)** Worn contacts, backstab connections, or arcing inside the outlet deliver unstable voltage or drop power intermittently.
- **Control panel or main control board fault (~2%)** A defective user interface or electronic control board fails to register the acknowledgment or misreads power status, but this is uncommon for a true PO code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Acknowledge the alert** by pressing the MEASURED FILL button on the control panel while the Confirm prompt is blinking. This clears the PO message if power has been restored and the refrigerator is running normally.
2. **Check for a known power event** by asking household members if the lights went out, looking at clocks for flashing displays, or checking your utility provider's outage map to confirm whether a utility interruption occurred.
3. **Inspect the circuit breaker** in your home's electrical panel. If the breaker for the refrigerator is tripped or loose, reset it firmly to the ON position and listen for the compressor to start.
4. **Examine the power cord and plug** for visible damage such as cracks, burn marks, or bent prongs. Unplug the refrigerator and check that the plug fits snugly in the outlet without wobbling.
5. **Test the wall outlet** by plugging in a lamp or small appliance to verify the receptacle delivers steady power. If the test device flickers or fails, the outlet or circuit wiring needs repair.
6. **Perform a hard reset** if the code does not clear. Unplug the refrigerator or turn off its breaker for 5 to 10 minutes, then restore power and check whether the display returns to normal operation.
7. **Monitor for recurrence** over the next few days. If the PO code reappears without any known outage, call a technician to test supply voltage stability and inspect the main control board and user interface for command registration faults.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the PO code returns repeatedly and you have confirmed the outlet and breaker are working correctly. A technician can measure supply voltage with a multimeter, check for intermittent drops or surges, and test the main control board and user interface for faults. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with electrical panels or if testing reveals that the house wiring or outlet needs repair, since that work requires a licensed electrician. If the refrigerator is under warranty, contact Whirlpool or an authorized service center before replacing any electronic components.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0–50 if only resetting or replacing a damaged cord, 10–30 min. A pro service call runs about $80–200 for a service call to diagnose intermittent power or replace outlet components.
