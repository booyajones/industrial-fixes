## What this code means
The E08 error code (also displayed as 8:1) on a Kenmore Elite dishwasher specifically indicates an Inlet Water fault defined as Low Water/Air in Pump. The control board detected that the water level inside the tub was too low during the fill cycle, or that air was trapped in the pump preventing proper circulation. This code is different from the E08 code on Bosch dishwashers, which refers to a heating element issue. On Kenmore models, the dishwasher did not fill with enough water to complete the cycle safely.

This fault triggers when the pressure sensor cannot detect the expected water level rise within a set time window. The machine stops the cycle to prevent damage to the pump or other components that require water for cooling and lubrication.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some people replace the control board or pressure switch first. Always clean the inlet valve screen and verify water supply pressure before replacing any electrical component.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted inlet valve or clogged screen (~35%)** Sediment or debris in the inlet valve filter blocks water flow so the tub cannot fill to the required level.
- **Low home water pressure (~20%)** The dishwasher requires more than 20 psi supply pressure, and insufficient pressure prevents adequate fill.
- **Siphoning from improper drain hose installation (~15%)** A drain hose installed too low allows water to drain out as fast as it fills, keeping the level below the sensor threshold.
- **Air trapped in pump or suction line (~12%)** Air pockets in the circulation path prevent the pressure switch from sensing the true water level.
- **Faulty water level sensor (pressure switch) (~10%)** The pressure switch fails to detect the water level rise even when water is present in the tub.
- **Failed inlet valve solenoid (~8%)** The valve coil does not energize or the valve mechanism is stuck closed, blocking water entry entirely.

<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Siphoning is unlikely. Test the pressure switch and check for air in the pump.<br><strong>No:</strong> Install a high loop (20 inches minimum) to prevent water from draining back out during the fill.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power and water** by unplugging the dishwasher or switching off the breaker, then closing the supply valve under the sink.
2. **Remove the lower kickplate** and locate the inlet valve at the left front corner where the water line connects.
3. **Unscrew the water supply line** from the inlet valve and pull out the inlet screen filter with needle-nose pliers.
4. **Clean the screen** under running water and inspect for sediment, scale, or debris that restricts flow.
5. **Test the inlet valve** with a multimeter for continuity across the solenoid coil (typically 500 to 1500 ohms, consult your model's table).
6. **Check the drain hose installation** to confirm it has a high loop of at least 20 inches before dropping to the drain or garbage disposal.
7. **Reassemble and reset the control** by pressing Heated Dry, Normal, Heated Dry, Normal in sequence (hold 2 to 3 seconds each), then press Cancel/Drain to clear the code and test a fill cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have verified full water supply pressure (above 20 psi), cleaned the inlet valve screen, confirmed a proper drain hose loop, and the error persists. A technician can use a vacuum pump to test the pressure switch threshold and check wiring continuity to the control board. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with water supply lines or removing the lower access panel, or if the inlet valve requires soldering or special tools to replace.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
