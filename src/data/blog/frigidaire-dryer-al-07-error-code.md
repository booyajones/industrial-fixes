## Frigidaire Dryer AL-07 Error Code — What It Means

AL-07 is not a documented fault code in Frigidaire's standard dryer code families. Most Frigidaire dryers use codes like E61, E63, and E64 for heating circuit faults. AL-07 may be a model-specific display, a misread code, or a nonstandard format on certain models. If your dryer shows AL-07 and stops heating or fails to dry, the underlying problem is almost always in the heating circuit. The most common verified Frigidaire heating faults point to a failed heating element, a stuck relay on the main control board, or a grounded or open heating circuit. Without official documentation for AL-07, the safest approach is to inspect the heating element and control board using the same diagnostic steps Frigidaire technicians follow for verified heat-related codes.

## Common Causes

- **Failed or grounded heating element** The heating element can burn out internally, develop an open circuit, or short to the dryer frame and trigger a fault.
- **Stuck or failed heating relay on the main control board** A relay that stays closed or fails open will prevent normal heat cycling and set a heating error.
- **Open or damaged heating circuit wiring** Loose terminals, burned connectors, or broken wires between the heater and control board interrupt continuity.
- **Heating element grounded to chassis** Any part of the heater assembly or its wiring touching the metal cabinet creates a ground fault condition.
- **Misread or model-specific code format** Some Frigidaire models may display non-standard codes that require model-specific service literature to decode correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the dryer completely before opening any panels or touching any internal components.
2. Record the full model number from the door frame or rear panel and search for model-specific code definitions if AL-07 does not match standard Frigidaire codes.
3. Remove the rear or lower access panel to expose the heating element canister and check for visible burn marks, loose connections, or wires touching the frame.
4. Test the heating element for continuity using a multimeter set to ohms. A working element will show a reading in the range of 20 to 30 ohms depending on the model. An open reading means the element is broken. A reading to ground means the element is shorted to the chassis.
5. Inspect the heating circuit wiring harness and terminals for corrosion, melted insulation, or loose plugs and repair or replace any damaged connectors.
6. If the element and wiring pass all tests, inspect the main control board for visible signs of relay damage or burn marks and test the heating relay for stuck contacts or failure to switch.
7. Reassemble all panels, restore power, and run a heat cycle to verify the fault clears and the dryer heats normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you cannot locate the AL-07 code in your owner's manual and the dryer continues to fault after inspecting the heating element and wiring. A professional has access to model-specific service manuals and can decode nonstandard error displays. Also call for help if you measure unexpected voltage on the heater circuit with the dryer unplugged, if the control board shows burn damage you are not comfortable replacing, or if the dryer faults immediately on every start even after parts replacement.
