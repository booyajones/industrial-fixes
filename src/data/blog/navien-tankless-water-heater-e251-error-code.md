## Navien E251 Error Code — What It Means

The E251 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with water flow detection or circulation. The unit's control board is not receiving the expected signal from the flow sensor, or the system is experiencing restricted flow that prevents proper operation. This can occur when the sensor itself is fouled, when inlet filters are clogged, when there is air trapped in the lines, or when system pressure is too low. Because the heater relies on flow sensing to activate burner operation and prevent overheating, the unit will shut down and display this code when flow conditions fall outside the expected range.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor when the real issue is a clogged inlet filter or trapped air. Clean all inlet screens and purge air from the system before ordering a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter (~40%)** Sediment, scale, or debris blocking the cold water inlet screen restricts flow below the threshold the sensor can detect.
- **Debris in flow sensor (~25%)** Small particles lodged in the paddle wheel or turbine of the flow sensor prevent it from spinning freely and generating the correct signal.
- **Air trapped in system (~15%)** Air pockets in the heat exchanger or piping create erratic flow readings and prevent the sensor from registering steady water movement.
- **Low water pressure (~10%)** Incoming supply pressure below the unit's minimum requirement causes insufficient flow to activate the sensor properly.
- **Failed flow sensor (~8%)** The sensor itself has failed electrically or mechanically and no longer sends a valid signal to the control board.
- **Control board fault (~2%)** The main PCB is not processing the flow sensor signal correctly due to a component failure or poor connection.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the breaker or disconnect switch and close the isolation valves on the cold inlet and hot outlet.
2. **Remove the front cover** and locate the cold water inlet filter housing, usually at the lower left of the unit where the supply line enters.
3. **Unscrew the inlet filter cap** using a wrench and pull out the mesh screen, then rinse it thoroughly under running water to remove all sediment and scale.
4. **Locate the flow sensor** on the cold water line inside the unit, disconnect the wiring harness, and remove the sensor from its housing using the retaining clips or screws.
5. **Inspect the sensor turbine** for debris or buildup, then clean gently with water and a soft brush, taking care not to damage the paddle wheel or internal components.
6. **Reinstall the sensor and filter**, reconnect the wiring, and open the isolation valves slowly while watching for leaks at all connections.
7. **Purge air from the system** by opening a hot tap downstream and allowing water to run until flow is steady and free of sputtering, then close the tap and restore power to the heater and check for code clearance.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you have cleaned all filters, purged the system, and verified adequate inlet pressure but the E251 code persists. A pro can test the flow sensor output voltage, inspect wiring continuity, and diagnose control board faults that require specialized tools. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working with water connections under pressure, if the unit is still under warranty, or if you discover signs of a more serious issue such as a leaking heat exchanger or corroded electrical contacts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-150 in parts if sensor replacement needed, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $150-300 for service call and diagnosis.
