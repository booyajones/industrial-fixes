## Frigidaire Microwave Turntable Not Turning — What's Happening

When your Frigidaire microwave turntable stops turning, the glass tray sits motionless while the unit may still heat food normally. This is not an error code on the display but a mechanical or electrical failure in the rotation system beneath the cooking cavity. The turntable motor located under the microwave floor drives a plastic coupling that spins a roller guide, which then rotates the glass tray. If any component in this chain fails, the tray stops moving and food heats unevenly.

The microwave control board usually does not detect this fault or stop operation. You will notice hot and cold spots in food or see the tray sitting still during a cook cycle. The problem ranges from simple debris blockage to motor burnout.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Worn or broken roller guide** The plastic ring with wheels accumulates food debris that seizes the wheels, or the plastic cracks and prevents smooth rotation even when the motor runs.
- **Failed drive coupling** The small plastic coupling connecting the motor shaft to the roller guide cracks or wears out on the inside hole, causing it to slip and not transfer rotation.
- **Burned out turntable motor** The motor itself fails due to age, overheating, or electrical surge, stopping all rotation at the source.
- **Obstruction or misalignment** Food debris under the tray, incorrect seating of the glass tray on the roller guide, or the tray placed upside down blocks movement.
- **Defective control board** The user control board fails to send power to the turntable motor, leaving the motor unpowered even though it is mechanically intact.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Unplug the microwave and remove the glass tray and roller guide from inside the cavity.
2. Inspect the roller guide wheels by spinning them manually to check for seized wheels, cracks, or food debris, then clean with warm soapy water or replace if damaged.
3. Remove the drive coupling (small plastic piece in the center of the floor) and inspect the inside hole for rounding or wear and the body for cracks, replacing if worn or broken.
4. Reassemble the roller guide and tray, plug in the microwave, and run a test cycle to see if the tray now rotates.
5. If the tray still does not turn, unplug the unit and remove the bottom access panel to expose the turntable motor beneath the base.
6. Plug the microwave back in and use a multimeter set to AC voltage to measure power across the motor terminals during a cook cycle (should read close to line voltage or transformer output, typically 24V AC or similar depending on model).
7. If voltage is present but the motor does not spin, replace the turntable motor.
8. If no voltage is present at the motor, trace wiring back to the control board and check for loose connections or replace the control board if all wiring is intact.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you find no voltage at the motor terminals and suspect control board failure, or if you are uncomfortable working with live electrical components during voltage testing. Also call a tech if the motor tests good electrically but still will not spin, as internal motor windings may be shorted in a way that requires advanced diagnostics. If you are unsure about removing panels or accessing the motor safely, a qualified appliance technician can complete the repair and avoid risk of shock or further damage.
