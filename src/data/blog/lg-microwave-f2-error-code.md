## LG Microwave F2 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 error code on an LG microwave indicates a thermistor fault or temperature out of range condition. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that monitors the internal cavity temperature to prevent overheating and make sure safe operation. When the control board reads a signal from the thermistor that falls outside expected limits (open circuit, short circuit, or abnormal resistance), it displays F2 and typically stops heating.

LG's official microwave support documentation states that this code requires service. The fault may be in the thermistor itself, its wiring harness and connector, or the control board input that reads the sensor signal. Less commonly, the code can reflect a real overheating condition caused by blocked ventilation or a failed cooling fan, though in most cases the thermistor circuit itself has failed.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the entire control board first when F2 appears, but the thermistor and its connector are far cheaper and account for most failures. Always test the thermistor resistance and inspect the harness before ordering the board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor itself has drifted out of specification, shorted, or opened internally, sending a signal the control board cannot interpret.
- **Loose or corroded thermistor connector (~25%)** The harness connector at the thermistor or control board has backed out, corroded, or accumulated grease and moisture, interrupting the signal.
- **Damaged thermistor wiring (~12%)** The wires between the thermistor and control board have been pinched, melted by heat, or severed, creating an open or intermittent circuit.
- **Control board input fault (~8%)** The control board circuit that reads the thermistor has failed or developed a cold solder joint, misreading an otherwise good sensor.
- **Actual overheating condition (~5%)** Blocked ventilation, a failed cooling fan, or extended high-power operation has caused true over-temperature, and the thermistor is reporting accurately.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the microwave or switching off the circuit breaker to eliminate shock hazard before any inspection.
2. **Remove the outer cabinet** by taking out the screws on the back and sides, then lifting or sliding the cover off. Keep hands away from the high-voltage capacitor.
3. **Discharge the high-voltage capacitor** using an insulated screwdriver to short the terminals together (or use a proper discharge tool). The capacitor can hold lethal voltage even when unplugged.
4. **Locate the thermistor** near the magnetron or waveguide. It is typically a small sensor with two wires clipped or screwed to a metal surface.
5. **Inspect the connector and harness** for corrosion, loose pins, or damaged insulation. Reseat the connector firmly and clean any corrosion with electrical contact cleaner.
6. **Measure thermistor resistance** using a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature most thermistors read several thousand ohms. Consult your model's service manual for the exact resistance curve.
7. **Replace the thermistor** if it reads open, shorted, or significantly out of range. Use the OEM part number from your model's wiring diagram or parts list.
8. **Reassemble the microwave** by reinstalling the cabinet, restoring power, and running a one-minute test cycle. Confirm that F2 does not return and normal heating resumes.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional microwave technician if you are not comfortable discharging the high-voltage capacitor, removing the outer cabinet, or working near the magnetron and transformer. Microwaves store dangerous voltage even when unplugged, and improper handling can cause lethal shock or microwave-radiation exposure if the door interlock system is disturbed. A technician will also have the service manual with exact thermistor resistance tables and the diagnostic tools to pinpoint control board faults versus sensor faults quickly. If the thermistor and harness test good but the code persists, the control board itself may need replacement, a repair best left to someone with OEM training and anti-static handling procedures.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–280.
