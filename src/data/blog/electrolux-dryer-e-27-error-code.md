## Electrolux Dryer E27 Error Code — What It Means

The E27 error code on Electrolux dryers indicates a communication or control system fault. Because error code meanings can vary across different Electrolux dryer models and production years, the exact definition may differ. Consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cabinet for the precise meaning on your specific model.

Generally, this type of code points to a problem with signal transmission between the main control board and another component, a sensor reading that falls outside expected parameters, or a wiring issue. The dryer may refuse to start, stop mid-cycle, or display the code on the control panel.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many owners replace the main control board first when E27 appears, but loose or corroded wiring connectors and failed thermostats or thermistors are more common. Inspect all wiring harnesses and test sensors with a multimeter before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** Vibration and heat can cause connectors between the control board and sensors to work loose or develop corrosion, breaking the communication path.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~30%)** A thermistor that has drifted out of specification or failed open or short will send incorrect signals to the control board and trigger a fault code.
- **Faulty control board (~20%)** The main electronic control board can develop solder cracks, component failures, or moisture damage that disrupts internal communication circuits.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** Wires can be pinched, abraded, or chewed by rodents, creating an open circuit or intermittent short that the control system detects as a fault.
- **Failed user interface board (~5%)** The display and button panel may lose communication with the main control board due to connector issues or board-level failure.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker, then wait two minutes and restore power to attempt a control reset.
2. **Access the cabinet** by removing the top panel or rear access cover according to your model's service instructions, using a putty knife or screwdriver to release clips.
3. **Inspect all wiring harnesses** running between the main control board (usually behind the console), the user interface, and sensors mounted on the blower housing or drum, looking for loose connectors, corrosion, or pinched wires.
4. **Reseat every connector** firmly by unplugging and reconnecting each one, and clean any corrosion with electrical contact cleaner and a small brush.
5. **Locate the thermistors** (small bead-type sensors with two wires) on the blower housing or exhaust duct, disconnect one, and measure resistance with a multimeter at room temperature, comparing the reading to your model's specification table.
6. **Replace any sensor** that reads open, short, or far outside the expected range by removing the mounting screw, disconnecting the wiring, installing the new sensor, and reconnecting the harness.
7. **Reassemble the cabinet**, restore power, and run a test cycle to verify the error code has cleared and the dryer operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical components, if the wiring diagram is difficult to interpret, or if you have tested all sensors and wiring and found no fault but the code persists. A technician has diagnostic software and experience to pinpoint communication faults quickly. Also call a pro if the control board shows visible burn marks or moisture damage, as this may indicate a larger electrical problem that needs professional evaluation.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-280.
