## Navien Tankless Water Heater E019 Error — What It Means

The E019 error on a Navien tankless water heater indicates the unit has detected an internal fault related to the main printed circuit board (PCB) or a communication error between the board and one of the sensors. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or wiring diagram for your specific unit. This code typically appears when the control board cannot read a sensor signal correctly, when there is damaged wiring, or when the board itself has failed due to a power surge or component wear.

Because the error points to the control electronics rather than a simple sensor or mechanical part, diagnosing the root cause usually requires testing voltage, inspecting wiring connections, and sometimes swapping the PCB. The code may appear suddenly after a lightning strike, brownout, or simply after years of operation.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace individual sensors when the real issue is a failed main control board or a corroded wiring connector. Inspect all wiring harness plugs and test for proper voltage at the board before ordering a new PCB.

## Common Causes

- **Failed main control board (PCB) (~50%)** Power surges, lightning strikes, or age can damage the board's circuits or memory, causing spurious error codes.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~25%)** Vibration or moisture can loosen harness plugs or corrode pins, interrupting signals between the board and sensors.
- **Power surge or brownout damage (~15%)** A sudden voltage spike or drop can corrupt the board's firmware or damage internal components.
- **Faulty sensor communication (~10%)** A sensor may send erratic data to the board, triggering an internal fault code when the board cannot interpret the signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the gas supply to the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and lifting it away.
3. **Photograph all wiring** connected to the main control board before disconnecting anything.
4. **Inspect each wiring connector** on the PCB for corrosion, burn marks, or loose pins. Clean any corroded contacts with electrical contact cleaner and reseat firmly.
5. **If connectors are sound**, test for proper incoming voltage at the power terminals on the board using a multimeter. Consult your model's wiring diagram for expected voltage values.
6. **If voltage is correct and wiring is intact**, the main control board has likely failed. Order a replacement PCB that matches your unit's model and serial number.
7. **Install the new board** by transferring each wire from the old board to the matching terminal on the new one, referring to your photographs. Secure the board in its mounting bracket and reinstall the front cover.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if you are uncomfortable working with line voltage, if you cannot locate the correct replacement board for your model, or if the error persists after replacing the PCB and inspecting all wiring. A tech can test each sensor circuit individually, verify gas valve operation, and make sure the new board is properly programmed. Because tankless water heaters involve both electrical and gas systems, professional diagnosis is the safest route when the fault is not immediately obvious.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $350-$700.
