## What this code means
F29 on Amana dryers indicates a moisture-sensor circuit fault detected by the control board. Whirlpool builds these dryers, and the manufacturer treats F29 as a control-detected issue requiring a power reset and timed-dry retest rather than a simple airflow or vent blockage. The code typically points to dirty or shorted moisture sensor bars, damaged wiring, or a fault in the main control itself.

The moisture sensor bars sit at the lint-screen or drum opening and measure fabric dryness by detecting conductivity between the metal electrodes. Detergent residue, fabric-softener film, or contamination on the bars can cause false readings or short the circuit, triggering F29. Loose connectors or broken wires in the sensor harness can also generate the fault. If the sensor and wiring test good and the code persists after cleaning and reset, the main control board is the remaining point of failure.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first when F29 appears. Clean the moisture sensor bars and inspect the sensor wiring before ordering any boards, because film or a loose connector causes the majority of F29 faults.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or contaminated moisture sensor bars (~50%)** Detergent, fabric softener, and mineral residue build up on the metal sensor electrodes, creating a short or false conductivity reading that the control interprets as a circuit fault.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring connector (~25%)** The harness plug at the sensor or control can work loose from vibration, or corrosion at the terminals can create intermittent opens or shorts that trigger F29.
- **Shorted moisture sensor circuit (~15%)** A damaged wire, pinched harness, or internal short in the sensor itself causes the control to see a continuous short to ground and log the F29 fault.
- **Failed main control board (~10%)** If the sensor and wiring test normally but F29 returns after reset, the control board's sensor input circuit has failed and the board must be replaced.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-reset the dryer** by switching off the circuit breaker or unplugging the unit for five full minutes, then restore power and attempt a timed-dry cycle to see if F29 clears.
2. **Locate the moisture sensor bars** at the lint-screen housing or drum opening (typically two parallel metal strips or electrodes) and inspect them for visible residue, film, or contamination.
3. **Clean the sensor bars** with a cloth dampened with rubbing alcohol to remove detergent buildup, fabric-softener film, and mineral deposits. Dry the bars completely before reassembling the lint screen.
4. **Inspect the sensor wiring harness** from the sensor electrodes back to the main control board. Check for loose or corroded connectors, pinched wires, broken conductors, or heat damage along the harness routing.
5. **Test the sensor circuit** if F29 persists after cleaning and wiring checks. Use a multimeter to verify there are no shorts to ground or opens in the sensor path. Consult your model's wiring diagram for connector pinouts.
6. **Replace the moisture sensor** if the bars are damaged, corroded beyond cleaning, or if circuit testing shows an internal short in the sensor itself.
7. **Replace the main control board** only if the sensor and wiring test normally, the code returns after cleaning and reset, and no other faults are present. The control's sensor input circuit is the final point of failure when the physical sensor checks out.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working around 240-volt dryer connections, if you cannot locate the moisture sensor bars on your model, or if F29 persists after you have cleaned the sensor, inspected the wiring, and completed a power reset. A technician has the test equipment to verify sensor circuit integrity, pinpoint shorts or opens in the harness, and safely replace the main control board if needed. Also call for help if you discover signs of arcing, melted connectors, or other electrical damage during your inspection.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-80 in parts (if sensor or harness replacement needed), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300 service call and parts.
