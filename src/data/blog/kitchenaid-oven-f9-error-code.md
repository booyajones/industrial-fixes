## What this code means
KitchenAid lists F9 or F9 E0 as indicating that the electrical outlet or electrical connection may be miswired. On electric wall ovens and ranges, the code will most often indicate that the appliance is wired incorrectly at the home electrical supply or at the hard-wire connection. KitchenAid specifically highlights the need to check that L2 and neutral (N) are not swapped at the terminal block.

In some cases, the code appears after a power outage or voltage anomaly, when unexpected power-line noise is interpreted as an incorrect voltage condition. Pressing control keys before the 10-second power-up delay completes can also trigger the fault on certain models. If the code persists after supply wiring is verified, the fault may point to a damaged harness, loose connector, or control board issue, but KitchenAid's own guidance emphasizes supply wiring first.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real issue is a swapped L2 and neutral wire at the terminal block. Always verify the supply wiring and terminal connections with a qualified electrician before replacing electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Miswired terminal block or hard-wire connection (~50%)** L2 and neutral swapped or other incorrect wire landing at the appliance terminal block.
- **Home supply wiring error (~25%)** Incorrect wiring upstream in the electrical panel or junction box feeding the oven.
- **Recent power outage or line noise (~15%)** Voltage anomaly or noise on the power line causes the control to detect a false supply fault.
- **Keys pressed during power-up delay (~5%)** Buttons pressed before the 10-second startup sequence finishes can trigger F9 on some models.
- **Loose or damaged terminal or harness connection (~3%)** Heat damage, corrosion, or poor contact at the terminal block or internal wire connectors.
- **Faulty main control board (~2%)** If supply wiring is verified correct and the code persists, the control may be misreading voltage or have an internal fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Reset power at the breaker or fuse.** Turn off the circuit for a full minute, then restore power and watch whether F9 returns.
2. **Verify the installation wiring** at the terminal block or hard-wire connection. Turn off power at the breaker before opening any access panels.
3. **Confirm L2 and neutral are not swapped.** KitchenAid's guidance specifically highlights this as the most common wiring error. Consult your oven's wiring diagram or installation instructions for the correct wire landing.
4. **Inspect all terminal connections** for tightness, heat damage, corrosion, or frayed wires. Re-seat or clean any suspect terminals.
5. **If the appliance was installed recently,** contact a qualified electrician to verify that home supply wiring matches the oven's requirements.
6. **If the code appeared after a power outage,** allow the control to complete its 10-second power-up sequence without pressing any buttons. Monitor for recurrence over the next few cycles.
7. **If the fault persists after verifying supply wiring,** a technician should inspect the terminal block, wiring harness, and connectors for intermittent faults. If all wiring is confirmed correct, further diagnosis shifts to the main control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified electrician or appliance technician immediately if you are not comfortable working with high-voltage wiring or verifying supply connections. KitchenAid's F9 or F9 E0 code points to a supply-wiring issue in the majority of cases, and incorrect work at the terminal block or home electrical panel can create a shock or fire hazard. If you have confirmed that the home supply is correct and the terminal block is landed properly, but the code returns, a technician should inspect the internal harnesses, connectors, and control board for intermittent faults. Always turn off power at the breaker before opening any panels or touching wiring.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $100-250.
