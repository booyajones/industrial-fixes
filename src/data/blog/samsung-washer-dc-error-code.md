## Samsung Washer DC Error Code — What It Means

On Samsung washers, DC indicates an unbalanced load that prevents the drum from spinning safely at high speed. The washer detects that laundry is bunched to one side or the machine itself is not level, so it stops or reduces the spin cycle to avoid damage. This code appears on both front-load and top-load models and is Samsung's way of telling you the load distribution or installation needs correction before the spin can complete.

The code does not mean a door-close fault in most cases. Samsung's own support documentation confirms that DC (and the related UE code) specifically signals load imbalance. The machine will not proceed until the issue is resolved by leveling the washer, redistributing the laundry, or clearing any installation problems such as shipping hardware left in place.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the door lock assembly or control board when DC appears, but the code almost always points to a load or leveling problem. Before ordering parts, open the drum and physically check that clothes are spread evenly and that the washer sits level on the floor.

## Common Causes

- **Laundry bunched to one side (~50%)** A single heavy item or clothes tangled together shifts the drum off balance during spin, triggering the DC code.
- **Washer not level (~25%)** An uneven floor or improperly adjusted leveling feet prevents the drum from rotating evenly at high speed.
- **Shipping bolts or base not removed (~10%)** New installations with transit hardware still attached restrict drum movement and cause immediate imbalance errors.
- **Too much or too little laundry (~10%)** Overloading or running a very small load makes it difficult for the washer to balance the drum automatically.
- **Water remaining in the tub (~3%)** A partial drain failure leaves water pooled unevenly, adding weight to one side and preventing proper spin.
- **Faulty lid lock mechanism (top-load models) (~2%)** On some top-load Samsung washers, a defective lid lock can produce a DC symptom or prevent the machine from advancing past the error.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and make sure no cycle is running before you open the door or inspect the machine.
2. **Open the door and remove all laundry** from the drum, then redistribute the items evenly when you reload so no single side is heavier.
3. **Check that the washer is level** by placing a bubble level on top of the machine front-to-back and side-to-side, and adjust the leveling feet until all readings are centered.
4. **Inspect for shipping hardware** if the washer is new or recently moved: remove any bolts at the rear panel and any plastic transit base under the drum.
5. **Drain remaining water manually** if the tub still holds water: open the debris filter access panel at the front lower corner, pull out the emergency drain tube, remove the cap, and let water flow into a shallow pan.
6. **Reload the washer with a properly sized, evenly distributed load** and restart the spin cycle to see if the DC code clears.
7. **Book a service call** if the code returns after leveling, load correction, and draining, because a component such as the lid lock (on top-loaders) or another sensor may be faulty.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the DC code persists after you have confirmed the washer is level, removed any shipping hardware, redistributed the load evenly, and manually drained the tub. On top-load models, a faulty lid lock can mimic or trigger a DC error, and that diagnosis requires testing the lock circuit with a multimeter. Samsung recommends professional service when basic installation and load corrections do not resolve the code, because the root cause may involve a sensor, wiring harness, or control board that requires model-specific testing and parts.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-20 (adjusting feet or redistributing load), 10-20 min. A pro service call runs about $100-200 for a service call if a component (such as a lid lock on top-loaders) is actually faulty.
