## Maytag Range F3E2 Error Code — What It Means

The F3E2 code on a Maytag range tells you the oven's temperature-sensing circuit is not reporting a valid signal to the control board. The electronic control expects to see a stable resistance value from the oven temperature sensor (an RTD or thermistor probe inside the oven cavity), and when that reading is missing, out of range, or shows an open or short, the control throws F3E2 and stops the bake cycle to protect the oven.

In practical service terms, this fault almost always points to the oven temperature sensor itself, the wiring and connector between the sensor and the control, or (less often) a failed electronic oven control board that cannot read a good sensor signal. The code does not mean your bake element is bad or that the oven door is failing. It is strictly a sensor-circuit problem.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first because the code sounds electronic. Test the sensor's resistance with a multimeter and inspect the connector pins before spending money on a control. A faulty sensor or a loose plug causes F3E2 far more often than a bad board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~65%)** The sensor probe inside the oven has drifted out of range, developed an internal open or short, or has been damaged by heat or moisture over time.
- **Loose, corroded, or damaged sensor connector (~20%)** The two-pin plug where the sensor wires meet the main harness can work loose, collect grease or moisture, or have bent pins that break contact.
- **Broken or pinched sensor wiring (~10%)** The thin wires from the sensor to the connector can be cut, pinched behind the oven frame, or chafed by sheet metal during past service work.
- **Defective electronic oven control board (~5%)** If the sensor and wiring both test good but the code persists, the control board's sensor-input circuit has failed and the board must be replaced.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range so you can work safely behind the unit and inside the control area.
2. **Pull the range forward** and remove the rear access panel or lower back cover to expose the oven temperature sensor connector and the electronic control board.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** inside the oven cavity (usually a metal probe about 1/4 inch diameter sticking through the back wall) and trace its wires to the two-pin connector.
4. **Unplug the sensor connector** and use a multimeter set to ohms to measure across the two sensor terminals at room temperature, then compare the reading to your model's service data (many Maytag ranges use sensors in the 1000–1100 ohm range at 70°F, but consult your wiring diagram).
5. **Inspect the connector pins and harness** for corrosion, bent terminals, or broken insulation, and if the sensor reads open, infinite, or zero ohms, replace the sensor.
6. **Install the new sensor** by removing the mounting screw or bracket inside the oven, sliding out the old probe, inserting the new one, and reconnecting the plug.
7. **Restore power and run a bake test** at 350°F for 15–20 minutes to verify the code does not return and the oven maintains temperature normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if you cannot access the back of the range because it is built into cabinetry, or if the new sensor and verified-good wiring still produce F3E2 and you need the control board diagnosed and replaced. A pro will bring the correct sensor, test equipment, and the ability to order and program a replacement control if needed. Also call for help if the range is still under warranty or if you see any signs of arcing, burned connectors, or damaged wiring that suggest a more complex electrical fault.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–45 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250 including service call and sensor.
