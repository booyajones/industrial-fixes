## KitchenAid F8E4 Error Code — What It Means

The F8E4 code on a KitchenAid dishwasher indicates that water has been detected in the base pan (drip tray) beneath the tub. When water collects in this pan, it lifts a float switch that triggers the leak protection system and halts the cycle. The dishwasher will not resume normal operation until the water is removed from the drip tray and the underlying leak is repaired.

This code is not a component failure itself but a symptom that water is escaping from the tub, door, or supply plumbing. The system is designed to protect your floor from flooding. Once the root cause is corrected and the drip tray is emptied and dried, the code can be cleared by pressing Cancel once to silence the alarm and a second time to reset the control.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or improperly sealed water supply connection** The inlet fitting at the front-left 90° elbow may be loose, cross-threaded, or missing its rubber washer, allowing water to drip into the base pan during fill.
- **Excess suds or foam from wrong detergent** Using non-dishwasher detergent or too much detergent creates foam that can force water out of the tub and into the drip tray.
- **Door gasket or tub seal leak** A torn, hardened, or misaligned door seal allows water to seep out during the wash or rinse cycle and collect in the base pan.
- **Float switch connector not fully seated** If the float switch wiring connector is loose or was disturbed during previous service, the switch may report a false leak condition.
- **Water left in drip tray from earlier leak** Even after a leak is repaired, residual water in the base pan will keep the float switch tripped and the code active until the pan is emptied.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Disconnect power to the dishwasher at the breaker or unplug the unit before beginning any service work.
2. Remove the toe-kick panel or lower access cover and inspect the drip tray in the base of the unit for standing water.
3. Carefully lift and tilt the drip pan to empty any water into a shallow container, taking care not to strain the float switch wire during removal.
4. Inspect the water supply connection at the front-left inlet elbow for wetness, loose fittings, or a missing or misaligned rubber washer, and hand-tighten the coupling before adding an additional 1/4 to 1/2 turn with pliers to seat the gasket.
5. Check the float switch electrical connector and press it firmly onto the switch terminal until the locking tab is visible over the back of the housing.
6. Look inside the tub for excessive suds or foam, and if present, allow the foam to dissipate and switch to a proper automatic dishwasher detergent in the correct amount.
7. After the base pan is dry and the leak source is corrected, restore power and press Cancel once to silence the alarm and a second time to clear the F8E4 code, then monitor the first full cycle for any sign of recurring leakage.

## When to Call a Pro

If you have emptied the drip tray and tightened the supply connection but the F8E4 code returns during the next cycle, the leak may be internal to the pump housing, spray-arm manifold, or recirculation plumbing. Tracing those sources requires removal of the lower rack, spray arm, and sump cover, and may involve testing the float switch for proper operation. A qualified appliance technician has the tooling to pressure-test the tub, measure float-switch continuity, and access service bulletins for model-specific seal updates. Call for service if you see water pooling under the unit between cycles or if the code appears even when the drip pan is bone-dry, as that points to a wiring fault or a failed float switch that needs professional diagnosis.
