## What this code means
D80 on an LG dryer is a restricted-airflow warning generated by the Flow Sense venting system. The dryer has detected that exhaust airflow is heavily restricted, commonly described as about 80% blockage in the vent path. Heat, moisture, and lint are not being evacuated normally through the exhaust system.

This is primarily a venting problem, not a control-board or electronic failure. The code tells you the dryer cannot push air through the vent run efficiently. The most common real causes are lint buildup, crushed or kinked hoses, excessively long vent runs, blocked exterior terminations, and unsafe vent materials such as foil or plastic ducting.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or thermostats before checking the vent system. Always pull the dryer out, disconnect the exhaust hose, and verify strong airflow at the dryer outlet and exterior hood before ordering internal parts.

## Common Causes

- **Lint buildup in the vent run (~50%)** Accumulated lint in the lint-screen housing, exhaust hose, wall duct, or exterior hood restricts airflow and triggers the D80 code.
- **Crushed, kinked, or disconnected exhaust hose (~25%)** A hose that is compressed behind the dryer, sharply bent, or pulled loose blocks airflow and causes the restriction warning.
- **Blocked or obstructed exterior vent hood (~10%)** Bird nests, ice, snow, or a stuck damper at the outdoor termination prevent exhaust air from escaping and create back pressure.
- **Excessively long or overly bent vent path (~10%)** A vent run with too many turns or total length beyond manufacturer recommendations reduces airflow below the threshold.
- **Use of unsafe vent materials (~3%)** Foil or plastic ducting collapses under heat and airflow pressure, restricting the exhaust path and trapping lint.
- **Faulty internal airflow or temperature sensor (~2%)** If the entire vent system is clean and open but D80 persists, an internal thermostat or airflow sensor may be reading incorrectly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** and pull it away from the wall to access the rear exhaust connection.
2. **Disconnect the exhaust hose** from the dryer outlet and inspect both the hose and the dryer outlet for visible lint, damage, or crushing.
3. **Clean the lint-screen housing** by removing the lint filter and using a vacuum or long brush to clear lint from the housing cavity and the duct behind it.
4. **Inspect and clean the entire vent run** from the dryer outlet through the wall duct to the exterior hood, removing all lint, debris, and obstructions, and verify the outdoor damper opens freely.
5. **Check for installation defects** including crushed or kinked sections, foil or plastic ducting, excessive length, and too many bends, and replace or shorten the vent run as needed with rigid or semi-rigid metal duct.
6. **Reconnect the exhaust hose** securely and restore power, then run the dryer on a timed dry cycle and verify strong airflow at the exterior hood.
7. **Monitor for code recurrence** over the next few cycles. If D80 returns after confirming the vent system is clean and open, consult a technician to test internal thermostats and airflow sensors.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot access or clean the full vent run yourself, if the vent path is built into walls or ceilings and requires tools or disassembly beyond your comfort level, or if D80 continues to appear after you have thoroughly cleaned and verified the entire exhaust system is open and unrestricted. A technician can test internal airflow and temperature sensors, verify blower operation, and diagnose any internal component failures that may mimic a vent blockage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (vent brush, replacement rigid duct if needed), 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $100-200 for vent cleaning and inspection.
