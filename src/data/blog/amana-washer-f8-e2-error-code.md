## What this code means
The F8 E2 error code on an Amana front-load washer signals a Dispenser System Error. The control board detects that the dispenser motor cannot reach or hold the correct position when trying to move the detergent drawer mechanism. The washer halts the fill cycle or prevents detergent from flushing into the tub because the system cannot confirm the drawer is in the open or flush position.

Whirlpool, Amana's parent company, defines this fault as the dispenser motor being unable to reach the desired position. The problem is almost always mechanical rather than electrical. The drawer or its linkage is blocked, jammed, or disconnected, preventing normal movement.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the dispenser motor or control board first. Instead, pull the drawer and inspect for caked detergent or broken linkage arms, which solve the problem in most cases.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged detergent drawer or cartridge (~50%)** Accumulated detergent residue, powder, or sludge physically jams the drawer and prevents the motor from moving it into position.
- **Broken or disconnected mechanical linkage (~25%)** The plastic arm connecting the motor to the drawer snaps off, slips out of place, or becomes obstructed by debris.
- **Stuck drawer slides or misalignment (~10%)** Corroded or misaligned drawer rails create excessive friction, so the motor cannot overcome the resistance.
- **Failed dispenser motor (~10%)** The motor itself has burned out or seized and cannot turn even when the linkage is free and clean.
- **Loose wiring harness or control board fault (~5%)** The connector to the dispenser motor is loose or corroded, or the control board fails to send the correct signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the washer or turning off the circuit breaker to the machine.
2. **Pull the detergent drawer** completely out of the housing by pressing the release tab (if present) and sliding it forward.
3. **Inspect and clean the drawer** and the housing cavity with warm soapy water and a soft brush, removing all caked detergent, powder residue, and debris from the nozzles and slides.
4. **Access the dispenser motor** by removing the top panel or dispenser hopper cover (typically a few screws at the rear or top of the cabinet).
5. **Check the mechanical linkage** between the motor and the drawer mechanism for breaks, disconnections, or obstructions, and reattach or replace any damaged plastic arms.
6. **Test the motor manually** by gently moving the linkage by hand to confirm it rotates freely, then restore power and run a test cycle to verify the error clears.
7. **Inspect wiring connections** to the dispenser motor for loose or corroded pins, and reseat the harness if necessary, or call a technician if the motor does not turn and the linkage is clear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the drawer and housing thoroughly, verified the linkage is intact and moves freely, and the error persists. A technician can measure the motor's voltage supply and resistance, test the control board's output signal, and confirm whether the motor or board requires replacement. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable removing the top panel or working near the machine's internal wiring, or if the washer is still under warranty and you want to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (if linkage or drawer needs replacement), 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call.
