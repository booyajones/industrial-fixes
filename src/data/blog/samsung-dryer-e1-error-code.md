## Samsung Dryer E1 Error Code — What It Means

The E1 error code on a Samsung dryer indicates that the thermistor resistance is too low or the thermistor is shorted. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that monitors heat inside the drum, and the dryer expects to see a resistance around 50 kΩ at 20°C (68°F). When the control board reads a much lower resistance or sees a short circuit condition, it throws the E1 code and stops the cycle to prevent unsafe operation.

Samsung also states that a restricted vent system or a clogged lint filter can trigger this code. Poor airflow can cause unusual temperature patterns that affect the thermistor circuit. In practice, the fault points to either an airflow problem or a defective thermistor, damaged wiring, or a corroded connector in the sensor circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Some people replace the main control board first. Always measure thermistor resistance at room temperature (should be 50 kΩ at 20°C) and inspect the wiring harness for shorts before ordering electronic parts.

## Common Causes

- **Shorted or failed thermistor** The thermistor itself can develop an internal short or drop to near-zero resistance, triggering the low-resistance fault that defines the E1 code.
- **Pinched or damaged thermistor wiring** Harness wires can be pinched by the drum or exhaust duct, creating a short to chassis or between conductors.
- **Corroded or wet connector** Moisture from steam or poor venting can corrode the thermistor plug and create a false low-resistance reading.
- **Clogged lint filter** Samsung lists a clogged lint filter as a cause because restricted airflow affects temperature sensing and can trip the code.
- **Restricted exhaust vent** A kinked, crushed, or lint-packed vent duct reduces airflow and can lead to abnormal temperature readings that trigger E1.
- **Loose thermistor connector** A partially unseated connector can cause intermittent shorts or erratic resistance readings that the control board interprets as a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the wall outlet or breaker, wait one minute, then restore power to clear any transient control glitch and confirm the code is persistent.
2. **Remove and clean the lint filter** thoroughly, and inspect the entire exhaust vent run from the dryer to the outside termination for kinks, crushing, or lint buildup that restricts airflow.
3. **Run a short heat cycle** to see if cleaning the airflow path clears the E1 code. If the code returns, proceed to thermistor diagnostics.
4. **Access the thermistor** by removing the rear service panel (or front panel on some models). The thermistor is usually mounted on the blower housing or exhaust duct and has a two-wire connector.
5. **Disconnect the thermistor plug** and use a multimeter set to resistance (Ω) to measure across the two sensor terminals at room temperature. Samsung specifies 50 kΩ at 20°C (68°F). A reading near 0 Ω or far below 50 kΩ means the thermistor is shorted or failed.
6. **Inspect the wiring harness and connector** for pinched wires, corrosion, or signs of moisture. Check continuity from each thermistor wire to chassis ground. Any continuity to ground indicates a short in the harness.
7. **Replace the thermistor** if resistance is out of spec or the sensor shows continuity to ground. Install the new sensor, reconnect the plug, reassemble the dryer, and run a full heat cycle to verify the E1 code is gone.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with a multimeter, if you cannot locate the thermistor on your model, or if the code persists after you have replaced the thermistor and verified all wiring is intact. A pro can quickly check for less common faults such as a defective control board or a grounding issue in the chassis. Samsung states that service is required if basic cleaning and troubleshooting do not resolve the E1 code.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–50 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–250.
