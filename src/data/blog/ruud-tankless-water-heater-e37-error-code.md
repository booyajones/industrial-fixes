## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E37 Error Code — What It Means

The E37 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically signals a problem with flame detection or ignition sensing. The unit may be failing to detect a flame after the ignition sequence, or the flame-sensing system is reporting an abnormal reading. This can occur when the sensor itself is dirty, corroded, or poorly connected, or when there is an actual ignition problem preventing proper combustion.

Because the exact definition of error codes can vary across Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the label inside the unit's cover for the precise meaning on your model. In general, E37-type codes relate to flame or combustion sensing rather than water flow or temperature.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the entire gas valve or igniter when the real problem is simply a dirty flame sensor. Before buying parts, clean the sensor rod with fine steel wool or a soft abrasive pad and check all wiring connections.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~50%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the sensor rod prevents accurate flame detection and triggers a false error.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~20%)** A poor electrical connection at the sensor plug or terminal causes intermittent signal loss.
- **Weak or intermittent ignition (~15%)** The burner lights but the flame is unstable or weak, so the sensor cannot confirm reliable combustion.
- **Failed flame sensor (~10%)** The sensor element itself has degraded and no longer generates a proper flame-detection signal.
- **Control board sensing circuit fault (~5%)** A problem in the board's flame-detection input path causes false or missing readings even when the sensor is good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to the heater.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the panel fasteners and setting it aside.
3. **Locate the flame sensor**, a thin metal rod extending into the burner chamber, usually near the igniter.
4. **Disconnect the sensor wire** by pulling the spade connector or unplugging the harness from the sensor terminal.
5. **Clean the sensor rod** with fine steel wool, a soft abrasive pad, or emery cloth until the metal is bright and free of soot or oxidation.
6. **Inspect the wiring and connector** for corrosion, burns, or looseness and clean or tighten as needed.
7. **Reconnect the sensor wire** firmly, reinstall the cover, open the gas valve, restore power, and test the unit by calling for hot water.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working near gas connections, if the unit still shows E37 after cleaning the sensor and checking wiring, or if you suspect a gas valve or control board fault. Professionals have combustion analyzers and multimeters to test flame current and can safely diagnose ignition-system problems. Also call a pro if the unit is still under warranty, since DIY work on gas appliances can sometimes void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 20-40 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
