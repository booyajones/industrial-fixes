## Bosch Refrigerator Water Leaking on Floor — What's Happening

A Bosch refrigerator leaking water onto the floor is not an error code. It is a physical leak condition. Normal condensate runs down a gully into a drain hole, then to an evaporation tray beneath the unit. When that drain hole becomes blocked with debris, water backs up inside the cabinet and eventually leaks out onto the floor.

Bosch does not display a fault code for this symptom. The leak can appear from the front bottom, from inside the refrigerator compartment, near the water filter housing, or from rear water connections. Timing and location help pinpoint whether the issue is the drain path, a supply-line problem, or a door-seal/leveling fault.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Blocked defrost drain hole** Debris clogs the drain hole at the back of the fresh-food compartment, causing water to back up and leak from the appliance.
- **Damaged or loose water supply line** A cracked, kinked, or loose supply line allows water to escape onto the floor.
- **Faulty water filter or filter connection** A clogged or damaged filter, or a loose filter head, can leak water during dispensing or ice-making cycles.
- **Improper leveling** If the refrigerator is not level, doors may not seal properly and condensate can run out onto the floor instead of draining correctly.
- **Broken door gasket or seal** A compromised seal allows warm air in, which increases condensation and the risk of water pooling and leaking.
- **Ice maker or water dispenser leak** Leaks in the ice-maker circuit or dispenser water line can drip down and appear as floor puddles.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Verify the leak location by checking whether water appears from the front bottom, inside the cabinet, near the filter housing, or from rear connections.
2. Unplug or switch off the refrigerator before working on the drainage system.
3. Inspect the drainage hole at the back of the fresh-food compartment and clear it with a cotton bud first, then a flexible wire if needed (avoid sharp tools).
4. Check the refrigerator's leveling by placing a bubble level on top and adjusting the front feet so doors close fully and condensate drains as designed.
5. Inspect the water supply line, all fittings, and the filter head or cartridge for cracks, looseness, or seepage.
6. Examine door gaskets for damage, gaps, or signs of warm-air infiltration and condensation.
7. Trace the drain tube from the hole down to the evaporation tray for ice, debris, or restriction if the hole itself is clear.
8. Restore power, run the water or ice dispenser if equipped, and monitor the floor, drain pan, and evaporation path for any repeat leak.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if clearing the drain hole and checking the water line do not stop the leak, if you find ice or blockage deep in the drain tube behind interior panels, or if the leak involves the sealed refrigeration system or internal ice-maker plumbing. A pro can pull panels safely, verify the evaporation tray and compressor area, and replace internal drain components or water valves that require refrigerant recovery or specialized disassembly.
