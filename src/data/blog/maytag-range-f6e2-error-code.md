## Maytag Range F6E2 Error Code — What It Means

The F6E2 error code on a Maytag range signals a communication fault between two internal control boards: the Appliance Manager Control and the Converter Control. Maytag states this alert points to a problem with either control board or the wiring harness connecting them.

Unlike fault codes that track sensors or heating elements, F6E2 is strictly an internal communication issue. The two controls cannot exchange data properly, so the range shuts down to protect itself until the connection is restored.

## Common Causes

- **Failed Appliance Manager Control** The Appliance Manager Control board has stopped responding or cannot transmit data to the Converter Control.
- **Failed Converter Control** The Converter Control board is not communicating back to the Appliance Manager due to internal component failure.
- **Loose or damaged wiring harness** The connector between the two boards is unseated, corroded, or has broken pins that interrupt the communication path.
- **Open or shorted interconnecting wires** Individual wires in the harness have broken, frayed, or shorted together, blocking signal transmission.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely, wait one full minute, then restore power and check if the F6E2 code clears on its own.
2. **Access the control compartment** by removing the rear panel or the control-console cover according to your model's service manual.
3. **Locate the Appliance Manager Control and Converter Control** boards, then identify the ribbon cable or wire harness running between them.
4. **Inspect all connectors** for bent pins, corrosion, or loose seating, press each connector firmly into its socket, and look for any burned or discolored terminals.
5. **Check the wiring harness** along its entire length for cuts, pinches, or signs of heat damage, and wiggle the harness gently while watching for intermittent code reappearance.
6. **If wiring and connectors are intact**, isolate the fault by swapping one control board at a time or consulting a multimeter continuity check on each wire in the harness.
7. **Replace the defective component** (Appliance Manager Control, Converter Control, or wiring harness), reassemble the range, restore power, and verify the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if the power reset does not clear the code, if you are uncomfortable working inside live electrical panels, or if you cannot visually identify which control board has failed. Technicians carry diagnostic tools to isolate communication faults quickly and stock OEM control boards that match your exact model number. Because both the Appliance Manager Control and Converter Control are expensive parts, professional testing prevents unnecessary replacement and ensures the wiring harness is fully verified before any board is swapped.
