## Kenmore Dryer E11 Error Code — What It Means

The E11 error code on Kenmore dryers typically indicates a communication or sensor problem between the main control board and one of the temperature monitoring components. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram behind the service panel to confirm which circuit is flagged. In most cases, the dryer will not start or will stop mid-cycle when this code appears.

The code is often triggered by a loose connector, a failed thermistor (temperature sensor), or a wiring harness that has been pinched or damaged. Less commonly, the main control board itself may be at fault. Before replacing any electronic parts, inspect all visible connections and test the thermistor for continuity according to your model's resistance table.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real problem is a loose harness plug or a failed thermistor. Use a multimeter to test the thermistor's resistance before ordering a control board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded connector (~40%)** Vibration or heat can work harness plugs loose over time, breaking the signal path between the thermistor and the control board.
- **Failed thermistor (~30%)** The temperature sensor can drift out of specification or fail open, sending no signal to the control board.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires pinched by the drum or melted by heat can short or open the thermistor circuit.
- **Faulty main control board (~10%)** The control board may fail to read the sensor signal due to a bad solder joint or component failure.
- **Incorrect or missing grounding (~5%)** A poor chassis ground can create erratic voltage readings and trigger false error codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and move it away from the wall to access the rear panel.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** or top panel (depending on your model) by unscrewing the sheet-metal screws around the perimeter.
3. **Locate the thermistor** (a small cylindrical sensor) mounted on or near the blower housing or heating element duct, and trace its wire harness back to the main control board.
4. **Inspect all connectors** along the harness path and firmly reseat each plug, checking for corrosion or bent pins.
5. **Test the thermistor** with a multimeter set to resistance (ohms); consult your model's wiring diagram for the expected resistance range at room temperature.
6. **Replace the thermistor** if it reads open (infinite resistance) or is far outside the specified range, or **replace the wiring harness** if you find physical damage.
7. **Reassemble the panel**, plug the dryer back in, and run a test cycle to confirm the E11 code is cleared and the dryer heats and dries normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the dryer cabinet, if you cannot locate the thermistor or control board using your model's wiring diagram, or if the error persists after replacing the thermistor and inspecting all visible wiring. A pro can perform advanced diagnostics with specialized tools to pinpoint control-board faults and can source model-specific parts quickly. If your dryer is still under warranty or you lack a multimeter, professional service is the safer choice.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
