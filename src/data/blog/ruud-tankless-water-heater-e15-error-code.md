## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E15 Error — What It Means

The E15 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with flame detection or combustion operation. The unit senses that ignition has failed or that the flame is not stable enough to continue heating. This safety feature shuts down the burner to prevent gas accumulation or unsafe conditions.

Because exact error definitions can vary across Ruud tankless models and firmware versions, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the front panel for the precise meaning on your unit. In general, E15 points to an issue in the ignition sequence, flame sensor circuit, or combustion air supply.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the flame sensor rod first, but a dirty or corroded flame sensor can often be cleaned with fine sandpaper. Inspect and clean the sensor before ordering a new one.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted venting (~40%)** Debris, nests, ice, or improper vent termination starve the burner of combustion air or prevent exhaust from leaving, causing ignition failure.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~25%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents it from detecting flame current, even when the burner lights.
- **Low or fluctuating gas pressure (~15%)** Insufficient gas supply, a partially closed gas valve, or regulator problems prevent stable ignition or flame holding.
- **Faulty igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked igniter, fouled spark gap, or loose wire stops the unit from generating spark and lighting the burner.
- **Failed flame sensor or wiring (~7%)** A broken sensor, corroded connector, or interrupted wire prevents flame signal from reaching the control board.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** Internal relay or microprocessor failure on the board can misread flame status or fail to energize the gas valve.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you see or hear the igniter sparking when the unit tries to fire?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The igniter is working. The problem is downstream: check gas supply, flame sensor, or venting.<br><strong>No:</strong> No spark means an igniter, electrode, or control board issue. Inspect the igniter wire and electrode gap.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are the intake and exhaust vent pipes clear and free of obstructions?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Venting is not the issue. Focus on gas pressure, flame sensor cleanliness, and electrical connections.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clear the blockage and test the unit. Restricted venting is the most common cause of E15 errors.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the error clear after cleaning the flame sensor rod with fine sandpaper?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The sensor was fouled. Monitor over the next few days to confirm the fix is stable.<br><strong>No:</strong> The sensor may be failed, or the root cause is gas pressure, igniter, or control board related. Call a technician.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas supply valve.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws and lifting the panel away.
3. **Inspect the intake and exhaust vent pipes** for blockages, nests, ice, or corrosion and clear any obstructions you find.
4. **Locate the flame sensor rod** near the burner assembly (a thin metal probe) and remove it by loosening the mounting screw or bracket.
5. **Clean the flame sensor** gently with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-abrasive pad to remove carbon and corrosion, then wipe it dry.
6. **Check the igniter electrode** for cracks, heavy carbon deposits, or a bent tip, and verify the spark gap matches your model's specification.
7. **Reinstall the flame sensor and igniter**, making sure all wire connectors are snug and free of corrosion.
8. **Open the gas valve**, restore power, and initiate a hot-water draw to test for ignition and stable operation.
9. **If the E15 error persists**, measure incoming gas pressure with a manometer or call a licensed technician to check the gas valve, regulator, and control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you find no obvious blockages, the flame sensor is clean, and the error persists. Diagnosing gas pressure, testing the gas valve and regulator, and replacing the control board all require specialized tools and knowledge of combustion safety. Any work involving gas lines, pressure testing, or high-voltage ignition circuits should be handled by a qualified professional to avoid fire, explosion, or carbon-monoxide hazards.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
