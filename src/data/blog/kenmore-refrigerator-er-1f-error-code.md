## What this code means
The ER 1F or Er IF code on Kenmore Elite refrigerators indicates a fault in the ice compartment fan, also called the ice maker fan or ice fan. This fan moves cold air through ducts in the freezer door to cool the ice maker area. When the fan cannot turn or the control board does not detect normal operation, the code appears. The exact definition can vary slightly depending on whether your Kenmore unit was built by LG or another manufacturer, so verify the code meaning in your model's service manual.

Most often the fan itself is not broken. Instead, ice or frost has built up in the fan housing or air ducts and jammed the blades so they cannot spin. Less commonly, the fan motor has failed electrically or mechanically, or a control board issue is preventing the fan from receiving power. Moisture intrusion through worn door seals can cause repeated icing and make the problem return even after a repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the fan motor or control board first. Before buying parts, unplug the unit and manually defrost the entire ice compartment and fan area for 24 hours, then check whether the fan spins freely by hand.

## Common Causes

- **Ice or frost packed in the fan housing (~60%)** Humid air or a door seal leak lets moisture freeze around the fan blades and air ducts, physically blocking the fan from turning.
- **Failed ice fan motor (~20%)** The motor winding opens or the bearing seizes so the fan cannot run even after all ice is cleared.
- **Blocked evaporator or airflow path (~10%)** Heavy frost on the evaporator coil or clogged vents restrict airflow and make the fan appear stuck or cause erratic operation.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~7%)** A damaged control board or loose harness connection fails to send power to the fan or misreads the fan feedback signal.
- **Worn door seal allowing moisture intrusion (~3%)** A torn or sagging gasket lets warm, humid air into the freezer, causing recurrent frost buildup on the fan and evaporator.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** and remove any food from the freezer and ice compartment to prevent spoilage during the repair.
2. **Locate the ice compartment fan housing**, usually behind a cover panel inside the freezer door or on the back wall of the ice maker area.
3. **Remove the cover screws** and gently pull the panel forward to expose the fan assembly and air ducts.
4. **Inspect for ice or frost** around the fan blades, motor, and ducts. If you see any buildup, leave the unit unplugged with doors open for 24 hours to fully defrost, or use a low-heat hair dryer carefully to melt the ice without damaging plastic.
5. **Check the fan blade by hand** after defrosting. It should spin smoothly with no grinding or binding. If it is still stuck or the motor shaft will not turn, proceed to replace the fan assembly.
6. **Test the fan electrically** by plugging the unit back in and listening for the motor. If the blade spins freely but the motor does not run, use a multimeter to check for continuity across the motor windings or replace the fan assembly.
7. **Inspect door seals and gaskets** around the freezer and ice compartment for tears, gaps, or deformation. Replace any worn seals to prevent future moisture intrusion and icing.
8. **Reassemble the cover panel**, plug the unit back in, and clear the error code by pressing and holding the refrigerator and freezer buttons together for five seconds (consult your model's manual for the exact reset procedure). Monitor the unit for 48 hours to confirm the code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable unplugging the unit and working inside the freezer compartment, or if the error persists after you have defrosted the fan area and verified the blade spins freely. A pro can test the control board, check all wiring harness connections, and confirm the defrost system is cycling correctly. Also call for help if the fan replacement does not clear the code, since a control board or sensor fault may be present. If your unit is still under warranty, contact Kenmore or the original installer before attempting any repair to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–80 for a replacement fan assembly, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call including labor and fan if needed.
