## What this code means
The C2 error code on a GE dishwasher indicates that the total pump-out cycle exceeded 7 minutes. This is a drain-time fault. The dishwasher attempted to evacuate water at the end of the cycle but could not complete the task within the allowed window. GE's guidance is to press RESET to stop the beeping. If the code returns on the next cycle, a service diagnosis is needed to identify the restriction in the drain path.

The fault does not mean the drain pump has failed outright. Instead, it signals that something is slowing or blocking water evacuation. Common causes include a clogged drain hose, a blocked air gap, a partially obstructed garbage-disposer connection (often a knockout plug left in place during installation), debris in the sump or filter area, or a drain pump that is weak or obstructed. The technician's job is to work through the drain path systematically until the restriction is found.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the drain pump assembly without first checking the air gap, hose, and disposer inlet. Clear the external drain path before ordering any pump parts.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain hose (~30%)** Food debris, grease buildup, or a kinked hose restricts flow and extends pump-out time beyond 7 minutes.
- **Blocked air gap (~25%)** The air gap on the sink or countertop fills with debris and prevents water from exiting the dishwasher quickly.
- **Garbage-disposer knockout plug not removed (~20%)** If the dishwasher drain connects to a disposer, installers must punch out a plastic plug inside the inlet, and forgetting this step blocks the entire drain path.
- **Debris in filter or sump area (~15%)** Food particles, broken glass, or small objects pack the dishwasher's internal filter and sump, slowing water flow to the pump.
- **Weak or obstructed drain pump (~10%)** If the external plumbing is clear but pump-out still takes too long, the drain pump impeller may be jammed or the pump motor may be losing strength.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press RESET** to stop the beeping and clear the fault from the display.
2. **Run a short rinse cycle** and watch the end of the drain phase to confirm whether the code returns and whether standing water remains in the tub.
3. **Inspect the air gap** on the sink or countertop by removing the cap and cleaning out any debris that blocks the two internal tubes.
4. **Disconnect the drain hose** from the air gap or disposer inlet and check for clogs, kinks, or low spots that trap water.
5. **Check the garbage-disposer knockout plug** if the drain hose connects to a disposer inlet. Remove the hose, shine a light into the disposer port, and confirm the plastic plug was punched out during installation.
6. **Remove and clean the dishwasher filter** and sump screen at the bottom of the tub to clear any food particles or debris that restrict flow to the drain pump.
7. **Inspect the drain pump and impeller** by accessing the pump assembly under the tub (consult your service manual for location). Remove any obstructions from the impeller and check that the pump runs smoothly when powered.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared the air gap, drain hose, disposer connection, and sump filter but the C2 code still returns. The drain pump may need replacement, or there may be a wiring or control-board issue that requires a multimeter and service manual to diagnose. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable disconnecting hoses under the sink or accessing the pump assembly under the dishwasher, or if you suspect a plumbing restriction beyond the appliance itself.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $10-40 in parts (hose, air gap, or filter cleaning supplies), 20-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250 service call and drain-path cleaning or part replacement.
