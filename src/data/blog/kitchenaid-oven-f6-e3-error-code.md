## What this code means
The F6 E3 error code on a KitchenAid wall oven or range indicates a control communication or power-supply fault. According to KitchenAid documentation, there may be a problem with the Appliance Manager Control, the Converter Control, or the wiring that connects them. This is not an oven-temperature-sensor code on KitchenAid and Whirlpool ovens, even though some third-party sites describe F6E3 as an over-temperature fault on other models.

The error means the two main control boards cannot talk to each other or one of them is not receiving stable power. The Appliance Manager Control is typically the user-interface board, while the Converter Control manages power distribution. When communication breaks down or power is interrupted, the oven shuts down and displays F6 E3 to protect the electronics.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace both control boards when only the wiring harness or a single loose connector is at fault. Inspect all wiring and connections between the two boards before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed Appliance Manager Control (~40%)** The user-interface board loses internal logic or power regulation and can no longer send valid messages to the Converter Control.
- **Failed Converter Control (~35%)** The power-distribution board fails internally and cannot respond to commands or supply stable voltage to the Appliance Manager.
- **Loose or corroded wiring harness (~15%)** Connector pins between the two control boards oxidize, vibrate loose, or suffer damage that interrupts the communication signal.
- **Intermittent power supply (~10%)** A tripped breaker, loose connection at the terminal block, or household voltage sag causes the controls to drop offline momentarily.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker(s) that supply the oven and wait one full minute to allow the controls to discharge.
2. **Restore power** and watch the display. If F6 E3 does not return, run a short test bake cycle and document the intermittent fault.
3. **If the code returns immediately**, pull the oven forward (wall ovens) or open the rear access panel (ranges) to expose the control boards.
4. **Locate the Appliance Manager Control** (usually behind the front panel or display) and the **Converter Control** (often mounted to the rear or side wall).
5. **Inspect the wiring harness** that runs between the two boards. Look for loose connector pins, corrosion, burn marks, or physical damage. Reseat every connector firmly.
6. **If wiring is intact**, consult your model's wiring diagram to identify which board is upstream. Test for stable voltage at the Converter Control input and valid communication signals on the data lines.
7. **Replace the suspect control board** with an OEM or compatible part that matches your model number. Transfer mounting screws and reconnect all harnesses in the same positions, then restore power and verify normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional when the F6 E3 code returns after a power cycle and you do not have the model-specific wiring diagram or a multimeter to test communication lines. Control-board diagnosis requires tracing low-voltage signals and power rails, and misidentifying the failed board wastes money on unnecessary parts. Technicians carry diagnostic software and voltage-reference tables that pinpoint whether the Appliance Manager, the Converter Control, or the harness is at fault. If you are uncomfortable working behind live 240 V connections or pulling a heavy wall oven out of its cabinet, professional service is the safer choice.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450 for diagnosis and single-board replacement.
