## What this code means
Whirlpool confirms E23 as a recognized error code for over-the-range microwave models, but the exact meaning is model-dependent. Unlike simple user-reset codes, E23 indicates a component-level or circuit fault that requires model-specific diagnosis. Whirlpool's public support pages list E23 among fault categories but do not provide a single universal plain-English definition across all models.

Because the code appears on the display and persists after power cycling, it typically points to a hardware failure rather than a temporary glitch. Your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the cabinet will contain the model-specific fault table. Without the exact model number, technicians cannot verify whether E23 refers to a sensor, relay, inverter, or control-board circuit on your particular unit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the main control board first because E23 appears on the display. Before ordering any board, locate your model number and consult the service sheet or wiring diagram inside the microwave cabinet to confirm which specific component E23 identifies for that model.

## Common Causes

- **Model-specific sensor or thermistor fault (~30%)** Many over-the-range models use cavity or magnetron temperature sensors that trigger fault codes when readings fall out of range or the circuit opens.
- **Control-board component failure (~25%)** The main or inverter control board may log E23 when an internal relay, triac, or monitoring circuit fails.
- **Inverter or power-supply circuit fault (~20%)** High-voltage inverter boards monitor output and can set error codes when current or voltage deviates from expected values.
- **Wiring harness or connector problem (~15%)** Loose, corroded, or damaged connectors between the control board and monitored components can produce intermittent or persistent fault codes.
- **Magnetron or cavity component issue (~10%)** A failing magnetron or associated waveguide component can cause abnormal current draw or temperature that the board flags as a fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Locate your model and serial number** on the label inside the door frame or on the side wall of the cabinet, and write down the complete alphanumeric string.
2. **Unplug the microwave** from the wall outlet and wait two minutes to allow capacitors to discharge and the control board to reset.
3. **Plug the unit back in** and observe whether E23 reappears immediately or only after attempting to cook.
4. **Find the service sheet or wiring diagram** by removing the top grille or unscrewing the cabinet access panel above the door, then look for the fold-out schematic taped inside.
5. **Consult the fault-code table** on the diagram to identify the exact component or circuit that triggers E23 on your model.
6. **Call Whirlpool service** at the number in your owner's manual with your model number and the E23 code, or schedule a qualified appliance technician to perform high-voltage diagnostics and component testing.
7. **Do not attempt disassembly or testing** of inverter boards, magnetron circuits, or high-voltage capacitors without proper training and discharge tools, as lethal voltage remains even when unplugged.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional immediately if you see E23 and cannot locate the model-specific fault definition, or if the code reappears after power cycling. Microwave repair involves high-voltage capacitors that retain lethal charge even when the unit is unplugged, and inverter boards require oscilloscope testing and specialized discharge procedures. Technicians have the service manuals, model-specific fault tables, and safety equipment to isolate sensors, test control boards under load, and replace magnetron or inverter assemblies without risk. Attempting DIY troubleshooting beyond locating the wiring diagram can result in electric shock or further damage to expensive components.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350 depending on part and labor.
