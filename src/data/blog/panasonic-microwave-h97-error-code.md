## Panasonic Microwave H97 Error Code — What It Means

H97 on Panasonic microwaves signals a fault in the high-power heating system. According to Panasonic, this code means the inverter is not functioning and the magnetron may also be faulty. Panasonic Australia notes that H97 or H98 can appear after a power surge or when the Power PCB or magnetron has failed. The microwave will not heat normally or may not operate at all when this code is active.

## Common Causes

- **Failed inverter or Power PCB** The inverter circuit that drives the magnetron has stopped working, which is the most common cause confirmed by Panasonic support material.
- **Magnetron failure** The magnetron itself may be faulty or have failed alongside the inverter, as both are listed by Panasonic as potential problems.
- **Power surge or line event** A voltage spike or surge on the mains supply can damage the Power PCB or inverter, triggering the H97 code.
- **Overheating or blocked ventilation** Poor airflow or blocked vents can cause the unit to overheat and throw a fault code, though this is less commonly the root cause than component failure.
- **Loose internal connector at the magnetron** A poor connection at the magnetron terminals can interrupt operation, though this is reported anecdotally rather than in official documentation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** from the microwave for at least one minute, then plug it back in and test to see if the error clears.
2. **Check the power supply** by confirming the unit is plugged directly into a wall outlet and not an extension cord or power strip that might have experienced a surge.
3. **Inspect ventilation** by clearing any obstructions around the vents and allowing the microwave to cool if it feels hot to the touch.
4. **Run a test cycle** after the reset and cooling period to determine if the fault is persistent or was a one-time glitch.
5. **Open the cabinet** (after fully unplugging and discharging high-voltage capacitors) and visually inspect the Power PCB and inverter board for burn marks, swollen capacitors, or loose connectors.
6. **Test or replace the magnetron** if the inverter board appears intact but the fault persists, or replace the Power PCB/inverter board if it shows visible damage or if component-level testing is beyond your capability.
7. **Consult the service manual** for your specific model if a factory-reset or service-mode procedure is documented, but treat these as advanced service steps rather than a universal fix.

## When to Call a Pro

Panasonic explicitly recommends using an authorized service center for H97 faults. High-voltage components in microwave ovens can retain a dangerous charge even when unplugged, and working inside the cabinet requires discharging capacitors and handling magnetron circuits safely. If a simple power reset and ventilation check do not clear the code, or if you are uncomfortable working with high voltage, contact a qualified appliance technician or a Panasonic authorized service provider.
