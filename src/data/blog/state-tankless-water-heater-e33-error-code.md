## State Tankless Water Heater E33 Error Code — What It Means

The E33 error code on a State tankless water heater is not standardized across all models, and the specific meaning depends on your unit's control board and firmware version. State tankless heaters, manufactured by A. O. Smith, use different diagnostic codes than other brands, so an E33 may indicate a temperature sensor issue, a flow sensor problem, or a communication error between components. Without the manufacturer's documentation for your exact model, attempting repairs based on codes from other brands can lead to misdiagnosis.

Because State does not publish a universal E33 definition across its tankless line, you should consult the service manual that came with your heater or contact State customer support with your model and serial number. The code will typically appear on the digital display during a fault condition, and the heater may lock out or fail to ignite until the underlying issue is resolved.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector** Vibration or moisture can loosen the plug on the temperature or flow sensor, breaking the signal to the control board.
- **Failed temperature sensor** A thermistor that has drifted out of range or shorted will send invalid resistance readings to the controller.
- **Control board communication fault** Internal firmware errors or a failing circuit board can generate false error codes even when sensors are working.
- **Wiring damage inside the cabinet** Rodents, installation mistakes, or heat can fray or melt the small-gauge wires running to sensors.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power at the circuit breaker and close the cold-water inlet valve to make the heater safe to open.
2. Remove the front cover by unscrewing the perimeter fasteners and gently pulling the panel forward to expose the internal wiring harness.
3. Locate the sensor connectors on the heat exchanger and control board, then unplug each one and inspect pins for corrosion or moisture.
4. Use a multimeter set to resistance mode to test each thermistor or sensor according to the temperature-resistance chart in your service manual.
5. Check every visible wire for cuts, melted insulation, or pinch points where the harness passes through sheet metal.
6. Reconnect all sensors firmly, reinstall the cover, restore power, and observe the display for five minutes to see if the code returns.
7. If the error persists after reseating connectors and confirming wiring integrity, note your model number and call State technical support for code-specific guidance.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or the State warranty service line if you cannot find an E33 definition in your manual, if resistance tests fall outside published ranges, or if the code reappears after you have reseated every connector. Control board replacement and sensor calibration require specialty tools and familiarity with gas-appliance safety interlocks. If your heater is still under warranty, DIY board swaps may void coverage, so verify your registration status before ordering parts.
