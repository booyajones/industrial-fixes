## Navien E336 Error Code — What It Means

The E336 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals a problem with the circulation temperature sensor circuit. This sensor monitors the temperature of water in the recirculation loop and reports data to the control board. When the board detects a signal outside expected parameters or no signal at all, it throws the E336 code and may shut down the unit to prevent damage.

The error typically points to a faulty sensor, damaged wiring between the sensor and board, or a corroded connector. Less commonly, the control board itself may be misreading a good sensor. Because the circulation system is critical for continuous hot water delivery, the heater will not operate normally until the fault is resolved.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a loose or corroded connector at the sensor. Check and clean all sensor connections before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Failed circulation temperature sensor (~55%)** The sensor develops an internal short or open circuit and sends no valid signal to the control board.
- **Corroded or loose wiring connections (~25%)** Moisture or age causes corrosion at the sensor plug or terminal block, interrupting the signal path.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~10%)** The wire between the sensor and board is cut, pinched, or shorted against metal housing.
- **Faulty control board (~8%)** The board's input circuitry for the sensor fails and misreads or ignores a good sensor signal.
- **Incorrect sensor installation or replacement part (~2%)** A replacement sensor was installed with wrong polarity or is not compatible with the model.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve to safely work on the unit.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws and lifting the panel away to access internal components.
3. **Locate the circulation temperature sensor**, typically mounted on the recirculation piping near the heat exchanger or return line.
4. **Disconnect the sensor wiring connector** and inspect both the plug and socket for corrosion, bent pins, or moisture. Clean with electrical contact cleaner if needed.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** using a multimeter set to ohms. Consult your model's service manual for the expected resistance range at ambient temperature.
6. **Replace the sensor** if resistance is out of range or infinite. Unscrew the old sensor, apply thread sealant or tape to the new sensor threads, and torque to manufacturer specifications.
7. **Reconnect the wiring harness**, replace the front cover, restore power and gas, and run a hot water tap to verify the error has cleared and the unit operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the error persists after replacing the sensor and verifying all wiring, or if you suspect a control board fault. A pro has diagnostic tools to test board inputs and can safely handle gas line work. Also call if you see any signs of water leaks around the sensor port, as improper installation can cause flooding or damage to the unit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $180-350.
