## Navien Tankless Water Heater E028 Error — What It Means

The E028 error code on a Navien tankless water heater typically signals a problem with the air pressure switch or combustion air flow. The unit's safety system has detected that proper airflow for safe combustion is not present. This can stem from blocked vents, a faulty air pressure switch, improper venting installation, or issues with the combustion blower.

The heater will not fire when this code appears because the control board is preventing operation until safe combustion air conditions are confirmed. The air pressure switch is a safety device that verifies the blower is creating the correct draft before allowing gas to flow.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the air pressure switch when the real problem is a blocked vent termination, bird nest in the exhaust pipe, or disconnected vent tubing. Always inspect all vent pipes and tubing connections before ordering a new switch.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted intake or exhaust vent (~45%)** Snow, ice, leaves, insect nests, or lint accumulation at the outdoor vent termination prevents proper airflow and triggers the safety switch.
- **Disconnected or cracked air pressure switch sensing tube (~25%)** The small rubber or vinyl tubing that connects the venturi or blower housing to the air pressure switch can fall off, crack, or develop leaks over time.
- **Failed air pressure switch (~15%)** The switch itself can fail electrically or mechanically, preventing it from closing even when proper airflow is present.
- **Combustion blower motor failure or weak operation (~10%)** A failing blower may not generate enough pressure differential to close the air pressure switch contacts.
- **Improper vent pipe installation or length (~5%)** Vent pipes that exceed maximum allowable length, have too many elbows, or are pitched incorrectly can cause inadequate draft and airflow issues.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shut-off valve for safety.
2. **Inspect the outdoor vent terminations** for blockages such as ice, snow, leaves, bird nests, or debris and clear any obstructions completely.
3. **Remove the front cover** of the heater and locate the air pressure switch, a small round or rectangular device with two or three wires and one or two small rubber tubes attached.
4. **Check all sensing tubes** connected to the air pressure switch and trace them back to their connection points on the venturi or blower housing, ensuring they are firmly attached and free of cracks or kinks.
5. **Test the air pressure switch** by carefully disconnecting the sensing tube and gently blowing into it while listening for a click from the switch, or use a multimeter to check for continuity across the switch terminals when pressure is applied.
6. **Inspect the combustion blower** by spinning the fan wheel by hand to check for binding or debris, and verify the blower runs when power is restored.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (air pressure switch, sensing tube, or blower motor) as identified by your testing, reconnect all wiring and tubing securely, and restore power and gas to test the unit.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, if the vent system appears improperly installed or exceeds recommended lengths, or if you have replaced the air pressure switch and sensing tubes but the error persists. A professional can perform combustion analysis, measure actual air pressure differentials, and inspect the heat exchanger for blockages that may restrict airflow. Also call a pro if the combustion blower motor needs replacement and you lack experience with electrical diagnostics and motor swap procedures.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-180 in parts, 1-2 hours. A pro service call runs about $180-400.
