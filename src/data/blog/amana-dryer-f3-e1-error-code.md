## Amana Dryer F3 E1 Error — What It Means

The F3 E1 code on an Amana dryer signals that the control board has detected an abnormal reading from the exhaust thermistor circuit. The exhaust thermistor monitors the temperature of air leaving the drum and helps the control manage cycle timing and heat output. When the circuit reads open, shorted, or otherwise out of range, the dryer throws F3 E1 and stops to protect itself.

Amana's official guidance names the exhaust thermistor as the component tied to this code. The fault is typically an open circuit, short circuit, or intermittent connection in the thermistor itself, its wiring harness, or the connector at the control board. Less commonly, the control board's input circuit may fail and misread a good sensor.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board first when F3 E1 appears. Test the thermistor resistance and inspect the wiring harness before buying a board, since the sensor and connectors fail far more often and cost much less.

## Common Causes

- **Failed exhaust thermistor (~50%)** The thermistor develops an open or short circuit internally, sending an invalid signal to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded connector (~25%)** Vibration, heat cycles, or moisture causes the thermistor plug at the board or sensor to lose contact or corrode.
- **Damaged wiring harness (~15%)** Wires running to the exhaust thermistor become pinched, chafed, or broken, creating an open or intermittent circuit.
- **Control board input circuit fault (~8%)** The main control board's thermistor input circuit fails and reads the sensor incorrectly even when the sensor is good.
- **Intermittent connection from vibration (~2%)** A connector that is not fully seated or a wire terminal that is loose causes the code to come and go during operation.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by switching off both breakers (or unplugging) and wait five full minutes, then restore power and run a timed dry cycle to see if the code returns.
2. **Access the exhaust thermistor** by removing the lower front panel or rear panel (consult your model's service manual for location, typically near the blower housing or exhaust duct).
3. **Inspect the thermistor connector** for corrosion, bent pins, or a loose fit, and check the wiring harness for pinched, chafed, or broken insulation along its entire run.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor plug** from the harness and use a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) to measure across the thermistor terminals at room temperature (typically 10,000 to 25,000 ohms, but verify against your model's specification sheet).
5. **Replace the exhaust thermistor** if the reading is open (infinite), near zero (shorted), or far outside the expected range, and secure all connectors before reassembling.
6. **Test harness continuity** if the thermistor measures good by checking each wire from the sensor end to the control board plug with the meter in continuity or resistance mode.
7. **Replace the main control board** only if the thermistor and harness both test good and you have confirmed the board input circuit is reading incorrectly (this is rare and should be a last resort after all other checks).

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with live voltage, if the thermistor and harness both test good and you suspect the control board input circuit, or if the fault persists after replacing the thermistor and you need definitive diagnostic equipment. A pro can also quickly identify intermittent wiring faults that are hard to catch with a simple meter check, and most carry the correct thermistor and board for your model on the truck. If your dryer is still under warranty, always contact Amana service first to avoid voiding coverage by attempting self-repair.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $25–70 in parts, 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280.
