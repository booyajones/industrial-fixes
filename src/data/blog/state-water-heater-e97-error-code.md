## State Water Heater E97 Error Code — What It Means

The E97 error code is not explicitly documented in publicly available State Water Heater manufacturer literature, so the exact meaning depends on your specific model and series. State directs homeowners to scan the QR code on the rating plate to access the correct manual and error-code definitions. Based on common State and A.O. Smith tankless platform faults, codes in this range typically indicate combustion or airflow problems, such as blocked vents, flame-sensing failures, or gas-supply issues.

Because error codes are model-specific, the safest first step is to identify your exact model number from the rating plate and retrieve the matching service documentation. Until you confirm the code's precise definition, treat E97 as a general combustion-related fault and focus on common airflow and flame-sensing checks.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked air intake or exhaust vent** Debris, snow, or lint can obstruct the intake or exhaust termination, preventing proper combustion airflow and triggering a fault.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor rod** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame sensor prevents the control board from confirming ignition, causing a lockout.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply** Closed gas valve, low gas pressure, or an empty propane tank will prevent the burner from lighting or maintaining a stable flame.
- **Clogged inlet water filter** Sediment in the inlet screen reduces water flow below the minimum threshold needed to activate the heater, mimicking a combustion fault on some models.
- **Failed igniter or ignition assembly** A weak or broken igniter cannot create a spark, so the burner never lights and the control board reports a fault.
- **Faulty control board or sensor wiring** Loose connections, damaged wiring, or a failing control board can misread sensor signals and throw false combustion codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the exact model and error-code definition** by locating the rating plate on the side or front of the unit, scanning the QR code, and downloading the matching service manual from State's online support portal.
2. **Turn off power and gas** at the dedicated shutoffs, then wait three minutes to allow any residual gas to clear and capacitors to discharge.
3. **Inspect the air intake and exhaust vent** for obstructions, including snow, bird nests, lint, or debris at the termination caps, and clear any blockages you find.
4. **Remove and clean the flame sensor rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth, wiping away carbon deposits and corrosion until the metal surface is bright and smooth.
5. **Check the inlet water filter** by unscrewing the cold-water inlet connection, pulling the screen, rinsing it under running water, and reinstalling it securely.
6. **Confirm gas supply and pressure** by verifying that the manual gas valve is fully open, checking propane tank levels if applicable, and listening for gas flow at the valve when the unit calls for heat.
7. **Restore power and gas, then reset** the heater by following the reset procedure in your manual (typically a button-hold sequence or power cycle), and observe whether the code clears and the burner lights normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if the error persists after cleaning the flame sensor and clearing vent blockages, if you smell gas at any point, or if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances. A technician can measure combustion air pressure, test gas valve operation, verify sensor voltages, and replace control boards or igniters that require specialized tools and training. Because the exact meaning of E97 is not confirmed without your model-specific manual, professional diagnostics are the fastest path to an accurate repair if basic airflow and sensor cleaning do not resolve the fault.
