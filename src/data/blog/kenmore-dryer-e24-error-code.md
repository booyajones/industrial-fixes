## Kenmore Dryer E24 Error Code — What It Means

The E24 error code on Kenmore dryers is not uniformly defined across all models. Different Kenmore dryer series may assign this code to different faults, ranging from control board communication errors to sensor issues or wiring problems. Because Kenmore dryers are manufactured by multiple OEMs under the Kenmore label, the exact meaning of E24 depends on your specific model number and production year.

To determine what E24 means for your dryer, consult the owner's manual or the wiring diagram attached to the dryer cabinet. The diagram typically lists error codes and their definitions for that model. In general, E-series codes on electronic-control dryers point to electrical faults rather than mechanical problems, so the repair will usually involve testing sensors, harnesses, or the control board itself.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when they see an error code, but a loose connector or damaged wire between the control and a sensor is often the real problem. Check all harness plugs and inspect wiring for damage before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded wiring connector (~35%)** A plug on the control board or sensor harness can work loose from vibration or develop corrosion that breaks continuity.
- **Damaged wire harness (~25%)** Wires that run near moving parts or sharp edges can chafe through insulation and short or open the circuit.
- **Failed temperature sensor or thermistor (~20%)** A sensor that reads out of range or open will trigger a fault code on models that monitor inlet or exhaust temperature.
- **Control board glitch or failure (~15%)** The main electronic control can develop solder cracks, failed relays, or corrupted firmware that generates error codes.
- **User interface board communication fault (~5%)** On two-board designs the ribbon cable or communication line between the UI and main control can fail.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the dryer** from the wall outlet and wait 60 seconds to reset the control board and clear any transient faults.
2. **Locate the wiring diagram** on the inside of the cabinet or console panel and note the definition of E24 for your model.
3. **Remove the top or rear panel** as required to access the control board and sensor harnesses, using a screwdriver or nut driver.
4. **Inspect every wire connector** on the main control, user interface, and any sensor plugs for looseness, corrosion, or bent pins, and reseat them firmly.
5. **Trace the sensor wiring** along the drum and blower housing, looking for abraded insulation, pinched wires, or breaks, and repair with heat-shrink tubing or replace the harness.
6. **Test resistance of temperature sensors** with a multimeter at room temperature and compare readings to the service table for your model; replace any sensor reading open or far out of range.
7. **Swap the control board** if all wiring and sensors test good and the code persists, matching the part number from your model's label or wiring diagram.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you cannot locate the wiring diagram or service manual for your model and do not know which component E24 refers to. Also call a technician if you are uncomfortable working with 120 or 240-volt connections, if multimeter testing is unfamiliar, or if the dryer uses gas and you need to disconnect the supply line to access internal parts. A qualified appliance technician will have access to model-specific service documentation and can perform voltage and continuity tests safely to pinpoint the fault without guessing.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-150 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-280.
