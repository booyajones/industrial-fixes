## Mitsubishi Mini-Split P8 Error — What It Means

A P8 error on a Mitsubishi mini-split indicates a protection fault in the outdoor unit, typically tied to compressor start-up problems or overheating. This code is most often associated with the outdoor unit's compressor attempting to start and then tripping into protection mode, or the outdoor electronics detecting an overheat condition. Unlike simple sensor faults confined to the indoor unit, P8 points to the outdoor unit's compressor circuit, inverter, or thermal protection systems.

The fault can stem from airflow restrictions that cause the outdoor coil or compressor to overheat, electrical supply issues that prevent correct compressor start behavior, or a failing compressor that cannot complete its start cycle. Some Mitsubishi models also trigger P8 when outdoor thermistors or inverter boards detect abnormal temperatures or phase-supply problems. Because code definitions vary across Mitsubishi series, always confirm your exact indoor and outdoor model numbers and consult the matching service manual before beginning repairs.

## Common Causes

- **Outdoor unit airflow blockage** Dirty condenser coil, blocked fan discharge, or debris around the outdoor unit prevents heat rejection and causes the compressor or electronics to overheat and trip protection.
- **Compressor start-up failure** The compressor attempts to start but cannot complete the cycle due to locked rotor, internal fault, or drive-circuit protection, triggering P8.
- **Outdoor fan motor failure** A stopped or slow outdoor fan cannot move air across the coil, leading to rapid overheating of the compressor and control board.
- **Wiring or terminal connection fault** Loose, corroded, or overheated connections between indoor and outdoor units disrupt control signals or power delivery, causing protection trips.
- **Inverter or control board overheat** The outdoor unit's inverter or power module overheats due to poor ventilation, component failure, or voltage irregularities and activates thermal protection.
- **Power supply or phase issue** Unstable voltage, phase reversal, or low supply voltage on three-phase models prevents the compressor from starting correctly and triggers the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power-cycle the system** by switching off the breaker or disconnect for 60 seconds, then restore power and observe whether the P8 code returns immediately or after a start attempt.
2. **Inspect the outdoor unit for airflow obstructions**: remove leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the cabinet, check that the fan spins freely, and confirm nothing blocks the coil or discharge grille.
3. **Clean the outdoor coil** using a soft brush and coil cleaner or gentle water spray from the inside out, taking care not to bend fins, then allow the unit to dry completely before restarting.
4. **Verify outdoor fan operation** by observing the fan during a start attempt and listening for unusual noise or complete failure to spin, which would indicate a motor or capacitor fault.
5. **Check all electrical connections** at the outdoor unit's terminal block and on the control board, looking for loose screws, discolored terminals, or signs of arcing or water intrusion.
6. **Measure supply voltage** at the outdoor disconnect and compare to the nameplate rating, ensuring voltage is stable and within tolerance during compressor start-up.
7. **Test the compressor start behavior** by listening and watching for the unit to attempt compressor start, noting whether it hums, trips immediately, or fails to energize, which points to compressor or inverter failure, then consult the service manual for model-specific thermistor resistance values and inverter diagnostics if airflow and power checks are normal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if the P8 code persists after you have cleaned the coil and confirmed good airflow, or if you lack the tools to safely measure voltage and test compressor start current. Diagnosing inverter boards, compressor windings, and refrigerant-side faults requires specialized gauges, meters, and knowledge of the specific Mitsubishi platform. Compressor and inverter replacement also involves recovering refrigerant, brazing, evacuation, and precise electrical work that must meet local codes. If the outdoor unit is still under warranty, unauthorized repairs may void coverage, so verify warranty status and use a Mitsubishi-certified contractor for any board or compressor work.
