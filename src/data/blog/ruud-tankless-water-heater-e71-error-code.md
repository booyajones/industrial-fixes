## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E71 Error — What It Means

The E71 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters typically relates to a problem in the combustion or gas delivery system, though the exact meaning can vary by model. Without model-specific documentation, the code may indicate a gas valve fault, flame sensor malfunction, or ignition sequence failure. Your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit will provide the precise definition for your specific model.

Because error codes are not standardized across all tankless models, even within the same brand, it is important to verify the code meaning in your unit's documentation before attempting repairs. The code may trigger when the control board detects an inconsistency during startup or operation that affects safe combustion.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when the real problem is a dirty flame sensor or loose gas valve connector. Clean the flame rod and check all wiring connections before ordering expensive parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~35%)** Carbon buildup or corrosion on the flame rod prevents the control board from confirming ignition, triggering a safety lockout.
- **Gas valve electrical fault (~30%)** A failed coil, stuck solenoid, or loose wiring connection at the gas valve stops gas flow or prevents proper valve operation.
- **Control board malfunction (~20%)** The main PCB may misread sensor signals or fail to send correct commands to the gas valve, especially after a power surge.
- **Low or interrupted gas supply (~10%)** Insufficient gas pressure or a closed upstream valve can prevent ignition or cause the flame to drop out during operation.
- **Loose or damaged wiring (~5%)** Vibration or rodent damage can break connections between the control board, gas valve, and flame sensor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve at the heater inlet.
2. **Remove the front cover** of the tankless unit and locate the flame sensor rod inside the combustion chamber.
3. **Clean the flame sensor** with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits and corrosion, then wipe it dry.
4. **Inspect all wiring connectors** at the gas valve, flame sensor, and control board for looseness, corrosion, or burn marks.
5. **Restore gas and power**, then initiate a heating cycle and observe whether the igniter glows and the gas valve clicks open.
6. **Check the gas supply pressure** using a manometer at the test port on the gas valve, and compare the reading to your model's specification table.
7. **If the error persists**, consult the unit's wiring diagram to identify the exact component the E71 code references and test or replace that part with a licensed technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the error returns after cleaning the flame sensor and resetting the unit, or if you lack the tools to measure gas pressure safely. Professional diagnosis is also necessary when the problem involves the control board or gas valve, since these components require precise testing and gas-safe installation. Any work on gas appliances should comply with local codes, and many jurisdictions require a licensed contractor for repairs that involve the gas train or electrical controls.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
