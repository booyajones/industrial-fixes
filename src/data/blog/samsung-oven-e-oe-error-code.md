## Samsung Oven E-OE Error Code — What It Means

E-OE (or E-0E) is a door lock error on Samsung ovens and ranges. The control board expects the door lock mechanism to report that it has successfully locked or unlocked, but it does not receive that signal. When the lock system fails to respond, the oven inhibits normal operation, especially self-clean cycles and certain bake functions that require a secure door.

In practical terms, the oven cannot proceed because it cannot verify the door is safely locked or unlocked. This fault typically points to a mechanical or electrical problem in the door lock assembly itself, a jammed latch, damaged wiring between the lock and control board, or a faulty lock-position switch that cannot sense the latch state.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when they see E-OE, but the fault is almost always in the door lock assembly. Inspect the lock mechanism and test its wiring and connectors before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Failed door lock mechanism or locking motor** The small motor or solenoid that drives the latch can burn out or seize, preventing the lock from moving and reporting its state to the control.
- **Broken or jammed latch** Grease, food debris, or mechanical misalignment can bind the latch so it cannot extend or retract, blocking the lock cycle.
- **Wiring or connector issue** Loose, disconnected, pinched, or corroded wiring between the lock assembly and the control board interrupts the feedback signal.
- **Faulty lock-position switch** A discrete switch that senses whether the latch is locked or unlocked may fail, even if the motor moves, so the control never sees a valid state.
- **Physical obstruction in the lock path** A mis-seated oven door or foreign object around the latch can prevent full travel and trigger the error.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the door close smoothly and sit flush with the oven frame?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The door hardware is not blocking the lock, so focus on the lock assembly and its wiring.<br><strong>No:</strong> Adjust or inspect the door hinges and latch area for debris or damage that prevents proper seating.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you hear or feel the lock motor trying to engage when you start a self-clean cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The motor runs but the lock may be jammed or the position switch is not registering, so inspect the latch mechanism.<br><strong>No:</strong> The motor is silent, pointing to a power or wiring issue to the lock assembly or a dead motor.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Are the wiring connectors on the lock assembly fully seated and free of corrosion?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Wiring is good, so the lock assembly or its internal switch is the likely fault.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clean or reseat the connectors and check for damaged wires before replacing parts.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range to de-energize the oven completely.
2. **Remove the oven door** (if needed for access) by lifting it off the hinges or following your model's instructions, then set it safely aside.
3. **Inspect the door latch area** for grease, debris, or any physical obstruction that could prevent the lock from moving freely.
4. **Locate the door lock assembly**, usually mounted inside the front frame or along the top of the oven cavity, and check that all wiring connectors are firmly seated and undamaged.
5. **Test the lock mechanism** by observing whether the latch motor attempts to move when power is restored and a lock cycle is initiated, or use a multimeter to check continuity and voltage at the lock harness if you have the wiring diagram.
6. **Replace the door lock assembly** if the motor does not actuate, the latch is mechanically broken, or the switch does not provide feedback to the control.
7. **Reassemble the oven**, restore power, and run a short self-clean or bake cycle to verify the lock engages and disengages without returning the E-OE code.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable removing the oven door, accessing internal components behind the control panel, or working with line-voltage wiring. Also call if you have replaced the lock assembly and wiring but the E-OE code persists, since the control board itself may have a failed lock-driver circuit. A qualified service tech has the wiring diagrams, voltage measurements, and diagnostic tools to pinpoint board-level faults and can safely test the lock circuit under power.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–120 in parts, 45–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
