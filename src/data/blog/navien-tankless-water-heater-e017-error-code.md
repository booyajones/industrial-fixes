## Navien Tankless Water Heater E017 Error — What It Means

The E017 error on a Navien tankless water heater signals that the unit is not detecting adequate water flow or the flow sensor is not reading correctly. This code typically appears when the heater expects water to move through the system but the flow sensor registers insufficient or no flow. The unit shuts down to prevent damage from running without proper water circulation.

The error can occur during startup or while the heater is running. It may be triggered by closed or partially closed fixtures, a clogged inlet filter, air in the lines, a faulty flow sensor, or wiring problems between the sensor and the control board. Because tankless heaters rely on flow to activate and modulate, even a brief interruption can trigger this code.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flow sensor when the real problem is a dirty inlet filter or air trapped in the lines. Clean the filter and bleed air from the system before buying a new sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet filter screen (~40%)** Sediment and mineral deposits accumulate on the cold-water inlet screen, restricting flow below the threshold the sensor needs to detect.
- **Air trapped in the water lines (~20%)** Air pockets can form after service, installation, or low city pressure, preventing the flow sensor from reading steady water movement.
- **Faulty flow sensor (~20%)** The turbine or hall-effect sensor inside the flow meter can fail mechanically or electrically, sending no signal to the board.
- **Low water pressure (~10%)** Incoming supply pressure below the minimum required prevents the heater from detecting flow even when taps are open.
- **Loose or corroded sensor wiring (~10%)** Connector pins or wiring between the flow sensor and control board can oxidize or vibrate loose, interrupting the signal.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the cold-water isolation valve on the inlet side of the unit.
2. **Locate the inlet filter screen** on the cold-water service valve. Consult your model's manual if the valve assembly is not clearly marked.
3. **Remove the filter screen** by unscrewing the service cap or valve bonnet. Catch any residual water with a towel.
4. **Rinse the screen** under running water and use a soft brush to remove sediment, scale, or debris. Hold the screen up to light to confirm all mesh openings are clear.
5. **Reinstall the filter screen** and tighten the cap or bonnet by hand, then snug with a wrench. Do not overtighten plastic components.
6. **Open the cold-water isolation valve** fully and check for leaks around the service cap. Tighten gently if needed.
7. **Restore power** at the breaker and open a hot-water tap fully for two minutes to purge air and test flow. Watch the heater display for the error code to clear.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you have cleaned the filter, bled air from the lines, and verified supply pressure but the E017 code persists. A professional can test the flow sensor electronically, inspect internal water pathways for blockages, and check the control board. Also call a pro if you are uncomfortable working with water connections, if the unit is still under warranty, or if you suspect low city water pressure that requires a booster pump or pressure regulator adjustment.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
