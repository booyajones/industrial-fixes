## Frigidaire Refrigerator Leaking Water — What's Happening

A Frigidaire refrigerator leaking water is a symptom, not a fault code. Water can appear inside the fresh-food cabinet, on the floor in front or underneath, at the back of the unit, or near the dispenser and filter area. The location of the leak helps narrow down the cause.

The most common source is a clogged or frozen defrost drain. When this drain becomes blocked, meltwater from the evaporator can't flow to the drain pan and instead backs up and overflows into the cabinet or onto the floor. Other frequent causes include a cracked or improperly seated water filter, loose or damaged water line connections, a faulty water inlet valve, a cracked drain pan, and ice buildup in the door chute or dispenser path that later melts and drips.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Clogged or frozen defrost drain** This is the most common cause of water inside the cabinet or on the floor, where ice or debris blocks the drain and meltwater backs up instead of flowing to the pan.
- **Water filter not seated or cracked** A filter that is not fully installed or has a crack can leak water into the cabinet or cause slow water flow at the dispenser.
- **Loose or leaking water line connections** Loose fittings at the water supply line, rear inlet valve, or internal tubing can drip water behind or beneath the refrigerator.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** A damaged or worn inlet valve can leak at the back of the unit or allow water into the dispenser path when it should be closed.
- **Cracked drain pan** A damaged pan under the refrigerator will leak condensate or defrost water onto the floor instead of holding it for evaporation.
- **Door left open or poor door seal** An open door or damaged gasket allows humid air in, which ices up the evaporator and then melts, overwhelming the drain or dripping onto the floor.
- **Ice buildup in door chute or dispenser path** Frost or ice in the chute area can melt later and run down into the cabinet or onto the floor.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Identify where the water is appearing: inside the cabinet, on the floor in front or underneath, at the rear of the unit, or around the dispenser and filter area.
2. Remove and inspect the water filter for cracks or damage, and confirm it is fully seated and properly installed. Replace if damaged.
3. Pull the unit away from the wall, wipe all water line connections dry, and look for fresh moisture at the supply line, compression fittings, and inlet valve. Tighten any loose fittings and replace damaged tubing or valves.
4. Access the defrost drain at the evaporator area or rear drain tube and flush it with warm water to clear debris. If frozen, thaw carefully until water flows freely into the drain pan.
5. Remove and inspect the drain pan for cracks or holes. Replace the pan if damaged, or check for upstream icing if the pan is overflowing but intact.
6. Inspect the door gasket for gaps, tears, or poor closure, and verify the door closes fully and seals tightly. Replace the gasket if worn or damaged.
7. Check the door chute and dispenser path for visible ice or frost buildup and remove any ice that could melt and drip later.
8. Monitor the unit for 24 hours after clearing the drain or making repairs to confirm the leak has stopped and water is draining properly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the defrost drain remains frozen after flushing, if you cannot safely access the evaporator area or rear drain tube, or if the leak continues after replacing the filter and tightening all visible connections. A pro should also handle refrigerant system work if the evaporator is icing abnormally or if the inlet valve or internal tubing requires replacement in a difficult-to-reach location.
