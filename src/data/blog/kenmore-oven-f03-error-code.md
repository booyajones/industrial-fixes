## Kenmore Oven F03 Error Code — What It Means

The F03 error code on Kenmore ovens generally indicates a problem with the oven temperature sensor circuit or the electronic control board that reads it. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the control panel for your specific unit. In most cases the control has detected a sensor reading that is out of the expected range, either too high or too low, or an open or short circuit in the sensor wiring.

This fault prevents the oven from heating properly because the control relies on accurate temperature feedback to regulate the heating elements. The code may appear at startup or while the oven is heating.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic control board first, but a simple resistance test of the oven temperature sensor with a multimeter will identify a failed sensor and save several hundred dollars.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~55%)** The sensor probe develops an internal open or short circuit, sending incorrect resistance values to the control board.
- **Damaged or loose sensor wiring (~25%)** The wires running from the sensor to the control board are frayed, pinched, or the connector has worked loose over time.
- **Faulty electronic control board (~15%)** The control board itself has a failed component in the sensor input circuit and misreads a good sensor.
- **Corroded sensor connector (~5%)** Moisture or heat has caused corrosion at the wire terminals, creating intermittent contact or high resistance.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the oven completely before beginning any work.
2. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** inside the oven cavity, usually on the upper rear wall. It is a metal probe about the diameter of a pencil protruding through the back panel.
3. **Remove the rear access panel** of the oven (or the control panel if rear access is not available) to expose the sensor wire connector and the electronic control board.
4. **Disconnect the sensor wire connector** at the control board. Note the wire colors or take a photo for reference.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter set to ohms. At room temperature a typical oven sensor will read around 1,100 ohms. Consult your model's service manual for the exact specification.
6. **Inspect the sensor wiring** for any signs of fraying, pinching, melting, or damage along its length from the sensor probe to the control board.
7. **Replace the oven temperature sensor** if the resistance reading is far out of range, if the sensor is visibly damaged, or if the wiring is compromised. Unscrew the mounting bracket inside the oven, pull the probe out, thread the new sensor through, and reconnect at the control board.
8. **Clear the error code** by pressing and holding the cancel or clear button, or by disconnecting power for one minute, then test the oven through a bake cycle to confirm the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with the oven's electrical components or removing panels to access the control board. Also call a technician if you have replaced the sensor and verified the wiring is intact but the F03 code persists, as this points to a control board fault that requires careful diagnosis and potentially programming of the replacement board. If your oven is still under warranty, contact Kenmore service to avoid voiding coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-80 in parts, 30-45 minutes. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
