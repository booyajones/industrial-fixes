## What this code means
LOC on a KitchenAid oven display means the Control Lock feature has been activated. This is not a failure code. When LOC appears, the control pads are locked and the oven door is locked to prevent unintended use. Pressing any control while the lock is active will display LOC as a reminder.

KitchenAid designed this feature to shut down the control pads and secure the oven door, often to prevent children or accidental touches from changing settings. The lock can be engaged or disengaged by the user through a key sequence specific to the model.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board or door latch motor thinking LOC is a fault code. LOC is a feature state. Try the unlock key sequence and a power reset before ordering any parts.

## Common Causes

- **Control Lock accidentally enabled (~85%)** A user or customer pressed the lock key combination without realizing it, activating the control lock feature.
- **Power event left lock state active (~10%)** A brief power outage or surge caused the control to retain or default to the locked state when power returned.
- **Stuck or shorted START/ENTER key (~3%)** A key on the control panel is mechanically stuck or the keypad membrane has moisture or debris causing a continuous lock signal.
- **Control panel or keypad assembly failure (~2%)** The keypad or control board cannot process the unlock command due to internal failure, though this is rare for a LOC display.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model and display.** Confirm the screen reads LOC and not another code (such as F9 or Loc with additional symbols).
2. **Turn off the oven and timer.** Make sure the oven is not in a cook cycle and the timer is not running before attempting to unlock.
3. **Press and hold START/ENTER for 5 seconds.** On most KitchenAid models, this is the unlock sequence. You should hear a tone and LOC will disappear if successful.
4. **If your model uses a different key, consult the owner's manual.** Some ranges require a dedicated Lock or Control Lock button held for 3 to 5 seconds.
5. **Perform a power reset if the lock does not clear.** Turn off the circuit breaker(s) that supply the oven for 1 full minute, then restore power.
6. **Monitor the display for 1 minute after power returns.** Confirm that LOC does not reappear and that the controls respond to touch.
7. **If LOC persists after the correct unlock sequence and power reset, inspect the control panel.** Check for stuck keys, moisture, or debris around the START/ENTER button or keypad membrane. If the panel is clean and the lock will not disengage, the control panel or electronic control board may require service or replacement.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if the LOC display cannot be cleared using the correct key sequence and a full power reset, or if you are uncomfortable working around the oven's control panel and high-voltage connections. A technician will verify the keypad input signals and check the electronic control board against the service literature for your exact model. If the control panel or board must be replaced, the technician will make sure proper connector seating and calibration. Do not attempt to disassemble the control or door-lock mechanism if you are unsure of the wiring, because other fault codes (such as F9 door-lock errors) require different diagnostic steps.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0, 1–5 min. A pro service call runs about $75–150 (service call if unlock fails and control requires replacement).
