## Electrolux EF5 Error Code — What It Means

EF5 indicates the washer has detected an out-of-balance condition during the spin cycle. The machine skips the final spin to protect the drum, motor, and cabinet from damage. On some models, Electrolux also lists a defective drain pump as a potential cause. If the washer cannot drain properly, water retention in the tub will worsen the imbalance and trigger the fault.

## Common Causes

- **Uneven or overloaded laundry** Heavy items bunched on one side or too much laundry in the drum prevent the tub from spinning smoothly.
- **Machine not level** If the washer rocks or sits unevenly on the floor, it cannot balance the load correctly during high-speed spin.
- **Drain pump defective** Electrolux lists a failing or clogged drain pump as a possible cause, since trapped water adds weight and throws off balance.
- **Blocked drain filter or hose** A restriction in the drain path prevents complete water evacuation, leaving the tub heavier on one side.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Cancel the cycle and open the door, then redistribute the load by separating tangled items and spreading heavy pieces evenly around the drum.
2. Check that the washer is level by placing a bubble level on top of the cabinet, then adjust the front leveling feet until the machine does not rock.
3. Inspect the drain filter at the lower front panel, remove any lint or debris, and rinse it clean before reinstalling.
4. Verify the drain hose is not kinked or elevated higher than the pump outlet, and that it drops into a standpipe or laundry sink below 96 inches.
5. Run a short spin-only cycle with a small balanced load to confirm the washer completes the spin and the EF5 code clears.
6. Test the drain pump if the code persists by listening for hum or checking for blockage at the pump inlet, and replace the pump if it does not run or drains slowly.
7. Clear the stored fault code by unplugging the washer for two minutes, then run a full test wash to verify normal operation.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the EF5 code returns after you have redistributed the load, leveled the machine, and verified the drain system is clear. Persistent imbalance faults can point to worn suspension rods, a damaged spider arm, or a control-board issue that requires diagnostic mode and specialized tools. A pro can measure pump resistance, check rotor balance, and access service codes that are not visible on the user display.
