## Weil-McLain A175 Error Code — What It Means

A175 is not a standard, manufacturer-published fault code that appears across all Weil-McLain boilers. Weil-McLain uses different control families, and fault codes vary by model and control board. The exact meaning of A175 must be confirmed against your specific boiler model manual and the control board's diagnostics or error history menu. Enter the diagnostics menu on your control panel and read the stored fault description and any lockout history to identify the actual problem before beginning repairs.

Common lockout and no-heat faults on Weil-McLain boilers involve low system water pressure, ignition or flame-sensing failures, thermistor or temperature-sensor problems, circulation issues from trapped air, and condensate drain blockages on condensing models. If your boiler is showing A175, note your model number and check the error history on the control. If the manual does not list A175, contact Weil-McLain technical support or a qualified service technician with the full error text and model information.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or flame sensor before checking system water pressure, trapped air in the zone piping, or a dirty flame rod. Verify cold system pressure is at least 12 psi and bleed all zones before swapping any electrical parts.

## Common Causes

- **Low system water pressure or air in the zones (~30%)** Cold system pressure below 12 psi or trapped air in the piping prevents circulation and triggers low-water or overheat safeties.
- **Flame sensor dirty or failing (~25%)** Carbon buildup or sensor wear prevents flame detection, causing ignition lockout after the burner fires.
- **Thermistor or temperature probe failure (~20%)** A failed or corroded thermistor sends an out-of-range signal to the control, triggering a sensor fault or limit lockout.
- **Circulator pump not running or check valve stuck (~15%)** No flow through the heat exchanger causes a high-limit trip or low-flow fault on certain controls.
- **Control board connector or wiring ground loose (~10%)** Intermittent wiring connections or poor grounds can produce transient faults that show as unfamiliar codes.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Power off the boiler** at the service switch or breaker and let it cool. Gas remains on at the manual shutoff for now.
2. **Check the system pressure gauge** on the boiler. If cold pressure is below 12 psi, slowly open the fill valve until the gauge reads 12 psi, then close the valve.
3. **Bleed air from all zones** at radiator vents or manifold bleed ports. Run the circulator and open each bleed until water flows steadily, then close.
4. **Enter the control's diagnostics or error history menu** by following the button sequence in your model manual. Write down every stored fault code, description, and time stamp.
5. **Inspect the flame sensor and ignition electrode** for carbon buildup or corrosion. Remove the burner cover, carefully pull the flame rod and igniter, and wipe both with fine emery cloth. Reinstall snugly.
6. **Test thermistors and low-water probes** with a multimeter if the error history points to a sensor fault. Consult the model wiring diagram for expected resistance values at room temperature, typically a few thousand ohms.
7. **Check all control-board connectors and ground wires** for looseness or corrosion. Reseat every plug, tighten ground screws, and verify the control transformer output voltage matches the specification on the board label.
8. **Clear the condensate trap** if you have a condensing boiler. Remove the trap, rinse it, and confirm the drain line is not frozen or clogged.
9. **Restore power, reset the control** per the manual, and watch the startup sequence. If the boiler locks out again, note the new fault in the error history and replace the specific component identified.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed boiler technician if you cannot find A175 in your model manual, if the error history shows multiple simultaneous faults, or if the boiler locks out again immediately after you restore power and reset. Gas-fired boiler work requires proper combustion testing and code compliance. A technician has the manometer, flue-gas analyzer, and multimeter to trace intermittent wiring faults, test flame rectification current, verify gas pressure, and replace control boards or sensors safely. Warranty claims with Weil-McLain require the failed part description and part number, so professional documentation protects your coverage. If you smell gas, hear a continuous hissing, or see water pooling under the boiler, shut off gas and power at once and call for emergency service.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $200-450.
