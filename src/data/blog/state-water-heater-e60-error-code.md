## State Water Heater E60 Error Code — What It Means

The E60 error code appears on State Residential Hybrid Electric Heat Pump water heaters and indicates an internal diagnostic fault within the heater system. State groups E10 through E60 codes together as heat-pump diagnostic faults that require electrical troubleshooting. The exact sub-fault definition for E60 is not published in general service literature, so you will need to consult your model's service manual or the manufacturer's fault-code chart to identify the specific component or circuit involved.

Because this code belongs to the heat-pump water heater family, it points to an issue in the electrical heating circuit, sensor network, control board, relay, or wiring rather than a combustion or gas-supply problem. State's technician training materials recommend using the unit's built-in diagnostics together with a multimeter to isolate the fault.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty heating element or circuit** A failed or degraded heating element can trigger diagnostic faults in the heater's control system.
- **Temperature sensor failure** Heat-pump water heaters rely on multiple temperature sensors, and a sensor reading out of range will generate an error.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections** Vibration and moisture can loosen terminals or corrode connectors in the heater's electrical harness.
- **Control board relay or output fault** The main control board may have a failed relay or output driver that cannot energize the heating circuit.
- **Wiring short or open circuit** Damaged wiring between the control board and heating components can create an open or short that the diagnostics detect.
- **Power supply irregularities** Low voltage, phase imbalance, or a tripped breaker can cause the control system to log a fault code.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. Turn off power at the breaker and verify zero voltage at the water heater's electrical box with a multimeter before opening any panels.
2. Record the error code display and consult your model's service manual or the fault-code chart inside the unit's cover to identify the specific E60 sub-fault definition.
3. Restore power and enter the unit's diagnostic mode according to the service instructions to read live sensor values and relay status.
4. Use a multimeter to measure resistance or continuity on the heating element terminals, temperature sensors, and wiring harness connections as directed by the diagnostic display.
5. Inspect all wire connectors and terminal blocks for corrosion, heat damage, or looseness, and clean or tighten any suspect connections.
6. Check incoming voltage at the breaker and at the water heater to confirm stable supply voltage within the nameplate specification.
7. If diagnostics and measurements point to a failed component such as a heating element, sensor, or control board, replace the part and clear the fault code, then monitor the unit through a full heating cycle to verify the repair.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are not comfortable working with live 240-volt circuits or if you do not have a multimeter and the service manual for your specific model. Heat-pump water heaters combine refrigeration and high-voltage electrical systems, and misdiagnosis can damage expensive components or create a safety hazard. A qualified technician has access to the manufacturer's fault-code database, the correct diagnostic procedures, and OEM parts to resolve the E60 fault quickly and safely.
