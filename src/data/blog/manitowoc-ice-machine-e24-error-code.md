## Manitowoc Ice Machine E24 Error — What It Means

The E24 error code on Manitowoc ice machines indicates that the control board has detected a problem with one of the machine's sensor circuits or control inputs. The exact meaning can vary by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the machine's panel for the specific definition on your unit.

This code typically appears when the control board cannot read a sensor properly, when wiring has come loose or corroded, or when a sensor has failed. The machine will usually halt ice production to protect itself until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board when the real problem is a loose connector or a dirty sensor probe. Always inspect all wiring connections and test sensor resistance before ordering a new board.

## Common Causes

- **Loose or corroded sensor connector (~40%)** Vibration or moisture can cause wiring plugs at thermistor or water-level sensors to back out or develop corrosion, breaking the signal path.
- **Failed thermistor or temperature sensor (~25%)** A thermistor that monitors evaporator or water temperature can drift out of range or fail open, triggering the error.
- **Damaged sensor wiring (~15%)** Pinched, cut, or chafed wires between the sensor and control board can create an open circuit or short.
- **Dirty or ice-coated sensor probe (~10%)** Mineral scale or ice buildup on the sensor probe tip can insulate it and prevent accurate readings.
- **Faulty control board (~10%)** A failed input circuit on the main control board may not read sensor signals correctly, though this is less common than wiring and sensor faults.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** to the ice machine by unplugging it or switching off the dedicated circuit breaker.
2. **Remove the front panel** or service cover to access the control board and sensor wiring (refer to your model's service manual for panel removal).
3. **Inspect all sensor connectors** on the control board and at each sensor probe for looseness, corrosion, or moisture, and firmly reseat any that are not fully seated.
4. **Locate the thermistor or sensor** associated with the error (consult the wiring diagram inside the panel) and check the probe tip for ice, mineral deposits, or damage.
5. **Test the sensor resistance** with a multimeter by unplugging it and measuring across its terminals; compare the reading to the resistance table in your service manual for the ambient temperature.
6. **Trace the sensor wiring** from the probe to the control board, looking for pinched, cut, or chafed insulation that might cause an open or short circuit.
7. **Replace the faulty sensor** if it reads open, shorted, or far outside the expected range, routing the new sensor's wire carefully and securing it away from sharp edges and moving parts.
8. **Restore power** and allow the machine to run through a cycle; if the E24 error returns after sensor replacement, test the control board's input circuit or replace the board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a commercial refrigeration technician if you are not comfortable working with electrical circuits, if you cannot locate the correct sensor on your model, or if the error persists after you have verified wiring connections and replaced the sensor. A technician has model-specific diagnostic tools and can test control board inputs directly to avoid unnecessary part replacement. Also call a pro if the machine uses a refrigerant-side sensor, since accessing it may require recovering refrigerant.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30-120 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-350.
