## Ruud Tankless Water Heater E22 Error Code — What It Means

The E22 error code on Ruud tankless water heaters generally indicates a flame detection or combustion problem. The unit is unable to establish or maintain a flame during the heating cycle. This can stem from fuel supply issues, airflow restrictions, or sensor faults. Because the exact meaning can vary slightly between model families, consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the unit cover for the precise definition. The heater will shut down to prevent unsafe operation until the fault is cleared.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the flame sensor or igniter when the real problem is simply a closed gas valve, insufficient gas pressure, or a clogged inlet filter. Check gas valves and clean the filter before ordering sensor parts.

## Common Causes

- **Gas supply restricted or insufficient pressure (~35%)** A partly closed valve, undersized gas line, or low utility pressure prevents the burner from lighting or sustaining a flame.
- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor (~25%)** Carbon buildup or oxidation on the flame-rod sensor prevents it from detecting the flame and triggers a false fault.
- **Clogged inlet water filter (~15%)** Sediment in the filter reduces flow below the minimum threshold, and the heater will not attempt ignition or will abort mid-cycle.
- **Blocked or obstructed venting (~15%)** Debris, ice, or kinked vent piping restricts airflow and starves the combustion chamber of oxygen.
- **Failed igniter or spark electrode (~10%)** A cracked ceramic insulator or corroded tip prevents spark generation, and the burner will not light.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Can you hear the igniter clicking or sparking when the heater tries to fire?</summary>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the exhaust vent clear of obstructions and free of ice or debris at the termination?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> Venting is likely adequate. Focus on gas pressure, flame sensor cleanliness, and water flow.<br><strong>No:</strong> Clear the obstruction or remove ice buildup, then reset the heater and test.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and close the upstream gas valve to make the heater safe for inspection.
2. **Remove the front cover** by unscrewing the retaining screws and lifting the panel away from the cabinet.
3. **Inspect the inlet water filter** at the cold-water connection and remove any sediment or debris, then reinstall and tighten.
4. **Locate the flame sensor rod** inside the combustion chamber (consult your model's diagram) and gently clean it with fine-grit sandpaper or a soft cloth to remove carbon deposits.
5. **Check the igniter electrode** for cracks or heavy corrosion and verify the gap between the electrode and burner (consult your manual for the correct spacing).
6. **Reopen the gas valve** and restore power, then initiate a hot-water draw to test whether the heater fires and the error clears.
7. **If the error persists**, use a manometer to verify gas supply pressure at the unit inlet (consult your model's specifications) or call a licensed technician to test combustion and check the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas connections, if the error persists after cleaning the flame sensor and filter, or if you measure low gas pressure at the inlet. Combustion testing requires specialized tools, and improper adjustments can create carbon-monoxide hazards. A pro will use a manometer to verify supply pressure, inspect the heat exchanger for scale or blockages, and test the control board and safety interlocks. Any work involving gas piping, pressure regulators, or burner assembly should be performed by a qualified technician.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
