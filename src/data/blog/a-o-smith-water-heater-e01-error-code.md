## A.O. Smith Water Heater E01 Error — What It Means

The E01 error code on an A.O. Smith water heater typically signals a problem with the combustion or ignition system. The control board has detected that the burner is not lighting properly or that the flame is not being sensed after ignition. This can occur when gas is not reaching the burner, when the igniter or flame sensor is dirty or failed, or when the gas valve is malfunctioning.

Because this code relates to the gas-fired components, it requires attention to make sure safe operation. The heater will lock out and stop attempting to fire until the fault is corrected and the system is reset. Consult your owner's manual for the exact meaning and reset procedure for your specific model, as error-code definitions can vary across product lines.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the gas valve first, assuming it is the problem. Instead, remove and clean the flame sensor with fine sandpaper or steel wool, which solves most E01 errors at no cost.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or oxidized flame sensor (~50%)** A layer of carbon or mineral deposits on the flame sensor rod prevents it from detecting the burner flame, causing the control to shut down and display E01.
- **Interrupted gas supply (~20%)** A closed valve, empty propane tank, or supply-line blockage prevents gas from reaching the burner, so no flame is present to sense.
- **Failed flame sensor (~15%)** The flame sensor rod itself has deteriorated or its wiring connection is loose, so even a clean sensor cannot signal the presence of flame.
- **Faulty gas valve (~10%)** The gas valve solenoid or electronic control inside the valve does not open to allow gas flow, preventing ignition.
- **Defective control board (~5%)** The electronic control board misreads the flame-sensor signal or fails to command the ignition sequence correctly.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and turn the gas valve to the OFF position.
2. **Wait five minutes** for any residual gas to dissipate, then remove the burner-chamber access cover.
3. **Locate the flame sensor** rod, a thin metal probe near the burner assembly, and disconnect its wire connector.
4. **Remove the sensor** by unscrewing the mounting bracket or nut that secures it to the burner chamber.
5. **Clean the sensor rod** with fine-grit sandpaper or steel wool until the metal is shiny, removing all oxidation and carbon buildup.
6. **Reinstall the sensor** and reconnect the wire, making sure the connection is snug and the sensor is positioned in the flame path.
7. **Replace the access cover**, turn the gas valve back on, restore power, and observe the ignition cycle to confirm the error is cleared.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances or if the error persists after cleaning the flame sensor. A professional can test the gas valve, measure flame-sensor microamps, inspect the igniter and burner assembly, and verify the control board is commanding the ignition sequence correctly. Because this repair involves both gas and electrical components, improper work can create safety hazards including gas leaks or carbon-monoxide risk.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-$350.
