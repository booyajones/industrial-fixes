## GE Dishwasher C5 Error Code — What It Means

The C5 code on GE electronic-control dishwashers means the pump-out time is too short. The control expects the drain solenoid to energize and the pump to remove water within a certain window, but it does not see the expected drain condition in time. This tells you the machine either cannot drain properly or the float assembly is reporting a premature empty condition.

The fault can be triggered by a stuck float that signals the tub is already empty, a blockage that slows the drain and confuses the timing, or a water-supply or fill issue that leaves too little water in the tub to drain normally. GE support documents point to the float cover in the front-left of the tub floor and the drain path (hose, air gap, disposer connection) as the most common causes.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or drain pump first. Before ordering parts, tap the float cover in the front-left tub corner and inspect the drain hose and air gap for clogs, since most C5 codes clear with those simple checks.

## Common Causes

- **Stuck float or float cover (~40%)** The float assembly in the front-left of the tub floor can stick in the up position, signaling the control that the tub is empty before the pump cycle completes.
- **Blocked drain hose (~25%)** A kinked, clogged, or improperly routed drain hose slows water removal and prevents the drain cycle from completing in the expected time.
- **Clogged air gap or disposer connection (~20%)** Debris in the air gap or at the garbage disposer knockout plug can restrict flow and delay drain completion.
- **Sump debris or blockage (~10%)** Food particles or broken glass in the sump area at the back-bottom of the tub can interfere with the drain pump or drain valve operation.
- **Water supply issue (~5%)** If the water supply is off or inlet flow is restricted, the dishwasher may not fill to the correct level and draining behavior will be abnormal.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the float cover in the front-left tub corner move freely up and down when you press it?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The float is not stuck. Move on to inspect the drain hose and air gap for blockage.<br><strong>No:</strong> The float is stuck. Tap the float cover gently to free it, then retry the cycle. If it sticks again, replace the float assembly.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Is the drain hose kinked, or does water back up when you disconnect it at the air gap or disposer?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> You have a drain blockage. Clear the hose, air gap, or disposer connection and retry the cycle.<br><strong>No:</strong> The drain path is clear. Check the sump area for debris and verify the water supply is turned on.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the code clear after you power-cycle the dishwasher (turn off the breaker for 30 seconds) and start a fresh cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The fault was latched or intermittent. Monitor the next few cycles to see if it returns.<br><strong>No:</strong> The fault is persistent. Inspect the sump and drain components or call a technician to test the drain solenoid and control inputs.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the breaker for 30 seconds to reset the dishwasher and clear any latched fault code.
2. **Verify the water supply** is turned on at the shut-off valve under the sink.
3. **Open the dishwasher door** and locate the float cover in the front-left corner of the tub floor.
4. **Tap the float cover** gently with your finger or a spoon handle to free it if it is stuck in the raised position.
5. **Inspect the drain hose** under the sink for kinks, clogs, or improper routing (it should rise to the underside of the countertop before dropping to the drain).
6. **Check the air gap** (if installed) on the sink deck and clean out any debris, or verify the disposer knockout plug was removed if the hose connects to a garbage disposer.
7. **Remove the lower spray arm and filter assembly** and inspect the sump area at the back-bottom of the tub for food debris, then reassemble and run a test cycle.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if the C5 code returns after you have freed the float, cleared the drain path, and verified the water supply. Persistent codes may point to a faulty drain solenoid, a wiring issue in the drain circuit, or a control-board input problem that requires service-level testing and part replacement. A qualified appliance technician can measure the drain-circuit signals and swap components under warranty if your machine is still covered.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-60 min. A pro service call runs about $150-250.
