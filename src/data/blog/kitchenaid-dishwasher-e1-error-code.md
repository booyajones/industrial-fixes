## KitchenAid Dishwasher E1 Error Code — What It Means

The E1 error code on KitchenAid dishwashers typically indicates a problem with water entering the machine or the leak detection system, though the exact meaning can vary by model. Some models use E1 to signal that water is not filling properly within the expected time, while others use it to indicate that the float switch has detected water in the base pan (a leak condition). Always consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the door for the precise definition for your specific model number.

In most cases, the code appears when the control board does not receive the correct signal from the water inlet valve, the fill sensor, or the leak float. The dishwasher may refuse to start a cycle, or it may drain and stop mid-cycle. Identifying which sensor or valve is triggering the fault is the key to a quick repair.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board when the real issue is a stuck float switch in the base pan or a clogged inlet valve screen. Check the float and clean the inlet screen before swapping electronics.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged inlet valve screen (~35%)** Sediment or mineral deposits block water flow into the tub, preventing the machine from filling within the timeout period.
- **Faulty water inlet valve (~30%)** The solenoid coil or valve diaphragm fails, stopping water from entering even when voltage is present.
- **Stuck or triggered leak float switch (~20%)** Water or debris in the base pan lifts the float and signals a leak, causing the machine to lock out and display the error.
- **Defective water-level sensor or pressure switch (~10%)** The sensor cannot confirm proper fill, so the control interprets the condition as a fill failure.
- **Wiring or connector corrosion (~5%)** Corroded terminals at the inlet valve or float switch interrupt the signal path to the control board.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker and turn off the water supply valve under the sink.
2. **Pull the dishwasher forward** enough to access the front lower panel and the water inlet connection at the left or right side of the base.
3. **Remove the lower access panel** and check the base pan for any standing water; if present, soak it up with towels and locate the source of the leak.
4. **Inspect the leak float switch** in the center or corner of the base pan; press it down and make sure it returns freely without sticking.
5. **Disconnect the water supply line** from the inlet valve and remove the small mesh screen inside the valve inlet port; rinse it under water and scrub away any sediment.
6. **Test the inlet valve** by removing the wire connectors and using a multimeter set to resistance (ohms) across the solenoid terminals; a reading of 500 to 1500 ohms is typical (consult your model's table for exact values); infinite resistance means the coil is open and the valve must be replaced.
7. **Reassemble and restore power**, then run a short rinse cycle to confirm water fills normally and the E1 code does not return.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you find water in the base pan but cannot locate the leak source, if the wiring harness shows widespread corrosion, or if replacing the inlet valve and float switch does not clear the error. A technician can pressure-test the tub, trace intermittent electrical faults, and verify that the control board itself is not at fault. Also call for help if you are uncomfortable working with household water lines or if your local code requires a licensed plumber to modify appliance plumbing connections.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-280.
