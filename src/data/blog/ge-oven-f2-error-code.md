## GE Oven F2 Error Code — What It Means

The F2 error code (or F20 on some models) tells you the electronic range control has detected that the oven cavity temperature exceeded a preset safe limit during cooking or self-clean operation. GE's official support documentation states that for an unknown reason the oven temperature has crossed a threshold the control considers unsafe. Technician sources describe this as a runaway-heat fault: the control believes the oven is heating out of control and shuts down to prevent damage or a hazard.

The fault can appear during normal baking, roasting, or self-clean cycles. In some cases the code is triggered by a real over-temperature event (for example poor ventilation or a stuck relay). In other cases the control is receiving a false high-temperature reading from a failed sensor or wiring problem, so the oven may not actually be too hot but the board thinks it is. On certain GE range and wall-oven models the F2 or F20 code can also indicate a keypanel or interface fault rather than a temperature problem, so always check your model's service manual if the usual temperature causes do not apply.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the electronic range control board first. Always test the oven temperature sensor resistance with a multimeter at room temperature before ordering a new board, since a bad sensor is far cheaper and more common.

## Common Causes

- **Failed oven temperature sensor (~50%)** The sensor probe inside the oven cavity has drifted out of specification or failed open or shorted, sending the control board a false high-temperature signal.
- **Damaged sensor wiring or connector (~20%)** The harness between the sensor and the control board is pinched, corroded, or has a loose terminal, causing an intermittent or out-of-range reading.
- **Faulty electronic range control or main oven control board (~20%)** A relay stuck closed, bad solder joint, or microprocessor fault on the ERC causes it to misread temperature or fail to shut off the bake element, creating a real or perceived runaway condition.
- **Poor ventilation or clogged oven vents (~5%)** Blocked air paths or excessive cabinet heat (especially after repeated self-clean cycles) can push the cavity temperature above the safety threshold.
- **Temporary electronic glitch (~3%)** A power surge, brownout, or static discharge caused the control to latch a fault that will clear when power is cycled off and back on.
- **Keypanel or membrane-switch fault (~2%)** On certain GE models the F2 or F20 code can indicate a stuck key or interface problem in the control panel rather than a temperature issue.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** at the circuit breaker or unplug the range completely before touching any internal parts.
2. **Reset the control** by leaving power off for two full minutes, then restore power and check whether the F2 code returns. If it stays clear, monitor the oven during the next few cook cycles.
3. **Locate the oven temperature sensor** inside the cavity, typically a quarter-inch metal probe mounted on the upper rear wall. Inspect it and its wiring for visible damage, burns, or corrosion.
4. **Pull the range forward** and remove the rear access panel to reach the sensor connector and harness. Check that the connector is seated firmly and that no wires are pinched or frayed.
5. **Measure sensor resistance** with a digital multimeter set to ohms. Disconnect the sensor leads and test across the two terminals at room temperature. Technician field checks report a good sensor reads around 1,080–1,100 ohms at room temperature, but consult your model's service sheet for the exact specification.
6. **Replace the sensor** if the resistance is far outside the expected range or if the sensor shows open or shorted. Reconnect the new sensor, restore the rear panel, and cycle power to clear the code.
7. **Test the wiring harness** for continuity from the sensor connector to the control-board pins if the sensor tested good. Repair or replace any damaged sections of the harness.
8. **Replace the electronic range control board** if both the sensor and wiring test normal and the F2 code persists. Transfer all wire connections carefully and note their positions before removal.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified appliance technician if you are uncomfortable working with 240-volt wiring, if you cannot safely pull the range away from the wall to access the rear panel, or if the fault persists after you have replaced both the sensor and checked the wiring. A pro has the service manual, wiring diagram, and experience to pinpoint whether the control board has a relay fault or solder problem that is creating a real runaway condition rather than a false reading. Also call a pro if your model shows the F2 code along with a stuck or unresponsive keypanel, since that combination may require control-panel replacement or a different diagnostic path specific to your range series.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $20–80 in parts (sensor or board), 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on whether the fix is a sensor or a control board.
