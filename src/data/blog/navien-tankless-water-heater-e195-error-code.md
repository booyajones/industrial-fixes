## Navien E195 Error Code — What It Means

The E195 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with the recirculation system or a related sensor. This code can appear when the unit detects that the recirculation pump is not operating correctly, when there is a wiring or control issue between the pump and the main board, or when a temperature or flow sensor is sending incorrect data during recirculation mode.

Because Navien models vary in their exact diagnostic logic, the precise meaning of E195 can differ slightly by model and firmware version. Consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram inside the heater's service panel for the exact definition for your unit. In general, this code points to a failure in the recirculation loop rather than the main heating circuit.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the main control board thinking it is the source of the error, when the actual problem is a failed recirculation pump or a loose connector at the pump. Always verify pump power and check for continuity across the pump windings before replacing the board.

## Common Causes

- **Recirculation pump failure (~45%)** The pump that drives hot water through the return loop has failed mechanically or electrically and no longer spins.
- **Wiring or connector issue at the pump (~25%)** A loose, corroded, or damaged wire connector between the pump and the main board interrupts power or feedback signals.
- **Temperature or flow sensor fault (~15%)** A sensor in the recirculation loop is sending erratic or out-of-range readings that trigger the error.
- **Main control board communication error (~10%)** The control board is not receiving expected signals from the recirculation system due to a board fault or software glitch.
- **Air lock or blockage in the recirculation line (~5%)** Trapped air or debris in the return piping prevents proper flow and causes the pump to run without moving water.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker or service switch and wait 30 seconds, then restore power to attempt a reset.
2. **Locate the recirculation pump** on the outside of the heater (usually near the bottom or side). Remove the front cover if needed to access it.
3. **Inspect the pump wiring harness** for loose, corroded, or damaged connectors. Unplug the connector, clean the terminals with contact cleaner if needed, and reseat it firmly.
4. **Use a multimeter** to check for voltage at the pump terminals when the heater is calling for recirculation. If no voltage is present, trace the wiring back to the control board for breaks or shorts.
5. **Listen and feel the pump** during operation. If you hear humming but no vibration or flow, the impeller may be jammed or broken. Remove the pump by unscrewing the mounting bracket and disconnecting the water fittings (have towels ready for residual water).
6. **Replace the recirculation pump** with a compatible Navien replacement part. Match the model number on the pump label or consult the parts diagram in your owner's manual.
7. **Bleed air from the recirculation line** by opening the purge valve or loosening a union fitting slightly while the pump runs, then retighten once water flows steadily without bubbles.
8. **Restore power and test** by running a hot water tap and observing whether the error code clears and the recirculation system operates normally.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or Navien-certified technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections inside the heater, if the error persists after replacing the recirculation pump and verifying all wiring, or if you suspect a control board fault that requires diagnostic software. A professional can use a service tool to read extended fault logs and verify sensor voltages. Also call a pro if your unit is still under warranty, as unauthorized repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $80-180 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $200-400.
