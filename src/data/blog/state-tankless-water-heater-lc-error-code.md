## State Tankless Water Heater LC Error Code — What It Means

The LC code on State tankless water heaters indicates scale or lime buildup inside the heat exchanger. This is a maintenance reminder rather than a safety shutdown. The heater will typically continue to operate while the display alternates between the LC code and your set temperature. The fault develops when hard water minerals coat the internal surfaces of the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and heat transfer. Unlike immediate fault codes that shut the unit down, LC allows continued operation but warns that descaling is overdue.

This code is most commonly seen in areas with hard water where the unit has not been flushed or descaled on a regular schedule. The scale layer insulates the heat exchanger, forcing the burner to work harder and eventually triggering the controller's scale-detection logic. If ignored, the buildup can permanently damage the heat exchanger and reduce hot water output.

## Common Causes

- **Scale accumulation in heat exchanger** Mineral deposits from hard water coat the internal surfaces of the heat exchanger, triggering the LC fault when buildup reaches a threshold.
- **Lack of routine descaling maintenance** Extended operation without periodic flushing allows lime scale to harden and thicken inside the heat exchanger over months or years.
- **High mineral content in water supply** Hard water with elevated calcium and magnesium levels accelerates scale formation even when the unit is flushed at normal intervals.
- **High temperature setpoint** Operating the heater at higher temperatures increases the rate of mineral precipitation and scale deposition on heat exchanger surfaces.
- **Infrequent or skipped annual service** Homeowners who skip manufacturer-recommended yearly flushing allow scale to build unchecked until the controller detects the condition.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Shut off electrical power** at the breaker or disconnect switch serving the water heater to prevent accidental firing during service.
2. **Close the cold water inlet and hot water outlet isolation valves** on the unit, then open the purge port caps or service valves to relieve any residual pressure.
3. **Connect hoses to the inlet and outlet service ports** and place the free end of the outlet hose into a bucket, then attach a submersible pump to the inlet hose in a separate bucket filled with white vinegar or commercial descaling solution.
4. **Circulate the descaling solution through the heat exchanger** for at least 45 minutes using the pump, allowing the acid to dissolve lime scale and mineral deposits throughout the system.
5. **Drain the descaling solution completely**, then flush the heat exchanger with clean water for several minutes by running fresh water through the service ports to remove all residue.
6. **Close the service ports, remove hoses, and reopen the inlet and outlet isolation valves**, then restore power to the unit.
7. **Reset the LC code** by cycling power off and back on at the breaker, or follow the controller reset procedure in your model's manual if a simple power cycle does not clear the display.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or water heater technician if you are uncomfortable working with gas or electrical connections, if the LC code returns immediately after descaling, or if hot water output remains poor even after a thorough flush. A pro should also evaluate the unit if you see visible corrosion or leaks at the service ports, if the heat exchanger shows signs of permanent scale damage during inspection, or if your area has extremely hard water that may require a water softener installation to prevent recurring scale faults. Technicians have commercial-grade descaling equipment and can perform pressure and flow tests to confirm whether the heat exchanger has been restored or needs replacement.
