## State Water Heater E104 Error — What It Means

The E104 code is not a documented fault for State Water Heaters (a brand of A.O. Smith). The code you are likely seeing is A104, as E and A are often confused in display readings or user transcriptions for State/Rheem hybrid and electric units. A104 indicates either an upper heater temperature sensor (thermistor) failure or an emergency cut-off (ECO) trip. If the sensor has malfunctioned, the circuit may be open or shorted, which disables the heater. If the ECO has tripped, the water temperature exceeded safe limits and the high-temperature electrical cut-off device shut down the unit.

Display indicators help diagnose the issue. If the sensor reads –40°F, the circuit is open (broken wire or failed sensor). If it reads +250°F, the circuit is shorted (internal failure). The ECO trip occurs when a heating element or thermistor fails and causes the water to overheat beyond safe operating temperatures.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first. Before replacing any board, disconnect the upper thermistor and check its resistance with a multimeter against the manufacturer's Ohm chart for ambient temperature.

## Common Causes

- **Failed upper thermistor (~40%)** The upper temperature sensor has physically failed (open or shorted circuit), causing incorrect resistance readings and disabling the heater.
- **ECO trip from overheating (~30%)** The high-temperature cut-off device tripped because the water temperature exceeded safe limits, often due to a stuck heating element or failed sensor.
- **Loose or corroded wiring (~15%)** Corroded, disconnected, or loose connections at the sensor or control board interrupt the thermistor circuit and trigger the fault.
- **Shorted or stuck upper heating element (~10%)** A heating element that is shorted or stuck in the closed position causes continuous heating, triggering the ECO or sensor fault.
- **Failed ECO device (~5%)** The ECO reset button will not stay engaged or trips immediately after reset, indicating the ECO device itself has failed and needs replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and unplug the water heater to make sure safe access to electrical components.
2. **Remove the upper access panel** and locate the upper thermistor (a small sensor with two wires) and the ECO reset button if your model has one.
3. **Inspect all wiring** at the thermistor and control board for loose, corroded, or disconnected connections. Clean and reseat any suspect wires.
4. **Disconnect the thermistor** from the control board and use a multimeter set to Ohms to measure its resistance. Compare the reading to the manufacturer's Ohm chart for the current ambient temperature. If the reading is out of spec, infinite (open), or near-zero (shorted), replace the thermistor.
5. **Check the ECO status** by pressing the reset button. If it clicks and stays engaged, restore power and test. If it trips again immediately, the ECO is faulty or an underlying overheating issue (stuck element, bad sensor) is present.
6. **Test the upper heating element** by disconnecting its wires and measuring resistance with a multimeter. A shorted or stuck element will show abnormal resistance and must be replaced.
7. **Enter diagnostic mode** (Service > Sensors) on the display if available. Verify that the sensor now reads a normal temperature. If –40°F or +250°F persists, confirm sensor replacement is needed.
8. **Restore power** and monitor the unit for 24 hours. If the code returns, replace any remaining suspect components (ECO, element, or control board if all sensors test good).

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or electrician if you are not comfortable working with high-voltage wiring or if resistance testing and ECO reset do not resolve the fault. A technician can safely verify the control board, perform accurate Ohm checks against the manufacturer's chart, and replace sealed or hard-to-reach components like the ECO or thermistor. If the code returns after replacing the sensor and element, the control board itself may be faulty and should be diagnosed by a professional to avoid unnecessary parts replacement.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-300.
