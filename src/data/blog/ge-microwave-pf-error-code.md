## What this code means
PF on a GE microwave stands for power failure. The control system detected that incoming power was interrupted or dropped out. This code appears after a utility outage, a tripped breaker, the unit being unplugged, or a brief voltage interruption. It is not a failure of the microwave's cooking system.

The code displays until you acknowledge it at the keypad and restore stable power. In most cases, PF is cleared by pressing the Clear, Off, or Cancel button and then power-cycling the microwave. If PF returns repeatedly while house power is confirmed stable, the control board or control panel assembly may have developed an internal fault.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the control board when PF keeps appearing, but the real cause is often a loose plug, poor receptacle contact, or a shared circuit overload. Always verify the outlet is live and stable with a meter or another appliance before ordering a board.

## Common Causes

- **Actual power outage or momentary utility interruption (~50%)** The most common trigger is a brief loss of grid power or a voltage dip that the control detects and logs as PF.
- **Tripped breaker or blown household fuse (~20%)** The circuit breaker serving the microwave may have opened due to an overload or fault, cutting power and triggering the code.
- **Microwave unplugged or loose plug (~15%)** A cord pulled partway out of the receptacle or poor contact at the outlet interrupts power and sets the PF code.
- **Shared circuit overload (~10%)** Another appliance on the same branch circuit may have caused a momentary voltage drop or tripped the breaker, interrupting the microwave.
- **Control board or control panel assembly fault (~5%)** If PF returns repeatedly after stable power is verified, the control board or panel assembly may have an intermittent power-sensing issue.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the code** by pressing the Clear, Off, or Cancel button on the keypad to acknowledge the power-failure message.
2. **Unplug the microwave** or turn off its circuit breaker, wait at least 60 seconds, then restore power to complete a hard reset.
3. **Verify the outlet** by plugging in a lamp or another small appliance to confirm the receptacle is live and does not flicker or drop out.
4. **Check the branch circuit breaker** serving the microwave and reset it if it has tripped. Replace a blown fuse if your panel uses fuses and local code permits.
5. **Inspect the microwave plug** for a secure connection and examine the receptacle for any signs of scorching, loose terminals, or corrosion.
6. **Test the microwave** by running a brief cook cycle. If PF does not return and the unit heats normally, the problem is resolved.
7. **Replace the control board** if PF reappears immediately after power-cycling and you have confirmed the house circuit is stable. Follow safe disassembly practices and disconnect power before opening the cabinet.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if PF returns continuously after you have verified the outlet is live and stable, power-cycled the microwave, and confirmed the branch breaker is not tripping. Repeated PF codes with good house power usually point to a faulty control board or control panel assembly. Microwaves contain high-voltage capacitors that retain a lethal charge even when unplugged, so internal diagnostics and board replacement are best left to a trained service professional who has the discharge tools and safety training. Also call if you discover the outlet or branch circuit has loose or burnt wiring, since that work must be done by a licensed electrician to meet local electrical code.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $0-10 if clearing the code or resetting a breaker, $100-200 for a control board if needed, 15-45 min. A pro service call runs about $100-250 for diagnosis and control-board replacement if required.
