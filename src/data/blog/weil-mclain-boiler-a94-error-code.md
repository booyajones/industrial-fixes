## Weil-McLain A94 Error Code — What It Means

The A94 code on Weil-McLain CGi and CGa series boilers indicates a lockout or fault condition, typically tied to an ignition or lighting failure. The boiler attempted to start but could not establish or prove a flame, so the control locked out to prevent unsafe operation. Weil-McLain's diagnostics system logs the fault in the control's history, and the exact meaning of A94 can vary slightly by model. Always retrieve the fault history from the control menu and consult your specific boiler's manual to confirm the recorded fault and the manufacturer's corrective steps.

Common ignition-failure faults on these boilers stem from interrupted gas supply, a dirty or failed flame sensor that cannot prove ignition, or a bad ignitor that does not spark reliably. Because the control has locked out, the boiler will not retry until you address the underlying cause and manually reset. Do not reset repeatedly. One reset to observe startup is acceptable, but if the fault returns immediately call a qualified technician to diagnose gas flow, flame sensing, and ignition components.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board when A94 appears, but the real fault is usually a dirty flame sensor or closed gas shutoff. Check gas supply and clean the flame sensor before ordering any electronics.

## Common Causes

- **No gas flow or gas supply issue (~40%)** The manual gas valve is closed, the supply is interrupted upstream, or a component in the gas train is stuck, so no fuel reaches the burner during ignition.
- **Dirty or failed flame sensor (~30%)** Carbon or oxide buildup on the flame-sensing rod prevents the control from proving ignition, even when a flame is present.
- **Bad ignitor or spark electrode (~15%)** The ignitor does not produce a reliable spark, or the electrode is cracked or fouled, so the gas cannot light.
- **Gas valve or gas train component failure (~10%)** The gas valve itself is defective or the pressure regulator or other gas train parts are not opening, blocking fuel flow.
- **Control board or fault history issue (~5%)** The control has logged a fault in its history or the board itself has failed, preventing normal ignition even when all field components are working.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the boiler at the service switch or breaker and close the manual gas shutoff valve.
2. **Retrieve the fault history** from the control's diagnostics menu by following the keypad instructions in your model's manual to confirm the exact fault recorded under A94.
3. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve is fully open and other gas appliances in the building are working normally.
4. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor** by removing it from the burner assembly, wiping the sensing rod with fine sandpaper or a cloth to remove carbon or oxide, and reinstalling it snugly.
5. **Check the ignitor or spark electrode** for cracks, heavy fouling, or poor wiring connections, and replace it if damaged.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then reset the boiler once and observe the full startup sequence to see if the ignitor sparks, the burner lights, and the flame proves stable.
7. **If the fault returns immediately**, stop resetting and call a qualified HVAC technician to test gas valve operation, flame current, and control board diagnostics before replacing any major components.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC or boiler technician immediately if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances, if you cannot locate or safely access the flame sensor or ignitor, or if the fault returns after you have confirmed gas flow and cleaned the sensor. A pro will use a multimeter to measure flame current, test gas valve operation and inlet pressure, retrieve detailed fault codes from the control, and verify that the ignition sequence meets manufacturer timing specs. Because natural gas and propane are explosive, any work beyond confirming the manual shutoff is open and cleaning accessible sensors should be left to a qualified technician. If the control board itself is suspect, a pro will test all field wiring and components first to avoid replacing expensive electronics unnecessarily.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on the part and labor.
