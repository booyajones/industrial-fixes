## Frigidaire/LG Washer OE Error — What It Means

The OE code on LG and Frigidaire-branded front-load washers is a drain fault. The machine detects that it cannot remove wash water normally, so the tub is not emptying within the expected time. You will typically see standing water in the drum and the cycle will stop during drain or spin. Note that your query mentions a Frigidaire refrigerator, but OE is a washer code, not a refrigerator code. The research confirms OE is specific to LG front-load washers and means the washer is unable to drain the water used during the wash cycle.

The code does not mean the pump has failed outright. It means the control board does not see the expected empty-tub condition within its timeout window. The blockage or failure can be anywhere in the drain path, from the tub outlet to the house standpipe.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump motor first without checking the filter or hose. Always open the pump filter access panel and inspect for debris, then verify the drain hose is not kinked or clogged before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain pump filter (~40%)** Lint, hair, coins, and small items collect in the pump filter trap and block water flow, triggering OE during drain or spin.
- **Kinked or crushed drain hose (~25%)** The drain hose behind the washer is bent, pinched, or routed too low, preventing water from leaving the tub.
- **Drain pump obstruction or failed impeller (~20%)** Foreign objects jam the pump impeller, or the impeller blades break, so the motor runs but does not move water.
- **House drain or standpipe backup (~10%)** The household plumbing is slow or clogged, so discharged water backs up and the washer cannot complete the drain cycle.
- **Wiring or connector fault to the pump (~5%)** A loose harness connector or damaged wire prevents the control board from powering the drain pump motor.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and pull it away from the wall so you can access the rear panel and drain hose.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** for kinks, crushing, or clogs. Straighten any bends and make sure the hose is not inserted more than 4–5 inches into the standpipe.
3. **Open the pump filter access panel** at the lower front of the washer. Place a shallow pan and towels on the floor, pull out the emergency drain tube if equipped, and let residual water drain.
4. **Unscrew and remove the pump filter cap**. Clear out any lint, coins, hair, or debris from the filter housing and impeller cavity. Rinse the filter under running water.
5. **Reach into the filter opening** and rotate the pump impeller by hand to check for obstructions or broken blades. The impeller should spin freely without binding.
6. **Reinstall the filter cap** firmly, close the access panel, and reconnect the drain hose. Plug the washer back in and run a spin-only or drain test cycle.
7. **Listen for pump operation** at the start of drain. A brief hum is normal as the pump starts. If the pump runs but water remains, replace the drain pump assembly. If the pump does not run at all, check the wiring harness and connectors to the pump, then replace the pump motor if connections are secure.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you have cleared the filter, verified the hose is straight, confirmed the house drain is open, and the OE code still appears. A professional can test the pump motor for correct voltage and resistance, inspect the sump hose between the tub and pump for hidden clogs, and check the control board outputs. Also call if you are uncomfortable working with water near electrical connections or if the washer is still under warranty and you want factory-authorized service.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15–50 in parts (if pump needed), 30–60 min. A pro service call runs about $150–280 service call plus parts.
