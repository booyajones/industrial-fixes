## State Water Heater E85 Error Code — What It Means

State water heaters do not publish a manufacturer-specific definition for an E85 error code in widely available documentation. However, independent troubleshooting sources suggest that E85 on gas control systems (such as White Rodgers controllers found on some State models) indicates a flame-sensing or gas-valve lockout condition. This means the burner may light briefly but the gas control shuts down because it cannot reliably detect flame presence. The heater enters a lockout state and will not resume normal heating until the fault is cleared and the control is reset.

Because State offers many models with different control platforms, the exact meaning of E85 depends on your specific unit. Always verify the model and serial number on the rating plate and consult State's official manual for your model to confirm the fault definition and recommended corrective action.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame sensor or thermocouple** Buildup of soot, dust, or corrosion on the sensor prevents it from proving flame reliably to the gas control.
- **Failing flame sensor or thermocouple** The sensor itself has degraded and no longer generates or transmits the correct signal even when flame is present.
- **Loose or damaged wiring at the gas valve or ignition assembly** Poor connections interrupt the flame-sense signal and cause the control to interpret it as a flame-failure.
- **Dirty burner or improper combustion** Carbon deposits or debris in the burner chamber can produce weak or unstable flame that the sensor cannot detect consistently.
- **Low or irregular gas supply pressure** Insufficient gas pressure at startup prevents reliable ignition and stable flame, triggering a lockout.
- **Gas control valve lockout or internal fault** The control itself may have entered a persistent lockout state or have an internal defect requiring replacement.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the model and control type** by locating the rating plate or QR code on the water heater and downloading the correct manual from State's support site to confirm the E85 definition for your unit.
2. **Turn off the gas supply** at the manual shutoff valve upstream of the heater and switch off electrical power at the circuit breaker or disconnect.
3. **Remove the burner access cover** and inspect the burner compartment for dust, debris, loose wiring, or signs of corrosion on the flame sensor, thermocouple, and gas valve connections.
4. **Clean the flame sensor or thermocouple** gently using a clean cloth or fine abrasive pad to remove any soot or oxidation from the metal sensing rod.
5. **Check and tighten all wire connections** at the gas valve, ignition assembly, and sensor to eliminate intermittent contact.
6. **Reassemble the burner compartment**, restore gas and power, and attempt to relight the heater according to the lighting instructions on the unit's label.
7. **Observe one full heating cycle** to confirm the burner lights, stays lit, and the error does not return; if the fault reappears, verify gas supply pressure and consider replacing the flame sensor or gas control valve per the manual's parts list.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or gas-appliance technician if the E85 fault persists after cleaning the flame sensor and checking wiring, if you smell gas at any point, if you are uncomfortable working with gas appliances, or if troubleshooting reveals that the gas control valve or ignition system requires replacement. Combustion and gas-control problems carry safety risks including gas leaks and carbon monoxide hazards, so professional diagnosis and repair are warranted when simple cleaning does not resolve the issue. A technician can also measure gas supply pressure and verify proper venting, both of which are critical for safe operation.
