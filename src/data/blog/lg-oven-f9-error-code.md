## LG Oven F9 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 error on an LG oven indicates that the upper oven is not heating properly. LG instructs users to press CLEAR/OFF and retry the function. The control board expects the oven to heat during preheat, and when the temperature rise is too slow or absent, it throws F9. Third-party technical sources describe the typical threshold as the oven failing to reach about 150°F within 5 minutes, though LG does not publish this exact number.

On electric models, this fault usually points to a break in the heating circuit or its sensing and control path. On gas models, it often means the ignition or heating sequence is not working correctly. The fault can originate from a failed heating element, a weak igniter, a faulty temperature sensor, an open thermal fuse, loose or damaged relay connectors, or a failed control board.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first. Before ordering a board, visually inspect the heating element for cracks or burn marks and check the relay connector for heat damage or looseness. A simple continuity test of the element and sensor often identifies the real fault.

## Common Causes

- **Heating element failure (~35%)** The electric bake or broil element has an open coil, short, or visible crack, preventing current flow and heat output.
- **Loose or damaged relay connector (~25%)** Heat damage, corrosion, or a loose plug between the control board and the heating circuit interrupts power to the element.
- **Main control or relay board failure (~20%)** A failed relay on the control board or relay board prevents the oven from energizing the heating element or igniter.
- **Temperature sensor fault (~10%)** The oven sensor is open, shorted, or reading out of range, so the control does not see the temperature rise.
- **Thermal fuse open (~5%)** A blown thermal fuse in the heating circuit cuts off power to the element, stopping all heat.
- **Gas igniter weak or failing (~5%)** On gas models, a weak igniter does not draw enough current to open the gas valve, so the burner does not light.

## Quick Diagnosis

Answer these to narrow it down fast.

<details class="dtree"><summary>Does the heating element glow at all when you start a bake cycle?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> The element is receiving some power. Check the temperature sensor and verify the oven door closes fully. If the element glows but the oven still does not reach temperature, the sensor or control board is likely at fault.<br><strong>No:</strong> No glow means no power to the element. Inspect the element for visible damage, then check the relay connector and thermal fuse. If those are good, test the control board relay output.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>On a gas model, does the igniter glow bright white for at least 30 seconds?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> A bright glow that does not open the gas valve usually means the igniter is weak and needs replacement. Gas igniters often fail by drawing less than the 3.0–3.6 amps required to trip the safety valve.<br><strong>No:</strong> No glow means the igniter is not receiving power. Check the relay connector, thermal fuse, and control board output to the igniter circuit.</div>
</details>

<details class="dtree"><summary>After you clear the fault and power-cycle the range at the breaker, does F9 return immediately on the next bake attempt?</summary>
<div class="dtree-body"><strong>Yes:</strong> An immediate repeat points to a hard electrical fault in the heating circuit, sensor, or control board rather than a transient error.<br><strong>No:</strong> If F9 does not return, the fault may have been a one-time glitch or a loose connector that reseated during the power cycle. Monitor the oven over the next few uses.</div>
</details>

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Clear the fault** by pressing CLEAR/OFF on the control panel, then turn off the range circuit breaker for about 5 minutes and turn it back on to reset the control board.
2. **Retry a normal Bake cycle** in the upper oven and watch whether the heating element (electric) or igniter (gas) energizes and whether the oven begins to warm.
3. **Inspect the heating element** (electric models) for visible cracks, blisters, burn marks, or separated coils. If damage is present, the element must be replaced.
4. **Check the igniter** (gas models) to confirm it glows bright white and that the burner lights within about 30 seconds. A weak glow or delayed ignition indicates a failing igniter.
5. **Unplug power and remove the rear access panel** to inspect the relay board and wiring harness. Look for loose connectors, heat damage, or corrosion at the relay board plug and element terminals.
6. **Test the temperature sensor** for continuity and resistance at room temperature. Consult your model's service manual for the expected resistance range. Many LG-style sensors measure around 1,080–1,100 ohms at room temperature, but verify your model.
7. **Check the thermal fuse** for continuity. If the fuse is open, replace it and investigate what caused it to blow (often a failed element or relay connector).
8. **Replace the control board or relay board** if all other components test good but the oven still does not heat. Verify the board part number against your model before ordering.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with live 240V circuits, if you have a gas model and suspect a gas valve or burner assembly fault, or if you have already replaced the heating element and sensor but F9 persists. A technician can perform voltage and current measurements on the control board, verify the relay operation under load, and diagnose intermittent wiring faults that are difficult to trace without a schematic and meter. If the range is still under warranty, contact LG or an authorized service center before opening the unit.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50–150 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–350.
