## LG Oven F9 Error Code — What It Means

The F9 code on an LG oven or range means the upper oven is not heating properly. Specifically, the control board expects the oven to reach about 150°F within roughly five minutes of starting preheat. If the temperature does not rise fast enough, the board sets F9 and stops the cycle.

On electric models this usually points to a failed bake element, bad relay board, or faulty temperature sensor. On gas models a weak or failed igniter is the most common cause, since the igniter must draw enough current to open the gas valve and light the burner. Wiring faults, blown thermal fuses, and control-board failures are also possible.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the main control board first, but the real culprit is usually the bake element, igniter, relay board, or temperature sensor. Test the element for continuity and inspect for cracks or burn marks before swapping the main board.

## Common Causes

- **Faulty bake element (electric) (~35%)** The element develops cracks, blisters, or breaks internally so it cannot heat the oven within the five-minute window.
- **Weak or failed igniter (gas) (~30%)** The igniter glows but does not draw enough current to open the gas valve, so the burner never lights and the oven stays cold.
- **Defective relay board or relay connector (~15%)** The relay that switches power to the element or gas valve fails or has burnt terminals, cutting the heating circuit.
- **Faulty temperature sensor or sensor wiring (~10%)** The sensor reads incorrectly or an open wire makes the control think the oven is not warming, even if heat is present.
- **Blown thermal fuse (~5%)** A thermal protection device opens the circuit if the oven overheats, preventing any further heating and triggering F9.
- **Failed main control board (~5%)** The control board itself develops a fault and cannot read temperature or switch the relay, though this is less common than element or igniter failures.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Press CLEAR/OFF** and turn off the circuit breaker or unplug the range for three to five minutes, then restore power to reset the control.
2. **Run a Bake cycle** and observe whether the oven heats and whether the code returns immediately or after a few minutes.
3. **Inspect the bake element** (electric) for cracks, blisters, burn marks, or broken terminals, or watch the igniter (gas) to confirm it glows brightly and the burner lights.
4. **Check wiring and connectors** at the rear control compartment, relay board, and heating circuit for burnt terminals, loose connections, or damaged harnesses.
5. **Test the temperature sensor** with a multimeter for continuity and the expected resistance (typically 1,080–1,100 Ω at room temperature), and check the thermal fuse for continuity.
6. **Replace the failed component** (element, igniter, sensor, relay board, or fuse), restore power, and run a full preheat cycle to verify the oven reaches set temperature without re-setting F9.
7. **If all components test good** but the oven still does not heat, suspect the main control board and consider calling a technician for further diagnosis.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are uncomfortable working inside the range or around 240-volt wiring. On gas models, if you smell gas, hear hissing, or are unsure how to safely disconnect the gas supply, stop and call for service. Also call a pro if you have replaced the element or igniter, tested the sensor and relay board, and the code still appears, since the main control board or a hidden wiring fault will require advanced diagnostics and tools.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $30–120 in parts, 30–90 min. A pro service call runs about $150–300.
