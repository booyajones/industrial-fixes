## Weil-McLain Boiler A92 Error Code — What It Means

The A92 code on a Weil-McLain boiler indicates a fault history or lockout entry stored in the control's diagnostics menu. The exact meaning of A92 is not universal across all Weil-McLain models and must be verified in your specific model's installation or service manual. The code tells you the boiler has shut down and logged a fault, but the underlying problem can range from ignition failure to a sensor trip or low water condition.

Because Weil-McLain uses different control platforms across its product line, the same code number can point to different faults depending on whether you have an Ultra, CGa, EG, or another series. Technicians access the fault-history menu on the control to see the stored event, then cross-reference the code in the manual to identify the actual component or condition that triggered the lockout. Common real-world causes include failed ignition sequence, dirty or faulty flame sensor, low system pressure, bad ignitor, or a tripped temperature or limit sensor.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first when the real fault is a dirty flame sensor or low system pressure. Check pressure gauge and clean the flame sensor before ordering any board.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or faulty flame sensor (~30%)** Carbon buildup on the flame sensor prevents it from detecting flame, causing the boiler to lock out after repeated ignition attempts.
- **Failed hot-surface ignitor (~25%)** A cracked or burned-out ignitor cannot light the gas, and the control logs an ignition-failure fault.
- **Low system pressure or water level (~20%)** Pressure below the safe operating range (often under 12 psi) or a tripped low-water cutoff will lock out the boiler and store a fault.
- **Gas supply problem (~15%)** Closed valve, empty propane tank, or low gas pressure prevents ignition and triggers a lockout code.
- **Failed temperature or limit sensor (~10%)** A shorted or open thermistor or high-limit switch falsely signals an unsafe condition and stops the boiler.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your boiler model** and locate the installation or service manual. The exact meaning of A92 is model-specific and must be confirmed in the manual's fault-code table.
2. **Access the fault-history menu** on the boiler control display. Navigate using the buttons or knob to see stored codes and verify that A92 is the current or most recent fault.
3. **Check system pressure** on the gauge. If pressure is below 12 psi, slowly add water through the fill valve until the gauge reads 12–15 psi when cold. Never exceed 25 psi.
4. **Verify gas supply** by confirming the manual gas valve upstream of the boiler is fully open and, if propane, that the tank has fuel. Listen for the gas valve clicking when the boiler calls for heat.
5. **Inspect the ignitor and flame sensor.** Remove the ignitor and flame sensor from the burner assembly. Clean the flame sensor rod with fine steel wool or emery cloth. Replace the ignitor if it is cracked, broken, or does not glow.
6. **Check circulator operation and flow.** Make sure the circulator pump is running and not air-locked. Bleed air from the system if needed. Verify no zone valves are stuck closed.
7. **Test temperature and limit sensors** with a multimeter if you have the service manual resistance tables. Replace any sensor that reads open, shorted, or far outside the expected range for the current boiler temperature.
8. **Reset the boiler** only after the root cause is corrected. Turn off power, wait 30 seconds, restore power, and press the reset button once. Do not reset repeatedly without fixing the fault.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional immediately if you smell gas, if the pressure gauge is above 25 psi, or if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances and 120V wiring. A licensed technician should diagnose any A92 code when the exact fault cannot be determined from the manual, when repeated resets do not clear the lockout, or when testing sensors and valves requires a multimeter and wiring diagrams. Gas-valve replacement, control-board work, and any repair inside the sealed combustion chamber or venting system must be handled by a qualified HVAC or boiler service company. If the boiler is under warranty, professional service may be required to preserve coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150–350 depending on part and labor.
