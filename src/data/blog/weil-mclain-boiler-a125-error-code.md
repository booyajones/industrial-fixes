## Weil-McLain A125 Error Code — What It Means

A125 on a Weil-McLain boiler is an ignition lockout. The boiler called for heat, started its ignition sequence, and did not establish or prove flame. The control then locked out to prevent unsafe operation. This is a failed-to-light fault, not a sensor error or routine shutdown.

Because Weil-McLain uses different control platforms across model families, verify the exact meaning of A125 in your specific model's installation and service manual. The code consistently points to a problem in the ignition chain: no gas reaching the burner, a weak or dirty ignitor, a fouled flame sensor, or a gas valve that does not open on call.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board or flame sensor first. Inspect and test the ignitor and verify gas supply before buying any control or sensor.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty, cracked, or failed ignitor (~35%)** The hot-surface or spark ignitor may be contaminated with soot, carbon, or corrosion, or it may have cracked ceramic, preventing reliable spark or glow and stopping ignition.
- **No gas supply or closed shutoff (~25%)** The manual gas cock at the boiler may be off, the building gas service may be interrupted, or the gas pressure may be too low to support ignition.
- **Dirty or failing flame sensor (~20%)** The flame rod or flame-sensing electrode may be coated with soot or oxidation, so even if flame is present the control does not prove it and locks out.
- **Gas valve not opening (~15%)** The gas valve may fail to receive its call signal from the control or may be mechanically stuck, blocking gas flow during trial-for-ignition.
- **Blower or venting problem (~5%)** If the inducer does not spin up correctly or the vent is blocked, the control may abort ignition before gas is allowed, triggering a lockout.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify your model number and pull the service manual.** Weil-McLain directs technicians to use the model-specific installation and service instructions for diagnostic codes, because control platforms vary by series.
2. **Confirm gas supply.** Open the manual gas shutoff at the boiler fully. Check that your building's main gas service is on and other gas appliances light normally. If you smell gas or suspect a leak, leave the building and call your utility or fire department.
3. **Power-cycle the boiler** by switching off the electrical disconnect or breaker for thirty seconds, then switching back on. Observe one ignition sequence. Note whether the inducer starts, whether you hear spark or see ignitor glow, and whether the code returns immediately.
4. **Inspect the ignitor.** With power off and gas off, remove the ignitor from the burner box. Look for cracks in the ceramic, heavy soot buildup, or corrosion on the electrodes. Clean gently with a soft brush or replace if damaged.
5. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor.** The flame rod or sensing electrode sits in the flame path. Remove it, wipe it with fine steel wool or emery cloth to remove oxide and carbon, and reinstall it in the correct position per the manual.
6. **Check gas-valve operation.** With the boiler calling for heat, verify that the gas valve receives voltage at its terminals during trial-for-ignition. If voltage is present but the valve does not click open, the valve coil or body is likely failed.
7. **Review the control's fault history.** Many Weil-McLain controls store a log of past lockouts in the diagnostic menu. Repeated A125 codes with no other faults confirm an ignition or gas issue. Mixed codes suggest control or wiring problems.
8. **Test fire after repairs.** Restore gas and power, initiate a call for heat, and confirm the boiler lights and runs through a full cycle without lockout. Do not leave the building until you verify stable operation.

## When to Call a Pro

A125 is a gas-ignition fault that requires working with live gas piping, electrical ignition circuits, and combustion safety. If your free checks do not solve the problem, call a licensed boiler technician. The technician will test gas pressure at the valve inlet, measure ignitor resistance and voltage, verify flame-sensor microamp current, and check the control's call sequence with a multimeter. Repeatedly resetting the lockout without finding the root cause can lead to dangerous conditions, including gas buildup or cracked heat exchangers. Professional diagnosis is the safe and correct path for any persistent ignition lockout.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
