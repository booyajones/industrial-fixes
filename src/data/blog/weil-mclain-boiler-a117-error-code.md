## Weil-McLain A117 Error Code — What It Means

The A117 code is not universally defined across all Weil-McLain boiler models. Weil-McLain uses different control platforms and fault code sets depending on the series (Ultra, Eco, CGa, etc.), so the exact meaning of A117 must be looked up in your specific model's service manual or fault history menu. Without identifying your boiler model and control family, you cannot reliably know what A117 indicates.

Because Weil-McLain documentation shows that fault codes are model-specific, the safe first step is to confirm your boiler model from the nameplate, then access the fault history on the control display and match the stored fault to the troubleshooting table in that model's manual. Many Weil-McLain lockout and ignition-related faults share common root causes such as dirty flame sensors, ignition failures, weak gas supply, blocked venting, or low system pressure.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the gas valve or ignition control board before checking the flame sensor or gas supply. Clean the flame rod and verify inlet gas pressure first, since contamination and low pressure cause most ignition lockouts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or contaminated flame sensor (~30%)** Carbon buildup or condensate residue on the flame rod prevents the control from proving flame, causing ignition lockout.
- **Ignition failure or unstable flame (~25%)** Weak spark, bad ignitor, or burner not lighting properly leads to repeated ignition attempts and shutdown.
- **Weak or incorrect gas supply pressure (~20%)** Low inlet gas pressure or a partially closed manual shutoff valve starves the burner and prevents stable combustion.
- **Blocked vent or intake air restriction (~15%)** Obstructed flue pipe, frozen condensate trap, or intake screen blockage causes combustion to fail or the boiler to lock out on safety.
- **Low system pressure or air in the system (~10%)** Pressure below 12 psi or trapped air in the heating loop can trigger low-water cutoff or circulation faults that shut the boiler down.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Identify your boiler model and control family** by reading the nameplate on the front or side panel and noting the series (Ultra, Eco, CGa, etc.).
2. **Access the fault history menu** on the control display using the instructions in your manual, write down all stored fault codes, and match them to the troubleshooting table for your model.
3. **Check system pressure** on the gauge (typically 12-25 psi on residential hydronic boilers) and add water if low, then bleed air from the system.
4. **Inspect the flame sensor or flame rod** for carbon buildup or corrosion, remove it following your manual's instructions, and clean it gently with fine sandpaper or a soft cloth.
5. **Verify gas supply and inlet pressure** by confirming that the manual gas shutoff valve is fully open and that the meter is delivering gas to other appliances, then call a technician to measure inlet pressure if ignition is still failing.
6. **Check vent and intake piping** on condensing models for blockage, ice, or debris at the termination, and inspect the condensate trap and drain line for clogs or freeze-up.
7. **Reset the boiler** using the control's reset button or power cycle only after you have addressed the likely cause, then monitor for stable operation and re-check fault history if the code returns.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified heating technician immediately if you cannot find the A117 code in your model's manual, if the boiler is locked out and will not reset, or if you are not comfortable working with gas appliances. A technician can pull the fault history, test gas pressure, verify combustion air and venting, and safely diagnose ignition or control faults. Weil-McLain recommends that all service and warranty work be performed by a qualified installer or service technician, and any work involving gas supply, venting, or sealed combustion components requires professional training and tools to complete safely.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
