## Navien E170 Error Code — What It Means

The E170 error code on Navien tankless water heaters typically indicates a problem with flame sensing or detection during the combustion cycle. The unit starts the ignition sequence but either cannot confirm a stable flame or detects an intermittent or unstable flame signal. This triggers a safety lockout to prevent unsafe operation.

The code may appear during startup or while the unit is running. Because flame sensing relies on a small electrical current passing through the flame to a sensor rod, anything that disrupts this signal path (dirt, corrosion, poor grounding, or a faulty sensor) can trigger the fault. The error protects against gas flow without proper ignition.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the igniter or gas valve when the real problem is simply a dirty flame rod or loose ground wire. Inspect and clean the flame rod and check all ground connections before ordering parts.

## Common Causes

- **Dirty or corroded flame rod (~50%)** Carbon deposits or mineral scale on the flame rod insulate it and prevent the sensor from detecting current through the flame.
- **Loose or corroded ground connection (~25%)** The flame sensing circuit requires a solid ground path, and a loose chassis ground or corroded terminal will interrupt the signal.
- **Failed flame rod or igniter assembly (~15%)** Over time the flame rod itself or its ceramic insulator can crack or fail, preventing reliable flame detection.
- **Weak or inconsistent gas supply (~7%)** Low inlet gas pressure or a partially closed valve can produce an unstable flame that the sensor reads as intermittent.
- **Faulty control board (~3%)** The main PCB reads the flame signal, and a damaged board or corroded connector can misinterpret a good flame as absent.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off electrical power** at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve supplying the water heater.
2. **Remove the front cover panel** by unscrewing the fasteners, then locate the burner assembly and flame rod inside the combustion chamber.
3. **Inspect the flame rod** for soot, carbon buildup, or white mineral deposits; if present, gently clean the rod with fine-grit sandpaper or a non-abrasive pad and wipe dry.
4. **Check the ground wire** attached to the unit's chassis; tighten the screw or bolt if loose, and sand any corrosion from the contact surfaces.
5. **Inspect the flame rod wire connector** for cracks, corrosion, or looseness, and reseat or clean the terminals as needed.
6. **Reassemble the unit**, restore gas and power, then initiate a hot-water call to test; monitor for stable ignition and absence of the E170 code.
7. **If the error persists**, measure inlet gas pressure with a manometer to confirm it meets the unit's specification, and consider replacing the flame rod or calling a qualified technician to test the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you are uncomfortable working around gas appliances, if cleaning the flame rod and checking grounds does not resolve the error, or if you suspect low gas pressure or a faulty control board. Professionals have calibrated manometers to measure gas pressure, multimeters to test flame current, and the training to safely diagnose ignition and combustion issues. Any work involving gas line connections or internal burner components should be performed by someone with the proper certifications and insurance.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-350.
