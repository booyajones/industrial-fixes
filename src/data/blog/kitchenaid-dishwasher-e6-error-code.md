## KitchenAid Dishwasher E6 Error Code — What It Means

The E6 error code on KitchenAid dishwashers typically indicates a problem with the water heating system or temperature sensing circuit. The exact meaning can vary slightly by model, so consult your owner's manual or the wiring diagram on the dishwasher door for model-specific information.

This code usually appears when the control board detects that water is not reaching the expected temperature within the programmed time, or when the thermistor (temperature sensor) sends an out-of-range signal. The dishwasher may stop mid-cycle or fail to start when this fault is present.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the control board first, but a simple continuity test of the heating element and resistance check of the thermistor will identify the actual failed component in most cases.

## Common Causes

- **Failed heating element (~40%)** The heating element develops a break in the coil or corrodes through, preventing water from heating and triggering the error.
- **Faulty thermistor (~30%)** The temperature sensor drifts out of specification or fails open or shorted, sending incorrect temperature readings to the control board.
- **Loose or corroded wiring connections (~15%)** Connectors at the heating element or thermistor become oxidized or vibrate loose, interrupting the circuit intermittently.
- **Control board failure (~10%)** The main control board develops a fault in the heating circuit relay or temperature input, though this is less common than component failures.
- **Low water fill level (~5%)** Insufficient water in the tub can cause the heating element to overheat or fail to heat properly, triggering a temperature fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** at the circuit breaker and shut off the water supply to the dishwasher before beginning any work.
2. **Remove the lower access panel** or kick plate at the front base of the dishwasher to expose the heating element and wiring.
3. **Disconnect the heating element terminals** and use a multimeter set to ohms to test for continuity (consult your model's documentation for the expected resistance range).
4. **Locate the thermistor**, usually clipped to the side of the sump or attached near the heating element, and disconnect its wire connector.
5. **Test the thermistor resistance** at room temperature with a multimeter and compare to your model's specification (typically tens of thousands of ohms).
6. **Replace the failed component** (heating element or thermistor) by removing mounting hardware and transferring connectors to the new part.
7. **Reassemble all panels**, restore power and water, and run a test cycle to verify the error code does not return and the dishwasher heats properly.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are uncomfortable working with 120-volt appliance wiring or if you have tested both the heating element and thermistor and found them within specification. A technician can perform advanced diagnostics on the control board and wiring harness, and has access to model-specific service bulletins. Also call a pro if the dishwasher is still under warranty, as DIY repairs may void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $50-120 in parts, 45-90 min. A pro service call runs about $180-320.
