## Samsung Refrigerator Freezing Food — What's Happening

When your Samsung refrigerator freezes food in the fresh-food compartment, the cabinet is running colder than the setpoint you selected. This is a symptom, not a fault code. The refrigerator's control system is either misreading the actual temperature, receiving incorrect sensor data, or experiencing an airflow problem that creates cold spots.

Samsung's guidance states that most freezing complaints come from incorrect temperature settings, blocked vents, or high-moisture foods placed near cold surfaces. If those factors are ruled out, the root cause is typically a defective temperature sensor (thermistor) in the fresh-food section or a related control issue.

## Most Likely Causes

- **Faulty refrigerator compartment thermistor** The temperature sensor reads warmer than actual, so the control board overcools the cabinet trying to reach setpoint.
- **Temperature set too low** The refrigerator compartment dial or digital setting is below the recommended 3 °C to 5 °C range.
- **Blocked air vents or poor airflow** Items packed against the back wall or vents create cold spots and prevent even air distribution.
- **High-moisture foods near cold surfaces** Produce or liquids placed directly against vents or the back wall freeze faster than other items.
- **Evaporator or air passage icing** Ice buildup disrupts airflow and can cause localized overcooling in parts of the fresh-food compartment.
- **Ambient installation conditions too cold** A refrigerator installed in an unheated garage or cold room runs colder than designed.
- **Dirty condenser coils** Reduced efficiency can contribute to erratic cooling behavior and uneven temperatures.

## How to Diagnose and Fix {#fix}

1. Confirm the complaint by checking which foods are freezing and where they sit in the compartment.
2. Check the refrigerator compartment temperature setting and reset it to 3 °C to 5 °C if needed, then allow 24 hours for stabilization.
3. Inspect the interior for blocked vents, especially along the back wall, and rearrange items to restore airflow.
4. Verify the installation environment is within operating range and the door gaskets seal properly.
5. Look for ice buildup on the evaporator or in air passages, and fully defrost the unit if present.
6. Access the evaporator cover and locate the refrigerator compartment thermistor (usually clipped to the evaporator or its tubing).
7. Test the thermistor for out-of-spec resistance or visible damage to the sensor or its wiring.
8. Replace the thermistor if faulty, reassemble, and verify the compartment holds the correct setpoint without freezing food.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a technician if you are not comfortable accessing the evaporator cover, handling sensor wiring, or testing electrical components. If the thermistor tests good and the problem persists, the main control board or sealed-system issue may need professional diagnosis. A pro can also handle refrigerant or compressor faults that are beyond the scope of sensor and airflow repairs.
