## Electrolux Washer E03 Error Code — What It Means

The E03 error code on Electrolux washers typically indicates a draining problem. The washer is detecting that water is not leaving the drum within the expected time, or the pressure sensor is not registering the correct water level change during the drain cycle. This can stem from a physical blockage preventing water flow, a failed pump unable to move water out, or a faulty sensor that cannot accurately detect water level.

Because Electrolux models vary and some error codes differ by product line, always consult your specific model's service manual or wiring diagram to confirm the exact meaning. On most front-load and some top-load Electrolux washers, E03 points to drain circuit faults, but the exact threshold and components monitored can differ.

## Before You Replace Anything

Many people replace the drain pump when the real problem is a sock, coin, or lint buildup in the filter or pump housing. Always remove and clean the drain filter and inspect the pump impeller for debris before ordering a new pump.

## Common Causes

- **Clogged drain filter or pump housing (~50%)** Lint, coins, hairpins, and small garments collect in the filter and impeller cavity, blocking water flow and triggering the error.
- **Kinked or clogged drain hose (~20%)** A bent hose or buildup of soap residue and lint inside the hose restricts drainage and causes the pressure sensor to time out.
- **Failed drain pump (~15%)** The pump motor or impeller may be broken, preventing it from moving water even when the circuit is energized.
- **Faulty pressure sensor or air trap (~10%)** The pressure switch or its connecting air hose can become clogged or damaged, sending incorrect water-level signals to the control board.
- **Control board or wiring fault (~5%)** Loose connectors, corroded terminals, or a failed relay on the main control can prevent the drain pump from running or misread sensor inputs.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Unplug the washer** and turn off the water supply valves to eliminate electrical and water hazards.
2. **Locate and open the drain filter access door** at the lower front of the cabinet (consult your model's manual for the exact position).
3. **Place a shallow pan and towels** below the filter to catch residual water, then slowly unscrew the filter counterclockwise and pull it out.
4. **Clean the filter thoroughly** under running water, removing lint, coins, hairpins, and other debris, then inspect the pump housing cavity with a flashlight for trapped objects.
5. **Check the drain hose** by disconnecting it at the back of the washer and inspecting both the hose interior and the standpipe or drain connection for clogs or kinks.
6. **Reinstall the filter** by screwing it clockwise until snug, close the access door, reconnect the drain hose, and restore power and water.
7. **Run a drain or spin cycle** to verify the error clears. If the code persists and the filter was clean, test the drain pump for continuity and replace it if open, or inspect the pressure sensor air trap and hose for blockage.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you have cleaned the filter and hose, tested the drain pump for continuity and found it good, yet the error remains. The fault may lie in the pressure sensor, its air-trap system, or the main control board, all of which require multimeter diagnostics and sometimes board-level repair. Also bring in a technician if you are uncomfortable working with electrical connections or if the washer is still under warranty, since owner repairs can void coverage.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-80 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $120-250.
