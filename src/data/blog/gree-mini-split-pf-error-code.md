## Gree Mini-Split PF Error Code — What It Means

The PF error on a Gree mini-split stands for power fault or low-voltage protection. The system has detected that the supply voltage is below the safe threshold or the DC-bus power supply is abnormal. This is a protective shutdown to prevent damage to the compressor and electronics. The unit will not run until the underlying voltage or power supply problem is corrected.

## Common Causes

- **Low incoming line voltage** The voltage at your panel or at the unit is below specification, often due to a brownout or weak utility supply.
- **Loose or corroded electrical connections** Poor contact at the terminal block, disconnect, or board connectors creates resistance and voltage drop under load.
- **Undersized or damaged wiring** Wire that is too small, damaged, or has high resistance causes excessive voltage drop between the panel and the unit.
- **Weak DC bus capacitors or power supply components** Failed capacitors or power supply circuits on the control board reduce bus voltage and trip the protection.
- **Faulty drive board or IPM module** A damaged inverter board or integrated power module can cause abnormal power supply readings and trigger the fault.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Measure the supply voltage** at the disconnect and at both indoor and outdoor terminal blocks while the system is running or attempting to start. Compare readings to your model's voltage specification.
2. **Inspect all power connections** at the indoor unit, outdoor unit, and disconnect for loose screws, oxidized terminals, overheated spades, or damaged wire insulation.
3. **Check for voltage drop** by measuring voltage at the panel, then again at the unit under load. A significant difference points to wiring, breaker, or connection problems.
4. **Tighten all terminal screws** and clean any corroded or oxidized contacts. Replace any burned, discolored, or damaged wire lugs or connectors.
5. **Power-cycle the system** after correcting wiring or voltage issues. Turn off the breaker for two minutes, then restore power and test.
6. **Test the control board power supply** if line voltage and wiring are good. Look for stable DC bus voltage and confirm the 15 V supply on the drive board is above 13.5 V.
7. **Replace the faulty component** (drive board, IPM module, or DC bus capacitor) and verify the system runs normally under load without the fault returning.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed HVAC technician if you are not comfortable working with live electrical circuits, if you do not own a multimeter, or if the fault returns after correcting wiring and connections. Board-level diagnosis and replacement of the drive board, IPM module, or power supply components require specialized tools, recovery of refrigerant in some cases, and familiarity with inverter electronics. A pro can also verify that your electrical service and wire sizing meet code and manufacturer requirements for your specific model.
