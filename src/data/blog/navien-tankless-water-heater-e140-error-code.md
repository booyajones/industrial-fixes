## Navien E140 Error Code — What It Means

The E140 error code on a Navien tankless water heater signals that the unit has detected abnormally high exhaust temperatures. This safety mechanism protects the system from damage due to overheating. The control board monitors exhaust temperature and triggers the code when readings exceed safe operating limits.

The error typically results from restricted airflow through the venting system or combustion chamber. When exhaust gases cannot vent properly, heat builds up and the temperature sensor responds. The heater will lock out until the fault is corrected and the system is reset.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners sometimes replace the exhaust temperature sensor when the real problem is a simple blockage in the vent pipe. Inspect all venting for obstructions, corrosion, or improper slope before ordering any sensors.

## Common Causes

- **Blocked or restricted exhaust vent (~45%)** Bird nests, ice, lint, or debris in the vent pipe prevent hot exhaust gases from escaping, causing heat to build up inside the unit.
- **Improper vent installation (~25%)** Vent piping that is too long, has too many elbows, slopes incorrectly, or uses wrong diameter pipe restricts airflow and traps heat.
- **Condensate trap or drain clogged (~15%)** A plugged condensate drain causes water to back up into the heat exchanger or vent, obstructing exhaust flow and raising temperatures.
- **Failing exhaust temperature sensor (~10%)** The sensor itself may give false high-temperature readings even when exhaust is normal, though this is less common than true blockages.
- **Heat exchanger scale or debris buildup (~5%)** Mineral deposits or sediment inside the heat exchanger reduce heat transfer efficiency, forcing the burner to run hotter and raising exhaust temperature.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Turn off power** to the water heater at the circuit breaker and close the gas shutoff valve.
2. **Inspect the outdoor exhaust vent** termination for blockages such as bird nests, ice, leaves, or lint; remove any obstructions you can safely reach.
3. **Check the intake vent** termination as well, since restricted intake air can also disrupt combustion and raise exhaust temperature.
4. **Examine all accessible vent piping** indoors for proper slope (typically quarter-inch per foot toward the heater), sagging sections, or corrosion that narrows the passage.
5. **Locate and inspect the condensate drain** line and trap; clear any clogs and confirm water flows freely when you pour a cup of water into the trap.
6. **Restore power and gas**, then reset the heater according to the owner's manual and observe whether the code returns during the next heating cycle.
7. **If the error persists** after clearing obstructions and verifying vent integrity, call a qualified technician to test the exhaust temperature sensor, inspect the heat exchanger for scale, and measure combustion airflow with a manometer.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed technician if you cannot safely reach the outdoor vent terminations, if the vent piping runs through walls or ceilings you cannot inspect, or if the error returns after you have cleared all visible blockages. Tankless water heaters involve combustion gases and require precise venting to operate safely. A technician can measure combustion efficiency, verify correct vent sizing and slope, descale the heat exchanger with a flushing pump, and test or replace the exhaust temperature sensor. Any work on gas connections, burner assembly, or internal sensors is best left to a professional to avoid carbon monoxide hazards and to preserve warranty coverage.

**Rough cost:** A pro service call runs about $150-400.
