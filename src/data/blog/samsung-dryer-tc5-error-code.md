## Samsung Dryer tC5 Error Code — What It Means

The tC5 code on a Samsung dryer indicates a temperature-sensing or thermal fault in the exhaust or heater circuit. The dryer has detected either abnormal heat, failed temperature feedback from the thermistor, or a protection device opening in the sensing and safety path. Samsung service guidance groups tC5 with other codes (tC, tS, t5, 1tCS) under hotter-than-usual dryer temperature or malfunctioning temperature sensors.

In practical terms, the control board is reporting that it cannot get reliable temperature readings or that the dryer is running too hot. This can be a real overheating event (often caused by poor airflow) or a sensor that is reading out of range or open-circuit. The dryer will shut down to prevent damage or fire.

## Before You Replace Anything

Homeowners often replace the control board first. Before ordering a board, clean the entire vent path and test the exhaust thermistor with a meter. A thermistor reading far from 10 kΩ at room temperature (or open/shorted) points to a sensor, not a board.

## Common Causes

- **Restricted airflow or vent blockage (~50%)** Lint buildup in the screen, crushed or kinked vent hose, clogged exterior hood, or excessively long vent runs prevent heat from escaping and trigger overtemperature protection.
- **Failing exhaust thermistor (~25%)** The temperature sensor reads out of range (shorted, open, or drifted resistance) and the control board cannot determine safe operating temperature.
- **High-limit thermostat or thermal cutoff open (~15%)** A safety device has tripped because the dryer actually overheated, often due to airflow restriction or internal lint buildup around the heater housing.
- **Wiring or connector problem in the sensor circuit (~7%)** Loose, corroded, or heat-damaged connectors and harnesses create intermittent opens or high resistance that the board reads as a sensor fault.
- **Control board fault (~3%)** A stuck relay, failed sensor input, or board-level component failure misinterprets sensor signals even when airflow and sensors are good.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Disconnect power** by unplugging the dryer or switching off the circuit breaker. Wait two minutes, then restore power and test to see if the code clears temporarily.
2. **Clean the lint screen** completely and inspect the entire vent hose from the dryer to the exterior hood. Look for crushing, kinks, excessive length, or blockages. Remove and vacuum the vent line if needed.
3. **Check exterior airflow** by running a heat cycle and feeling the exterior vent hood. Strong, hot airflow confirms the path is clear. Weak or no flow points to a vent restriction.
4. **Open the dryer cabinet** (unplug first) and inspect the internal air path, especially around the heater housing and blower wheel, for lint accumulation. Vacuum and clean thoroughly.
5. **Test the exhaust thermistor** by disconnecting its harness and measuring resistance across the sensor terminals. Field guidance commonly expects about 10 kΩ at room temperature, but consult your model's wiring diagram for the exact specification.
6. **Check the high-limit thermostat or thermal cutoff** for continuity with a meter. An open reading means the safety device tripped due to overheating. Investigate the root cause (usually airflow) and replace the failed part.
7. **Inspect all wiring and connectors** in the temperature-sensor circuit for looseness, heat damage, corrosion, or intermittent contact. Repair or replace damaged harnesses.
8. **Reassemble and test** the dryer on a timed dry cycle. Monitor for normal operation and verify the code does not return. If the code persists after airflow and sensor repairs, evaluate the control board.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a professional if you are not comfortable opening the dryer cabinet, working with 240 V wiring, or using a multimeter to test sensors and thermostats. A technician should also diagnose the fault if you have cleaned the vent, verified strong exterior airflow, and replaced the thermistor but the code returns. Control board diagnosis and replacement require experience to avoid misdiagnosis, and a pro can cross-check sensor inputs against the schematic to confirm the board is at fault rather than an overlooked wiring or thermal issue.

**Rough cost:** DIY runs about $15-50 in parts, 30-90 min. A pro service call runs about $150-300.
