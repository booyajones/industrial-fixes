## Mitsubishi AL-29 Error Code — What It Means

AL-29 does not appear in Mitsubishi Electric heat pump documentation as a standard error code. Mitsubishi systems typically display two-digit numeric codes on the remote controller or indoor unit LED. The code may be misread, model-specific, or displayed in a non-standard format. If your system is showing a communication or control fault, Mitsubishi troubleshooting starts with verifying the exact code from the unit's error history or display, then checking power and wiring between indoor and outdoor units.

Many Mitsubishi communication faults occur when the outdoor unit powers up before the indoor unit, when an indoor disconnect is off, or when wiring connections between the two units are loose or interrupted. Normal communication voltage between S2 and S3 terminals is a fluctuating 10 to 24 volts DC, and each of the S1, S2, and S3 terminals carries 120 volts AC to ground.

## Common Causes

- **Misread or non-standard code display** The code may be a model-specific format or a different alphanumeric sequence that does not match standard Mitsubishi documentation.
- **Indoor unit powered off or delayed** Communication faults occur when the outdoor unit receives power before the indoor unit or when an indoor disconnect is open.
- **Loose or corroded wiring terminals** Control wiring between indoor and outdoor units at the S1, S2, and S3 terminals can lose continuity or develop high resistance.
- **Failed indoor or outdoor control board** Either the indoor control PCB or the outdoor inverter PCB may have a fault preventing serial communication.

## Step-by-Step Fix {#fix}

1. **Verify the exact code and model numbers.** Write down the complete indoor and outdoor unit model numbers and the exact text or LED pattern on the display, then consult your model's service manual or contact Mitsubishi support to confirm the code meaning.
2. **Turn off all power for three to five minutes.** Switch off both the indoor and outdoor circuit breakers, wait, then restore indoor power first if your system has separate disconnects.
3. **Restore outdoor power and observe the display.** If the code clears and the system runs normally, monitor for recurrence and check for intermittent wiring or power issues.
4. **Inspect wiring and terminal connections.** With power off, open the indoor and outdoor service panels and verify that all control-wire connections at S1, S2, and S3 are tight and free of corrosion.
5. **Use the service controller or error-history function.** Mitsubishi systems store fault history accessible through the wall controller or a technician remote, which can reveal the true numeric code and when it occurred.
6. **Measure communication voltage if the fault persists.** With power on and the system in standby, measure AC and DC voltage at S2 and S3 to confirm the indoor and outdoor boards are communicating.
7. **Replace the faulty control board or wiring.** If voltage is absent or incorrect and wiring is intact, the indoor or outdoor PCB is the likely cause and must be replaced by a qualified technician.

## When to Call a Pro

Call a qualified HVAC technician if you cannot confirm the exact error code, if power cycling does not clear the fault, or if you are uncomfortable working with 120-volt AC wiring and control boards. Mitsubishi systems require specialized tools and training to diagnose communication faults and replace control boards safely. A technician can retrieve error history, measure communication signals, and source the correct parts for your specific model and production year.
